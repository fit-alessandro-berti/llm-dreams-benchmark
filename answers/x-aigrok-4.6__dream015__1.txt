You find yourself in a post-apocalyptic world, with resources scarce and dangers everywhere. Despite the hardships, you decide to leave the relative safety of your hidden bunker and trek toward the distant mountains, chasing rumors of an untouched valley where clean water still flows and the soil can still grow food.

The journey is brutal. Dust storms blot out the sun for days at a time. Packs of feral dogs and worse things hunt at night. You ration every drop of water and every scrap of scavenged jerky, sleeping in the skeletons of old cars or under collapsed overpasses. Yet something keeps pulling you forward—an inexplicable certainty that the valley is real.

On the twelfth day you crest a ridge and see it: a hidden bowl of green, fed by a waterfall that sparkles like glass. You descend, half-convinced it’s a mirage. Instead you find other survivors already there—tired, wary people who have been tending small gardens and repairing an old hydroelectric turbine. They don’t turn you away. They hand you a shovel and a share of the evening stew.

That night, sitting around a real fire with real laughter, you realize the greatest resource wasn’t water or food. It was the decision to keep walking when everything said to stop. The dream doesn’t end there; it simply becomes something you no longer want to wake from.
