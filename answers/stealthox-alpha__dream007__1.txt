...you decide to stop talking and simply look at them.

In the dream, this feels like a decision of enormous consequence — like stepping off the edge of something very high. The moment you go quiet, the kitchen around you begins to change. The argument was about something specific — a promise broken, a secret kept too long, a choice one of you made without asking — but the details keep slipping away like water through your fingers. What remains is the heat of it, sitting between you at the table like a third person neither of you will acknowledge.

Your friend's face flickers. For a moment they are ten years old, gap-toothed, the way they looked the summer you met. Then they are a stranger. Then themselves again, crying without any change in their expression, which is somehow the saddest thing you've ever seen.

You reach across the table. Your hand is full of small gray stones — you don't remember picking them up — and you set them down one at a time, and you understand somehow that each stone is something you meant to say and didn't. Your friend picks them up one by one and puts them in their pockets, and their pockets begin to glow softly, like they're holding embers instead of stones.

"I kept them all," they say, though you're not sure if they mean the stones or the words or the years.

The floor tilts gently, the way floors do in dreams, and the kitchen is now a shoreline, and the argument is now the sound of waves, and you realize you can't remember what you were fighting about — only that you didn't want to lose them. Your friend takes your hand, or the sea does, and everything goes warm and blue at the edges.

You wake with your jaw clenched and your phone in your hand, their contact open on the screen, the cursor blinking in an empty message box.
