…written in your own handwriting, but they describe things that haven’t happened yet.

**1. Why did you leave the city without saying goodbye?**  
**2. Who was waiting for you at the station?**  
**3. Which decision saved your life, and why did you almost not make it?**

You look up, but the other students are writing calmly, as if none of this is strange. Their pens scratch in perfect unison. Above them, the clock is moving backward.

The proctor approaches. It is you, many years older, wearing an expression you can’t quite read.

“I haven’t studied any of this,” you whisper.

“No,” the proctor says. “You’ve only lived the prerequisites.”

You turn to the final page. It contains one question:

**What did you want before you learned to be afraid of wanting it?**

The room becomes very quiet. You try to think of the career, the salary, the people you hoped to impress, but each thought dissolves before you can write it down. At last, almost without meaning to, you write:

**To make something with my own hands and give it to someone who needs it.**

The clock stops.

The proctor reads your answer, then shakes their head. Your stomach drops.

“That isn’t the correct answer,” they say.

Then they smile.

“It’s only yours.”

They tear the exam in half. Behind the paper is not another sheet, but a doorway filled with morning light. Somewhere beyond it, a train is arriving, and someone is calling your name—not the name printed on the exam, but the one you had as a child.

You stand.

“How much time do I have?” you ask.

The proctor hands you a blank page.

“The rest of your life,” they say. “You may begin when you’re ready.”
