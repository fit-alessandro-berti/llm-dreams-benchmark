...notice the walls beginning to breathe. At first it's subtle, a slow swell in the hedges of stone, like the maze is sleeping and you are walking through its dream.

The first puzzle was a door with no handle, only a brass plate of twelve rotating rings engraved with symbols you somehow already knew. You turned them without thinking, and the door sighed open. That should have been a warning. In dreams, the easy things always cost something later.

The second puzzle was a hallway of mirrors, each reflecting a different version of the corridor. Only one reflection showed the ceiling stitched with stars. You closed your eyes, counted your footsteps backward, and walked *into* the mirror that didn't match. It rippled like cool water around your shoulders.

By the third puzzle, the maze had started to learn you. The riddles were no longer written on plaques. They were whispered in your own voice from somewhere just behind your ear:

*"What do you keep by giving away, and lose by holding too tightly?"*

You paused. The answer tasted like a word on the tip of your tongue, then it bloomed: *"Trust."*

The floor beneath you turned from slate to soft moss, and a lantern you hadn't been carrying appeared in your hand, lit with a small steady flame.

Deeper still, the puzzles grew quieter and stranger. One was only a chessboard with a single piece, a tiny silver key, where the queen should have been. One was a clock whose hands spun backward until you stopped watching them and simply *decided* what time it was. The clock obeyed.

At last you reached a round chamber, hushed and bright. In its center stood a pedestal, and on the pedestal sat one final puzzle: a smooth, blank stone with no seams, no symbols, no hints.

You circled it. You thought harder than you had all night, running through patterns, sequences, and tricks. Nothing worked.

Then, exhausted, you set down your lantern and sat on the floor, and the answer arrived the way answers do in dreams, sideways and obvious: the puzzle wasn't asking to be solved. It was asking if you were willing to stop.

You laid your palm on the stone. It was warm, like something that had been waiting for you.

The chamber walls dissolved into morning light. The maze unfolded like paper, corridor by corridor, until you stood in an open field of pale gold grass. In your hand, where the lantern had been, was the tiny silver key. Somewhere far off, a door you hadn't yet imagined was waiting.

And just as you took your first step toward it, you began to wake...
