…and each one seems designed to test a different part of you.

First comes the Clockwork Labyrinth: corridors of filing cabinets and ringing phones that rearrange whenever you run. The only way through is to stop rushing, listen to the faint hum beneath the noise, and follow the one sound that feels like your own heartbeat. You make it out just as the final bell rings, though you’ve lost your shoes and somehow gained a pocketful of blue buttons.

Next is the Team Challenge. You’re paired with strangers who each have exactly the skill you lack—one can calm a room with a sentence, one can build anything from paperclips, one can remember every word ever said but not where they left their coffee. Together you construct a bridge from mismatched résumés. It holds long enough for everyone to cross, and you realize the prize may not be about being the best single competitor.

Then comes the Mirror Interview. You sit across from a version of yourself who asks, “What would you do even if no one paid you?” Your practiced answer dissolves. You say something honest instead, something small and strange and true. The mirror-you smiles, which is unsettling but also kind.

At last, you reach the final room. It is empty except for a door with a blank brass nameplate. A voice says, “The grand prize is your dream job.” You open the door and find a room full of morning light, unfinished projects, good pens, and a window that looks out on a city that hasn’t been invented yet. There is no title on the door. There is only a pen.

You pick it up and write your own name.

The dream begins to dissolve, but not before you hear the voice say, “Position filled.” You wake with a vague memory of blue buttons in your hand, a strange sense that your dream job has been waiting all along—not as a place to arrive, but as a way of working, choosing, and becoming that you carry with you.
