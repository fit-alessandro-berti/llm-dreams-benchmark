…symbols that won't hold still. When you look straight at them they behave — settling into ordinary carvings, leaves or waves or some kind of script — but in the corner of your eye they crawl, rearranging themselves into something almost like handwriting. Almost like a word you know.

You trace one groove with a fingertip. It's warm. Warmer than it should be, as if the box has been lying in sunlight for hours, though there's no sun here — only the dim amber glow that dreams use in place of lamps.

Each carved line, followed far enough, ends in a tiny emblem: a key, a door, a bird, an eye. Without deciding to, you press the eye.

Nothing happens. But when you look up, the room has changed. Or rather, it's the same room, except now it is also your grandmother's kitchen, and someone is humming in the next room — a tune you know but can't name, and the knowing-without-naming makes your chest ache in a way that has nothing to do with the box.

The box is lighter now. You tip it, and instead of a rattle you hear breathing. Small and patient, in and out, like something sleeping.

And you understand — the way you understand things in dreams, without ever learning them — that the key has been in your pocket all along. You're certain of it. You checked. But when you reach in, your fingers close around cold metal teeth: a key shaped like the smallest carving, the bird with folded wings.

It doesn't fit. The lock is gone. Where it used to be there's only another carved line running around the box's edge, and you follow it with your finger, once, twice, three times, and the box opens along its seam like a flower. Or a mouth. Or an eye.

Inside: more boxes. Dozens of them, each smaller than the last, each one breathing.

You pick up the smallest. It fits in your palm. It isn't even locked. You lift the lid, and inside is this room, tiny and perfect, and kneeling in the middle of it a small figure holding a smaller box — and the figure is you. You watch yourself look down into it. You watch yourself begin to open it.

You lean closer, because you very much need to see what's inside.

And that, of course, is when you wake — hand still curled around nothing, ears still full of a tune you almost know, and the faint, unbearable certainty that somewhere, the box has just finished opening without you.
