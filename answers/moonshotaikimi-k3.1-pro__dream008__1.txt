…open the project file. It contains a single instruction:

**DELIVER THE FINISHED PROJECT BEFORE IT IS ASSIGNED.**

Your boss checks their watch. The hands are spinning in opposite directions.

You start typing, but the words refuse to stay on the screen. They slide off the monitor and scatter across your desk: paper clips, commas, tiny doors. Your coworkers rush past carrying stacks of blank paper. “Hurry,” they whisper. “It’s almost this morning.”

The clock strikes a time you’ve never heard of.

You run for the elevator, but it opens onto your desk again. You try the stairs and find yourself climbing through the office ceiling, into another office exactly like yours, where another version of you is already panicking.

Then you understand.

You turn around and walk backward.

You walk backward through the corridors, unspilling your coffee, unanswering emails, unhearing your boss explain the deadline. The office grows brighter. The clocks slow. Finally, you are standing outside your boss’s door at 8:59, holding an empty folder.

At 9:00, your boss looks up.

“I have an impossible project for you,” they say.

You place the folder on the desk.

Inside is yesterday, neatly folded.

Your boss smiles. “Excellent work. Right on time.”

For a moment, everything is still. Then the windows begin opening like browser tabs, and beyond each one is a different morning.

“Your next project,” your boss adds, “is due last week.”

You laugh—and the whole building laughs with you.

You wake to your alarm. As you reach to silence it, you notice a new calendar reminder on your phone:

**DREAM — STATUS: OVERDUE.**
