

You start to run, but your feet feel strangely light, as if the pavement is only pretending to be solid. The city unfolds around you in impossible layers—streets curving upward into the sky, alleyways that blink like tired eyes, buildings leaning close to whisper their names.

You ask a passerby for directions, but when they turn, their face is made of clock hands, all spinning at different speeds. “The meeting is where you forgot to arrive,” they say, pointing toward a tower that wasn’t there a moment ago.

You hurry toward it, passing markets selling bottled memories and bridges built from folded maps. The closer you get, the more the city rearranges itself, as though testing your urgency. Doors appear where walls should be. Staircases descend into clouds. A train glides silently overhead, its windows filled with versions of you, each one sitting calmly in a different life.

Finally, you reach the tower. Inside, an elevator opens before you can press the button. You step in, and instead of buttons, there are small, glowing words: *Regret*, *Courage*, *Childhood*, *Tomorrow*. Without thinking, you press *Now*.

The doors open into a quiet room. Everyone is already seated, but they are all empty chairs filled with soft light. At the head of the table sits a version of you who looks calm, unhurried, and kind.

“You’re right on time,” they say.

And suddenly you understand: the meeting was never about a place. It was about learning how to arrive in your own life.

You sit down, and the dream exhales.
