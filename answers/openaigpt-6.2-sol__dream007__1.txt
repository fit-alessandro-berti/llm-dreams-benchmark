“That I should have known,” they say.

You open your mouth to tell them that’s not what you meant, but you did mean it. You’ve meant it for weeks. You just hadn’t realized how much you’d been counting on never having to say it plainly.

While you’re thinking, your friend reaches across the kitchen table and turns off the stove.

You’re about to ask how they did that from all the way over here when you notice the table has gotten very small. Your knees are pressed against theirs. You’re sitting at the little red table from their childhood kitchen, the one you used to eat at after school. There’s a clementine between you with a thumbnail sunk into its peel.

“I wish you’d called me,” you say.

“I almost did. Twice.”

You wait for them to explain. They pick up the clementine, work their thumb under the peel, and hand you half. The smell is so sharp and sweet that when you wake up, you check your fingers.
