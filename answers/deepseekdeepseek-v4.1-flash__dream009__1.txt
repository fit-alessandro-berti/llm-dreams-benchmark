You realize you need to find the door you came through—except there isn’t one. The world is all timber and thatch, woodsmoke and horse dung, iron bells and hand-woven wool. No wires, no engines, no glowing screens. When you try to speak, the words come out in a language you don’t know but somehow understand. A girl with soot on her cheeks points past the wheat fields and says, “The Keeper has been waiting since the last moon.” You look down and see your shadow facing the wrong way. So you realize you need to reach the hill before sunset, ask the Keeper for your true name, and slip back into your own bed before the dream notices you’re gone. But when you start walking, the road stretches longer with every step, and the sun begins to rise in the west.
