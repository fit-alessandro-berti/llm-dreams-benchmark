You decide to build a small sanctuary in the ruins—not a fortress, but a place where people can rest without flinching at every sound. You scavenge cracked solar panels, rain barrels, and half-buried seed packets. Each evening you light one weak lamp in the window, a signal to anyone lost in the dark.

Word spreads slowly. A child arrives with a rusted key and no doors left to open. An old mechanic trades stories for soup. A stray dog becomes your first guard, though it mostly sleeps. Together you turn a collapsed parking garage into a greenhouse, coaxing tomatoes from poisoned soil with patience and filtered rain.

One night, raiders circle the perimeter. Everyone waits for you to choose between hiding and fighting. Instead, you open the gate a little and leave out bread, water, and a warning written in three languages. The raiders take the food. One of them stays.

By dawn, the lamp is still burning. The world is still broken, but in your small corner of it, something green has pushed through the concrete. You decide that is enough to keep going—and then the dream begins to hum like a radio tuning into morning.
