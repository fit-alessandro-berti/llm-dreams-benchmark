And you decide to trust them.

One of them, an older woman with kind eyes, gently takes your hand - not to pull you, but just to let you know she's there. Another, a young man with paint on his jacket, points down a street you hadn't noticed before, a quieter one where the neon lights of the bustling city soften into a warm, golden glow.

As you walk together, the noise of the city doesn't feel so overwhelming anymore. It becomes a kind of music - distant laughter, footsteps, the hum of traffic. The strangers don't rush you. They walk at your pace, chatting softly, sharing stories that make you smile for the first time in what feels like a long time.

They lead you to a small, hidden courtyard right in the middle of all the chaos. There's a fountain there, and string lights overhead, and a small cafe with an open door. On the table is a map of the city, and for the first time, you can see exactly where you are - a small, bright dot - and you can see all the paths leading home.

But more than that, you realize you aren't just finding your way on the map. That heavy, lost feeling in your chest has lifted. You feel seen. You feel connected. You feel found.

You sit with them a while longer, sipping something warm, and you understand that even in the busiest, most overwhelming places, kindness can find you - and you were never as alone as you thought you were.

You wake up with that warmth still in your chest, carrying the feeling that help is closer than it seems.
