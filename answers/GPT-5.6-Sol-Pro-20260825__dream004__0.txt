You are in a world where colors and shapes are constantly changing. As you explore this surreal landscape, you come across a blank canvas and a brush suspended in the air before it.

The moment you take the brush, the ground beneath you turns from violet sand into a sheet of clear glass. Far below, enormous silver fish swim slowly through a dark sky filled with stars. You dip the brush into a pool of blue light and paint a small door in the center of the canvas.

The door opens.

A warm wind pours through it, carrying the smell of rain, old books, and a home you cannot quite remember. On the other side is a narrow hallway lined with portraits. Every portrait shows you at a different age, but each version of you is looking toward something outside the frame.

As you walk past them, they begin to whisper.

“Not that way.”

“Go back.”

“You forgot something.”

At the end of the hallway, you find a little girl sitting on the floor. She is drawing the strange world you have just left—the shifting colors, the glass earth, the fish beneath the stars. When she looks up, you recognize her. She is you, though you do not remember ever being her.

“You took a long time,” she says.

“To do what?”

“To finish it.”

She hands you a crayon the color of something that has no name. On the wall beside her is another blank canvas. You draw a circle, but it becomes an eye. The eye blinks, and the entire hallway folds inward like paper.

Suddenly, you are falling through all the pictures at once.

You fall through birthdays you have forgotten, houses you never lived in, conversations that have not happened yet. You see yourself laughing with strangers who feel like family. You see an empty chair at a crowded table. You see a red thread stretching through every moment, tying each version of you together.

You reach for it.

The falling stops.

You are standing once more before the original canvas, but it is no longer blank. It now shows you holding the red thread, surrounded by every place you have ever loved and every place you have feared entering. In the corner of the painting, the little girl has written a sentence:

**You were never lost. You were only unfinished.**

Behind you, something enormous takes a breath.

You turn and see that the whole landscape—the mountains, the sky, the changing shapes—is one sleeping creature. Its eyes slowly open. Inside each pupil is the room where your waking body lies asleep.

The creature speaks without moving its mouth.

“You may wake now,” it says, “or you may paint one more thing.”

You look at the brush in your hand.

Then you smile and paint a sunrise in a color the waking world has never seen.
