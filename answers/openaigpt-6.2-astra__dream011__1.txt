**1. When someone asks what you do, what would you like to be able to tell them?**

You know the answer you practiced. It’s the one that makes your father nod and say, “Good, good,” twice. You put the tip of your pen to the paper. Before you can write it down, the girl beside you raises her hand.

“Can we change an answer later?”

The proctor looks up from his paperback. “I should hope so.”

You turn the page.

**2. What did you want to do before you knew the names of jobs?**

This one is easy. When you were seven, you wanted to mend the moon. You’d seen it come up one night with a piece missing, and your mother found you in the yard in your pajamas, dragging the aluminum ladder out from under the porch.

You write: *I wanted to fix things.*

Somewhere behind you, a chair stops squeaking.

*Actually,* you add, *I wanted to get close enough to see what was wrong.*

The clock gives a single loud tick. Everyone starts handing in their papers. You haven’t answered the first question yet. You turn back to it, hurriedly, and find that the ink has faded everywhere except beneath the words *what you do*.

The proctor holds out his hand. “Ready?”

“Not quite.”

“All right.” He takes his coat off the back of his chair. “Lock up when you’re done.”

Soon you are alone in the exam hall. Rain is coming in through the high windows, dotting your paper. You look up. One of the panes has slipped its catch.

Under the last row of seats, exactly where you remember leaving it, is the aluminum ladder.
