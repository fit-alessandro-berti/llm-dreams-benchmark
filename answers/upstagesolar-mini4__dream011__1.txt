...not written in text.

The notebook before you is blank, save for a single, glowing line of equations in the center of the page. In the margin, written in elegant, looping script, is a familiar phrase: **"The solution is not in the numbers. It is in the space between them."**

You hear a soft chime. A voice, calm and familiar, seems to come from all directions at once.

*"Open your notebook,"* the voice says.

You turn the page. Instead of the usual syllabus or notes, you find a mirror. Your own reflection stares back—not looking at you, but at something just beyond your sight. The face in the mirror is yours, but slightly older, eyes sharper, posture straighter. The reflection smiles, but you do not.

*"This exam does not test what you remember,"* the reflection says. *"It tests what you are willing to question."*

You look back at the equations. They hover, shifting, rearranging into a pattern that resembles a circuit, a conversation, a question mark. The symbols are not symbols at all. They are footprints. Each one is a choice you've made, a path you've followed, or one you've left behind.

The exam asks a single, deceptively simple question:

**"What is the thing you have avoided because you are afraid it might reveal you are not ready?"**

Your pulse quickens. You expected formulas. You expected names, dates, closed-ended answers. Instead, you realize the exam is not a test of knowledge, but of honesty. The dream does not offer a multiple-choice key. It offers only the space in front of you: the space between your thoughts, between what you know and what you feel, between the question and the silence it requires.

You take a breath. You do not try to force a formula. You do not search for a hidden key. You simply look at the equation, then look down at your own hands, noticing the faint lines of ink, the texture of the paper, the quiet certainty that you do not need to prove anything right now.

The reflection in the mirror finally meets your eyes. A single, steady nod. The chime returns, softer this time. The dream begins to dissolve, not into alarm, but into the hum of a new question: *What do you wish to build next?*

You realize, with a quiet clarity, that the exam was never about escaping into the future. It was about stepping into it with your eyes open. And as the page fades to white, you write, for the first time, a single sentence in your own words:

*"I will not let fear narrow what I am capable of."*

The dream ends. The last thing you remember is not an answer, but an opening.
