...the air grows thick and electric, tasting faintly of copper and rain. The trees around you begin to sway in ways trees shouldn't—bending toward you rather than away from the wind, their leaves whispering something almost like words.

You start to run, but your legs feel heavy, moving through honey. Behind you, thunder cracks—not a sound so much as a *feeling*, deep in your chest, like a heartbeat belonging to something enormous. The sky above splits open, but instead of rain, soft white feathers drift down, catching in your hair, dissolving on your skin like snow.

A light appears between the trees ahead. Warm. Golden. You stumble toward it as the storm howls at your back, and when you break through the last wall of branches, you find yourself standing at the edge of a lake so still it mirrors the churning clouds perfectly—a calm eye at the center of chaos.

In the water's reflection, you notice the storm has a shape: a great bird made of cloud and lightning, wings spread wide, watching you. It doesn't feel threatening anymore. It feels like it's been waiting for you.

You kneel at the water's edge, and your reflection smiles back at you a half-second before you do.

"You remembered," the reflection says, though your lips never move.

Remembered what? But even as the question forms, you feel it—the storm isn't chasing you. It's been *carrying* you, all this time, through every quiet forest and winding path. The feathers falling around you are its way of letting go.

The great bird exhales one final roll of thunder, gentle now, like a lullaby ending. The clouds unravel into stars. The lake glows.

And just before you wake, you realize you're no longer afraid of storms—you never were. You were only ever afraid of standing still.

You open your eyes. Rain taps softly against your window, and somehow, it sounds like applause.
