The carvings shift when you look away—a bird becomes a key, a key becomes a winding path, a path becomes an eye that watches you with patient recognition.

You trace one of the grooves with your thumb, and the box gives a soft click, not opening, but acknowledging you. A voice that isn’t a voice says, *The lock isn’t in the box. It’s in the question you haven’t asked.* You close your eyes, and behind your lids you see a door you’ve never noticed before, painted the color of your oldest memory. You push it open.

There is the box again, already unlocked. Inside is a small folded note in your own handwriting. It reads: *You were always the key.*

When you open your eyes, the box is gone. But your hand is still curved around its shape, warm and humming faintly, as if something inside you has just clicked into place.
