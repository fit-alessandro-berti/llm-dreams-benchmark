For a moment, you can’t remember what you came to say. Your notes are blank. The audience sits in perfect silence, every face tilted toward you with the patience of someone who has traveled a very long way to hear your voice.

“Once,” you begin, “I dreamed I was giving a speech. I woke up just before I said my first word.”

A ripple of laughter moves through the crowd, warm and approving. You almost stop, but something makes you continue.

“This time, I think I know what I came to say.”

Behind you, where the wall should be, a blue door appears. It stands slightly open, and through the gap comes the scent of rain on grass. The audience begins to rise, all turning toward it.

You look down at your notes again. A single sentence has appeared in your handwriting:

**Don’t be afraid to wake up.**

The blue door swings wider. Beyond it is a dark room, and standing at the lectern is a smaller you, mouth open, waiting for the words that will help everyone fall asleep.

You step off the stage toward them.

For the first time, you know how the speech ends.
