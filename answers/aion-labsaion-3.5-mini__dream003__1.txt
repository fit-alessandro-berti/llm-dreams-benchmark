You run your fingertips across the carvings. They're warm, almost feverish, and as you trace one of the spirals, you feel a faint hum travel up your arm, like the box is purring, or breathing. The patterns aren't decoration, you realize. They're a language — one you almost remember knowing, the way dreams let you fluently speak tongues you've never learned.

There's no keyhole. Instead, at the center of the box, there's a small indentation shaped like a question mark. You press your thumb into it, and the box whispers — not words exactly, but the feeling of words, like a voice heard through a wall.

*"What do you hope is inside?"*

And the strange thing is, the moment you try to answer, the box grows heavier. The patterns dim. The harder you want it open, the more it seals itself, the lid fusing shut like scar tissue.

So you stop wanting. You let your hands go still. You just sit with it, the way you'd sit with a sleeping animal, and you ask nothing.

That's when the lid drifts open on its own — slow as a cloud moving — and inside you find no gold, no secret, no answer. You find a small, folded map of the dream you're currently in, drawn in your own handwriting, with a red X marking the exact spot where you're sitting. Beneath the map is a tiny light, no bigger than a firefly, and it climbs onto your palm and settles there, warm as the carvings.

The edges of the dream begin to soften and blur. The box is dissolving, the room is dissolving, but the light stays.

You wake with your fist loosely closed on the mattress, and for a moment — just a moment — your palm is faintly warm, and you can still feel the hum.
