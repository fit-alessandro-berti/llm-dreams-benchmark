...a single brush lying across it like a fallen leaf, its bristles still wet with a color that has no name yet.

As you step closer, the canvas begins to breathe. The blankness ripples like water held in a cupped hand. You reach out to touch it—and your fingertip sinks through the surface into a warm, humming silence that tastes faintly of rain on hot stone.

Then the dream turns.

The color of the brush spreads across your skin and you realize your arm has become a river, and the brush the delta, and the canvas the sea it has been waiting all along to join. Shapes rise from the riverbed in slow procession: a staircase made of folded moths, a bell whose sound arrives before it does, a doorframe with nothing behind it that somehow still opens.

On the far side of the doorway stands a small figure wearing your face exactly, but older, or younger—the dream refuses to commit. It holds out a paint made from the crushed remains of every color that ever lost an argument.

"You'll need one," it says, "for the part you haven't imagined yet."

You look down at your hand. The brush is there, waiting to be held.
