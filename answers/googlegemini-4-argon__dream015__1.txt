...plant a garden inside the hollowed-out hull of a rusted subway car. 

Because it is a dream, the rules of the ruined world bend slightly. The soil isn't dirt, but finely crushed glass and ash that somehow feels warm and soft in my hands. From my pockets, I pull seeds that look like tiny, glowing embers. When I press them into the gray earth, they don't grow into food—they grow into melodies. 

Translucent vines snake up the shattered windows of the train car, blooming into bioluminescent flowers shaped like gramophone horns. Instead of scent, they release the faint, crackling sound of old jazz records, the laughter of children, and the steady rhythm of falling rain. 

Outside the subway car, the acidic fog rolls back. The terrifying, shadowy figures that usually stalk the wasteland—creatures made of twisted scrap metal and static—stop at the edge of the light. Drawn by the music, they slowly approach. I feel a spike of fear, waiting for them to attack, to tear down this fragile sanctuary. 

Instead, they sit in the dust. As the music washes over them, the jagged rust flakes away from their bodies, turning into flocks of paper birds that fly up into the bruised purple sky. 

I look down at my hands and realize the glowing vines are wrapping around my wrists, anchoring me to the floor. I am becoming part of the garden. I don't fight it. I take a deep breath of the metallic air, exhale a cloud of golden pollen, and just as the brightest flower blooms right in front of my face, blinding me with a warm, humming light... I wake up.
