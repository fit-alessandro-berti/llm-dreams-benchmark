...walk away to cool off, turning toward the living room door. 

Except when I pull the handle, the door doesn't open to the hallway. It opens into a vast, waist-deep ocean that stretches to the horizon, lit by two massive, purple moons. 

The anger in my chest instantly evaporates, replaced by that strange, heavy logic that only exists in dreams. Suddenly, I can't remember what we were fighting about—was it a broken promise, or the fact that we had forgotten to feed a giant clockwork bird? It still *feels* monumentally important, though.

I turn back to my friend, who is no longer standing in a living room, either. The walls have melted into towering bookshelves made of drifting sand. My friend isn't yelling anymore. Instead, they are frantically trying to catch their own words, which are now floating out of their mouth as glowing, golden origami cranes. 

"If they touch the water, we'll forget the secret!" my friend shouts, though their voice comes from somewhere behind me. 

Realizing the argument was actually a mission we were failing together, the tension between us transforms into urgent cooperation. I dive forward—moving in frustrating, underwater slow-motion—and snatch the largest golden crane just before it hits the waves. 

The moment my fingers close around the paper bird, it turns into an old, freezing-cold brass key. My friend looks at me, relieved, and nods toward the bottom of the ocean, where a single wooden door is glowing in the dark depths. 

Knowing instantly what we have to do, we both take a deep breath and dive under the surface—and the sudden chill of the water wakes me up.
