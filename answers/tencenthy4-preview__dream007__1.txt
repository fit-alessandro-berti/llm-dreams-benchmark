...stop. 

Not to shout louder, not to storm off, not to deliver the perfectly rehearsed line that would finally make you understood—but to simply cease. 

The air between you, thick with unsaid things and sharpened syllables, begins to shimmer. The room around you stretches and warps; the furniture elongates like taffy, and the ceiling dissolves entirely, giving way to a sky the color of a healing bruise—deep violet fading into a soft, watery gold. 

Your friend’s mouth is still moving, still forming the shape of their argument, but instead of words, monarch butterflies spill out—hundreds of them, then thousands—filling the space between you with the dry, rustling sound of soft wings. You look down and realize the thing you were fighting about—that heavy, important, terrible thing that felt like it was tearing you both apart—is sitting in the center of the room. It’s a small, glowing cube, no bigger than a die, pulsing with a light that feels like a slow, steady heartbeat.

You both reach for it at the same time. Your hands brush, and the cube doesn’t shatter—it dissolves. It turns into a swirl of golden dust that envelops you both, warm and sweet-smelling, like crushed sunlight and old books. In that moment, the anger doesn’t just fade; it evaporates, leaving behind a profound, liquid clarity. You realize that in the strange, fluid logic of this dream, being *right* was never nearly as important as being *here*, together.

"I never wanted to fight," your friend says, but their voice is the sound of a distant cello, rich and mournful and beautiful.

"I know," you answer, and you mean it more than you’ve meant anything in the waking world.

The ground beneath you turns to still, dark water, and you’re both floating, side by side, looking up at a sky where the stars are rearranging themselves into the shape of a doorway. You take their hand. It feels exactly as you remember it, and also like nothing you’ve ever touched before.

And as you step through the door together, you wake up—not with a gasp, but with a slow, rolling sense of peace, the dream still clinging to you like sea mist, leaving you with the quiet certainty that some things don’t need to be solved, only felt.
