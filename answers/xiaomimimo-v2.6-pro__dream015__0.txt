You find yourself in a post-apocalyptic world, with resources scarce and dangers everywhere. Despite the hardships, you decide to **search for others**.

The cracked asphalt crunches beneath your boots as you follow what used to be a highway, now swallowed by creeping vines and rusted-out cars. You've been alone for weeks — maybe months. Time has a way of losing its edges when the sunsets all blur together in hues of ash and amber.

You cling to a rumor, barely more than a whisper you overheard from a dying stranger in a collapsed gas station: *"There's a settlement near the river. They have water. They have gardens."*

That's enough.

You ration the last of your canned beans and fill your canteen from a rain barrel, filtering it through a scrap of cloth. Somewhere in the distance, you hear the telltale groaning of the **Revenants** — the hollowed-out people who wandered too close to the blight zones and came back... changed. Their joints bend the wrong way now. Their eyes glow a sickly violet.

You duck behind a crumbling overpass and hold your breath.

They pass. They always pass.

Days blur forward. You trade with a lone scavenger at a crossroads — a pocketknife for a map that might be outdated, might be wrong, but is *something*. On it, someone has circled a spot near the river in faded red ink.

And then — you hear it.

Laughter. *Children's laughter.*

You push through a wall of overgrown brush and stop. Before you is a walled compound built from shipping containers and reclaimed lumber. Solar panels dot the rooftops. A woman with a crossbow spots you first and calls out sharply.

"Hands where I can see them!"

You raise your hands slowly, smiling despite everything.

"I heard you have gardens," you say.

A long silence. Then the gate creaks open.

Inside, a dozen people work the soil, tend small fires, and *live*. A boy offers you a plate of roasted sweet potatoes and fresh greens, and the taste is so vivid, so *real*, that your eyes sting.

"You staying?" the woman with the crossbow asks.

You look back at the world you survived to cross — the empty roads, the violet-eyed horizons, the endless gray.

Then you look at the garden.

"Yeah," you say. "I'm staying."

And for the first time in a long time, when you close your eyes... you dream of something other than survival. 🌱
