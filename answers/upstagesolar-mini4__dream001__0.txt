You start to walk down a street that seems to stretch endlessly in every direction, each block made of identical glass and steel. Everywhere you look, there are people rushing past, everyone hurrying toward somewhere, but you're the only one standing still, the only one without a destination. A warm breeze carries the scent of rain and roasted coffee, and you notice a small, glowing café on the corner. You walk in, not because you need coffee, but because you need to breathe.

Inside, the chairs are soft, the lights are low, and someone nearby is reading a book you almost recognize. The barista smiles at you and asks, "Did you lose your way, or are you taking a moment to find it?" You hesitate, then you laugh—a small, shaky sound. "I have a meeting in an hour, but I don't know where."

The barista sets down a cup and says, "Then you're already here." They hand you the coffee. Warm. Bitter. Real. And as you take the first sip, you notice the clock on the counter reads 45 minutes to your meeting. You have just enough time to stand up, stretch, and look at the street outside. It's calm. The lights are softer. The people are moving differently—slower, as if they've all stopped rushing.

You step back out. The city doesn't feel unfamiliar anymore. It feels heavy with possibility. And you realize, with a quiet certainty, that where you need to go isn't a place you have to find. It's a direction you have to choose.

You head toward the first door that opens into a hallway lined with windows, and for the first time, you're not afraid of being late. Because sometimes, in the middle of a city that doesn't make sense, the most important meeting is the one you're about to have with yourself.
