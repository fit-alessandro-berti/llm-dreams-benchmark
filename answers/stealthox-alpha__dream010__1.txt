...and each one is stranger than the last.

The first challenge is an interview, but the panel is made up of every teacher you've ever had, seated at a table that grows longer the more you walk toward it. They ask questions in a language you somehow understand perfectly but could never repeat aloud. Each time you answer, a warm smell drifts through the room — fresh bread, rain on hot pavement — and you feel calmer.

The second challenge is a written test, except the paper is an ocean, and you have to write your answers in waves. You panic for a moment, then realize you know every answer — but only while you're underwater. So you dive in, holding a breath that never runs out, and your answers bloom in the deep like luminous jellyfish.

The third challenge is to teach a room full of strangers something you love. When you open your mouth, nothing comes out — the old dream silence. But then you start moving your hands, and the shapes you draw in the air lift off and become small glowing birds, and the strangers nod like they understand completely.

In the waiting room between rounds, you study the other competitors. Some of them have your face. One is you from ten years ago, chewing a pen, terrified. One might be you from ten years from now, utterly serene. The serene one leans over and whispers: *"The prize was never the job."*

Then the host announces the final round: there isn't one. A judge simply places an old brass key in your palm. The door it opens isn't an office or a studio — it's a perfect recreation of the first place you ever lost track of time doing something you loved. Dust motes in the light. The same worn chair. Everything humming with possibility.

You step inside, and just as you turn to ask what happens next, the walls go soft, the key becomes a bird and flies out through the window, and something — an alarm, a knock, someone saying your name — pulls you gently upward.

You wake with your heart pounding and one strange, certain thought:

You already know what your dream job is. You've known all along. You just hadn't been calling it that yet.
