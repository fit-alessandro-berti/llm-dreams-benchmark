...a single brush lying beside it, its bristles glowing faintly, like embers that forgot how to burn.

The canvas is the only thing in this place that doesn't move. Everything else shifts. The ground ripples from violet to amber beneath my feet, and the trees fold themselves into spirals and triangles. Even the sky breathes in slow waves of turquoise and rust. But the canvas stays white, perfectly still, as if it's waiting for me.

I pick up the brush. It's warm, and it hums in my hand like it's been expecting me for a very long time.

When I touch the bristles to the canvas, no paint comes out. A *memory* does. A thin line of gold spreads across the white, and suddenly I can smell my grandmother's kitchen, cinnamon and burnt sugar. The line curls into the shape of a doorway.

I paint again, and the colors come from somewhere inside me. A streak of deep blue carries the feeling of swimming at night. A smudge of green holds the sound of laughter I haven't heard in years. The brush moves faster than I can think, and the canvas fills with a landscape I almost recognize: a town I've never visited, but somehow know I've lived in.

Then I notice something strange. The world around me is slowing down. The shifting colors are settling. The spiraling trees relax into ordinary oaks and willows. The sky holds still in a soft evening lavender. It's as if every stroke on the canvas is anchoring the dream, giving it a shape it can finally keep.

When the painting is nearly finished, I step back. The golden doorway I painted first is glowing now, and it's slightly open. Warm light spills out onto the painted street, and then, impossibly, onto the real ground at my feet.

A small figure stands at the threshold. It's a child, and it takes me a moment to realize it's *me*, much younger, holding a crayon and grinning as if we share a secret.

"You finally came back," the child says. "I've been keeping it safe."

"Keeping what safe?"

The child points at the brush in my hand, then at the canvas, then at the whole quiet, glowing world around us.

"The part of you that makes things."

I look down at the brush. The bristles have run dry, and the glow is fading. When I look up again, the child is holding the door open wider, and beyond it I can see countless more blank canvases, stretching into the distance like pages in an endless book.

"There's always more," the child says. "You just have to remember to come back."

I take a step toward the door. The colors around me begin to swirl again, gently this time, like a lullaby. I feel myself rising, lightening, the edges of the dream softening...

And just before I wake, I hear the faint whisper of bristles against canvas, as if somewhere, something I started is still being painted.
