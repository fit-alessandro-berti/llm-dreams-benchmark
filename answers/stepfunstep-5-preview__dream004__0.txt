…and it pulls at you like a held breath. It hangs in the air with no frame, no hook, no ground holding it up, perfectly still in a world that refuses to stay still.

When you touch it, the air goes quiet.

A brush appears in your hand—not made of bristles, but of light, warm and humming. You lift it, and against the blank white you paint a single line.

The line lifts off the canvas and becomes a river.

The river turns purple, then silver, then a color your waking mind has no name for. Shapes melt along its banks: trees made of spirals, mountains folding into staircases, clouds hardening into glass and drifting away as tiny bells.

You realize, with dream-logic certainty, that the canvas is not a picture of this world. It is where this world is written.

So you paint a door.

It grows small windows that show memories you don’t remember having. You paint a horizon, and the ground beneath you shifts to meet it. You paint a bird, and it flies into the sky and scatters into a thousand rearranged stars.

Finally, you paint yourself stepping through the door.

The moment you do, the world exhales. The colors settle. The shapes stop spinning. On the other side of the canvas, the landscape is calm and impossibly familiar, as though it had been waiting for you all along.

You step through.

Behind you, the blank canvas goes white again—except for one tiny mark in the corner, like a signature.

And just as you turn to see what it says, morning pulls you awake.
