You don’t paint on it. It paints on you.

The first color—a soft, pulsing violet—leaks from your fingertips and flows up your arms, staining your skin with constellations you’ve never seen. Your shadow stretches sideways, splits into three, and begins to hum in a key you remember from a childhood lullaby.

You step back, but the canvas follows. It tilts like a mirror made of weather. Inside it, you see a version of yourself walking through a forest of glass flowers, each one chiming as it catches the ever-shifting light. You realize: that’s not a reflection. That’s a possibility. And it’s waiting for you to decide which shape to become.

So you close your eyes, take a breath, and let the world change you back.

When you open them, you’re no longer standing in front of the canvas. You’re inside it. The colors have become your thoughts, the shapes your memories, and the blank space ahead is the only thing left that has never been dreamed.

You take a step forward—and the dream begins again, this time from the other side.
