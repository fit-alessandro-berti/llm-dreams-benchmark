

…you realize you are holding a brush made of moonlight.

The canvas is not truly blank, though. It is waiting. As your hand moves, the world around you leans closer, curious, and the shifting colors pause for a moment to watch what you might paint.

You touch the surface, and instead of paint, a sound appears: a soft, silver hum that spreads outward like ripples. The shapes in the distance begin to rearrange themselves into questions—circles with no centers, squares with extra corners, triangles that look like folded doors.

You keep painting.

Where you draw a line, a path opens beneath your feet.  
Where you make a dot, a star falls upward into the sky.  
Where you erase, something older returns: a forgotten melody, a scent of rain, a face you almost recognize but cannot name.

The canvas grows larger as you work, or perhaps you are growing smaller. It is hard to tell. The dream has no edges anymore.

Then, at last, you finish the painting.

It shows the world exactly as it is: changing, unfinished, impossible, and beautiful.

And as you turn to show it to the changing colors and shapes around you, you notice they have become viewers—silent, watchful, and waiting to see whether you will remember it when you wake.
