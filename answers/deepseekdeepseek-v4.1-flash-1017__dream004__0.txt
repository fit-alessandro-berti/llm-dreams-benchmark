…and it isn’t blank for long. The edges begin to breathe, curling like wet paper, and colors seep out of your fingertips before you even decide to paint. You touch the canvas, and it touches back—not with hands, but with memory. A sky you’ve never seen blooms across it, filled with birds made of folded light. Rivers run upward, carrying small doors that open onto rooms you almost recognize. Every brushstroke you imagine appears a second before you think it, so that the canvas seems to be dreaming you in return. You paint a figure standing at the horizon, and it turns its head. You paint a path, and it unrolls beneath your feet, pulling you into the picture. The world’s shifting colors settle for one breath—just long enough to show you a shape that feels like home—then dissolve into a thousand harmless stars. As you step through the canvas, you hear something faintly humming on the other side: the quiet sound of yourself waking up, still holding an invisible brush.
