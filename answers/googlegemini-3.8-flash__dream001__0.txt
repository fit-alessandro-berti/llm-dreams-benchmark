The figure turns. Where their face should be, there is only a gently rippling pool of dark water that catches the neon signs of the street. "You’re already late," they whisper, the voice coming from somewhere behind your own ribs. "They’ve been waiting since before the tide came in."

Panic gives way to the strange, fluid gravity of dream-logic. You don't ask for directions again; your feet simply take over, launching you into long, suspended leaps that carry you over traffic-choked avenues and quiet, flooded alleys where gondolas drift between parked sedans.

You approach, your footsteps echoing like heartbeats across the void. As you pull out the heavy chair at the head of the table and sit down, the spinning clock in the distance suddenly freezes, falling into dead silence.
