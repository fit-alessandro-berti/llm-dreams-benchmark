Here is how your dream continues:

As you examine it closely, you notice intricate patterns and realize they aren't patterns at all.

They're tiny, winding lines of light just beneath the surface of the wood — like veins, or rivers seen from far above. When you hold your breath, they slow down. When your heart beats faster, they pulse faster, perfectly in sync with you.

There is no keyhole, no latch, no seam where a lid should be. Just smooth, warm wood and that living map of light.

You run your thumb across it, meaning to wipe away some dust, and the lines ripple away from your touch like water. Where your fingerprint was, the box has now taken its shape. A perfect whorl of light, your thumbprint, glowing softly among the rivers.

Then you understand: the pattern *is* the lock.

You lean closer and see deeper now — the lines aren't abstract. One curve is the street you grew up on. One knot is the window of your childhood bedroom. One faint, flickering thread is a face you haven't thought of in years, but instantly ache to remember.

The box is not locked to keep you out. It's locked to keep it all in.

You whisper, without meaning to, "Open."

It doesn't click. It *breathes*.

The sides soften and fold inward like paper, like lungs, and there is no loud revelation inside. No gold, no light bursting out.

Inside is just another, smaller box. Identical to the first, with its own swirling patterns.

And inside that one, another. And another. Each one smaller, each one humming a little higher, like a note climbing a scale.

You reach for the smallest one — now no bigger than a dice in your palm — and somehow you know that inside *that* one is not a box, but a single folded piece of paper with something written on it in your own handwriting. You just can't read it yet because the letters are still swimming into place.

And just as they start to settle and form a word, you get that familiar, floating feeling — the dream realizing it's a dream.

You try to hold on, to read the paper, but the box gently closes itself in your hand, warm and satisfied, as if to say: *not yet. You'll remember when you need to.*

Then you wake up — with your palm still faintly tingling where you were holding it.
