When you trace one curve with your fingertip, the box grows warm, then ice-cold, then warm again, synchronizing with the rhythm of your pulse.

You notice there is no lock mechanism, no keyhole, only a seam that runs vertically down the center—hairline-thin, pulsing with a soft amber light. And where a latch should be, there is a depression shaped exactly like your palm.

When you press your hand against it, the box doesn't open outward. It opens *inward*—like a mouth, like a flower blooming in reverse, like the horizon folding up to meet the sky. Inside, there is no darkness but a landscape: an endless library where the books are made of stained glass and hum lullabies when the wind touches them (and the wind is you, breathing). At the center of the room sits a chair, and in the chair sits a figure wearing your face but older, wearing the lines of a life you haven't lived yet, crying tears that crystallize into birds as they fall.

The figure looks up and smiles, though their lips don't move. *You finally found the way in,* the thought arrives directly in your mind, warm as honey. *The key was never missing. You were just holding it all along, clenched so tight you forgot you had fingers.*

You reach out to touch them, but your hand passes through the glass, through the smoke, through the dream itself. The figure is dissolving into the amber light, becoming the patterns on the box, becoming the seam, becoming the pulse in your wrist.

And then you wake—or perhaps you don't—lying on your back with the distinct sensation of a hairline seam running down the center of your chest, pulsing with that same soft amber glow, waiting for you to press your palm against your own heart and remember how to open yourself.
