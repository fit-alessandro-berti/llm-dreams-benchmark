…and each group seems to have a different answer.

A circle of people in silver masks tells you your friend is by the fountain, though there is no fountain. A table of elegantly dressed strangers insists they just saw them near the doors, but the doors keep opening into other crowded rooms. A choir in matching blue jackets hums your friend’s name, then forgets it halfway through the song.

You move through the gathering as the music changes tempo without warning. The chandeliers drip light like warm rain. Someone hands you a glass of sparkling water that tastes like birthday cake. Someone else asks if you’re “with the others,” and though you don’t know who the others are, you nod.

Every time you think you’ve spotted your friend—a familiar sleeve, a laugh, the back of their head—the person turns around and becomes someone else. Still, no one seems surprised. They simply smile and say, “They were just here.”

At last, you step onto a balcony where the crowd’s noise softens into a distant sea. Your friend is sitting there at a small round table, two cups of coffee steaming in the cool air.

“Where were you?” they ask.

You try to explain: the masks, the fountain, the doors, the impossible rooms. But when you look back, the ballroom is quiet and empty, as if everyone has been waiting for you to notice.

Your friend slides one cup across the table.

“I know,” they say gently. “Dreams are like that.”

You reach for the cup, and the warm ceramic touches your fingers—then the whole balcony folds into white light, and you wake with the sound of music still echoing in your chest.
