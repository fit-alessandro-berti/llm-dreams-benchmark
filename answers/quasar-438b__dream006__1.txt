You are about to give a speech to a large audience. As you step onto the stage, you see the crowd looking expectantly at you, and you begin to speak.

But the words that come out aren't your own. They're someone else's entirely—someone you've never met, speaking from a place you've never been. Your voice carries a weight and resonance that seems to bypass your throat entirely, as if the words are being dropped directly into the air from some height above you.

The audience leans forward. Not in mockery, not in challenge, but in genuine curiosity. They sense it too—that something is off-track, that some current has diverted you from wherever you meant to stand.

You keep speaking because the current insists. The words become more specific. Names appear—Lucia, Hadrien, Vo—spoken as though you should know them, as though they should mean something. Places too. A square. A river, narrow and cold. A road that only ever goes where it's told.

And then the stage seems to widen. The seats behind the crowd recede, stretch, blur into something like fog or forest. The faces in front of you sharpen, clarify—you can see the textures of their coats, the weight of their silence.

Then, at the very front of the audience, a figure that was not there before has materialized fully. Lucid and still. She stands parallel to you, facing slightly outwards—towards you, towards the river behind. Hallsleeve-to-evergreen.

She is weather you into a thought. She does not speak. But the figure catches at some height that your gesture cannot amend.

And something tells you: the river wants…
