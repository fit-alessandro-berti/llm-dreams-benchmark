In the ash-choked ruins, where rusted cars line the cracked highways like sleeping beasts, you find a forgotten backpack half-buried in dust. Inside is a cracked compass, a dented tin of peaches, and a photograph of a garden that no longer exists. You keep the food, pocket the compass, and study the photo until the flowers seem to breathe.

You walk toward the only place that ever gave you a dream in the waking world: the old library on the hill, its windows gone, its shelves still standing like the ribs of a buried ship. Every step is careful—the earth creaks, crows watch with too many eyes, and the wind sounds like someone whispering your name.

There, beneath a fallen ceiling, you find a notebook left by another survivor. Pages describe clean water beyond the eastern ridge, a hidden garden where seeds grow in moonlight, and a group of people who still speak of kindness as if it were a currency. You tear out a map, write your name on the first page, and add your own rule:

If you pass this way again, know that you are not alone.

As the sun sets, the ruined world glows amber, and for one impossible moment, the dead trees around you bloom.
