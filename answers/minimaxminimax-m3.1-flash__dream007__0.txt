The room goes unnaturally quiet. The walls of the café stretch upward, and every table except yours is empty. Your friend’s face flickers between the person you know now and someone much older, someone you have not yet met.

“What are you afraid I’ll say?” they ask.

You realize, with dreamlike certainty, that the argument has never really been about the issue. It is about the years when you chose silence, the promises you broke by telling half-truths, and the times you made them feel unimportant.

So you say, “I’m afraid you’ll leave—and that you’ll be right to.”

Your friend exhales. The café becomes the kitchen at home, warm and yellow, filled with the smell of coffee. They reach across the table and push an old photograph toward you. In it, both of you are younger, laughing for a reason neither of you can remember.

Then you decide on something you should have decided long ago: not merely to apologize, but to change.

In the dream, change begins quietly, with the next true thing you say.
