Then I hear it. A whisper from inside the box. Not words exactly, but a melody — like a lullaby half-remembered from childhood. It tugs at something behind my eyes, and suddenly I understand: the box isn't locked. It's *listening.*

I close my eyes and think of something — not a word or a number, but a feeling. The feeling of standing barefoot in cool grass at dawn, watching the sky catch fire with gold. The air is sharp and full of possibility.

The box shudders.

The symbols flare to life, glowing brilliant blue, then white, then every color at once. The seamless surface peels open like a flower blooming in time-lapse, petals of ancient metal folding back in impossible directions.

Inside, there is no treasure — at least, not the kind I expected. There is a mirror. My reflection stares back at me, but it is *wrong.* The figure in the mirror is wearing my face, but its eyes are older. Knows things I don't. It smiles — not unkindly — and raises one hand.

I raise mine in return.

The glass ripples like water.

Suddenly I am falling — not down, but *through* — through the mirror, through the dream, through layers of half-remembered dreams nested like Russian dolls. And then I land. Softly. On grass that feels exactly like that cool dawn I was thinking of.

I open my eyes.

I am standing barefoot in a field of impossible green, stretching to every horizon. The sky is stitched together with constellations I've never seen — though they feel familiar, like old friends whose names I've forgotten.

Behind me, the box sits closed again. Sealed. Patient.

In front of me, a path winds forward into the golden light.

And on the wind, the whisper returns — but now I can hear the words clearly:

I take a breath. I step forward.
