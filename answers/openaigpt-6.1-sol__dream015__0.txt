build a garden.

Not a fortress. Not a weapon. A garden, on the roof of an abandoned supermarket, where the sun still reaches through the dust.

You carry soil upstairs in a cracked cooking pot. You collect rain in shopping carts lined with plastic. Your first seed is something you find in the pocket of a coat hanging in an empty house. You don’t know what it will become.

At night, something enormous walks between the buildings. You hear its footsteps through the concrete, but when you look over the roof’s edge, the streets are empty.

On the seventh morning, the seed sprouts.

By noon, it has grown into a door.

It stands upright among the dirt and tiny weeds, painted the blue of a sky you barely remember. Behind it, someone is humming your mother’s favorite song.

You open it.

On the other side is a kitchen. Warm light. Bread cooling on a table. Your mother sits beside the window, younger than you are now.

“You took your time,” she says.

You want to stay. The chair opposite her is pulled out, and you can already feel how good it would be to sit down. But beyond your shoulder, on the roof, you hear the scrape of your neighbor’s empty water bucket. She has climbed six flights to see whether the rain barrels caught anything.

“Can I bring someone?” you ask.

Your mother looks past you. “We’ll need more chairs.”

You return to the roof and hold the door open.

Below, the enormous footsteps stop.

A little girl emerges from the stairwell carrying a broken mug. She stares at the kitchen, then at you.

“Is there enough?” she asks.

You look back. Your mother is cutting the loaf into slices.

“For breakfast,” you say, and take the mug from her so she can climb through.
