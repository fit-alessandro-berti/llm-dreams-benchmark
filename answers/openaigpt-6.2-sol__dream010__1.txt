As the competition progresses, you face various challenges and begin to suspect that whoever designed them has been reading your notebooks.

In the first round, you have to explain something you love to a room full of people who would rather be elsewhere. You’re halfway through when a woman in the back takes off her coat. For some reason, this feels better than applause.

In the second round, the judges hand you a project you abandoned years ago. You recognize the coffee ring on the first page. The person at the next table leans over and asks why you stopped.

“I couldn’t get this part to work,” you say, pointing.

“Oh,” she says. “I know how to do that.”

By the time you finish, the judges have gone to lunch. The two of you go looking for them and find a break room with a crooked table. One of the judges is on his knees, trying to wedge a folded napkin under the short leg.

“Did we miss the final?” you ask.

“You’ve got twelve minutes,” he says. “Could you lift this end?”

When you set the table down again, there’s a set of keys on it. The woman from the next table picks up one ring, and you pick up the other.

Outside, your bus is waiting. Through its windows you can see your old commute laid out in perfect detail: the pharmacy, the left turn that takes forever, the bakery that isn’t open yet.

“Monday?” the woman calls from the doorway.

You let the bus go.

“Is tomorrow too soon?”
