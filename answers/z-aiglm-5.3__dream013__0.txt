...notice the puzzles beginning to change.

The clean logic that opened the first chambers—sequences, patterns, careful deduction—starts to fail you. The next door doesn't respond to answers at all; it only opens when you admit, out loud, that you don't know. The walls lose their edges. Corridors you've already walked rearrange themselves behind your back, and you stop trusting your own memory.

In one chamber, a puzzle carved into the floor asks you to place seven stones in the correct order. Each time you solve it, the stones remember a different order, and slowly you understand: the puzzle isn't testing whether you can order the stones. It's asking whether you can let go of being right.

Deeper still, the maze stops pretending to be stone. The walls become doors, the doors become mirrors, and in each mirror the puzzle is *you*—your own face with one detail wrong. A crooked tooth you never had. Eyes a shade too dark. You must choose which reflection is the true one, and the whole maze hums while you consider, patient as a held breath.

You choose. The mirror dissolves.

The final chamber holds no puzzle—or so you think, until you notice the exit has no handle, no keyhole, no seam. Only a small inscription:

*You built this place. You already know the way out.*

And you do. You've known since the first corridor. The way out of the maze was never *through* it. You close your eyes and take a step forward without looking—

—and wake, sheets tangled, morning light crawling across the ceiling, carrying nothing but the fading certainty that you solved it. The answer is already dissolving on your tongue, the way answers always do in dreams: perfect a moment ago, unreachable now, but somehow you're sure it was beautiful.
