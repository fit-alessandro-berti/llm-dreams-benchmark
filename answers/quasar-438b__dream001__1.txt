You check your watch; the face is a whirlpool of changing numbers, draining the minutes like sand in an hourglass. Forty minutes left. You stop a stranger—a woman with a face like shifting smoke—and ask for directions. She doesn't speak, but simply points to a puddle of mercury resting on the curb. You look into the puddle. It isn't a reflection; it's a map. 

The map shows your destination: a pulsing red dot three blocks away and straight up. 

You scale a vine-covered chimney, pulling yourself onto the roof of a building that hums like a tuning fork. The sky is the color of stale coffee. You run along the edge of the roof until the path runs out, and then you simply step off the edge into nothing. Instead of falling, you land on a moving walkway of velvet that carries you through a window shaped like an ear. 

You arrive. The room you enter is vast, cold, and smells faintly of mothballs and ambition. You take your seat at a table that stretches into the horizon. Across from you sits the person you are meeting: a towering figure with no features, just a silhouette cut out of the room's fluorescent light. They nod at you, though they have no head to nod with. 

You open your mouth to speak, to explain the delay, but the words come out as birds. Small, white birds that fly across the table and disintegrate into ink just before reaching the other side. The figure receives the ink. They understand. 

The clock on the wall spins backward. The hour was never an hour; it was a velocity. The meeting is happening, has happened, is happening now. The city folds around you like an origami crane, and you realize the meeting was never the point. The meeting was just the door you needed to pass through to get back to the real world, where the sheets are cool and the alarm clock rings Tuesday.
