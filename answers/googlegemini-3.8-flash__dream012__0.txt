The wind howls, tearing at the sails, but beneath the roar of the gale, the dream begins to bend. The compass on the binnacle spins wildly, its needle pointing not North, but straight up toward the churning clouds. The ocean below is no longer just dark water; it glows with deep bioluminescent currents, tracing constellations beneath the hull that mirror the storm above.

Your first mate’s eyes are wide with terror, his mouth moving, but the wind swallows his words. You don’t shout back. In the strange logic of the dream, you don’t need to. You simply meet his gaze, and a sudden, unshakeable stillness settles over you. 

"Trim the sails," you say, and your voice carries across the deck with the resonance of a distant bell, cutting effortlessly through the tempest. "We aren't running from it."

Instead of turning the bow away from the leviathan wave rising before you—a wall of black water higher than the mainmast—you spin the wheel hard into its face. The crew moves without hesitation, bound by a shared, wordless trust. Ropes pull taut, the timber groans in protest, and the ship surges forward, climbing the sheer face of the wave.

As the bow breaches the crest, the roar of the sea suddenly cuts to absolute silence. 

The ship doesn't plunge back down. It lifts. 

The turbulent ocean drops away beneath your keel, replaced by a sea of mist, and the ship sails smoothly into the quiet eye of the storm, suspended among drifting clouds and an endless canopy of clear, cold stars. 

You look back at your crew. They are smiling, bathed in starlight, breathing easily again. You let go of the helm, watching as the ship glides forward into the quiet sky, right at the fragile edge of waking up.
