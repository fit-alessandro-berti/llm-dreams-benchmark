And you look down and realize you have nothing to write with.

Your hands are empty. No, not empty - you're holding a cold cup of coffee that you don't remember making, and it's already half-empty and has lipstick on the rim that isn't yours.

Your boss is standing right in front of your desk, smiling. Too wide. Patiently.

"The client needs it by the end of the day," he says. His voice sounds like it's coming from the hallway behind you, even though his mouth is moving right in front of you.

You look at the project file on your desk. It's a single, impossibly thick stack of paper. On the cover it just says: **DUE: 5 MINUTES AGO.**

"Five minutes ago?" you try to say, but your voice comes out muffled.

"Plenty of time," your boss says, and taps his watch. You glance at it and see his watch has no numbers, just a single eye blinking slowly. All the clocks in the office are like that now - blinking, staring.

You open the file. The first page says: *1. Count every window in the building. 2. Re-paint the sky so it looks more productive. 3. Don't forget to breathe.*

You laugh nervously, expecting your boss to laugh too. He doesn't. He just keeps smiling and slowly starts to sink into the carpet, like it's shallow water.

Panic rises in your chest. You try to stand up but your office chair has rolled six feet back without you moving. The hallway behind your boss, which should be ten feet long, suddenly stretches for miles. Fluorescent lights flicker infinitely into the distance. Your coworkers are at their desks, typing furiously on keyboards with no keys, not looking up.

You look at your computer to start the project, but the screen is just a mirror. In the reflection, you're not in your office clothes. You're in your pajamas, sitting at your office desk, which is now somehow also your childhood bedroom desk.

That's when it clicks.

The deadline is impossible because time isn't real here. The boss is waiting because *you're* waiting for yourself to realize it.

You take a deep breath. The air smells like ozone and old paper. You close the impossible file. You stand up. The carpet is solid again.

"What if I just... don't?" you say to your boss, your voice clear this time.

He stops sinking. His too-wide smile softens into something almost kind, almost like your own face. He checks his eye-watch one more time.

"Then you can finally wake up," he says.

And the fluorescent lights above you all wink out at once ---
