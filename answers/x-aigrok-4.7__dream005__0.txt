You weave through the crowd and the first cluster you pass is arguing, very seriously, about whether the punch bowl is actually a small inland sea. Someone offers you a ladle “for navigation.” You decline and keep moving.

The next group is all wearing name tags that say your name, but none of them look like you. They wave like old friends. One of them mouths, “She’s by the stairs,” and points in three directions at once.

Near the food table a circle of people is laughing at a joke you almost remember telling. When you get close enough to hear it, the punchline is just the sound of a door opening somewhere else in the house. They all turn to you, expectant, as if you’re supposed to finish it.

You spot a familiar coat across the room—the one person you know—and start toward them. The floor tilts gently, like a boat. Conversations smear into music. Just as you reach out, the lights flicker, and you realize the gathering has been happening inside a single long breath you haven’t finished yet.

You wake up still reaching.
