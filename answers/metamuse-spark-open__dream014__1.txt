Neon signs flicker overhead, traffic hums all around you, and hundreds of faces flow past in both directions. No one seems to see you standing still in the middle of the sidewalk, map creased in your hands, unsure which way is home.

Then one of them stops.

A woman with a kind smile and a coffee in her hand notices you looking around. "Lost?" she asks, not in a rush, not impatient.

Before you can even answer, two others pause beside her — an older man with a newspaper tucked under his arm, a teenager with headphones around his neck. They don't crowd you. They just make a little circle of quiet in the middle of all that noise.

You show them where you're trying to go, your voice a little uncertain. The woman nods like she knows exactly that feeling. The teenager pulls out his phone to check the route. The older man points down the avenue and says, "You're closer than you think. Just two blocks that way, then a left. You can see the lights from there."

And suddenly the city doesn't feel so huge. Someone walks with you to the corner to make sure you see the street sign. Someone else tells you a quick story about getting lost in this same neighborhood on their first day here. You laugh a little, and the tightness in your chest begins to ease.

You realize you were never as alone as you felt — you were just surrounded by people you hadn't met yet.

You wake with the feeling of the city lights still warm behind your eyes, and the quiet reassurance that help is often much closer than it seems.
