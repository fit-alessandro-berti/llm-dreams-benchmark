You are walking through a peaceful forest when suddenly the weather changes dramatically. You feel a storm approaching and turn to see the sky bruised a deep violet behind the canopy. The wind doesn’t gust—it *inhales*, drawing the scent of ozone, crushed pine, and something like vanilla toward you. Every bird falls silent at once, and in the hush, you realize the rustling leaves are whispering your name, or maybe a name you haven’t used yet.

Lightning doesn’t strike so much as *unfold*, branching overhead like a silver tree mirroring the real ones. But the rain, when it comes, falls upward in shimmering threads, rising from the moss and drifting into the clouds. The temperature plummets, and your breath becomes visible, swirling into shapes—first a key, then a door, then a hand waving you forward.

Ahead, the path splits. On the left, a grove of birch trees bends inward to form a tunnel. On the right, a fallen log splits open, revealing a staircase spiraling down into warm, amber light, though the forest floor was solid a moment ago.

You choose. The moment your foot moves, the storm contracts around you, pressing in close as a blanket, and the sky goes white. When you open your eyes, you are no longer in the forest, but standing in a room lined with mirrors, each one showing a different version of the storm—some where you ran, some where you flew, and one where you are simply dancing in the downpour, utterly unafraid.

A clock ticks backward on the wall. The door behind you creaks open to the sound of rain. And you wake—or think you do—until you notice your shoes are still wet with forest soil.
