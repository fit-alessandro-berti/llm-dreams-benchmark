Lightning splits the sky, and for one heartbeat the sea turns silver. In that flash, you see not waves but mountains of glass rising and shattering around the vessel. Your crew’s voices fade into the wind, replaced by a strange, deep singing rising from the water itself.

You know, the way one knows things only in dreams, that the storm is not trying to destroy you. It is asking a question.

So instead of fighting the sea, you turn the ship toward its darkest eye. The waves part like curtains. The wind falls silent. And below the hull, the ocean opens into a sky full of stars.

The ship sails downward, weightless, through clouds of glowing fish and drowned constellations. Your crew no longer looks afraid. They look as though they have remembered something they had forgotten long ago.

At the center of that underwater heaven, you see a lighthouse made of moonlight. Its beam sweeps across the deep, and wherever it touches, the storm becomes calm.

You understand then: you were never meant to escape the sea.

You were meant to become its keeper.

And when you open your eyes, the dream does not end.

It only waits, like the tide, for you to return.
