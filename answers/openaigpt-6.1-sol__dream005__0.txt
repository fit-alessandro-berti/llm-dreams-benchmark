A circle of musicians pauses mid-song as you pass. “You just missed them,” says a woman holding a violin full of rainwater. She points toward a staircase that wasn’t there a moment ago.

Upstairs, the gathering continues in a room much larger than the building could possibly contain. People sit around long tables, laughing over photographs that change whenever you look away. You catch sight of your friend’s coat disappearing through a doorway at the far end.

You follow, but the doorway opens onto a quiet garden at dusk. The noise of the party becomes the rustle of leaves. Hundreds of empty chairs face a small pond, and your friend sits in the only chair turned backward.

“I’ve been looking for you,” you say.

“I know,” they reply. “That’s why I stayed.”

You sit beside them. In the pond, instead of your reflections, you see the gathering from above. The groups you passed are still talking, still laughing, but now you notice that each contains a different version of you: older, younger, braver, quieter.

“Do I have to go back?” you ask.

Your friend picks a small white flower from the grass and places it in your hand.

“Only when you’re ready to meet someone.”

Then the chairs begin to fill—not with strangers, but with birds—and somewhere inside the house, someone calls your name in your own voice.
