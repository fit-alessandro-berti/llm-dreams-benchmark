...begin to realize the puzzles are changing.

At first, they were exactly what you expected. A door sealed by a rotating cipher of suns and moons. A corridor of tiles that would crumble unless you stepped in the prime-number sequence. A room where nine levers had to be pulled in an order hinted at by a poem carved backwards into the ceiling.

You solve each one. Your head aches with the effort, but there is a clean, satisfying *click* every time, and another stone arch slides open.

Deeper in, the logic starts to slip.

You enter a circular chamber with no doors at all. In the center is a pedestal, and on it, a perfect glass replica of the maze itself, no larger than your hand. When you touch it, the walls around you shudder. When you rotate the little maze to the left, the real room rotates to the right. You spend what feels like hours turning the model, watching tiny hallways align, until suddenly you understand: you're not moving through the maze. You're turning it around yourself.

The next puzzle is a hallway filled with mirrors. Each mirror shows you slightly older, slightly younger, slightly more afraid. One of them isn't mimicking you. She — he — *you*, but with eyes that know something — raises a hand and points, not at you, but behind you. You turn. There's a door that wasn't there before, made entirely of quiet. It only stays visible while you're not looking directly at it.

After that, the puzzles stop asking for answers.

A bridge over darkness that only becomes solid when you stop trying to calculate each step and just walk.

A locked gate with a keyhole shaped like an ear. It opens when you tell it a secret you've never said out loud, not even to yourself. You whisper it, and your voice sounds like it's coming from far down the corridor, from the center of the maze, calling back to you.

A final staircase descending not down, but *inward*, each step softer than the last, like walking on the surface of deep water in winter.

At the center, there is no minotaur. No treasure. No exit sign.

There is a small, plain room, warmly lit. A table. Two chairs. And sitting in one of them is the part of you that built the maze.

They look up, unsurprised. They've been waiting, patiently solving nothing.

"You made it harder at the beginning," they say, "so you would be too busy thinking to be afraid of getting here."

They push the second chair out for you.

On the table between you is the last puzzle: a blank white door, standing by itself with no walls around it. It has no handle, no lock, no riddle. You understand, with dream-certainty, that it will open the moment you stop trying to open it — the moment you decide whether you want to wake up, or stay a little longer and ask yourself what you've been avoiding out there.

You sit down.

Outside the dream, somewhere very far away, your real body breathes in.

In here, in the quiet center of everything you built to keep yourself out, you finally rest.
