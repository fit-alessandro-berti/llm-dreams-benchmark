You are in a world where colors and shapes are constantly changing. As you explore this surreal landscape, you come across a blank canvas and it is standing upright in the middle of the shifting dunes, as still and white as a held breath.

It is taller than you. The edges are soft, like it was cut out of fog, and it does not ripple when the colors around it pulse and fold into each other. A low hum comes from behind it, not a sound so much as a feeling in your teeth.

You reach out and your fingertips leave no mark. Then you notice the canvas is not blank at all. From a distance it is white, but up close the white is made of tiny, sleeping things — moths, or thoughts, or unspoken names — all curled up and waiting.

In your hand you find a brush. You don't remember picking it up. The handle is warm, the bristles are made of light and they keep changing color to match whatever you are looking at. You lift it, and hesitate, because in a dream you know that painting something makes it real.

A voice that sounds like your own, but from far away, says: *Don't fill it in. It's for you.*

So you don't paint a picture. You press your palm to the surface. The sleeping things wake up. The canvas inhales, and the white pulls in, revealing the landscape behind it — not the one you were walking through, but the one you left when you fell asleep. Your kitchen table. The half-finished mug. The window with rain on it.

The canvas folds around you like paper, or like a thought returning to itself. The colors and shapes stop changing. For a moment everything is quiet and still and very, very clear.

Then the brush slips from your hand, the canvas ripples, and you are awake, with paint under your fingernails and no memory of what you painted.
