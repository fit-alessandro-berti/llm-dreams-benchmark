You dig into your coat pocket and pull out a crumpled slip of paper where you’re certain you wrote the address. When you unfold it, the ink is still wet, swirling lazily across the page until it forms a symbol that looks half like a compass and half like a musical note. You blink, frustrated, and check your phone instead—only to find the screen is made of dark, rippling water. Beneath the surface, the time reads *11:74*.

Panic prickles at the back of your neck. Around you, the city towers impossibly high, its buildings woven from pale stone, brass pipes, and green glass that reflects a bruised, twilight sky. Elevated trains glide silently overhead without tracks, leaving faint trails of silver mist. 

You stop a passerby—a tall woman wearing a trench coat and carrying a birdcage filled with glowing paper lanterns—and ask where the meeting is. She doesn't seem surprised that you didn't specify *which* meeting. She simply tilts her head, listens to something far away, and points toward a narrow staircase spiraling down into a subway entrance that wasn't there a second ago.

"Take the blue elevator," she says, her voice echoing slightly as if she’s speaking from the bottom of a well. "And don't forget your umbrella. They hate it when you're dry."

You hurry down the stairs, two at a time, until the subway tiles seamlessly give way to the carpeted hallway of a grand hotel. You recognize the wallpaper—it’s the exact pattern from your childhood bedroom, except the faded flowers are slowly blooming and closing in time with your heartbeat. At the end of the hall stands a single gilded elevator. You press the button, and the doors slide open immediately.

Inside, there are no floor numbers, only a row of buttons labeled with words like *North*, *Yesterday*, *Lobby*, and *Almost*. Instinctively, you press *Almost*. 

The elevator doesn't go up or down; it lurches sideways with a gentle hum. Through a small brass porthole in the door, you watch entire neighborhoods blur past—rooftop gardens, flooded libraries, a vast courtyard where people in business suits are flying enormous black kites. You realize with a sudden jolt of dread that you aren't wearing shoes, and that you’ve forgotten the presentation you were supposed to give. You pat your pockets frantically, finding only a handful of smooth river stones and a tarnished skeleton key.

*Ding.*

The doors open onto a massive, sun-drenched conservatory. Giant ferns curl toward a glass ceiling where slow-moving whales drift through the clouds outside. In the center of the room sits an impossibly long mahogany conference table, hovering a few inches above a shallow pool of clear water. 

Seated around the table are a dozen figures—some look like old friends you haven't seen in years, others have faces that shift softly whenever you try to focus on them. At the head of the table sits someone who feels familiar in a way you can't quite place. They look up as you step out of the elevator, your bare feet splashing quietly in the warm water.

Your chest tightens. You open your mouth to stammer an apology—that you're late, that you lost the address, that you don't have your notes. 

Before you can speak, the figure at the head of the table smiles with quiet relief and gestures to the empty chair beside them.

"There you are," they say softly. "You brought the stones. We can finally begin."

As you pull the smooth river stones from your pocket, they begin to hum with a warm, golden light. All the urgency and anxiety melts away like frost in the sun. The glass walls of the conservatory dissolve into an endless breeze, the distant hum of the city fades into the sound of morning rain against a windowpane, and the dream begins to gently unravel at the edges.
