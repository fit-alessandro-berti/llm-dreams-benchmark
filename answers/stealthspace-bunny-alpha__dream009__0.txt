You realize you need to **find fire before the cold finds you.**

The village is built from stone and timber, its thatched roofs sagging beneath layers of moss. There are no engines, no glowing screens, no wires stretching between houses—only narrow dirt paths, muddy fields, and the lowing of cattle.

The people here wear rough woollen clothes. They watch you pass with expressions that are less curious than wary. No one asks where you came from. Perhaps they already know.

An old woman points toward a dark, abandoned house at the edge of the settlement. Inside, you find a circle of cold stones and a pile of damp wood. Without matches or electricity, you kneel and search for another way. You gather dry grass, bark, and wisps of old cloth. Then you strike a piece of flint against steel, again and again, until a tiny spark catches.

The flame grows.

Everyone in the village emerges to stare at you. The old woman steps forward, her eyes reflecting the firelight.

“So,” she says in a language you suddenly understand, “the dreamer has returned.”

She offers you a bowl of warm stew and a bed beside the hearth. For the first time since waking, you feel safe.

But her words trouble you. *Returned?*

As the fire crackles between you, you realize you no longer need only warmth and shelter.

You need to discover why they have been expecting you all along.
