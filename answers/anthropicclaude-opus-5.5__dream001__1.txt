...walk, because walking feels like the only thing that makes sense. The streets are wide and gray and polished like river stones, and every building has too many windows, hundreds of them, all lit, though it's the middle of the afternoon.

You check your pocket for your phone and find a folded paper map instead. It's clearly a map of this city, but the street names are written in a language you almost understand. One street is called something like *Tuesday Remembering*. Another looks like *The Avenue of Your Third Grade Teacher*. You feel certain the meeting is somewhere on the map, but every time you look away and back, the streets have rearranged themselves slightly, as if the map is breathing.

You stop a woman carrying a birdcage full of keys and ask her where the meeting is. She doesn't ask *which* meeting. She just nods, as though she's been expecting you, and points toward a staircase that goes down into the sidewalk. "It's on the fourteenth floor," she says. "You'll have to go down to get up."

This makes perfect sense.

The staircase opens into a subway station, except the trains are long wooden boats drifting on a canal of dark, still water. You step onto one. The other passengers are all wearing business suits and holding briefcases, and you notice with a jolt that you're wearing pajamas. Nobody seems to mind. A man across from you is quietly eating a peach, and he offers you half. It tastes like the summer you were eleven.

You glance at your watch. The hour has become forty minutes, then two hours, then "soon." The numbers won't hold still.

The boat drifts into a tunnel lined with doors, each one painted a different color. You know, the way you only know things in dreams, that one of them is the meeting. You step off onto a narrow ledge and start trying doors. Behind the red one is your childhood kitchen, the radio playing softly. Behind the blue one is an endless library where the books are whispering to each other. Behind the yellow one is a field of tall grass under an enormous moon, and you stand there longer than you should because it's so beautiful.

Finally, you find a plain gray door with a small brass plaque that just says your name.

You open it.

Inside is a small, sunlit room with a single table and two chairs. Sitting in one of them is... you. An older version, maybe, or a calmer one, it's hard to tell. They smile like they've been waiting a long time and aren't bothered by it at all.

"You made it," they say. "I wasn't sure you'd come. You always think you're late."

You sit down. You want to ask what the meeting is about, what you're supposed to have prepared, whether you're in trouble. But the words dissolve before you can say them.

"There's no agenda," your other self says, pouring two cups of tea. "I just wanted to tell you that you're doing better than you think. And that you can slow down a little. The city will still be here."

Outside the window, the buildings with too many windows begin, one by one, to switch off their lights, like a city going to sleep. The tea is warm in your hands. Somewhere far away, very faintly, you hear an alarm clock, but it sounds gentle, almost apologetic, like it knows it's interrupting something important.

You take one last sip, and the room fills with soft white light, and you begin to wake up...
