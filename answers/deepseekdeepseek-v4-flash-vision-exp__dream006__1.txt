…and you begin to speak, but the words come out as a soft, melodic hum. The audience doesn’t seem confused; instead, they lean forward, swaying gently in unison. The stage begins to soften beneath your feet, like moss, and the microphones bloom into wildflowers. Every sentence you try to form turns into a ribbon of light that drifts out over the crowd, and they catch it, weaving it into a shimmering canopy above the room.

You realize you aren’t giving a speech anymore—you’re planting a garden of echoes. The audience’s faces become windows, each one showing a different sky. Some have rain, some have sunrise, some have stars. You understand that they aren’t listening to you, but listening *through* you—to the quiet part of themselves that came to this room hoping to be remembered.

Then the lights grow warm, like the inside of a hearth. You raise your hand, not to wave, but to let go of the last word you were holding. It drifts up like a lantern. And the whole audience rises with it, not in applause, but in weightlessness.

The dream ends with you standing alone on a stage that is no longer a stage—just a warm, wooden floor in a house you loved as a child. And you hear your own voice, quiet and safe, saying the only thing that matters:

“I’m not afraid anymore.”
