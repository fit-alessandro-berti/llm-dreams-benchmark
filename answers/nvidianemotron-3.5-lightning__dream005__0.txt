You are at a large social gathering where you only know one person. As you try to find them, you encounter various groups of people and each conversation feels like a snippet from a different life—some discussing a party you don’t remember hosting, others debating a book you’ve never read, and a few speaking in a language that sounds familiar yet foreign. Faces shift and overlap; a stranger’s laugh morphs into the one you’re seeking, only to dissolve into someone else’s story the moment you draw near. The room itself seems to rearrange—doorways appear where walls were, ceilings lower and rise like breath. You start walking down a hallway that wasn’t there a minute ago, guided by a faint pull in your chest, and just as you’re about to round the corner, you hear a voice behind you say, “I’ve been looking for you too,” and you turn to find the person you came with stepping out of a shimmering curtain, smiling as if they’d been standing there all along, waiting for you to notice.
