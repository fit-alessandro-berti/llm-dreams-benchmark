You pull your hand away, leaving a blue fingerprint. It spreads into a lake, and a small yellow boat drifts up to the edge of the frame. Inside is the mitten you lost when you were six. It’s still damp with snow.

You climb into the boat. Behind you, the hills fold flat, one after another, until the whole world fits inside the frame. For a moment you can see your bedroom on the other side: the crooked lamp, the glass of water, the shape of yourself under the covers.

You mean to call out, but the boat bumps gently against something.

You open your eyes. Your hand is hanging over the edge of the bed, and someone has put the mitten on it.
