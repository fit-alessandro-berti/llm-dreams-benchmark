The first asks, **“What do you truly want?”**

The second asks, **“What would you choose if you knew you could not disappoint anyone?”**

Around you, the other students are writing furiously. Their pens scratch against the paper like hundreds of tiny insects. You look toward the clock, but instead of numbers, its face shows different versions of your future: one where you are successful but exhausted, one where you have failed and begun again, and another where you are standing somewhere peaceful, doing work you never knew existed.

You try to answer, but your pen produces only invisible ink. Panic rises in your chest. The examiner walks toward you, their footsteps echoing through the enormous hall. When they reach your desk, you realize they have your face—only older, calmer, and far less afraid.

“You are answering the wrong question,” they say.

Suddenly, the exam room disappears. Your desk is now balanced at the edge of a vast railway platform, with dozens of trains waiting below. Each train bears the name of a different career, and every door is closing.

You clutch the blank exam paper, certain you must choose quickly. Then you notice a narrow path leading away from the station into the morning light. It has no sign, no destination, and no one else is taking it.

Your older self places the pen in your hand.

“The exam was never about knowing the correct answer,” they whisper. “It was about deciding which answer would be yours.”

You write a single sentence. The moment the final word touches the page, the paper folds itself into a small white bird and flies toward the unmarked path.

You follow it.

Behind you, the trains depart one by one, but instead of feeling that you have missed your future, you feel—for the first time—that you are walking toward it.
