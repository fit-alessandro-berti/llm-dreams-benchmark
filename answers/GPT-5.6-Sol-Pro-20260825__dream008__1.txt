You are at your workplace, and suddenly, you are given a project with an impossible deadline. Your boss is waiting for you to start, and you notice that the clock on the wall is spinning faster with every second.

You open the project folder, but instead of documents, it contains hundreds of tiny doors. Each door is labeled with a different task: **RESEARCH**, **BUDGET**, **PRESENTATION**, **APPROVAL**. Whenever you complete one, two more doors appear.

Your coworkers offer to help, but when you turn toward them, their desks are empty. Only their voices remain, drifting through the office:

“Almost finished?”

“Have you sent it yet?”

“The deadline was five minutes ago.”

You begin working faster. Your fingers move across the keyboard without touching it, filling the screen with words you cannot read. The office stretches into an endless corridor, while your boss stands at the far end, watching silently.

Finally, you stop.

“I can’t finish this alone,” you say.

The clock freezes.

All the tiny doors swing open at once, revealing not more work, but a bright garden outside. Your boss smiles as though this was the answer they had been waiting for.

“The project was never the assignment,” they say. “The assignment was to recognize the impossible.”

You step through the nearest door. The office dissolves behind you, the deadline disappears from your calendar, and you find yourself standing beneath a tree whose leaves are made of blank pages.

One page floats into your hand.

Written on it are the words:

**Begin again—but this time, choose your own deadline.**
