At the market, you ask a woman selling pears how long the king has reigned.

“Since Tuesday,” she says. “But he’s getting better at it.”

She gives you a pear and refuses your money. The coin, you notice as you put it away, has your face on it.

At noon the bells ring thirteen times. Everyone sets down what they’re carrying and looks toward the hill. A boy in a paper crown comes running down the road, barefoot, holding a door key in both hands.

“You left before I could give you this,” he says.

You recognize the key by the crooked tooth you used to worry with your thumbnail on the walk home from school. You ask him where he found it.

“Under the mat, of course.”

He leads you to a door standing alone in a field. On the other side, your mother is washing dishes. You can hear the little clink her wedding ring makes against the glasses. You haven’t heard it in twenty years.

You put the key in the lock. Then you take it out and knock, just to hear her say, “Come in.”
