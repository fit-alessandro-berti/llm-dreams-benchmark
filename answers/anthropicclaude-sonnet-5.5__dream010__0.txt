**...As the competition progresses, you face various challenges and each one bends the rules of reality a little more.**

The first challenge takes place in a hallway that keeps rearranging itself. Doors swap places, and the numbers on them melt and re-form. The instructions, printed on a card that keeps changing fonts, say: *"Find the door that belongs to you."* The other contestants rush to the shiny doors with gold handles. You notice a plain door with a scuffed knob and a sticker you put there when you were seven. It opens.

The second challenge is a tightrope stretched across an enormous room full of ticking clocks. Halfway across, you realize the clocks aren't counting time, they're counting *hesitations*. Every doubt you've ever had is a tick. The contestant ahead of you freezes and falls upward into the ceiling. You don't try to silence the clocks. You just keep walking, and the ticking becomes background music.

The third challenge is a table with a single chair opposite a judge whose face keeps shifting between people you know: your old teacher, a stranger from a train, yourself at age twelve. The judge slides over a blank sheet of paper and says, "Show us what you'd do if no one were grading you."

Your hands already know. You begin drawing, or writing, or building. It changes depending on when you look, but it's the thing you always do when you lose track of time. The room goes quiet. Even the clocks from the last room seem to lean in and listen.

Then the judge smiles with all of its faces at once. "That's the job," it says. "That's always been the job. The competition was just the way we found out whether you'd remember."

The walls fold down like paper, and behind them is a sunlit workspace with your name already on the door, in the handwriting of your seven-year-old self. A window is open, and somewhere outside, a kettle is whistling.

You step inside. As the dream begins to soften at the edges, you notice the desk drawer is slightly open. Inside is a note: *"Welcome. You can wake up now. You'll still know the way back."*
