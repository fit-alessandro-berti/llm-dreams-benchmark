...the sky darkens from gold to deep violet in a matter of seconds. The trees begin to sway, their leaves turning silver and chiming like thousands of tiny bells. The wind doesn't feel cold — it feels *alive*, wrapping around you like a curious animal.

The first raindrop lands on your hand, but instead of water, it's a small glowing moth that dissolves into your skin, leaving a trail of warmth. Thunder rolls overhead, but it sounds less like sound and more like a voice — a deep, patient voice calling your name from somewhere beyond the clouds.

You start running, not from fear but from a strange exhilaration. The forest floor softens beneath your feet until you're barely touching the ground, each stride carrying you farther than it should. Lightning splits the sky, and for one frozen moment, you see the forest as it truly is: the trees are growing *downward*, their roots reaching toward the churning sky, and the storm isn't above you — it's below you, and you've been walking on its surface all along.

Gravity hesitates, uncertain.

In the distance, an old stone door stands alone between two ancient oaks, completely dry despite the downpour, slightly ajar, warm light spilling from the crack. You can hear laughter on the other side — familiar, though you can't place it.

The thunder calls your name again, more insistent now.

You reach for the door...
