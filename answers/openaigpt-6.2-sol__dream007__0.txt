But the moment you go quiet, so does the room. The refrigerator stops humming. Rain hangs motionless outside the window. Your friend opens their mouth, and a small brass key drops onto the kitchen table.

You know what it opens: the door of the first apartment you shared, back when neither of you could afford a couch. You try it in the pantry anyway.

On the other side, you’re both twenty again, sitting on the floor, eating noodles out of the same saucepan. Your younger selves are arguing about whether the moon can be seen from every country at once. Neither of you is right. Neither of you minds.

“I’m not asking you to agree with me,” your friend says behind you. “I’m asking you to believe I remember it differently.”

You wake before you can answer. Rain taps the window. For a moment, you can still feel the teeth of the key pressed into your palm.
