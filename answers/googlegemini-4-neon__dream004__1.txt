There is no easel, no palette, and no brush. Drawn by an inexplicable pull, you step closer and press your bare palm against the cool, unyielding weave. 

The moment your skin touches it, a pool of deep indigo blooms from your fingertips—stolen directly from the sky, which was just melting into floating spiral staircases above you. A soft, musical hum vibrates through your wrist.

You drag your hand across the surface. With every sweeping stroke, the chaotic world around you sighs and slows down. You trace a broad arc of warm gold, and the spinning geometric clouds in the distance soften into gentle, sunlit hills. You draw a single, steady horizontal line of muted blue, and the fractured kaleidoscope beneath your feet settles into the calm, glassy stillness of a twilight lake.

Realizing the canvas isn't just reflecting this world—it is taming it—you step back to look at what you’ve anchored. Then, you reach out once more. In the very center of your quiet horizon, you trace the simple, dark outline of a doorway, finishing it with a small, gleaming circle for a handle. 

The painted brass catches a light that shouldn't exist. When you wrap your fingers around it, it feels startlingly solid and cool. 

A gentle breeze begins to blow *out* of the canvas, carrying the scent of ozone, pine, and a memory you can’t quite name. As you turn the knob and push the door open, the wooden frame of the canvas dissolves into mist, expanding until there is no boundary left between the painting and the dream. You step over the threshold into the bright, unknown stillness on the other side—just as the colors behind you begin to whisper, ripple, and shift all over again.
