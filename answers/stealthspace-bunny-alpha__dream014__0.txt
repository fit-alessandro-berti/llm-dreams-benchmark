You hesitate—then nod.

The strangers gather close, and the city seems to exhale. Its rumbling footsteps soften. The impatient horns become distant music, and the crowd opens like a curtain.

“Follow the red umbrella,” says a woman in a bright coat. “Turn left where the streetlights flicker. And when you hear rain, don’t look up—it’s only the city dreaming.”

You walk together, weaving through strangers who smile as they pass. No one asks your name. No one demands to know where you’re going. They simply offer small pieces of the way: a shortcut, a warm glance, a hand to steady you when the pavement tilts.

At last, they lead you into a hidden courtyard where an old tree grows upside down, its branches full of glowing leaves. Beneath it, there is a bench—not grand, not magical, just waiting.

“What happens now?” you ask.

The woman in the red coat sits beside you. “Now you rest,” she says. “The rest of the way will still be there when you’re ready.”

You sit together beneath the impossible tree, and the bustling city fades into silence. You realize you were never asked to carry the whole journey alone.

As you close your eyes, you hear one of the strangers whisper, “You found us.”

And then the dream begins again—but this time, you are not lost.
