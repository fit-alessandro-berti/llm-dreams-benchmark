You start to walk, because walking is the only thing your body remembers how to do.

The city is built out of places you have almost been. A stairway from your grandmother's house rises between two towers of blue glass. The smell of a hotel corridor you passed through once at nineteen. Every street is named in your own handwriting, and the names keep changing when you look away — *Ferndale*, then *The Way Back*, then something you can't read because the letters are wet.

Your watch says 11:04. It says 11:04 for a very long time.

You stop a man carrying a ladder sideways through a doorway. You ask him where the meeting is. He looks at you with enormous kindness and says, "Oh, you're the one who called it," and then he is inside, and the door is a wall, and the wall has always been a wall.

There's an address on your palm. You don't remember writing it. It's a number so large it makes your chest hurt, and beneath it, in smaller letters: *bring the thing you forgot*.

You begin to run. The running is the slow kind, the kind where the pavement is generous with itself, giving you more of the same cobblestone over and over like a dealer who won't let you leave the table. A tram passes going the other way and everyone aboard is watching you with mild, sympathetic interest, the way people watch a dog trying to carry a stick through a gate.

Somewhere a bell begins. Not an alarm — a dinner bell, warm, insistent, the sound of being expected.

You turn a corner and there it is: an ordinary door in an ordinary building, the kind you'd walk past a thousand times. Your hand knows the handle. Inside, a staircase that goes down further than the building is tall, and the further down you go the more you understand that you are late in a way that cannot be fixed by arriving.

At the bottom, a long table. Green felt, like a card table. Twelve chairs, eleven of them occupied by people whose faces you can hold only one at a time — look at one and the others go soft, like names on the tip of your tongue. Your old teacher. Someone you sat beside on a bus in another country. A person you have not met yet and will recognize instantly when you do.

The twelfth chair is yours. There's a folder in front of it. You open it.

One page. One line.

*We've been waiting to hear what you decided.*

And you realize — with the flat, obvious certainty that only dreams permit — that you were supposed to have made a decision years ago, that the whole city was built around your not making it, that the towers and the trams and the ladder and the wet street names are all scaffolding holding up a question you keep walking around.

You open your mouth to answer.

Your watch says 11:04.

Someone down the table slides a glass of water toward you, and says, gently, "Take your time. We have the hour."

And you understand that the hour is not sixty minutes. The hour is however long it takes.

You sit down.
