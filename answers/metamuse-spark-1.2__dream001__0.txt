You start to run.

Or try to. Your legs feel heavy, like you're moving through shallow water, even though the sidewalk beneath you is dry and cracked and warm from the sun.

You pull out your phone to check the address - but the screen is swimming. The numbers slide off the glass like ink in water. 3:47 becomes 3:4? becomes 8:∞. When you try to type "where is my meeting," the keyboard melts into smooth, blank tiles under your thumbs.

You look up for a street sign and realize you can't read any of them. The letters are all familiar but rearranged, like a language you knew as a child and forgot. *VONDEL STRÆT. EXIT 8½. PLAZA NO.*

People stream past you, none of them seeming to notice you're standing still in the middle of the rush. A woman with a stroller that holds only a large potted plant. A man in a perfect business suit walking a tortoise on a leash. You try to ask one of them for directions.

"Excuse me, do you know where the -"

But you can't remember what the meeting is for. Or who it's with. Only that it is *very* important, and that if you are late, something irreversible will happen.

The man with the tortoise smiles kindly and says, "Oh, you're close. Just past the library that was never built."

As if that makes sense. And in the dream, it does.

You turn the corner where he pointed and the city shifts. The vast avenues suddenly fold in on themselves. The towering glass buildings are now narrow brick row houses, the kind from your hometown. The air smells like rain on hot pavement, even though it's sunny. A bell tower chimes - one, two, three - and each chime lasts far too long, stretching out like taffy.

In your pocket, you find something that wasn't there before. A small, folded piece of paper, soft from being held too many times. You unfold it.

Handwritten in your own handwriting, it says:

*MEETING: 4th floor. Room with the blue door. Don't be early. Don't be late. We have been waiting for you.*

On the back, a hand-drawn map. Just one building, circled. And you realize with the sudden, absolute clarity that only happens in dreams - you know this building. You've been here before. You live here.

You look up, and it's right in front of you.

It's not an office tower. It's your childhood elementary school, impossibly tall, stretching up into the clouds. Every window is lit, even though it's daytime. In one window on the fourth floor, you can see a single blue door standing open, waiting.

You have 7 minutes left. Or maybe 7 hours. Time has gone wobbly.

You take a deep breath, push open the heavy front doors, and step inside, your footsteps echoing down the empty, sunlit hallway as you finally remember where you're supposed to go.
