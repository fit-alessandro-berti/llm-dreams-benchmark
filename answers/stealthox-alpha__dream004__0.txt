...you come across a blank canvas and feel an inexplicable certainty that it has been waiting for you.

You reach out to touch it, and your fingertip sinks through the surface like water. Color blooms outward from the point of contact—not from any brush, but from the memory of every hue you've passed in this shifting world. You begin to paint with your bare hands, dragging pigment straight from the air: the violet of the melting hills, the amber dripping from the floating staircases, the impossible green of the sky beneath your feet.

As you work, you realize you're painting a door. You don't remember deciding to—your hands simply knew. It takes shape in deep blues and golds, and when it's finished, it stands slightly ajar, breathing.

Through the gap you glimpse a room that is somehow both vast and tiny, filled with every version of yourself you've ever been in dreams. They all turn to look at you at once, and they smile.

One steps forward and presses a key made of light into your palm. "You left this here," they say, though you've never been here before. "Before you woke up."

You close your fingers around the key, and the world begins to soften at its edges—colors bleeding into white like watercolor left out in the rain. The hills dissolve. The staircases drift apart like dandelion seeds. The last thing you see is the canvas, empty once more, patient, waiting for whichever dreamer wanders here next.

And then—

Morning. Sunlight through curtains. Your hand still half-curled, as if holding something small and bright that has already faded.

But somewhere behind your eyes, faintly, the smell of wet paint lingers.
