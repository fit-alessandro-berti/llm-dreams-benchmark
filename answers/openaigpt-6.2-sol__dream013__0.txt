begin to notice that the maze is solving puzzles of its own.

Behind the walls, stone grinds against stone. You press your ear to a seam and hear someone counting backward in your voice.

At the next junction, a brass plaque asks:

*What can you leave behind without ever putting it down?*

You try *a footprint*, then *a memory*. Neither works. Finally, tired of standing there, you turn to go back.

The passage opens.

Beyond it is your childhood kitchen, warm enough to fog the windows. Someone has set out two bowls of soup. From beneath the table comes the soft, deliberate scrape of a pencil.

You kneel. A child is drawing a maze on the underside of the table. You recognize the kitchen at its center, the two bowls, and yourself on your knees. The child has drawn everything except a way out.

“Where do you want to go?” the child asks.

You look at the soup. You’ve been hungry for hours, you realize, but none of the puzzles asked you about that.

“Nowhere yet,” you say, and pull up a chair.
