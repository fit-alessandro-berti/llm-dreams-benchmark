A circle of strangers in silver masks turns at once and raises their glasses. A woman near the piano hands you a slice of cake with a candle in it, though it isn’t your birthday. Children in paper crowns chase a white cat across the rug, chanting a name that sounds almost like yours.

You keep searching for the one person you know, but every time you glimpse them, the room rearranges itself. Doorways open into other parties. The hallway stretches longer than the building outside. A staircase leads down into a library where people whisper your secrets in foreign languages.

At last, you find them standing by a window that looks out over an ocean instead of a street.

“There you are,” they say, smiling. “We were beginning to think you wouldn’t come.”

You ask where you were supposed to be, but the music swells and everyone begins dancing in slow circles. Your friend takes your hand, and suddenly you are dancing too, though your feet no longer touch the floor.

Then the lights stretch into long white threads, the room folds like a paper map, and you wake with the sound of applause in your ears and the strange certainty that you were the guest of honor.
