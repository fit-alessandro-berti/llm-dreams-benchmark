As you sit down to start the exam, you notice the questions are written in your own handwriting, dated ten years from now.

They do not ask for facts. They ask: *Why did you stop? What did you believe about yourself that was almost true? Which promise did you keep because you were afraid to lose it?*

Every time you try to answer, the words appear on the paper before you think them. The classroom smells like rain, chalk, and old books, and the proctor has no face—only a hall pass that says your name. Your pen rolls off the desk and becomes a key. A bird lands on the empty chair beside you, whispers an answer you already know, and then flies out through the ceiling.

You understand, suddenly, that the exam cannot be passed. It can only be completed if you admit, in front of the impossible classroom, that you never wanted to be here—but that you do want to become someone who can leave.
