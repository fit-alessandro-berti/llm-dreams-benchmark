and the room keeps changing shape around you, like it’s breathing.

At first you pass a group of people in matching togas, all laughing in slow motion. They’re holding glasses filled with glowing liquid, and when one of them turns to you, their face is a soft blur, like a smudged painting. They offer you a drink, but you politely shake your head—you’re looking for someone.

You move through a hallway that wasn’t there a second ago. You find another group clustered around a piano, but no one is playing. The keys depress themselves, a lullaby you almost recognize. A woman with silver hair looks at you and says, “They went toward the garden. But they’re not the same person they were when you knew them.”

You nod, because in a dream, that makes perfect sense.

You walk through a door that bends like it’s made of water, and suddenly you’re outside under a sky full of upside-down stars. The garden is enormous, full of people holding tiny lanterns. Then you see them—your friend—standing alone on a small hill, wearing a coat that shimmers like a seashell. When they wave, their hand moves in slow motion.

You call their name, but your voice comes out as a song from somewhere far away. They turn, and you feel a huge relief—until they start walking toward you, but their face keeps changing: now a stranger, now you, now a child, now just sunlight.

You wake up just as you reach them, and your pillow is still warm.
