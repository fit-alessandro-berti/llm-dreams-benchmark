You are at a large social gathering where you only know one person. As you try to find them, you encounter various groups of people and none of them will quite look at you.

The house is much bigger on the inside than it should be. You came in through a small front door on a quiet street, but now there are ballrooms opening into gardens, staircases that lead to kitchens, a long hallway lined with coat racks where hundreds of people are laughing too loudly at conversations you can almost hear.

You are holding a drink you don't remember being given. It's warm now.

"Have you seen—" you start to ask a group by the fireplace, and they all smile at you like you've just finished a joke. One woman touches your arm in a familiar way, but you've never seen her before. She says, "Oh, he's around," even though you never said who you were looking for.

You move on.

In the dining room, a dozen people are sitting around a table set for a dinner that never gets served. Empty plates. Full glasses. They are passing around a photograph and nodding solemnly. When you lean in, the photograph is just a blurry picture of this exact room, with all of them sitting at the table, and you standing behind them, leaning in.

In the backyard — which is somehow also on the roof — people are dancing to music that seems to come from underwater. String lights hang impossibly high in a black sky with no stars. Couples turn in slow circles, not quite touching. You weave between them, certain now that you will recognize the back of his head, the shape of his shoulders in that dark green sweater he always wears.

Every time you think you see him, the person turns and it's someone else. A stranger with his laugh. An old man with his eyes. A little kid who waves at you exactly the way he does, with just two fingers.

Your phone buzzes in your pocket. It's a text from him:

*where are you? i'm by the door*

You look up. There are now doors everywhere. Doors in the garden fence, doors standing alone between dancers, a door in the middle of the pool. Each one opens a little as you look at it, spilling warm yellow light and the sound of another party, even louder, inside.

You start opening them.

Behind the first: a childhood birthday party. Balloons, a cake with your name misspelled, adults whose faces you can't quite make out, all singing.

Behind the second: an office you've never worked in, where everyone stands up from their cubicles as you enter and begins to applaud.

Behind the third: just a closet full of winter coats, smelling of rain, and from deep inside the coats, someone breathing quietly.

You close that one quickly.

Your phone buzzes again.

*don't keep looking in the rooms. i'm the one looking for you too*

And suddenly you understand the strange feeling that's been following you all night — glimpsed over shoulders, reflected in windows, always just turning a corner ahead of you. Someone else has been moving through this party, asking groups of strangers, holding a warm drink, looking for the only person they know.

You turn around.

He is standing right behind you, closer than a stranger would stand, looking as relieved and as out of breath as you feel. He isn't wearing the green sweater. He's wearing what you're wearing. He has a small smear of cake on his cheek from a party you haven't been to yet.

"You found me," he says, and his voice is yours, but younger.

He holds out his hand like he wants you to dance. The underwater music gets clearer. Now you can almost make out the song — it's the one that was playing in the car on a road trip you took years ago and haven't thought about since.

You take his hand, and all the groups of people — by the fireplace, at the empty table, in the garden — finally look at you at the same time and smile, not like you're a stranger at all, but like the party can finally start now that you're both here.

The lights get very bright.

And then, in the way of dreams, you are awake in your dark room, your heart beating fast, with the unshakable feeling that you left your drink somewhere at a party, and someone who knows you is still holding theirs, waiting for you to come back.
