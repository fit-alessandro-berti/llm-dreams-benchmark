You try picking the lock with a toothpick, then your own fingernail, but your fingers grow translucent on contact, as if the box refuses to be opened by force. You set it down, and that’s when the patterns shift—slowly at first, like shadows adjusting to new light. They form a door. Not on the box, but within your peripheral vision: a doorway standing in the corner of the room that wasn’t there before, made of the same swirling silver and black.

You understand, with the soft logic of dreams, that the box is the door, and the patterns are the key. You don’t unlock it; you step into it.

Inside, there is no interior space. Instead, you stand in a garden at twilight, and the box is buried in the soil like a seed. Next to it, you find a figure—your own reflection, but carrying a version of the box that is already open. They hand you a single thread from the patterns. You take it, and suddenly you are back in your waking room, the box still locked, but lighter, as if something has finally been acknowledged rather than taken.

You look down. In your palm is a small, warm coin made of starlight—no value, no purpose, except that when you turn it over, you see your own face smiling, but without the slight dread that usually lives behind your eyes. You realize you have been carrying this box inside you all along, and now that you have seen its patterns clearly, it is no longer locked.

You wake with the taste of silver and rain on your tongue, and for a moment, you don’t know if the box is still on your nightstand—or if you are the one inside it, gradually opening.
