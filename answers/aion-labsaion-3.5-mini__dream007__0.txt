I turn my back on her mid-sentence, which is a rude thing to do in waking life but feels perfectly justified here, and I start walking down a street that wasn't there a moment ago. Behind me, her voice keeps going, but the words have changed — she's no longer arguing about the thing we were arguing about. She's reciting my childhood phone number, over and over, getting one digit wrong each time, and each wrong digit stings like she's found a new accusation.

The street is made of that rubbery material from elementary school gym floors. My footsteps make no sound at all, which frightens me more than the argument did. I test it — stomp, jump — nothing. Silence, total, like the street is eating the noise on purpose.

Then the dream does its familiar trick: I'm suddenly aware I've been walking for hours but the house at the end of the street is the same house we were standing in front of when the argument started. It just waited for me to come back around. The porch light is on. She's sitting on the steps.

"I got the number right," she says.

And I realize I'm not angry anymore. I sit down next to her on the concrete step, cold through my pants, and I try to explain the thing that was so important, but my mouth is full of moths. They flutter out when I open it, soft and gray, and she watches them go up toward the porch light like it's the most reasonable way to have a conversation.

"That's what I've been trying to tell you," she says. "That's exactly it."

I wake up with my heart pounding and the strange, aching certainty that we had resolved something — something big — and that the resolution is already dissolving, moth-like, into the ceiling of my bedroom.
