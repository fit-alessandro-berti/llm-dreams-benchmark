You look down at your hands on the wheel. The wood is warm and pulsing, like it's alive. The compass in front of you isn't pointing north anymore. It's spinning wildly, then slowly, deliberately, it stops - pointing straight at *you*.

And you understand.

This storm isn't on the sea. This storm *is* the sea.

You take a deep breath - a breath that feels like your first breath in years - and instead of shouting to brace for impact, you do the opposite.

You let go of the wheel.

Your crew gasps as you step back. You place your palm flat on the soaked deck and whisper, though no one can hear you:

*"Calm."*

For a second, nothing happens. Then, the ocean listens.

The towering wave that was about to break over the bow freezes in mid-air, water suspended like glass. You can see straight through it - fish frozen mid-swim, moonlight trapped inside the water like stars. The dark clouds above begin to thin and peel away, not blown by wind, but as if they were a curtain being drawn back.

Beneath the receding storm, the sky isn't night anymore. It's a vast, soft dawn, pink and gold. And the sea is no longer turbulent water - it's perfectly smooth, black glass, reflecting the new sky so perfectly you can't tell where the ocean ends and heaven begins.

Your ship isn't sailing anymore. It's floating. Flying.

Your crew is no longer afraid. One by one, they move to the edge of the ship and look over, seeing their own reflections smiling back at them, younger, calmer.

You finally understand why they were looking to you. Not because you knew how to fight the storm - but because you were the only one who knew you didn't have to.

You close your eyes, the warm dawn light on your face, and you let the ship drift wherever the stillness wants to take you.

Then you wake up.
