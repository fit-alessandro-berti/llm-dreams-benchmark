Despite the hardships, you decide to follow the faint green light flickering on the horizon.

It leads you through ruined cities where vines glow like veins beneath cracked streets, and past silent rivers that reflect skies full of drifting ash. Along the way, you meet others—scarred, wary, but still carrying small, stubborn hopes: a child with a jar of seeds, an old man who remembers birdsong, a stranger who offers you half a loaf of bread without speaking.

When you finally reach the light, you find it is not a fire, nor a beacon, but a single tree growing from the roof of a collapsed library. Its leaves shine with every color you thought the world had lost. Beneath it lies a book, untouched by rot, its pages blank until you touch them.

Words begin to appear:

**“What survives is not strength, but the choice to begin again.”**

And when you wake, you remember the tree more clearly than anything else—like a promise planted somewhere in the dark.
