the leaves turn their pale undersides to the sky. You hurry toward a hollow in an old oak, but just before you reach it, the first raindrop falls upward.

Then another. All around you, water lifts from the moss in silver beads. A whole puddle rises past your face, and in it you glimpse the kitchen where you grew up. Someone has left the light on over the stove.

You reach for it. The puddle breaks around your fingers, warm as dishwater.

Thunder rolls, and for a moment you’re sure it’s the sound of someone upstairs getting out of bed. You wait for the next footstep. Instead, a fox slips from the hollow oak and sits beside you. It’s dry except for the tip of its tail.

Somehow you know it’s waiting, too.
