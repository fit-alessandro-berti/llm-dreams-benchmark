...and you realize you can't hear the storm anymore.

The wind is howling, tearing at the sails until they snap like paper. The waves are mountains of black water, lifting your ship straight into the sky and dropping it again with a shudder that splinters the deck beneath your boots. Rain hammers your coat, salt stings your eyes — but somehow, it's all silent.

Your crew is shouting, but their mouths make no sound. Their faces are turned to you, waiting. The old first mate with his hand bleeding from a rope burn. The young deckhand clutching the mast for dear life. They aren't looking for an order to save the ship. They're looking for permission to be afraid.

That's when you feel it.

The wheel in your hands isn't shaking anymore. It's steady. Too steady. As if it's no longer connected to the rudder, to the sea at all.

Lightning cracks the sky in half, and in that white flash you see it — underneath the water, far below the churning waves.

Another sky.

Deep, clear, and full of stars, shining *up* through the water while the storm rages above. Constellations you don't recognize slowly turning. And sinking, right beneath your hull, is the faint reflection of another ship — your ship — sailing calmly upside-down on that starry sea, its captain also holding the wheel, looking straight up at *you*.

The choice hits you with sudden dream-clarity: You can keep fighting the storm, gripping the wheel until your knuckles turn white, trying to steer through a sea that doesn't want to be steered.

Or you can let go.

Let go, and fall through.

Your crew is still watching you. The waves hold their breath for half a second at their peak.

What do you do, Captain?
