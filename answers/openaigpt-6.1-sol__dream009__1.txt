The road beneath your feet is packed earth. Beyond it, thatched roofs crowd around a stone church, and smoke rises into a pale morning sky. A cart rattles past, pulled by an ox with a blue ribbon tied to one horn.

You reach for your phone. Your pockets contain only a brass key and a pear.

“You’re late,” someone says.

A girl stands beside a well, holding two wooden buckets. She looks at you as though she’s been waiting a long time.

“Late for what?”

“The bell.”

From the church tower comes a single note. Every person in the square stops moving. Even the ox lifts its head.

The girl drops her buckets and grabs your wrist.

“Don’t listen to the second one.”

She pulls you through an alley and into a baker’s shop. The baker opens a trapdoor without asking a question. You climb down into darkness.

Above you, the second bell begins.

Dust falls from the ceiling. Through a crack in the boards, you watch the baker’s boots turn slowly toward the door.

Then the boots are gone. In their place stand two small bare feet.

You hold your breath.

The girl lights a candle. Along the cellar wall hang dozens of objects: a wristwatch, a plastic comb, a car key, a photograph of a beach.

“Where did these come from?” you whisper.

“People who arrived before you.”

You look down at the brass key in your hand. Its teeth are changing, bending into a shape you recognize.

Your own front-door key.

Somewhere behind the cellar wall, your phone starts ringing.
