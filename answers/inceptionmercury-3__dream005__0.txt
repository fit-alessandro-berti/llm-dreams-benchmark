…and each group seems to be waiting for you to say something specific, though no one tells you what it is.

The first group is standing around a fountain filled with glowing orange water. They are all wearing identical nametags, but the names are smudged, as if written in wet ink. A woman with a kind face offers you a cup of tea. You take it, but when you look down, the cup is made of glass and contains a tiny goldfish. You set it down gently. She nods as if you’ve passed a test.

You move on. The next group is seated at a long table, playing a board game with no board. A man in a velvet jacket asks, “Do you remember the rules?” You don’t, but you say, “Yes,” and somehow that is the right answer. They all stand up, applaud, and hand you a key that doesn’t fit any lock you’ve ever seen.

Then you see a doorway that wasn’t there before. It’s too small, about the size of a cat door, but it glows with warm light. You hear laughter from the other side—and you recognize it. It’s your friend. You crouch down and crawl through, and the room on the other side is enormous, like a cathedral made of pillows. Your friend is sitting on a throne of laundry baskets, wearing a crown made of paperclips.

“Finally,” they say. “I was starting to think you’d never find me.”

You try to tell them about the fountain and the board game and the key, but when you open your mouth, all that comes out is a soft, melodic hum. Your friend laughs, and you realize you’re both humming the same tune.

You wake up still humming, the key still in your hand, though you have no idea where it came from.
