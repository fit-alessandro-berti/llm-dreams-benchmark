You are in the middle of a heated argument with a close friend. The disagreement is about something important to both of you, and you decide to walk away before you say something you can’t take back.

But the hallway outside the room is much longer than it should be. Framed photographs line the walls: the two of you at a beach, at a birthday party, on a train. In each picture, one of you is missing. You stop at the last frame. It shows the room you’ve just left, with your friend standing alone inside it.

“You always do this,” they say.

Their voice comes from the photograph. You turn, and they’re at the far end of the hallway, holding the door open. You can’t remember what the argument was about. You only remember how certain you felt.

You walk back. With every step, the hallway shortens, until your friend is close enough to touch. Neither of you speaks. Then they hold out a photograph you haven’t seen before: the two of you sitting on the floor, laughing so hard you’ve tipped against each other.

“I don’t want to lose this,” they say.

“Neither do I.”

The room is still there behind them, and so is the argument. You go in together and sit down.
