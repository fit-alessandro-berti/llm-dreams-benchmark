Despite the hardships, you decide to **plant a garden.**

It doesn't make sense to anyone else. The others in the caravan tell you it's a waste of water, a waste of time. The soil is ash and cracked concrete, the sky is always that strange, hazy amber, and the wind carries dust that gets in your teeth.

But in your dream, you found something - tucked in the pocket of your patched jacket that you don't remember putting there. A small glass jar, the kind that used to hold jam, filled with seeds. They rattle when you shake them. Sunflower, tomato, basil, marigold. The paper label is faded and hand-written: *FOR AFTER.*

So you do it.

You choose the middle of the dead intersection, right where the old traffic lights still sway even though there's no power. You drag an abandoned bathtub into the center of the road - it's the only thing deep enough to hold soil. You haul dirt from the riverbank, ash and all, bucket by bucket. Your hands get filthy. The sun, huge and strangely close in the dream-sky, beats down on your back.

Dangers pass you by while you work. A pack of scavengers roars past on patched-together motorcycles, but they don't even look at you. A dust storm rolls in from the east, turning the world orange for a moment, but it parts around you like water around a stone. That's how you know it's a dream. The world is soft at the edges.

An old woman who you somehow know, though you've never met her, stops and watches you press the first seed into the damp earth.

"Nothing's grown here in ten years," she says.

"I know," you hear yourself answer, and you smile. "That's why."

You water it with the last of your canteen. And then - because dream time is strange - it happens all at once.

A green point breaks the soil. Then another. Then a dozen. Vines curl impossibly fast around the rusted traffic light pole. A sunflower erupts beside you, taller than you are in a single breath, its huge yellow head turning to look at you. Tomatoes blush red. The air, which smelled like dust and metal a moment ago, suddenly smells like summer and rain and living things.

People stop. First the old woman, then two kids, then the whole caravan. They just stare at this impossible patch of green in the middle of the grey world.

You don't save the world. You just make one small corner of it alive again. And in the dream, that feels like enough to change everything.

You wake up with the feeling of soil still under your fingernails, and for a second, you can still smell the sunflowers.
