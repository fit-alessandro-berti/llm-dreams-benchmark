You notice the questions are not what you studied for at all.

In fact, they're not really *questions*.

At first glance the paper looks normal - crisp, white, stapled in the corner with your name already printed at the top in perfect type. But as your eyes try to focus, the ink shifts.

**Question 1** reads:

> *Draw the shape of the person you wanted to be when you were 8 years old.*

You blink. The words wobble and re-settle.

**Question 2:**

> *List every time you said "I'll do it later" and actually meant it.*

**Question 3:**

> *If you fail this exam, who will you disappoint the most? Be specific.*

Your pen feels suddenly heavy. You look around the exam hall for context, for reassurance that everyone else is seeing the same thing. They are. And they aren't.

The hall is impossibly long, stretching further than any building should. Fluorescent lights hum overhead, flickering slightly out of rhythm with your heartbeat. Everyone else is writing furiously, calmly, as if this is perfectly normal. The person to your left is you, but 10 years older, wearing a suit you don't own. The person to your right is you at 12, swinging your legs because your feet can't touch the floor. Neither of them looks up.

You look back down. The questions have changed again while you weren't looking.

Now there are only two:

> *1. Why are you so afraid of being finished?*
> *2. Show your work.*

And beneath them, at the bottom of the page in faint pencil, is an answer already written in your own handwriting. You don't remember writing it. It says:

> *I already know this. I've always known this.*

Your hand starts to move on its own, not to answer, but to turn the paper over. On the back, there is no exam. There is just a door, drawn in simple black lines. It wasn't printed. It's *drawn* onto the paper, and it's slightly open, with light coming through. And you understand, with the flawless logic that only exists in dreams, that this was never a test of what you had memorized.

It was a test of whether you were brave enough to leave the desk.

The invigilator — whose face you can never quite see — leans over your shoulder and whispers, "Your time is almost up. Are you going to answer, or are you going to go?"

You stand up. The chair makes no sound.

And then you wake up.
