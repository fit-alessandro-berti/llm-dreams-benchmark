…and you do the unthinkable: you let go of the wheel and walk to the bow. The crew watches in horror, bracing for the end, but you stand before the towering wall of water with your arms wide open. "The sea isn't our enemy," you say, your voice oddly calm, cutting through the howling wind. "It's a mirror."

You look down and see the waves aren't water at all—they're a vast, churning tapestry of forgotten dreams, thrashing because they've been neglected. Faces and fragments swirl in the foam: a child's lost kite, a lover's unsent letter, a city that never was. The storm is just the collective sigh of a thousand sleeping minds.

You kneel and whisper to the water, one by one, speaking names you somehow know. The crew, trembling, joins you, and soon they're humming a lullaby older than language. The thrashing slows. The rain turns into petals of soft, luminous light, and the ship begins to glow from the keel up. The dark clouds part to reveal a sky full of constellations that rearrange themselves into a doorway.

"Hold fast," you tell them, though there's nothing to hold onto anymore. The ship lifts, light as a dandelion seed, and sails straight into the constellation's embrace. As the deck dissolves beneath your feet, you feel a profound sense of relief—you were never really captain of a ship, but of the space between waking and sleep.

And then, gently, you let the exhale carry you back. You wake with the taste of salt on your lips, the sound of distant singing in your ears, and a faint, lingering warmth in your chest, as if the storm had been a hug from the universe itself.
