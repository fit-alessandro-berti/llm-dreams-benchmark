...a single wooden paintbrush resting on an easel made of coiled silver smoke. Unlike the restless sky—which melts from violet to bruised amber—or the ground beneath your feet that ripples between soft moss and translucent glass, the canvas refuses to change. It stands as a stubborn, quiet square of pure white.

You pick up the brush. It feels warm, humming faintly against your palm like a tuning fork. There is no palette and no jar of paint, yet when you touch the bristles to the blank fabric, a ripple of deep midnight blue spreads outward like ink dropped in water. 

With another stroke, the chaotic world around you seems to exhale, pouring itself through your arm and onto the weave. Crystalline spires sprout across the linen, while rivers of liquid gold begin to flow toward the edges of the frame. You notice that with every brushstroke, the surreal landscape outside the painting grows paler, draining of its restless color and motion as the canvas drinks it all in.

Soon, the ground beneath you fades into a quiet, featureless mist, leaving only the glowing, vibrant world you’ve trapped within the wooden frame. Before the last of your footing vanishes, you reach out and press your palm against the painted river. It yields like cool silk. You step forward—not around the easel, but straight through it—falling gently into the stillness of your own creation, just as a distant, rhythmic chime begins to pull you toward the surface of waking.
