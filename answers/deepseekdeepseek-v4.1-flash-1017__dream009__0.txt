…you realize you need to find the village bell before it rings again. The streets are mud and straw, the sky too bright, and everyone stares as if they’ve been expecting you. A child tugs your sleeve and points toward a hill where a crooked tower leans against the clouds. You climb it barefoot, though you don’t remember losing your shoes, and inside the bell chamber you find a modern wristwatch hanging from a rope, ticking backward.

The bell swings on its own. With every chime, the world around you forgets a little more—the baker forgets his name, the road forgets where it leads, your own hands blur at the edges. You understand: this era is not a place but a borrowed dream, and someone has wound it too tight. You need to stop the bell. You reach for the watch, press the tiny crown, and hold it until the ticking stops.

Silence floods in. The villagers freeze mid-step, the sky peels back like painted canvas, and the tower becomes your bedroom ceiling. You wake with dust on your feet and a faint bell tone fading from your ears. On your nightstand sits an old wristwatch, ticking forward again.
