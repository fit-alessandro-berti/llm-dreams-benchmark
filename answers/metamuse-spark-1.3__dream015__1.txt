Here's how the dream continues:

You find yourself in a post-apocalyptic world, with resources scarce and dangers everywhere. Despite the hardships, you decide to **keep one small light on.**

The city around you is cracked and overgrown — highways split by roots, stores hollowed out, the sky always the color of pale ash. Everyone else is scavenging, hoarding, hiding.

But you find an old library still standing, its roof half caved-in. Inside, the books are dusty but safe. And in the atrium, where the glass ceiling broke long ago, rain now falls straight in — and somehow, impossibly, a wild garden has grown up between the shelves. Tomatoes, mint, sunflowers pushing through the tile floor.

You decide to stay. To protect it.

Each day in the dream you gather rainwater in buckets, trade a story for a battery, a tomato for a blanket. You sweep the ash off the steps. At night, you climb to the roof and light a big, warm lantern made from a bicycle light and a jam jar — not to keep yourself warm, but so other travelers can see it from miles away.

And they come. A tired mother and kid. An old man who remembers how to fix radios. A dog who adopts you instantly. You feed them from the garden, you read to them by lantern-light from the surviving books, and for a few hours the dangers outside don't matter.

The dream gets hazy and golden at the end. The garden is bigger than it should be, glowing faintly. You realize the library is breathing, and the light on the roof has become a small sun that you can hold in your hand.

Then you wake up — with that warm, stubborn feeling still in your chest that even when everything is scarce, you can still choose to grow something and share it.
