begin to notice that the puzzles are getting easier, but the answers are getting harder to give.

At the first door, you arrange thirteen mirrors so that a candle casts no reflection. At the second, you teach a stone to count backward. At the third, a brass plate asks what you would say to someone you will never see again.

You sit there for a long time. Then you say it.

Beyond the door is your childhood kitchen. Rain taps against the window. Someone has left a spoon across the rim of a cup, and every time a drop strikes the glass, the spoon moves a little closer to falling.

On the table is a map of the maze. You trace your route with a finger and realize that all those winding passages spell a word. You have to turn the map sideways to read it.

*Stay.*

The spoon falls. From the next room, a voice you know calls, “Could you get that?”

You bend to pick it up. The floor is warm under your bare feet. For the first time all night, you do something without wondering whether it will open a door.
