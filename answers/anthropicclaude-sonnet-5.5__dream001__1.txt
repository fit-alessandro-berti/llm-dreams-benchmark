...check your pockets for an invitation, but all you find is a brass key with a number worn off and a folded napkin. The napkin says, in your own handwriting: *"Ask the clock that's running backward."*

So you walk. The city rearranges itself as you go. Streets tilt upward like pages turning, and the buildings lean in to eavesdrop. Everyone you pass is carrying something slightly wrong for the weather: an umbrella in the sun, a birdcage holding a small thunderstorm, a briefcase that hums.

At the third intersection (there is always a third intersection) you find it. A huge clock tower stands with its hands spinning counterclockwise, and the numbers on its face are melting into the names of people you've forgotten. Beneath it sits a man selling minutes out of a cardboard box.

"Looking for your meeting?" he asks, without looking up. "Everyone is, eventually."

"How much for directions?"

"Directions are free. It's *arriving* that costs something." He nods at the key in your hand. "That opens a door that isn't built yet. You'll need to walk toward it like you already know where it is."

You're about to protest when you notice your feet are already moving, and you realize you've been walking this route for years. The air smells like rain and old libraries. Ahead, between a bakery and a shop with no sign, stands a plain green door with a keyhole at exactly the height of your heart.

The key fits. The clock in the distance chimes thirteen.

Behind the door is a long table, and every chair is filled. They all turn to look at you with a mix of relief and mild impatience, and one of them, a person who looks like you but calmer, slides out the empty chair and says,

"Good. We can finally begin."

You sit down. You still don't know what the meeting is about, but as you open your mouth to speak, you understand that somehow you've prepared for this your entire life, and the words are already there, waiting...
