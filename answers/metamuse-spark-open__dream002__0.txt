You feel a storm approaching and —

At first, it's just a change in pressure. The air gets heavy, thick enough to feel on your skin. The peaceful green light filtering through the canopy dims, not because the sun has gone behind a cloud, but as if someone turned down the brightness of the world.

The birds go silent. All at once.

Then you notice the trees are not swaying. The wind has stopped completely. Leaves hang perfectly still in the air, frozen. A single leaf drifts down in front of you and hovers, suspended.

A low rumble rolls through the forest floor, up through the soles of your feet. It’s not thunder. It feels too deep, too close, like the earth itself is humming.

You look up. The sky between the branches is no longer blue or gray. It’s swirling — deep violet and amber, clouds moving impossibly fast in a perfect spiral directly above you, like a great eye opening.

A cold wind suddenly rushes in from nowhere, scattering the frozen leaves into a vortex around you. It doesn't feel threatening, though. It feels *aware*. The forest is holding its breath with you.

Then — the first drop of rain hits your forehead, surprisingly warm.

And the moment it touches you, the dream shifts. The storm doesn't break. The trees around you light up, faintly, from within. Like lanterns. Each raindrop that falls glows for a second as it hits the moss, the branches, your outstretched hands — tiny sparks of light.

You realize you are not caught in the storm.

You *are* the storm passing through.
