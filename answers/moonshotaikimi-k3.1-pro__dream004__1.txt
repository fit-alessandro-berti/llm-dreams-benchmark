You paint a blue stroke. Somewhere behind you, an ocean clears its throat.

You paint a spiral, and a staircase curls out of the air, climbing through the belly of a cloud. You paint seven green triangles, and they become birds, each singing in a different geometric shape. Mountains fold themselves into envelopes. Rivers untie their silver knots. Yet whenever you look back, the canvas remains perfectly blank.

At last, you paint a small red door—the color of every door in every house you have ever loved.

It opens.

Beyond it, a figure sits with its back to you, painting furiously. On its canvas is this world: the melting mountains, the spiral staircase, the triangular birds, and you, tiny at the center, holding your breath.

The figure turns. It has your face, but much older. Or perhaps much younger.

“You’ve nearly finished,” it says.

“Finished what?”

“The dream.”

It offers you its brush. You understand suddenly that one final stroke will wake you, and everything here will vanish—the birds, the ocean, even the red door. So instead of painting an ending, you paint a narrow border around the edge of the world: a small, unfinished space where anything can still happen.

The colors settle. The shapes begin, gently, to breathe.

Then the blank canvas folds itself into a paper boat and sails away across the sky. You follow until the ground becomes water, the water becomes music, and the music becomes your bedroom ceiling.

When you wake, your hand is empty. But on the wall, where there was only blankness before, a small red door is slowly opening.

From the other side comes the sound of an ocean clearing its throat.
