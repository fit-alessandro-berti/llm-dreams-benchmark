...and the challenges keep changing shape before I can finish them.

First, the written exam—except the questions rewrite themselves while I'm reading them. *What do you know?* becomes *What do you want to know?* becomes *What will you admit you don't know?* I answer all three at once, somehow, and the examiner—who is also my third-grade teacher, and also no one I've ever met—stamps my paper with a stamp that leaves no visible mark. But I feel it anyway.

Next, the practical round: build something that lasts. The other competitors build towers—tall, gleaming, impossible. I build a small room with two chairs in it, facing each other. The judges walk straight past the towers to sit in my room. They stay a long time. One of them is crying, but the good kind of crying.

Between rounds, we wait in a hallway that keeps getting longer. The other competitors and I talk while we wait. One woman is competing to be a lighthouse keeper; she says she isn't afraid of the dark, only of ships. One man wants to be the person who decides when it's autumn—he says somebody has to decide, and he's tired of it just happening to him. When I tell them about the job I want, they both nod like it's the most natural thing in the world, and I realize I've never once said it out loud.

The final round is a single question, asked in unison by a panel made of everyone I've ever helped and everyone I couldn't.

*"Why do you want it?"*

I open my mouth to answer—

—and I'm awake. Ceiling. Morning light. The ordinary world.
