You start to ask a passerby for directions, but before you can speak, she points to the envelope in your hand.

You hadn’t noticed you were holding one.

Inside is a single sheet of paper: **TOP FLOOR. BRING THE THING YOU FORGOT.**

You check your pockets. A key, a coin, a small blue feather. None of them feels like yours.

Above you, every building leans slightly inward, as though the city is trying to hear what you’ll say next. You choose the tallest tower and hurry toward it. But each time you turn a corner, the tower appears behind you.

Your watch says you have forty minutes.

Then twenty.

Then it shows a tiny picture of the sea.

At last, you find a door standing alone in a narrow alley. The key fits.

Beyond it is an elevator with no buttons. You step inside, and it begins moving sideways.

“First time?” asks a voice.

Beside you stands a child wearing your old winter coat.

“I’m going to be late,” you tell them.

The child shakes their head. “That’s not what you’re worried about.”

The elevator opens onto a rooftop garden. At the far end, a long table is set with water glasses and unopened notebooks. Everyone turns as you approach.

They are all people you almost became.

One has paint on their sleeves. One looks exhausted. One is laughing. An empty chair waits between them.

“I didn’t bring it,” you say.

The child reaches into your pocket and takes out the blue feather.

Immediately, you remember where you found it: beneath a tree outside your childhood home, on an afternoon when you had nowhere you needed to be.

The person nearest the empty chair pulls it out for you.

There is still an hour before the meeting. Someone offers you a glass of water, and you sit down.
