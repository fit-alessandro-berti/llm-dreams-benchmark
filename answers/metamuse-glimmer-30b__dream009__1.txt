You wake up in a completely different era, with no modern technology around. As you explore your new surroundings, you realize you need to move.

The air is cold and smells like woodsmoke and wet stone. You’re standing in a narrow cobblestone street lined with timber-framed houses, their upper stories leaning out over you like they’re listening. No wires, no hum, no glowing screens. A woman in a patched wool skirt carries a yoke of buckets from a communal well, and a boy herds geese with a stick. Nobody looks at you strangely, which is stranger still.

You try your phone. You don’t have one. You try to remember your name and it slips like water through your fingers. The only thing that feels solid is the growing weight in your chest, that familiar dream-panic that the dream is about to end and you haven’t learned the rule yet.

You need to find the place you woke up in.

You run past the market square, past a bell tower where a man in black rings the hour with a rope. The sound vibrates in your teeth. A sign painted on wood points to the gate. You don’t know which way is home, but in dreams the way back is always the way you came in.

You turn back down the alley where you first opened your eyes, where a low wooden door stood ajar in a stone wall. It’s still there. The door is breathing. You push it open and the cobblestones give way to your bedroom carpet, the ceiling fan ticking overhead, and the soft blue glow of your alarm clock.

You are back.
