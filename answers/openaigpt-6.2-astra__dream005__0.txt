You keep seeing your friend from behind. First she’s by the piano; then she’s leaning through a hatch into the kitchen. Each time you tap her shoulder, a stranger turns around and seems genuinely pleased to see you. One of them hands you a pear. You carry it for a while because there are no tables left, only little groups of people standing where the tables were.

At last you hear her laugh. It’s coming from under the piano.

She’s down there with her shoes off, trying to coax a small grey cat out of the pedals. You crawl in beside her. Above you, someone is playing a waltz very badly.

“Thank God,” she whispers. “You brought his pear.”

You set it down. The cat sniffs it, puts one paw on it, and begins to purr. Neither of you thinks to ask why. You sit shoulder to shoulder, watching the guests’ feet turn slowly on the parquet, until your friend nudges you.

“Come on,” she says. “I want you to meet the woman with the canoe.”
