You are at your workplace, and suddenly, you are given a project with an impossible deadline. Your boss is waiting for you to start, and you cannot remember what the project is.

The brief reprints itself on the desk. First it says *Q3 deliverable*. Then *invent a color no one has seen*. Then *apologize, by Friday, to everyone you have ever disappointed*. Then just your name, over and over, in a font that looks like your own handwriting.

Your boss stands in the doorway and does not blink. Behind them, two more bosses appear, checking watches that have no hands. “Whenever you’re ready,” they say, though the clock on the wall finishes a whole hour every time you look away.

You sit. The keyboard has no letters, only deadlines: NOW, YESTERDAY, TOO LATE. Each key you press jumps the clock forward and turns the coffee in your mug from hot to ice to dust. You try to ask what the deliverable is. Your voice comes out as an out-of-office reply.

Cubicles stretch into a corridor that keeps adding floors. Someone in accounting is stapling a shadow to a report. Slack multiplies with people you have never met, all waiting on a file that does not exist, in a format that has not been invented. You try to say you need more time and the sentence arrives already formatted: *circling back, looping in, close of play*.

You look down. You have already written fifty pages. They are blank. Your boss nods, pleased. “Ship it.”

The floor opens into a stairwell you do not recognize. You fall upward toward Monday, still wearing yesterday’s shirt, and at the top your boss is waiting again, holding a mug that says WORLD’S FINEST START, asking, gently, why you haven’t begun.
