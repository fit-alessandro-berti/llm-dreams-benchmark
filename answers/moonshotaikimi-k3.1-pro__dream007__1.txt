Your friend opens their mouth to answer, but instead of words, a flock of white birds spills out and circles the ceiling. The walls dissolve, and suddenly you’re both standing on a narrow bridge above a dark, restless sea. Behind you is the life you shared; ahead, two separate paths disappear into the mist.

“I’m not afraid of being wrong,” your friend finally says. “I’m afraid that if we don’t agree, you’ll decide I’m not worth keeping.”

You realize you’ve been afraid of exactly the same thing.

The argument begins to seem very small, though the thing beneath it doesn’t. You sit down together on the bridge, your feet dangling over the water, and promise to disagree without turning each other into an enemy. The sea grows calm. Far below, your reflections remain standing, still arguing, while the two of you quietly walk back the way you came.

Just before you wake, your friend takes your hand and says, “Next time, don’t wait until it’s a dream.”
