You accept their offer, and as the strangers—each wearing a faint, amber glow—hand you a tiny brass compass that hums softly in your palm, they begin to walk beside you. Their footsteps echo like distant drums against the pavement, and the bustling city around you seems to soften: the honking horns turn into a melodic rhythm, the neon signs flicker in warm hues of gold and teal, and the crowd thins into a gentle tide of silhouettes. Together you drift down a narrow alley lined with ivy‑clad walls that pulse with faint light, and the strangers whisper directions that feel more like riddles than maps—“Turn where the moonlight meets the stone,” “Follow the scent of rain on concrete,” “Listen for the laugh of a hidden fountain.”

Soon you arrive at a quiet courtyard that appears out of nowhere, its cobblestones smooth as glass and a single fountain spouting crystal‑clear water that never runs dry. In the center stands an old wooden bench, and as you sit, the strangers fade like mist, leaving behind only the compass now glowing brighter, its needle pointing not north but toward a soft, pulsing light within your own chest. A gentle voice, neither male nor female, whispers, “You were lost, but you were never alone; the kindness of strangers is the map you carry inside.” As the light grows, the city’s noise recedes, and you feel a calm certainty settle over you—like a lantern lit from within, guiding you home, even if home is still a question waiting to be answered.
