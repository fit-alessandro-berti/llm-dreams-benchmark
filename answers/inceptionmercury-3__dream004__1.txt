…and the moment you touch it, the canvas is no longer blank.

It hums like a struck bell, and the world around you slows—just enough for you to notice that the shifting colors and shapes are not random at all. They are echoes. A red that keeps melting into orange is the memory of a sunset you once watched from a train. A triangle that keeps breathing into a circle is the shape of a secret you told yourself in a mirror.

The canvas waits. It doesn't ask you to paint. It asks you to *remember*.

You place your hand flat on its surface, and suddenly you are not standing in the landscape anymore—you are inside the memory of it. The colors become sounds. The shapes become temperatures. You feel the weight of every change, every flicker, every moment that was ever lost in the chaos of this world. And you understand: this world was never changing randomly. It was changing *for you*. It was trying to show you something you had forgotten.

The canvas begins to glow. It pulls from your mind a single image—a room you haven't seen in years. A window. A chair. A plate of fruit, slightly too ripe. And in the corner of the room, a small easel with a blank canvas just like this one.

You realize: you are the painter. This world is your painting. And the blank canvas is not a beginning—it's a mirror.

You pick up a brush that wasn't there a second ago. You dip it into the air, where a color condenses like dew. You make the first stroke.

And the world finally holds still.
