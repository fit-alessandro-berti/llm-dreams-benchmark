...and symbols that seem to shift when you aren't looking directly at them.

The box sits on the low table between two windows full of a night that has no stars. It's black, or a brown so deep it's a kind of black, and the patterns are inlaid in something pale — silver, bone, mother-of-pearl — in curling vines that break and reform into figures that almost mean something. You lean closer. They almost do mean something.

You turn it over. The pattern continues, seamless, so that the box might have been carved from a single piece of something that grew rather than made. On the bottom, a brass plate, rubbed blank by many hands or none. No keyhole. No hinges. You run your thumb along a spiral and feel — there — a seam. But the seam runs in the shape of a key, which cannot be, because a key-shaped seam is a lock, and a lock is a place where a key goes, and there is no key.

Your fingers find the seam and the box is warm. Not sun-warm. Blood-warm. And from inside comes a sound: a ticking, or a whisper, or your own breath slowed down to a crawl.

You try, of course, to open it. You pry with your thumbnail. You say *open* in a voice that sounds like someone else. Nothing happens, and in the way of dreams, the nothing happens with such finality that you understand: this is a true thing. A stubborn thing. A thing the dream has decided.

So you stop trying to force it, and you look at your hand instead.

There, in the grey dust your palm left on the lid, a small pale print — the pattern, shrunk to the size of a coin. You look at the print. You look at the pattern. You press your hand down.

And the box takes your hand as its key.

The lid doesn't lift so much as *loosen*, the way a held breath loosens, and warm gold spills out of the seam and pools across the floor, and inside the box there is not a thing but a room — small, familiar, a room you haven't been in for years — and in the room, asleep in a chair with your chin on your chest, is you. Smaller. Older. Curled around a box exactly like this one, and in your sleeping hands is a little brass key, worn bright at the teeth.

You understand, then, though you'd rather not: the box isn't locked. It's a promise. Every night, something true gets set inside it — a day, a grief, a name you couldn't hold — and every night the box gives you back one small forgotten piece, and the key it asks for is always the same. The key is the wanting. The lock is the waking.

You reach inside. The smaller you doesn't stir. The little brass key is warm, and it fits the key-shaped seam in your hand — which is how you know, in the way dreams know things, that it's yours.

And you could take it. You could wake.

Instead you close the lid gently, and the gold goes out like a lamp being pinched, and the patterns on the box settle into stillness. You lie down on the floor beside it, cheek against the wood, and you hear the whisper inside resume its patient ticking, and you whisper back: *not yet. One more hour of this.*

And the dream folds over you like a hand, warm as a held breath, and you go on dreaming, and the box breathes in time with you, and somewhere in the dark, the key turns.
