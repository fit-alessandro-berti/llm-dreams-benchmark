...and you step to the helm, your hands locking onto the worn, salt-crusted wheel. 

The roar of the gale is deafening, yet the moment your fingers touch the wood, a strange, profound quiet ripples through the deck. You look down at the brass compass mounted before you, but the needle isn't pointing North—it’s spinning wildly, etching glowing, golden spirals into the glass.

The crew’s eyes are wide, their faces shifting subtly in the lightning—one moment they are seasoned sailors, the next they are people from your waking life, distant memories, and silhouettes of starlight. They don’t need nautical commands; they need you to remember how this world works.

You look ahead at the wall of water rising before the bow, a rogue wave so immense it blots out the storm clouds, its crest shimmering like shattered mirrors. 

Instead of turning the ship away, you smile. You pull the wheel hard toward the sky.

*"Catch the wind!"* your voice echoes, sounding not like a shout, but like a low, resonant bell that calms the roaring squall. *"Unfurl the sails to the dark!"*

The ship groans, but not in pain—in awakening. As the bow strikes the colossal wave, the vessel doesn't shatter; it tilts upward, riding the sheer face of the water like a staircase. The canvas sails catch a sudden, weightless draft, glowing with faint luminescence, and the heavy wooden hull lifts clean out of the sea.

The spray turns to floating motes of dust and tiny, suspended stars. Below you, the turbulent ocean shrinks into a distant, swirling pool of ink, while above, the storm clouds part to reveal a velvet sky filled with constellations that rearrange themselves into ancient maps. 

The deck steadies. The crew begins to laugh, their voices drifting off like music into the calm, endless night, and you stand at the helm—steering a ship that now sails smoothly through the quiet ocean of the stars.
