The strangers don't ask where you're going. One of them, a woman with silver thread braided through her hair, simply says, "We'll know it when we see it." Strangely, that's enough. A boy carrying a lantern in broad daylight hums a tune you almost remember. An old man in a bright yellow raincoat, though the sky is cloudless, points out things you'd never have noticed: a door painted the exact blue of your childhood bedroom, a fountain where the coins glow faintly like fireflies, a staircase that seems to lead up into the clouds.

At the top of the staircase the city falls quiet. Below you it spreads out like a glittering map, and you realize you can read it. Every street is a choice you've made, every light a person who has crossed your path. The lost feeling loosens in your chest and turns into something closer to wonder.

"This is as far as we go," the woman says, smiling. She presses something small and warm into your palm. When you open your hand, it's a key, though you can't tell what it opens.

"You'll find the lock," the boy says, his lantern flickering. "It's been waiting for you."

You turn to thank them, but the stairs are empty, and the sound of cellos swells as the clouds part. In front of you is a single door, and it's the same blue as your childhood bedroom.

You step forward, the key already glowing in your hand, and as your fingers touch the handle, you feel the dream begin to soften at the edges, like morning light coming through curtains. You aren't afraid to wake. You know now that you were never really alone. You were just on your way.
