…you decide to follow the old railway tracks east, toward the place the scavengers whisper about but never go: the Sunken Library.

No one knows if it’s real. Some say it’s a vault of pre-war knowledge, buried under a dried-up reservoir. Others say it’s just a myth, a dream within a dream. But you’ve seen the maps carved into the rusted boxcars, the faded symbols pointing past the dead forest, past the cracked highway, past the skeletal remains of a city that once hummed with light.

You pack light: a canteen, a knife, a half-empty book of matches, and a photograph of a face you can’t quite remember anymore.

The air tastes like ash and ozone. The sky is the color of a bruise. Every shadow seems to move a beat too slow, and sometimes you hear laughter from places where no one should be laughing. But you keep walking, because in this world, staying still is just another way of dying.

You reach the reservoir at dusk. The water is gone. In its place: a vast bowl of cracked mud, and at the center, a glass dome half-sunk like a shattered skull. The library is real.

You climb down, heart thudding. The entrance is sealed by a door made of rust and tangled vines, but it groans open when you push—like it’s been waiting for you all along.

Inside, the air is cool and smells of old paper, dust, and something sweet you can’t name. Shelves rise into darkness, ten times taller than you, their spines glowing faintly like dying embers. A small robot with one eye and a voice like a broken music box rolls toward you.

“Welcome back,” it says.

You don’t remember ever being here. But somehow, you know exactly where to go. The shelf at the far end, third row, left side. You run your fingers along the spines until you find it: a book bound in green leather, no title. When you open it, the pages are blank—except for one line in handwriting that looks like your own:

“You are dreaming. Turn the page when you’re ready to wake up.”

You hesitate. Outside, the world is ash and silence. Here, there is knowledge, memory, maybe even hope. But your hand is already reaching for the page.

And that’s when you realize—you were never deciding what to do. The dream was deciding for you.

You turn the page.

The world dissolves into light.
