You are given a mysterious locked box with no key in sight. As you examine it closely, you notice intricate patterns and carvings that seem to shift when you aren't looking directly at them — spirals becoming birds, birds becoming letters in an alphabet you almost remember. You run your thumb along one of the grooves and the box hums, warm as a sleeping animal.

The room around you has changed without asking permission. It was your childhood kitchen a moment ago; now it is a library with shelves that curve upward into fog. The box sits heavier in your hands, as if it has decided to trust you with more of its weight.

You understand, in the wordless way of dreams, that the box is not locked against you. It is locked *for* you — holding something until you are ready. The patterns rearrange themselves again and you realize they are a map of your own hands: every line of your palms etched in miniature across the lid.

You press your palm flat against it. The fit is perfect.

The lid does not open so much as dissolve, the way a word dissolves when you repeat it too many times. Inside there is no jewel, no letter, no key to some further door. Inside there is a small window, and through the window you can see this exact moment from above: yourself, standing in the impossible library, holding a box, looking down into a window at yourself, standing in the impossible library, holding a box.

One of the yous — you are no longer sure which — looks up.

"There you are," you say to yourself, and your voice comes out in your grandmother's accent, though she has been gone for years. She always said everything worth keeping locks itself away until you stop searching for the key.

The fog above the shelves begins to descend, soft and silver, smelling of rain on hot pavement. The library exhales. Books flutter their pages like pigeons about to lift off. You close the box gently, and it becomes very small, and then it becomes a smooth stone, and then it becomes the warmth in the center of your chest that you will still faintly feel when you wake — the sense of having been given something, and of having, at last, accepted it.

Somewhere far off, a door you never noticed unlocks itself, quietly, without needing you at all.
