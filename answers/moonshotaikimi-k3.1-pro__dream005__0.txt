A circle of people in green paper hats bursts into laughter whenever someone says “Tuesday.” Nearby, several couples dance while carrying their own chairs, gliding solemnly from one end of the room to the other. At the piano, a crowd applauds a song you cannot hear. When you ask whether they have seen the person you know, everyone points in a different direction.

You follow all of them.

The gathering stretches into corridors that were not there before. You pass a room where guests are exchanging shadows instead of business cards, and another where everyone wears a name tag bearing your name. “There you are,” they say when you enter, but before you can explain, they begin arguing about whether you have arrived too early or too late.

Beyond the kitchen, you find a narrow door you somehow recognize. Inside, the person you have been searching for is sitting at a small table set for two.

“I was beginning to think I’d have to come looking for you,” they say.

You try to describe the dancers, the silent piano, the corridors, but the details dissolve as soon as you speak. Instead, you sit down. They pour tea from an empty pot, and the steam smells like rain on a summer pavement.

You realize you cannot remember their name. You cannot even quite see their face. Yet you feel completely certain that you know them.

“Names are difficult here,” they say, as though you have spoken aloud.

Outside, the party grows quieter. One by one, the rooms go dark, not with the click of switches but with the soft closing of eyes. You ask if you should leave too.

“In a moment,” they say, and slide a small paper crane across the table. “Take this.”

When you pick it up, it unfolds beneath your fingers, becoming larger and larger—a white sheet, a white field, a white sky—until it is simply the ceiling above your bed.

You wake before you can thank them.

All day, whenever you enter a crowded room, you find yourself listening for a familiar voice saying, *There you are.*
