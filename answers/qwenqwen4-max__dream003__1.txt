...and the patterns are breathing. Not sliding, not shimmering — rising and falling like the ribs of something asleep beneath the wood. You understand, with the flat certainty dreams hand you, that the box has been holding its breath for a long time and is only now, in your hands, remembering how.

There is no keyhole. There is a shape where a keyhole should be, and it is the shape of an ear.

So you lean close and tell it something true. You don't decide what; your mouth already knows. You say the name of the street where you learned to ride a bicycle, and the wood warms, and somewhere inside there is a sound like a swallow.

The lid opens onto a smaller box. Of course. You are not surprised, only tired, the way you are tired in dreams when a task turns out to have stairs. The second box is carved with the same breathing pattern, but finer, and its ear is smaller, so you must whisper. The third box you hold between two fingers like a tooth. By the fifth you are kneeling on a floor that has become vast and pale, and the box is a grain of something, and you are speaking into it with the whole of your attention and no voice at all.

Inside the last box is the room you are kneeling in.

You can see the back of your own head, bowed. You can see the enormous pale floor. You can see, in the tiny room, a tiny you, holding something too small to see, and you feel a lurch of tenderness so strong it wakes the wood — the pattern surges, all of it at once, every box breathing in.

And you finally understand what you have been carrying: not a secret but a listener. Something that has been waiting through all these nested rooms for you to get quiet enough to say the thing you actually came to say.

You open your mouth.

The box closes. Gently, the way a hand closes over another hand.

You are standing in a hallway you have never seen and have always lived in, and your palms are warm, and there is a weight in your coat pocket that was not there before, and from somewhere below the floor comes the sound of a lock turning — patiently, one tumbler at a time, as though it has all the time there is, as though it were you down there, working your way up.
