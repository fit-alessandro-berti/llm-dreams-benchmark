You start to run, but the cobblestones ripple beneath your feet like water, and each step sends up spirals of dust that form words in a language you almost understand—warnings, perhaps, or just the names of streets that don't exist yet.

The city breathes. The buildings inhale and exhale slowly, their windows darkening and lightening like heavy eyelids. You follow a thread of red string that unwinds from your pocket, leading you up the side of a structure that shouldn't support vertical climbing, the bricks rearranging themselves to form stairs where there were only blank walls a moment before.

Halfway up, you look down and see your own shadow walking in a different direction, carrying a leather briefcase, already at the meeting. You try to call out, but your voice comes out as music—specifically, the sound of a clock striking thirteen.

At the top, you find a door made of smoked glass. Behind it, you can see a conference room where twelve versions of yourself sit around a long table, each wearing a different outfit representing a different decade of your life. They all look up simultaneously. The youngest one—the child you—waves with a hand that has too many fingers. "Hurry," they mouth, though you cannot hear the words, only feel them vibrating in your teeth like a tuning fork. "The meeting starts when you stop running."

You reach for the handle. On the other side, the room is empty. The chairs are still warm. On the table sits a single envelope addressed to you, and when you open it, instead of paper, you find a mirror—and in the reflection, you are already seated, waiting, watching yourself from the other side with a smile that is both proud and terribly sad.

Outside, the city begins to dissolve into fog, and you realize, with the particular horror of dreams, that you were never late at all. You were the meeting.
