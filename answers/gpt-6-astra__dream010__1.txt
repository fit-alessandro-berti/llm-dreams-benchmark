The first challenge is to build a bridge from things you have lost. Everyone else reaches into their pockets and pulls out keys, photographs, letters. You find only a sentence you never said.

You stretch it across the gap.

It holds.

The second challenge is an interview in a room filling slowly with seawater. The judges wear diving helmets.

“What is your greatest weakness?” one asks.

You begin your practiced answer, but a silver fish swims out of your mouth.

“I don’t know if I want this job,” you say instead.

The water stops rising. One of the judges makes a note.

By the final round, only you and a small child remain. The child is wearing your old school shoes.

Ahead stands a door marked **YOUR FUTURE**. The rules are simple: whoever reaches it first wins.

You run. So does the child. But halfway down the corridor, you hear the familiar slap of a loose sole. You turn and see the child sitting on the floor, trying to tie a broken lace.

You go back.

“Do we have to win?” the child asks as you kneel.

“I thought we did.”

From here, you can see beneath the door. There is no office on the other side. There is grass.

You take off your shoes too.

Together, you push the door open and step into a garden full of unfinished things: a crooked table, a half-painted boat, a ladder leaning against a pear tree.

Someone has left two places set for lunch.

In your pocket, the job description rustles. You unfold it once more.

This time, it is a seed packet.
