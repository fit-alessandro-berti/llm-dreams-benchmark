You decide to stop talking—not to surrender, but to listen. As you open your mouth, the words you were about to say turn into bright moths that flutter up and settle gently on your friend’s shoulders instead of flying out as bullets. The room doesn’t stay a room; its walls begin to breathe like lungs of cream-colored silk, and the floor becomes warm sand that stretches into an endless, glowing shoreline.

The “important disagreement” reveals itself to be a single, shared memory you’d both forgotten: a beach from childhood where you were building a sandcastle together, and a wave came that was too big for either of you to stop alone. You realize the argument was never really about the issue at hand—it was a proxy for the terror of coming apart, of drifting into separate currents.

Your friend, suddenly not a body but a kind of luminous outline, steps closer. Instead of a hand, they offer you a lantern made of still water and moonlight. You take it. The heat of the fight doesn’t vanish so much as transform into cool, deep pressure, like sinking into a tide that holds you gently instead of dragging you under. You sit down together on the sand, and the dream begins to fold in on itself, leaving only the echo of laughter that sounds exactly like your own voice, but softer, and already growing distant.

When the dream ends, you wake with the words *“I don’t want to lose you”* already formed on your lips, and the peculiar, lingering feeling that forgiveness is not a wall you build between you, but a tide you both decide to swim in together.
