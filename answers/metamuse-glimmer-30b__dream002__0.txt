You are walking through a peaceful forest when suddenly the weather changes dramatically. You feel a storm approaching and the air turns thick and metallic in your lungs.

The birds fall silent all at once. The leaves above you, which were golden and still moments ago, begin to shiver and darken as a low, bruised cloud rolls in impossibly fast, swallowing the sun in one gulp. The temperature drops. Your breath comes out in small white puffs even though it was warm before.

The trees seem to lean in toward you, their trunks widening as if listening. A wind picks up, not howling but whispering your name in a voice made of rustling bark and dry leaves. The path you were on dissolves into soft moss that gives under your feet like a sponge.

Lightning cracks, but it doesn't illuminate the forest — it illuminates something *inside* it. For a half-second you see the shapes between the trees: tall, thin figures made of mist and branches, standing still and watching. They don't move when the thunder hits.

Rain doesn't fall. Instead, droplets of light begin to fall upward, rising from the forest floor into the sky, and each one you touch feels warm, like a memory of a summer you can't quite place. The storm is right on top of you now, but you are completely dry.

You realize you are not walking toward the storm. You are walking *through* it, and the forest is walking with you. The path ahead opens into a clearing you swear wasn't there before, and in the center of it stands a wooden door, perfectly square, set into the trunk of an enormous tree that wasn't a tree at all.

You wake up just as your hand reaches for the knob.
