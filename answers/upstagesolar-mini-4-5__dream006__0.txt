I walk to the center of the stage, but my legs feel heavier than they should, like I'm walking through warm water. The lights are blinding, and I can't tell how many people are out there—only that the silence has a texture, a pressure, like a held breath.

I reach for my notes, but my hands are clumsy, and the paper slips from my fingers. It flutters down, and for a moment I think I might catch it, but I don't. The audience murmurs, a soft wave of sound.

Then I notice something strange: the crowd isn't faces. They're rows of small, open books, their pages fluttering gently. And I understand, in the way you understand things in dreams, that I'm not speaking to them—I'm writing to them. Each word I say becomes a letter, each pause becomes a paragraph. The silence isn't empty; it's the space between sentences.

I step back from the podium and start again, slower. The books begin to fill with ink, and the ink is warm. I feel a tickle on my shoulder—someone has turned a page.
