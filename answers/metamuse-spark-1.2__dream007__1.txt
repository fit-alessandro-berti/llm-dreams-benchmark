You decide to just... stop talking.

Mid-sentence, mid-shout, you just stop.

And in that sudden silence, something strange happens.

Your friend is still shouting, but their voice sounds muffled now, like you're hearing them from underwater. You look around and realize the room you're arguing in isn't where you thought you were. It's your childhood kitchen. Or your old school hallway. The walls are the wrong color, the perspective is stretched too long, the way it only ever is in dreams.

Your friend is still there, face red, hands gesturing - but you notice for the first time how tired they look. How small their shoulders are. How this argument that felt so HUGE a second ago suddenly feels like you're both just scared about the same thing.

You decide to do something you would never do in real life while angry.

You decide to get closer instead of pulling away.

You step forward and sit down right on the floor. You pat the floor next to you, inviting them to sit too. To your surprise, they do. The anger drains out of the room like water receding.

And as you both sit on that impossibly tilted floor, you finally say the thing *underneath* the argument - not the point you were trying to win, but the truth:

> "I'm afraid this is going to change us."
> or
> "I just need to know you still care."
> or
> "I don't want to lose you."

As soon as you say it, the house stops shaking. The air, which had felt so thick and hot, turns cool and clear. Outside the window, it starts to rain inside-out - light falling upwards from the ground to the sky.

Your friend doesn't have a perfect answer. They just nod. They look relieved, like they were waiting for you to say that the whole time. They reach out, not for a dramatic hug, but just to bump your shoulder with theirs. A small, quiet truce.

You both sit there for a moment, not fixing anything, just breathing in the same quiet room together while the strange, beautiful rain falls the wrong way.

And you wake up with that feeling still in your chest - the echo of the argument, but even stronger, the warmth of sitting on the floor together.
