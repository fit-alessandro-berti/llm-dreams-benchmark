You are in a maze filled with complex puzzles. Each puzzle requires intense concentration and logical thinking to solve, and as you move deeper into the maze, you find that the puzzles begin to notice you back.

The first chambers were almost honest: weighted scales, missing numbers, a bridge of liars and truth-tellers. You solved them the way you always have—quietly, with a held-breath attention that made everything else go thin. Deeper in, the walls are unfinished equations. Corridors rearrange when you blink. A hallway of identical doors is also a liar’s paradox. Your own footsteps arrive a few seconds before you do.

Concentration becomes the weather. The harder you think, the narrower the path. You work a proof on a blackboard that is also a window, and through it you see yourself three turns back, still writing the same line. A locked gate asks not for a key but for the thought you discarded three rooms ago. You give it up. The stone drinks the sentence. Each correct answer shaves something off—a memory, a name, a reason you entered.

At the center there is no prize. Only a table, a chair, and one last problem in your own handwriting: *If this is a maze, who built the hunger to leave it?* You pick up the pencil. The lead is warm. Outside the walls, something that might be morning is assembling itself from leftover logic.
