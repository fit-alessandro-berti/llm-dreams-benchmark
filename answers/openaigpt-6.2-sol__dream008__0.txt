Behind you, your boss clears her throat. You click Save, and the office goes dark.

When the lights come back on, you’re both standing in a laundromat. Rain taps against the windows. Your boss is in slippers, feeding quarters into a dryer.

“What was the project?” you ask.

She takes a folded sheet from her pocket. It’s a permission slip for a field trip you missed in fourth grade. At the bottom, where your mother’s signature should be, someone has written *Please return by yesterday.*

“Oh,” your boss says. “You can still go. The bus is out back.”

You push through the door. All your classmates are there, just as you remember them, except everyone has your boss’s sensible haircut. The kid who never let you sit next to him lifts his backpack off the seat.

“You made it,” he says, a little annoyed. “We’ve been driving around the block for years.”
