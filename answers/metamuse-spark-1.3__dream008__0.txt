Of course — let's slip back into it:

---

You are at your workplace, and suddenly, you are given a project with an impossible deadline. Your boss is waiting for you to start, and you...

look down at the brief in your hands, but the words won't stay still. The deadline at the top says **DUE: IN 10 MINUTES**, and every time you blink, the number gets smaller. 9 minutes. 8 minutes.

Your boss is just standing there by your desk, smiling, but not blinking. The fluorescent lights above you start humming louder and louder until the hum becomes a buzzing, like bees.

You try to open your laptop to start, but the keyboard is suddenly made of jelly. Your fingers sink in. You try to type your name and the letters swim off the screen like little fish. You try to say, "I need more time — this is impossible," but your voice comes out slow and underwater.

Everyone else in the office is typing perfectly, furiously fast. *Click-clack-click-clack.* Stacks of paper on your desk start multiplying on their own. One folder becomes two, becomes ten, becomes a tower wobbling toward the ceiling.

Then you look at the clock on the wall. The hands are spinning backwards, faster and faster, until the clock melts right off the wall and *plop* — lands on the carpet.

And that's when it clicks: *Wait. This is a dream.*

The second you realize it, everything gets quiet. Your boss's frozen smile softens. The buzzing lights dim to a warm golden glow. You stand up, push the wobbling tower of paper aside — it feels light as feathers now — and you say, "I'll get it done."

You pick up one single paperclip from your desk, hold it up, and it unfolds into a key. You walk calmly past your boss, past the melting clock, straight toward the office door that was never there before. You open it, and outside is not the parking lot, but a wide, sunny beach at 5pm on a Friday.

You step out, and the deadline, the buzzing, the jelly-keyboard all dissolve behind you like mist.

And then you wake up.
