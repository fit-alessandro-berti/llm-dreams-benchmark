You wake in a room the color of old honey, the air thick with woodsmoke and the smell of lard burning somewhere below. There are no zippers, no hum of electricity, no plastic—only roughspun wool beneath your fingers and a single high window letting in a blade of dusty light. Your phone is gone. Your clothes have become something else: a linen shirt, leather shoes, a weight at your hip that feels like a small iron key.

You step outside into a village that seems to have been painted by someone who had only heard rumors of Europe. The streets are mud-churned cobblestones. Horses clip-clop past with faces too large, too knowing. The people wear masks—not for plague, but for ceremony—and they speak in a tongue that tastes like Latin on your teeth. You understand them just enough to be frightened.

A woman with indigo-stained fingers grabs your wrist. Her eyes are wet and bright. "The Mist Walker," she hisses in that half-language. "You are the one who fell from the sky without wings. You must go to the chapel at the cliff's edge before the moon bleeds."

As she speaks, you feel the truth of it settle in your chest like a stone. You need to find the chapel at the cliff's edge, because buried beneath its altar is a mirror that reflects not your face, but the place you came from. You need to look into it and remember—*really* remember—the particular noise of traffic, the shape of your own front door, the taste of cold water from a tap. The villagers believe that if you forget your origin, the era will swallow you whole, and you will become a ghost haunting a timeline that was never yours.

So you run.

The journey is not measured in miles but in breath. You cross fields of rye that whisper your name. You wade a river where the fish have hands. Night falls like a curtain, and the stars wheel overhead in patterns that are almost—but not quite—familiar, as if the sky itself belongs to a slightly different universe.

At the cliff, the chapel stands half-swallowed by fog. Inside, the air smells of iron and ozone. Beneath the altar, you find the mirror. It is small, framed in bone, and when you look into it, you do not see your bedroom or your street. You see yourself—this version of you, in the linen shirt, with the iron key at your hip—standing in the chapel, holding the mirror. And behind you, a door that should not be there.

You turn. There is a door. Through its keyhole, you can see the soft blue glow of a screen, the never-quite-silent sigh of your world.

You need to choose: turn back, or open the door.

Your hand finds the iron key at your hip. It slides into the lock perfectly.

But as you turn it, the chapel begins to dissolve like wet ink, and you realize with dream-logic certainty that the door does not lead home. It leads to the next era. And the one after that. And that you have always been walking, through every when, carrying only the terrible, precious weight of remembering that there is a "now" to which you cannot return.

The door swings open. You step through.

And you wake in a room the color of old honey...
