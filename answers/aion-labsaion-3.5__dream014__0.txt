...and you follow them through streets that seem to shift and rearrange themselves as you walk. The buildings grow taller and stranger, their windows glowing with colors you can't quite name. The strangers speak in soft, musical voices, and though you can't make out their exact words, you understand them anyway—the way you understand things in dreams.

One of them takes your hand, and their palm is warm, impossibly warm, like holding sunlight. They lead you down a narrow alley that opens into a space the city shouldn't contain: a garden, vast and green, with a fountain at its center. The water sounds like laughter.

"This is where you were trying to go," one of them says, and somehow you know they're right. You sit by the fountain's edge, and the strangers sit with you, and for the first time in what feels like forever, the lost feeling drains out of you like water through sand.

The sun is setting, though it was noon just a moment ago. The sky turns colors no sunset has ever been—deep violet, molten gold, a soft green like new leaves. You want to thank them, but when you turn, the strangers are fading, becoming translucent, smiling gently.

"You were never really alone," one whispers, just before they dissolve into motes of light that drift up toward the impossible sky.

You wake with the phantom warmth of a hand still in yours, and for a moment, the morning light spilling through your curtains looks almost like that garden.
