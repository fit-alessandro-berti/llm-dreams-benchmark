...you begin to realize that you have no idea what you are supposed to say.

The notes in your hand are blank. The microphone feels like a foreign object. The faces in the audience blur into a sea of polite, patient smiles. Your heart thumps.

Then you remember: this is a dream. You can do anything.

You set down the microphone, step away from the podium, and begin to speak in the voice of a gentle, echoing cathedral bell. The audience doesn't seem to mind. Their eyes shimmer like television static. Somewhere in the back row, a man is slowly turning into a sunflower.

You tell them the truth — the whole truth, the one you've never dared to say aloud. You tell them about the secret door behind the refrigerator, about the whale that lives in the basement, about how you once swallowed a star and it's still glowing inside you.

The crowd leans forward. They are not listening with their ears. They are listening with their bones. The lights dim and then brighten, like a heartbeat.

You realize you don't remember your own name. You don't need it. The speech was never about words. It was about the silence between them — that beautiful, shimmering silence that you now let grow, like a garden, until it fills the entire hall.

When you finally stop, there is no applause. Instead, everyone stands and takes a deep breath at the same time. The air smells like rain and freshly baked bread. You bow, and the stage dissolves into a vast, starry ocean, and you are swimming, weightless, toward a moon that is smiling at you.

You wake up with the feeling that you gave the best speech of your life — one you'll never remember, and never need to.
