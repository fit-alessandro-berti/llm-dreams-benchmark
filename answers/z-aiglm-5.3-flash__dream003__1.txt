...carvings that don't quite hold still. Every time you blink, the spirals have shifted a little closer to the corners, like ivy growing in fast-forward. One pattern looks like a key. Another looks like a door. Another looks disturbingly like your own signature, though scrawled by someone who learned to write from the wind.

You tilt the box toward the light. It's heavier than it looks—or maybe lighter than it should be; even gravity can't seem to make up its mind. From somewhere deep in the wood comes a faint ticking. Not clockwork. Something more patient. A heartbeat counting something much longer than seconds.

Your fingers find a seam in one of the patterns, and without thinking—which is how everything happens in dreams—you press it.

A hum begins, low, felt more than heard, rising through your palm like a swarm of bees made of light. The carvings race now, flowing across the surfaces like mercury, all converging on the center of the lid, where they coil and tighten into a single word you somehow already know how to read:

*Remember.*

The lock clicks—not with the sound of metal, but with the unmistakable creak of your childhood front door.

The lid rises on its own.

Inside is your grandmother's kitchen. Steam curling from a kettle. A square of sunlight on the wall where the window should be, but isn't. And there she is, back turned, humming a tune you didn't know you remembered until this exact moment.

She doesn't look at you. She just says, gently, the way she always did when you came in muddy from playing outside: *"Wash your hands. Dinner's almost ready."*

You glance down at your hands—

—and they are very old hands, wrinkled and spotted, though you cannot remember ever growing older.

Somewhere, far away, an alarm is ringing. Or perhaps it's only the kettle beginning to sing.

In the dream, you decide it's the kettle. There is still time for dinner.
