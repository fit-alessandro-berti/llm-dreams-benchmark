


You are walking through a peaceful forest when suddenly the weather changes dramatically. You feel a storm approaching and the sky darkens from a pale afternoon blue to a deep, bruised purple. The trees around you begin to sway — not gently, like they do in wind, but as if they are alive, reaching, leaning away from something unseen.

Then you hear it: a low, rolling thunder that doesn't come from above. It comes from *below.* The ground beneath your feet begins to hum.

You look down and see the moss splitting apart, peeling open like skin. Beneath it is not soil, not root, but a smooth black surface — like glass, like obsidian — and in it, your reflection. But your reflection is doing something you are not.

It is looking up at you.

The storm never breaks. It only *gathers.* The trees stop swaying and instead begin to close in, their trunks thickening, bending toward you like the ribs of some enormous creature slowly swallowing you. The air grows warm and wet, like breath. You smell something unfamiliar — not pine, not earth — something like metal and sea and old lightning.

A rain begins, but the rain moves sideways. The drops hover. They float upward before they fall, as if gravity has become uncertain, as if it is asking you a question it cannot phrase.

And then the forest floor speaks. Not in words. In light. A pale vein of illumination crawls out from the black surface and traces a shape on the ground around you — a shape you recognize without knowing why. A door. A threshold. A doorway drawn on the world, waiting.

The storm does not strike. The storm *opens.*

You step through.

Behind you, the forest seals itself shut. The trees stand straight again. The sky forgets its purple. Everything returns to peaceful — except you. You are somewhere now where the air has weight, where silence has flavor, where memory works sideways.

And in your hand, though you never picked one, there is a stone.

It is warm.

It is beating.
