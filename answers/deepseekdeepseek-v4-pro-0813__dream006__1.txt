You begin to speak, but the words do not come out as sound. They materialize in the air above you, glowing cursive letters dripping like honey from your lips. The audience gasps softly; a child in the front row reaches up and catches the word “hope” in her palm, and it glows between her fingers like a firefly.

You take a breath and try again, and this time your voice arrives from the back of the room, as if a future version of you has already finished the speech and is applauding you from the shadows. The stage lights soften into a warm, underwater blue, and the faces in the crowd begin to blur into a single, gentle sea. You realize you are no longer standing at the podium but floating just above the floor, your shoes filled with small, silver birds.

The audience begins to speak your next sentence with you, their mouths moving in perfect unison, but the sound is not words anymore—it is the rustling of leaves, a tide pulling away from shore, the low hum of a dream recognizing itself. You smile, though you cannot remember what you intended to say.

Somewhere far away, an alarm clock starts to ring, but inside the dream it sounds like applause. You bow to no one and everyone, then step backward off the stage into a soft, bright morning.
