…questions are not what you studied. They are written in your own handwriting, in ink that glows faintly, as if it were pulled from your dreams.

The first question asks: "What did you leave behind to get here?"
The second: "Who taught you to be afraid?"
The third: "What would you do if you could not fail?"

The room grows quiet. The clock’s hands are spinning backward. The professor is not the professor—it is you, from ten years ago, smiling patiently. A stack of blank pages sits before you, but instead of questions, they reflect a thousand possible futures.

You realize the exam has no right answers. It has only choices. And the choice you make will not decide your career—it will decide which dream you wake into.

You pick up the pen. It feels impossibly light. You write the truth, even though you’ve never said it aloud. And as you write, the paper begins to bloom, the room fills with morning light, and you understand: you were never being tested. You were being remembered.

You wake up smiling, still holding the pen in your hand.
