You run your fingers over the surface, and the patterns respond like ripples in water, rearranging themselves into a map of stars. A soft hum rises from the box, not mechanical, but almost like a voice trying to remember a word.

You realize there is no keyhole—only a small hollow in the lid shaped like a hand.

When you place your palm against it, the box grows warm. The stars on its surface spin faster, and suddenly you are no longer holding it. You are standing inside it, surrounded by floating drawers, each one labeled with moments from your life: a laugh you forgot, a door you never opened, a promise you almost made.

At the center hovers a tiny silver key made of light.

When you reach for it, the key dissolves into your hand, and you understand: the box was never locked to keep something out. It was waiting for you to remember how to open it.

The dream folds gently inward, and the last thing you feel is the box opening—not outward, but within you.
