The first group stands near a towering punch bowl, dressed in sharp evening wear, communicating entirely through the rhythmic clinking of their empty crystal glasses. As you squeeze past them, you realize the red wine isn’t in their cups at all—it’s floating in lazy, crimson spheres just above their shoulders. You try to ask if anyone has seen your friend, someone whose face is currently blurry in your mind, though you know with absolute certainty they are wearing a yellow jacket. Your voice comes out as a puff of visible, silver smoke, and the group politely nods in unison as if they agree with your point.

You press deeper into the house, which seems to be expanding the further you walk. The hardwood floor beneath your shoes softens into damp, mossy turf, though the crystal chandeliers still hang impossibly high above, swaying slightly in an indoor breeze. 

Near a grand staircase that spirals upward into literal clouds, you bump into a second group. They are huddled in a tight circle, wearing vintage diving helmets and tailored tuxedos. They are passing around a heavy, glowing picture frame, laughing hysterically at whatever is inside. One of them taps your arm and holds the frame up to you; inside is a moving photograph of your childhood bedroom, except it’s completely flooded with water, and goldfish are swimming out of the dresser drawers. You feel a sudden, urgent need to explain that you left the window open, so you apologize profusely and hurry away.

Just then, you catch a glimpse of yellow fabric slipping through a swinging door at the back of the room. Relief washes over you. 

You push through the door expecting a kitchen, and instead step onto a sprawling wooden pier jutting out into a calm, midnight-blue ocean. Party guests are scattered along the dock, casually leaning against the railings, eating hors d'oeuvres that chime like tiny bells whenever they take a bite. 

At the very end of the pier stands your friend in the yellow jacket, looking out at the horizon where two moons are slowly colliding in complete silence. Your footsteps make no sound as you run toward them, the wood stretching like taffy, making the distance take forever to cross. 

Finally, breathless, you reach out and tap their shoulder. You suddenly realize you can’t remember their name, only the overwhelming, anchoring relief of finally finding them. Slowly, they turn around to face you—but just as you see their smile, the colliding moons burst into a blinding white light, and the gentle lapping of the ocean waves sharpens into the steady, rhythmic beeping of your alarm clock.
