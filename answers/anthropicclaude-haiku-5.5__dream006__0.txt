The spotlight is warm on my face, and the crowd blurs into a sea of faces that somehow seem to be made of soft light. I open my mouth to speak, but the words that come out aren't mine. They're a song, low and resonant, and as I sing, the audience begins to sway in unison, like wheat bending in a wind I can't feel.

Then I notice my notes have changed. The paper in my hands is no longer typed text but a map, its ink still wet, showing a city I've never visited, with streets that fold back on themselves and rivers flowing uphill. I look up, and a woman in the third row is smiling at me with the recognition of an old friend, though I have no memory of ever meeting her. She lifts her hand and a small bird made of golden thread lands on her palm.

I want to explain myself, to tell them what I came to say, but the thought slips away like water through my fingers. Instead, I step off the stage and walk down into the crowd, and the people part for me. The floor beneath me becomes a gentle slope, then a meadow, then the shore of a lake where the audience has quietly transformed into a gathering of lanterns floating on the water.

Someone whispers my name, though I don't recognize the voice. I answer with a question, and the whole lake answers back, and somewhere far above, the stage lights dim and rise again, like the slow breathing of something enormous and kind.
