Around you, the room you were standing in—was it a quiet library, or an empty train station?—begins to blur at the edges, melting into a wide field of waist-high silver grass under a bruised violet sky. It doesn't startle you. In the dream, it makes perfect sense.

You lift the box to your ear and hear the distant murmur of an ocean, mixed with the muffled ticking of a clock running backward. Without quite knowing why, you realize the box doesn't want a key; it wants a secret you haven't remembered yet. You lean close and whisper a single word into the seam—a word you’ve never heard before, though it tastes faintly of rain and old cedar.

A soft sigh escapes the box. The carved patterns lift off the surface like dark, delicate moths and flutter away into the violet sky. Without a click or a creak, the lid simply unlatches and drifts upward, dissolving before it hits the grass.

You peer inside. The interior is far deeper than the box itself—a bottomless well of swirling starlight and warm golden fog. Floating right at the center, turning lazily in midair, is a single heavy brass key, far too large to have ever fit the box.

You reach in and close your hand around the cold metal. The moment your fingers tighten, the weight of the box vanishes completely. You look up to find yourself standing in front of an impossibly tall wooden door, standing entirely alone in the middle of the silver field, its surface covered in slowly shifting patterns.

You raise the key, slide it into the lock, and turn it. As the heavy door swings open toward a blinding wash of morning light, the ground gently tilts beneath your feet, and you start to fall—right up to the edge of waking.
