You realize you need to find out *when* you are before anyone notices you don’t belong.

Outside, a bell rings over a street of mud and cobblestones. Carts rattle past timber houses, and a woman carrying firewood gives your unfamiliar clothes a long, suspicious look. You duck into an alley and spot a faded date carved above a doorway: **1847**.

A scrap of newspaper blows against your shoe. Its headline announces a fire that will begin tonight—at the inn beside the bridge.

You know the inn. You passed it moments ago, its windows glowing warmly in the morning mist.

Then you notice something stranger: beneath the headline, in a photograph that should not exist, you are standing in the crowd watching it burn.

The bell rings again, and this time it sounds like a warning.
