

…each one seems impossibly familiar.

One group is laughing over a table made of piano keys. Another is arguing softly while holding up old photographs that are still moving. A woman in a silver coat tells you, without looking at you, that you’ve already found them, but you’re just not standing where you think you are.

The music shifts from a dance band to rain.

You push through a doorway that wasn’t there a second ago and find yourself in a long hallway lined with windows overlooking the same party from above. People below look up at you, then quickly look away, as if they are also searching for someone.

A child walks past carrying a stack of clocks. The clocks are all running at different speeds.

“You’re looking for the one person,” the child says, “but dreams don’t count people. They count moments.”

Then, at the end of the hallway, you see them.

They are standing exactly as you remember, only different in a way that is almost unbearable. They turn and smile, and for one instant the entire gathering falls silent.

But when you step closer, their face changes—becomes your own.

You wake up with the sound of applause still fading in your ears, certain that you had been right on the edge of something important.
