You are at your workplace, and suddenly, you are given a project with an impossible deadline. Your boss is waiting for you to start, and you realize your computer screen is blank—not because it is off, but because every document you open turns into falling sand.

You begin typing anyway, and each keystroke makes a soft chime, like a distant clock counting down. Your coworkers move around you in slow motion, their voices stretched into echoes, but when you look up, their faces are blurred, as if they are still being dreamed into existence.

The deadline appears above your desk like a red digital sun: five minutes left. Then four. Then three.

Your hands move faster, but the work keeps changing shape. One page becomes a map of a city you have never seen. The next becomes a letter you forgot to send years ago. The next becomes a mirror, and in it you see yourself calmly walking out of the office, leaving everything unfinished.

Your boss leans closer and says, “Do you know what this project actually is?”

You look down. The papers are covered in your own handwriting, but the words are from dreams you had as a child.

Then the office lights flicker, the red countdown stops at one second, and your boss smiles strangely.

“Good,” they say. “You finally stopped pretending it was urgent.”

The walls dissolve into morning light, and you wake up with the strange, quiet feeling that the deadline was never real—only the part of you that believed it was.
