...obstacles, each stranger than the last, though none of it strikes you as strange at the time.

The first challenge takes place in a library where the shelves stretch upward into clouds. A judge in a velvet coat, whose face you can never quite focus on, hands you a blank index card and says, "Find the book that hasn't been written yet." You climb a rolling ladder that grows taller with every step. Somewhere near the top, you realize the pages of every book are fluttering like wings, and the one you need is the only book sitting perfectly still. When you open it, it's filled with your own handwriting. You don't remember writing it, but you pass.

The second challenge is a kitchen. You're told to bake a cake that tastes like your earliest memory. The other contestants are frantic: one is a man made entirely of origami, another is your third-grade teacher, and a third is somehow you, but ten years older, who keeps glancing at you as if they want to warn you about something. You reach for the flour, and it turns into sand from a beach you visited as a child. You bake it anyway. When the judges take a bite, one of them starts quietly crying and says, "I remember the seagulls." You move on.

The third challenge doesn't announce itself. You're walking down a hallway toward the final round, and the hallway keeps getting longer. The doors on either side are labeled with jobs: *Astronaut. Lighthouse Keeper. Composer of Rain. Professional Finder of Lost Things.* Behind each door you hear a version of your life happening: laughter, applause, the hum of machinery, silence. You feel the pull to open one, any one, just to stop walking. But the older version of you appears beside you and says, "The competition was never about the prize." Then they vanish, and the hallway ends.

The final stage is an empty auditorium. A single spotlight. The judge in the velvet coat sits in the front row, and now you can see their face clearly. It's yours. They slide an envelope across the stage.

"Congratulations," they say. "You've won your dream job."

You open the envelope. Inside is a single sheet of paper that reads:

*Position: Whatever you choose to become.*
*Start date: When you wake up.*
*Requirements: Already met.*

You look up to ask what it means, but the auditorium is dissolving into morning light. The seats turn into sunlight through blinds, the spotlight becomes the glow of your alarm clock, and the applause fades into birdsong.

You wake up with the strange, lingering feeling that you've just been hired, and that somewhere, a cake that tastes like the ocean is still sitting on a judge's table, waiting for you to come back.
