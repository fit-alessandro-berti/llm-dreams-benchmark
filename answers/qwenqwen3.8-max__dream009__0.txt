You wake up in a completely different era, with no modern technology around. As you explore your new surroundings, you realize you need to find your way to the river before dusk, because the village elders say the water will remember your name if you were truly meant to return.

The air smells of woodsmoke and damp earth. Your clothes are rough-spun, your feet bare, and every step presses warm dust between your toes. Around you, people carry baskets, speak in a language you shouldn’t understand—but somehow do. Their words feel older than sound, like echoes rising from inside your bones.

A child tugs your sleeve and points toward a line of silver willows in the distance. Beyond them, you hear it: the river, moving slow and deep, humming a melody you recognize from dreams you’ve already forgotten.

You begin walking.

Along the path, you pass a blacksmith hammering iron into something that looks like a key, a woman weaving thread that glows faintly blue in the shade, and an old man carving symbols into a stone that matches the mark on your palm. You don’t remember receiving it, yet there it is—etched into your skin like a promise.

When you finally reach the river, the water is so clear it reflects not your face, but another version of you: older, calmer, standing in a time not yet born. The reflection reaches upward.

You kneel.

And just as your fingers touch the surface, the river whispers, “You are not lost. You were sent back to remember.”

The world ripples.

Then you wake again—somewhere else entirely.
