each group seems to be telling a different part of the same story. By the punch bowl, a woman says, “So he folded the lake in half.” Near the stairs, someone adds, “But he forgot about the ducks.”

You spot your friend’s coat on the back of a chair. When you touch the sleeve, you hear them laugh from somewhere inside it. You reach into the pocket and pull out a little brass doorknob, warm from their hand.

There’s nowhere to put it, so you press it against the wall. A door swings open onto a dock. Your friend is sitting at the far end, shoes off, trailing their feet in the water.

“I’ve been looking everywhere for you,” you say.

“I know,” they say, moving over. “You passed me twice.”

You sit beside them. Behind you, the party goes quiet, and for a moment you worry that everyone is waiting for you to come back. Then a duck paddles out from under the dock, followed by another, and another, until you lose count.
