

…and you realize the helm is no longer wood, but something alive—warm beneath your hands, pulsing like a heartbeat between you and the storm.

The wind screams, the waves rise like mountains, and you do not shout. Instead, you sing.

The crew turns toward you in disbelief as your voice cuts through the thunder, low and certain, as though it has always been there beneath the sea. Each note becomes a thread of silver light binding the rigging, steadying the sails, calming the panic in their eyes.

“Not away from the storm,” you tell them. “Into it.”

The ship surges forward.

Lightning splits the sky, and for a moment you see it: at the heart of the storm, an opening like a doorway, filled with silence. The crew braces themselves. You hold the course.

The ship leaps into the eye of the storm—

—and the sea becomes glass.

The clouds curl back like curtains. Above you, stars hang so close they brush the masts. The waves no longer crash; they breathe. The ship drifts into a harbor that should not exist, lit by moonlight and old lanterns floating on the water.

Your crew smiles, no longer afraid.

You try to speak, but the dream is already thinning. The ship dissolves into mist, the stars into morning light, and the wheel beneath your hands becomes the feeling of waking.

You open your eyes.

But somewhere far off, beyond the window, the sea is still singing your name.
