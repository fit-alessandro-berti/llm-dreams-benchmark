You are given a mysterious locked box with no key in sight. As you examine it closely, you notice intricate patterns and shifting filigree that rearrange themselves whenever you blink—vines becoming circuits, stars becoming fingerprints, your own name dissolving into a language you almost remember. The metal grows warm under your palms. A low harmonic hum starts in the box and travels up your arms until it resonates in your teeth. Without touching a latch, the lid sighs open of its own accord.

Inside is not treasure but a smaller, identical box. And inside that, another. Each one opens as you reach for it, the cascade accelerating until you are falling through an infinite nesting of boxes, each interior larger than the last, each pattern more personal: childhood rooms, half-forgotten conversations, faces you loved and never named. At the center you finally land on something that is not a box at all—just an open sky the exact color of the moment before you wake. The last lock clicks shut behind you, and you understand, with the perfect logic of dreams, that you were the key all along.
