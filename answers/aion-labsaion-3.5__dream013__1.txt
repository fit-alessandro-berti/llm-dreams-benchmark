At first, you fight it. You try to apply the same rigid logic that worked in the outer rings of the maze, but the deeper chambers don't reward logic anymore. One puzzle presents you with a locked box and no key, and you spend what feels like hours searching the room, running your hands along the cold stone walls, until you realize with a jolt that the box only opens when you stop wanting to open it. The moment you set it down and turn away, you hear the soft click of the latch behind you.

The walls of the maze grow narrower, then impossibly tall, then suddenly low enough that you have to crawl. Time becomes unreliable. You solve a puzzle that seems to take minutes, but when you pass through the next archway, the light has changed, as if a whole day has gone by.

In the deepest chamber, you finally find the center of the maze. You expect a monster, or a treasure, or some final impossible puzzle. Instead, there is a mirror. And in the mirror, you see yourself—but not as you are now. You see yourself as you were when you were a child, standing in a maze of your own making, waiting patiently for you to arrive.

The child in the mirror holds out a hand.

You reach for it, and as your fingers touch the glass, the maze dissolves around you—not collapsing, but blooming outward, the walls unraveling into ribbons of light that spiral up and away like birds startled from a field.

You wake with the strange, lingering certainty that the maze wasn't something you were trapped in.

It was something you were building.
