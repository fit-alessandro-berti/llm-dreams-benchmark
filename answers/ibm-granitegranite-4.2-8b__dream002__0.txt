You are walking through a peaceful forest when suddenly the weather changes dramatically. You feel a storm approaching and the air grows thick, heavy with the scent of wet earth and ozone—no birdsong, no rustle, just a low, humming silence. The sky cracks open in a single, impossible seam of pale gold light, not rain yet, but a slow, shimmering *unfolding*: leaves on the trees begin to glow faintly from within, pulsing like soft embers, and the wind doesn’t howl—it *whispers* the names of forgotten places you’ve never heard but instantly recognize (a creek you dreamed of as a child, a hill you once climbed in a half-remembered nap). Then, instead of downpour, a single, perfect drop of liquid light falls from the seam—not cold, not wet—and lands on your bare ankle. It doesn’t splash. It *blooms*: tiny, warm blossoms of deep indigo and silver unfurl on your skin for three heartbeats, humming the same quiet note as the whispering wind, and for that moment the storm *inside* you—the tightness in your chest—softens, replaces itself with a quiet, wordless knowing: *this is the forest remembering you*. The gold light widens, the glowing leaves pulse once, gently, and the sky settles back to soft gray, not empty but full of hovering, dust‑like sparks that drift downward like sleepy fireflies. You don’t run. You bend down, touch the blossom on your ankle—it feels like cool rain on sun-warmed stone—and a single, clear thought rises, not spoken but *felt*: *you were never leaving it*. You smile, small and sure, and keep walking, the forest breathing around you again, peaceful now, deeper, as if the storm wasn’t an interruption but the forest taking a slow, necessary breath. The path ahead glows just a little brighter where your footfalls touch it.
