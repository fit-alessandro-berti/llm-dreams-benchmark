You are walking through a peaceful forest when suddenly the weather changes dramatically. You feel a storm approaching and the light goes green, the way it does under deep water. The birds stop all at once, not one after another but together, as if someone has lifted a needle from a record. The leaves turn their pale undersides up toward the sky. You notice that you can hear your own heartbeat, and then you notice that it is not coming from your chest but from somewhere ahead of you, further down the path, slow and patient, like a drum in an empty hall.

The first drops of rain do not fall. They rise. They lift off the ferns and the moss and drift upward in slow silver beads, and when one touches your cheek it is warm and tastes faintly of salt, and you understand without being told that the forest is crying in reverse, taking back something it had given away a long time ago.

You keep walking because there is nowhere else the path goes. The trees grow closer together and taller, their trunks smoothing into columns, and their bark begins to show grain like the grain in old furniture, the kind in a house you lived in as a child. You run your hand along one and feel the ridges of a doorframe. There are pencil marks at different heights with dates beside them. The highest one is above your head, and the date beside it has not happened yet.

Thunder comes, but it arrives as a sentence. You cannot make out the words, only the shape of them, the way you can tell someone is asking a question from another room. The sky answers itself. Lightning flickers, and in the flash you see that the forest floor is not floor at all but the surface of a lake, very still, and beneath it the trees continue downward, roots becoming branches, a second forest hanging upside down in the dark water with its own small storm brewing beneath its own inverted sky. A figure is walking down there too, on the underside of the path, and when you stop, it stops. When you raise your hand, it raises its hand. But it does so a half-second before you do.

The wind arrives at last, and it is full of paper. Letters, receipts, pages torn from notebooks, all written in your handwriting, all things you meant to say and did not. They wrap around your ankles and press against your coat. One flattens itself against your chest and you read it without looking: I was never angry. I only wanted you to stay a little longer. You do not know who wrote it or who it was for, and you realize that in dreams these are the same question.

The heartbeat is louder now. The path opens into a clearing, and in the center of the clearing stands a single door with no wall around it, painted the blue of a kitchen you can almost remember. The rain rises around it like a curtain of beads. The storm is circling the clearing but will not enter it, the way a dog will not enter a room where someone has died. The knocking is coming from the other side of the door. Slow. Patient. It has been knocking for years.

You put your hand on the knob and it is warm, as if someone has just let go of it. You think: if I open this I will wake up. You think: if I don't open this I will never wake up. You cannot tell which of these thoughts is the fear and which is the hope.

You open the door.

On the other side is the forest, peaceful, unbroken, sunlit, with birdsong threaded through it and no storm anywhere. And walking down the path toward you, from the far distance, is someone whose face you cannot see yet but whose walk you would know anywhere. They lift a hand. You lift yours. This time you move first.

Behind you, softly, the rain begins to fall the right way.
