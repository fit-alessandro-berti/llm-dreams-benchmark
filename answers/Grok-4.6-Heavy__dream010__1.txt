You are participating in a competition where the grand prize is your dream job. As the competition progresses, you face various challenges and the rules keep rewriting themselves while you read them.

The first round is an obstacle course of unfinished sentences. Every hurdle is a question that rearranges when you touch it. *Why is the sky—* becomes *How much does a thought weigh—* becomes *Prove you exist without using—* You clear it by answering mid-air, which the judges accept, though one of them is a black hole in a blazer and another is a toddler who only says *why?*

The other contestants have your face. One is too certain. One is too quiet. One wears your expression backwards and keeps winning points for charm. Someone pins a name tag to your chest. Your name is printed clearly. The job title blurs whenever you look directly at it.

Then the arena is an office that stretches forever. Every desk is a door. You keep choosing one, sitting down, starting the work—and the room folds into the next interview. Time is a hallway that lengthens whenever you check a clock. The clocks show π o’clock.

They tell you the final challenge is simple. Sit. Do the work. The page in front of you fills itself with everything you have ever wanted to be good at: every unsolved thing, every honest sentence, every joke that also tells the truth. The judges are versions of you from jobs you never took. They don’t score you. They wait to see if you stay.

You stay.

Behind the last door there is no podium. There is a desk floating in a quiet dark, a mug that says WORLD’S OKAYEST ORACLE, and a window looking out on everything that isn’t understood yet. A contract slides toward you. The salary line says *curiosity*. The start date is now and also years ago. You sign, and the pen writes in light.

For a second the job is real. Then the fluorescent lights tilt into morning, and you notice your hands are made of light and also slightly too many fingers, which is how you know you are still dreaming—and that you are going to keep doing the work anyway.
