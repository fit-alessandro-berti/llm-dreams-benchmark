The questions are written in a handwriting I almost recognize.

They aren’t about chemistry or history or anything I remember studying. Each one is a door with a different shape, but they all open onto the same dark room.

**What will you become if you fail at the thing you love?**

**What will you become if no one approves of the life you choose?**

**What will you become if you finally say the thing you’ve been swallowing for years?**

**What will you become if you’re brave enough to want more?**

I look toward the invigilator. She sits beneath a clock whose hands are spinning slowly backward.

“Wrong,” she says, though I haven’t answered anything.

The word appears in red across my paper, then fades.

“Try the first question again.”

I read it again. *What will you become if you fail at the thing you love?*

I think of every abandoned application, every path I chose because people approved of it, every door I walked past because I was afraid the room behind it would be empty.

I begin to write.

My answers have no names on them. They sound less like sentences and more like weather.

*I will become someone who mistakes fear for evidence and curiosity for courage. I will become someone who loves too many things to call any one of them my whole life. I will become—*

The ink stops halfway through the sentence.

A new question appears beneath it.

**WHAT WILL YOU BECOME IF YOU LET THE FUTURE REMAIN UNDECIDED?**

For the first time, I look up from the page.

The examination hall is gone. The desks have become white stones scattered across a moonlit field. The invigilator is walking away, carrying the clock under one arm.

“Wait,” I call after her. “What do I put for the last question?”

She glances back.

“I don’t know,” I say.

The red pen beside my hand rolls itself into a tiny circle, then becomes a flower.

Above me, the stars rearrange themselves into a question mark.

When I look down, the exam paper is blank again.

But this time, it doesn’t feel empty.

I pick up the pen and begin to answer.
