…all written in your own handwriting.

But you have never written them.

The first asks, **What do you want when no one is trying to impress?**

The second: **Which fear have you mistaken for a plan?**

The third: **What would you attempt if failure could not follow you?**

There are no equations. No definitions. No questions about anything you studied. These questions open like doors inside your chest, each one leading somewhere you have spent years refusing to look.

You glance up at the invigilator. They are watching you with an expression of recognition—not stern, but almost proud. At the next desk, a student tears their answer sheet into small squares. Each square unfolds into a paper bird and flies toward the window.

The clock counts down: 3, 2, 1.

Then it begins again.

You turn to the final page. Beneath the last question—*What will you choose to become?*—a smaller sentence has been pressed so hard into the paper that the letters shine.

**There is no wrong answer. Only the answer you can live with.**

The room tilts. Beyond the window, the sky splits into hundreds of possible mornings. In one, you are successful but strangely absent. In another, you are frightened, surrounded by people who believe in you. In a third, you are alone—but content.

You cannot see which life is yours.

The pen is warm in your hand.

You understand then that the exam has not been deciding your future. It has been showing you that you have been deciding it all along: in every small courage, every abandoned ambition, every choice you made simply because it was safe.

You lower the pen to the page.

For once, you do not write what you think the future wants.

You write what **you** want.

The paper beneath your hand softens, becoming a map. Lines appear where answers should be. The professor’s chair creaks. Somewhere beyond the walls, a door begins to open.

And this time, you are the one standing on the other side.
