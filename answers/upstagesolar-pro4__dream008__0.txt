Your boss is waiting for you to start, and you reach for the keyboard, but your fingers pass right through the keys like they're made of smoke. 

The office around you begins to lose its edges. The fluorescent lights overhead stretch into long, golden beams, like sunlight breaking through water. Your boss opens their mouth to speak, but instead of words, a cascade of colorful clocks spills out, tumbling across the desk and melting into puddles of ticking sound. 

"Initiating sequence," the voice of your boss echoes, not from in front of you, but from inside your own head. 

Suddenly, the monitor doesn't display a document anymore; it shows a window looking out onto a starry night sky. You realize the deadline isn't tomorrow—it's yesterday. You turn back to your boss, but they have turned into a giant hourglass, sand flowing upward against gravity. You aren't supposed to work on the project; you were supposed to wait it out. You wake up just as the sand runs out.
