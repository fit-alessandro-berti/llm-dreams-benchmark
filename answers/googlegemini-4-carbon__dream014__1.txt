Instantly, the deafening roar of sirens and traffic softens into the gentle, rhythmic hum of a distant cello. You notice then that these strangers aren't entirely ordinary; one wears a coat that seems woven from twilight, and another carries a glowing paper lantern that smells faintly of rain and cinnamon. 

"You're trying to read the streets," the woman with the lantern says, her voice echoing softly in your mind without her lips ever moving. "You have to let the streets read you."

She hands you a brass compass. Its needle spins wildly for a second before pointing straight up. Laughing softly, the group begins to walk—not down the crowded sidewalk, but right up the side of a towering glass skyscraper. You take a breath and follow, marveling as gravity shifts effortlessly beneath your soles. 

With every step skyward, the heavy knot of loneliness in your chest unravels. Looking back, the chaotic, intimidating grid of the city melts into a glittering constellation below your feet. By the time you reach the clouds, which are tinted neon pink and gold by the city lights, you realize you can’t even remember the address you were so desperately looking for. Walking beside them in the quiet air, you are no longer lost at all. 

At the very top of the building, the glass beneath your feet turns into calm, shallow water reflecting a rising sun. The strangers turn to smile at you, their forms slowly dissolving into golden morning light, leaving you wrapped in a profound sense of peace just as your eyes begin to flutter open.
