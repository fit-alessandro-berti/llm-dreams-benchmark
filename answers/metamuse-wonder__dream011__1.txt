...in your own handwriting.

You lean closer, confused. You definitely don't remember writing them, but it's yours — the same messy loops, the same way you cross your t's.

**Question 1:** *Name one thing you pretended to understand, but never actually did.*

Your pen hovers. Around you, everyone else is already writing furiously, like this is perfectly normal. The clock on the wall isn't ticking forward. It's ticking *louder*, and every time it ticks, the hands move a little closer to your desk.

**Question 2:** *What were you doing at 3:17 PM on the Tuesday you decided what you wanted to be when you grew up?*

You don't know. How could anyone know that? But as you stare at the blank line, you can suddenly smell rain on hot pavement and hear a kitchen radio playing somewhere far away. Your hand starts writing before you decide to.

**Question 3:** *If you fail this, who are you still allowed to be?*

The room shifts. The professor at the front of the class is no longer your professor. It's you, maybe ten years younger, wearing an oversized graduation gown and looking just as nervous as you feel.

"Five minutes left," younger-you announces, even though you just started.

You flip the page desperately, hoping the next questions will be normal — something about formulas, dates, definitions, anything you could have studied for.

**Question 4:** *Write down the career you would choose if nobody would ever find out whether you succeeded at it.*

This one has a huge blank space. Pages and pages of it. Your pen doesn't run out of ink no matter how much you write, and the words disappear as soon as you finish a sentence, making room for more.

**Final Question:** *Are you ready to wake up and try again?*

You look up. The classroom is empty now. All the other desks are gone, the clock has stopped, and younger-you is holding out your graded exam.

You reach for it —

And you wake up at your desk, textbook open, highlighter still in your hand, five minutes before your alarm.
