You take shelter by habit, ducking under a beech. In a hollow between its roots sits a little green frog, watching the last of its puddle drift away. Soon it is perched on bare mud, blinking. You hold out your hand. It steps onto your palm with the grave confidence of someone who has been expecting a lift.

The thunder rolls once, very close. For an instant every tree is transparent, and you can see the water climbing inside the trunks in bright, narrow streams. High among the branches hangs a pond. Its surface trembles with the arrival of each drop. The frog leans toward it.

“All right,” you say, and turn your hand over.

Instead of falling, the frog sails slowly upward, hind legs trailing. You watch until it slips through the silver surface with scarcely a ripple. Then the clouds move on. Sunlight returns to the path, and you walk home with one cool, webbed footprint drying in the middle of your palm.
