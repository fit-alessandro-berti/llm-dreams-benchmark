Here's a continuation of that dream for you:

You feel a storm approaching and the air itself seems to hold its breath.

The peaceful hush of the forest is replaced by a low, electric hum. The wind picks up - not cold, but charged - making the leaves shiver and turn silver-side up, all at once, as if the trees are bracing together. Sunlight filters through the canopy in sharp, slanted beams, even as the sky above the branches bruises from blue to a deep violet-gray.

You can smell it coming - pine and damp earth and ozone - and you feel the pressure drop in your ears like being underwater.

Then the first crack of thunder. It's not loud, but deep and rolling, vibrating up through the soles of your feet from the roots below.

The forest doesn't become frightening. It becomes *alive*. The path ahead of you glows faintly, as if the coming rain has already painted everything more vivid. A few fat, warm drops begin to fall, tapping on leaves like fingertips on drums.

You run - not from fear, but from instinct - toward the massive, hollow oak you noticed earlier but somehow forgot until now. Its trunk is wide enough to step inside, and its interior is dry, soft with moss, and lit by the strange, stormy light leaking through the knots in the wood.

Inside, you are safe and completely still while the storm finally breaks outside. Rain pours in sheets, wind sings through the branches, and flashes of lightning illuminate the forest in perfect, silent photographs. You watch the storm wash the whole world clean, and for a moment, you feel like you are breathing in time with the forest itself.

And then, just as suddenly, the rain softens to a whisper, the clouds part, and a single ray of sunlight finds its way directly to you.
