You grip the wheel with both hands, rain hammering your shoulders, and cry out over the wind. Your voice booms louder than it ever does in waking life — one sign, faintly registered, that you are still asleep.

"Hard to starboard!" you shout, though you couldn't define *starboard* until the moment the word leaves your mouth. The crew moves anyway, perfectly in step, as if your intention arrives in their bodies before your voice reaches their ears. Another sign.

A wave rises ahead, impossibly tall, curling green-black like a wall being built by angry hands. You steer straight for it, because some deep part of you knows that's what captains do — and sure enough, the wave lifts you instead of swallowing you. From the crest you can see forever: lightning stitching across the clouds, and below it all, inexplicably, a warm golden glow shining from beneath the surface of the sea.

Down there, you're certain without knowing why, is something you forgot. Something important.

The first mate grips your sleeve. He has no face, yet somehow he looks concerned. "Captain," he says, "let go of the wheel."

"If I let go, we crash."

"No." His voice is familiar — almost like someone you once knew well. "We're already home. The storm is only how it *felt*."

You look down, and the wheel is gone — has been gone — and your hands were never holding anything at all. The ship sails itself toward the light. The sea flattens like a sheet smoothed by unseen hands, and the crew begins humming something you realize, with sudden ache and relief, you've known your whole life.

You wake with the hum still in your chest, hands curled around nothing, resting on the blanket as if they're still steering.
