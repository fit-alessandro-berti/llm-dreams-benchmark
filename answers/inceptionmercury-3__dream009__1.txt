…you need to figure out how to survive.

You step out of whatever bed you’ve landed in—an odd, creaky thing stuffed with straw and rough wool—and feel the cool morning air on your skin. No hum of electricity. No glow of screens. Just birdsong, the distant clang of a blacksmith’s hammer, and the smell of woodsmoke and wet earth.

You’re wearing clothes that aren’t yours: rough linen, a simple tunic, boots worn soft at the toe. You pat your pockets for a phone that isn’t there. For a moment, your mind grasps at the familiar—maps, messages, instant answers—but the silence in your head is loud.

You walk outside. The village is small, huddled around a stone well. People glance at you, nod, then go back to work. Nobody seems startled that you exist. They just assume you belong, or at least that you’re someone else’s problem.

The sky is wide and almost impossibly clear. You notice the sun is lower than it should be. The stars, if you could see them, would be different. You realize, slowly, that there is no going back. No time machine. No Wi-Fi. No rescue.

So you need to start small: find water, find food, find shelter. Learn the rhythms of this place. Watch how they plant, how they harvest, how they talk. Smile when you don’t understand. Trade your knowledge—about hygiene, about medicine, about the future—carefully, so no one thinks you’re a witch or a fool. And above all, stay curious, because curiosity is the one technology you’ve always had.

You take a deep breath. You walk toward the well, ready to ask for a drink and a name. The dream is strange, but it’s yours now. And maybe, just maybe, that’s enough.
