You find yourself in a post-apocalyptic world, with resources scarce and dangers everywhere. Despite the hardships, you decide to **stay.**

Not to fight. Not to run.

You wake up in the wreck of what used to be a city, ash on your tongue and the wind whistling through broken rebar. The sky is the color of old bruises. Scavengers watch from rooftops. Your pack is light and your water is almost gone.

And yet you start walking toward the noise.

It isn't a safe place. It's a half-collapsed school. The chalkboard is still there, cracked but legible. Someone has been coming here. A small fire pit. A child's drawing pinned to a desk with a bent nail: a sun, a house, a stick figure holding hands with another stick figure.

You sit down in the dust and begin to sort what you found on the road: a tin cup, three dry beans, a working pencil, a paperback with its spine broken. You clean the whiteboard with your sleeve and write in shaky letters: *WE ARE HERE. WATER AT THE WELL. SAFE TONIGHT.*

You don't know who will find it. Maybe no one will. Maybe a patrol will tear it down.

That doesn't matter. In the dream you can't save the world, you can only tend a small, stubborn corner of it. You plant the three beans in a cracked flowerpot you found by the door and cover them with soil from the floor. You whisper to them like they're real, which in a dream they are.

Outside, something growls in the dark. The wind picks up. But inside, the fire is small and warm and you are not alone with the apocalypse anymore — you have made a place where someone could be.

You fall asleep there, the pencil still in your hand, and the dream doesn't end with survival. It ends with meaning.
