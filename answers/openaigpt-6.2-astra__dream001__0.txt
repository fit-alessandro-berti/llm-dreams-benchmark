You start to ask a woman selling oranges for directions, then realize you have no idea what to ask for.

“Who are you meeting?” she says.

You can’t remember that either. What you do remember, quite distinctly, is that it was your idea. This makes being late considerably worse.

The woman gives you an orange and points you toward the station. “They have a lost-property office. Start there.”

The clerk at the station listens without surprise. He takes down a ledger and runs a finger past umbrellas, spectacles, a wedding ring, the entire summer of 1998. “An appointment, is it? Let’s see your handwriting.”

He pushes a pencil across the counter. Without thinking, you draw a little house with smoke curling from the chimney. He turns the ledger around. There, in the margin of a page gone soft with age, is the same house. Underneath it someone has written, in your handwriting before you learned cursive: *Saturday. One o’clock. Come back and tell me.*

You know the way now.

Beyond the station is a footbridge, and beyond the footbridge is the patch of dirt behind the apartments where you grew up. You have to get down on your hands and knees to enter the fort. Inside, a child sits on an upturned bucket, trying very hard to peel an orange in one piece.

“Well?” the child asks.

You had expected, in the few minutes it took to cross the bridge, to be asked if you got rich or famous or at least got a dog. You had been preparing some fairly elaborate excuses.

“Well what?”

“Did we ever see a whale?”

You sit down in the dirt. “Yes. From a ferry. Almost everybody missed it because they were inside buying chips.”

“How big?”

You hold your hands apart, which is ridiculous. The child waits. You start again. You describe the morning, the cold rail against your chin, the dark back rising for so long that for a moment you mistook it for an island. As you speak, the floor of the fort begins to rock. Somewhere just outside, water slaps against a hull.

The child presses a finger to your lips. Together you lift the corner of the blanket and look.
