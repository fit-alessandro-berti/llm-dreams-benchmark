...feel your heart begin to race as you sit down at your desk. The computer screen flickers to life, but instead of your usual desktop, you see a single document titled "PROJECT_DUE_TOMORROW_FINAL_v2_FINAL(1).docx"

You start typing, but the words come out backwards. You delete them and try again—now they're in a language you almost recognize, like something from a half-remembered textbook. Your boss appears behind you, but his face keeps shifting between three different coworkers, and he's holding a clock whose hands spin wildly, sometimes clockwise, sometimes not.

"The deadline moved up," he says, his voice echoing like it's coming through a fan. "It's due in ten minutes now."

You look down at your keyboard and the letters have rearranged themselves into a smiley face. You try to type anyway, and each keystroke produces a small paper crane that flutters out of the monitor and lands on an ever-growing pile beside you. The pile is somehow organized by color.

A coworker leans over: "Oh, you didn't get the memo? Everyone else finished theirs last week." You turn around and the entire office is empty except for a single chair slowly rotating in the corner.

Panic rises in your chest—that familiar dream-panic, heavy like swimming through honey. You realize you're not wearing pants. Then you realize it doesn't matter because the office has become your childhood kitchen, and the project is now a cake you were supposed to bake for a test you didn't study for, on a bus you're missing, while forgetting your locker combination.

Just as the walls begin to close in—

*You wake up.* Your heart pounds. You check the time: 3:47 AM. You lie there, relieved, then remember you actually *do* have that presentation tomorrow.

And drift back to sleep, right back into the same dream, but this time the paper cranes are helping you type.
