I feel a storm coming—not in the sky, but in the air itself. The birds stop singing. The leaves flatten and point away from the wind. Then the first cold drops hit my face, each one landing like a tiny bell. I take a path I’ve never noticed before, lined with mossy stones and tall grasses. The wind grows stronger, swirling around me, but it doesn’t push me back—it seems to be guiding me forward.

Ahead, a clearing opens up. In the middle stands a single tree, its branches glowing with soft, silver light. Rain falls all around, but under the tree, it’s dry and calm. I step beneath the branches, and the storm becomes a distant roar, like a secret the forest is keeping. I realize the storm isn’t trying to scare me—it’s trying to show me this place. I sit down at the root, close my eyes, and listen.

When I open them, the storm is gone. The tree is gone. I’m standing at the edge of a quiet lake, and the sky is full of stars. Something tells me I’ve been here before. Something tells me I’m not alone. And then the dream gently turns into a question: *Do I want to remember this when I wake, or let it drift away like the rain?*
