You start to run.

The city seems to lengthen around you. Sidewalks stretch into silver ribbons, intersections fold into themselves, and towers lean over the streets like enormous, curious listeners. You run past a café where every table is set for two, past a fountain filled with floating clocks, past a woman standing motionless beneath an open umbrella in a sky without rain.

Your watch says 10:13.

It said 10:13 when you started.

You pull out your phone. There are no bars, no notifications, no record of the meeting. Only a blank notification that disappears when you tap it.

At the next corner, the buildings begin repeating.

Not exactly. The one with the green door becomes the one with the blue awning, which becomes the one with the shattered window, which somehow becomes the first again. Their windows glow with fragments of your life: a childhood bedroom, an empty hospital corridor, the back seat of a car you almost recognize. You force yourself not to look.

The woman with the umbrella calls after you.

“You can’t find it by running away from here!”

You stop.

She hasn’t moved, but you’re suddenly ten steps closer to her. Rain begins tapping against the pavement, though the sky remains pale and dry.

“Where is the meeting?”

She points behind you.

You turn. A narrow alley has opened between two buildings that certainly were not standing apart before. At its far end, beneath a single flickering lamp, is a door.

“No name,” she says. “No address. You’ll know it when you reach it.”

“And if I don’t?”

Her expression softens.

“Then keep looking.”

The alley is much longer than it appeared from the street. Your footsteps return from somewhere above, as if an entire audience is accompanying you. The buildings on either side are blank except for doors, hundreds of them, each painted the same shade of tired blue.

Then you see a brass plaque beside one door.

**HERE.**

The handle is warm.

When you open it, the noise of the city falls away. Beyond the door is a small room with a round table, two chairs, and a window overlooking an ocean of lights. The clock on the wall has no hands, but you feel the time: one minute to eleven.

Someone is already seated.

They look up, and their face is your own. Not a reflection. Not a trick of shadow. Their eyes hold years you haven’t lived yet.

“You came,” they say.

“Did I?”

They gesture to the empty chair. A blank map lies between you, along with a pen and a cup of coffee still steaming.

“What is this meeting for?” you ask.

Your other self smiles.

“To decide where we go next.”

Beyond the window, the impossible city waits with its hundreds of nameless streets, its wrong clocks, its doors opening onto moments you have never reached.

You sit down.

For the first time since arriving, you are exactly on time.
