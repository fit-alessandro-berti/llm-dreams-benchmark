...realize you need to **offer the silent bell in your throat to the Weaver of Tides, or your name will wash away with the morning dew.**

The thought doesn't arrive as words, but as a physical weight settling in your chest, heavy and insistent, like swallowing a stone that refuses to dissolve.

You are standing in a valley where the grass blades are shards of polished emerald, chiming softly as the wind moves through them. The sky above is not blue, but a bruised purple, streaked with rivers of slow-moving silver that drift upward instead of falling. There are no roads, only stepping-stones of translucent glass that appear beneath your feet only when you trust them with your full attention.

You pat your pockets instinctively, your fingers brushing against nothing but rough-spun wool. Your phone, your watch, your keys—all gone. Replaced by three smooth river stones that pulse with a faint, rhythmic warmth against your thigh. You realize now why you are wearing this tunic stained with ink and ash, and why your hands are calloused despite being those of the modern world. In this dream, the history is already written.

You begin to walk across the glass stones. They hold your weight, humming a low resonance that travels up your legs. To your left, a forest of giant mushrooms glows with bioluminescence, inhabited by creatures that look like foxes woven from smoke and autumn leaves. They watch you with eyes like amber beads, tails flickering. They do not run; they wait.

Ahead, the terrain rises into a cliff face carved entirely of mirrors. But the reflections do not show your current form. Instead, they show glimpses of the life you left behind: a ticking digital clock melting into sludge, a crowded subway car filled with flowers instead of passengers, the face of someone you love blurred by static.

A figure sits at the base of the mirror cliff, spinning thread from a spindle made of bone. This is the Weaver. She does not look up as you approach, though her movements stop instantly. Her shuttle hangs suspended in mid-air.

"You've come," she says, her voice sounding like pages turning in a library. "But the tide is high today, little walker. The waters of forgetting are rising."

You open your mouth to explain, to ask how to get home, to demand answers, but when you speak, only a vibration emerges. You clamp a hand over your throat. You feel it there—a hollow, bell-like shape vibrating deep within, emitting a tone you cannot hear but can feel shaking your ribs. It is the source of your modern urgency, the constant mental chatter, the ability to list and categorize and worry. It is the noise that keeps you from hearing the emerald grass sing.

"The Bell must be rung backwards," the Weaver murmurs, finally looking at you. Her eyes are swirling galaxies. "Or the echo will drown you. Give it to me, and you may keep your name. Hoard it, and the dew takes everything."

Panic flares—the desire to run, to search, to solve the puzzle. But the dream logic is absolute. You know, with terrifying clarity, that running is impossible. The glass stones behind you have already dissolved into mist. The only path is forward, into the vulnerability of giving up the noise you've carried since birth.

You take a breath that smells of ozone and ancient paper. You focus on the bell in your throat, isolating the sound. You imagine winding it down. You imagine the frantic pace of your old life slowing, stopping, inverting.

You let go.

A sound pours out of you, not a scream, but a reverse tone, a suction of silence so profound it pulls the colors from the air. The bell in your throat shatters into motes of gold dust that float upward, swirling into the spindle held by the Weaver. She catches them with her needle, weaving your noise into a tapestry that depicts the exact moment you fell asleep, bridging the gap between the two worlds.

The pressure in your chest vanishes. The chime of the emerald grass becomes a symphony. The purple sky brightens to a gentle pearlescent white. You feel lighter, unburdened, your name etched clearly onto your skin in glowing script, safe from the tide.

The Weaver hands you a single spool of thread, still warm. "Carry this," she whispers. "When the alarm calls, pull this thread, and you will remember you were ever the weaver, too."

The mirror cliff begins to dissolve into mist. The fox-creatures bow their heads. The glass stones reform into a familiar bedroom ceiling, the crack in the plaster looking suddenly like a river delta. Your heart rate slows, matching the rhythm of the chiming grass.

As consciousness rushes back, the last thing you hear isn't an alarm, but the faint, reverse tolling of a bell, fading into the distance, promising that the silence you left behind is still waiting for you to return.
