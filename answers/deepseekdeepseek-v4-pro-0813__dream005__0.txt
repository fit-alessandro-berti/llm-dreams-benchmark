...and each group seems to know you, though you don’t know them. They make space, then close it, offering canapés that taste like old memories and handshakes that linger a second too long. The room keeps reorganizing itself—doors lead back to the same buffet, conversations repeat with small but meaningful differences. You glimpse your one person across the room, but the distance stretches like taffy. You call out, but your voice arrives as a subtitle beneath their speech. Eventually you stop trying so hard. You step onto a balcony that wasn’t there before, and the whole party turns out to be a single enormous room made of weather—chandeliers of rain, a dance floor of warm wind. Your person is suddenly beside you, saying, “There you are. I’ve been introducing you all evening.” You look back at the crowd, and every face is yours, or a version of you, wearing the same expression of recognition. The dream then thins at the edges, and you wake with the feeling that you have just been remembered by a roomful of strangers.
