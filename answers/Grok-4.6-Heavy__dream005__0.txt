You are at a large social gathering where you only know one person. As you try to find them, you encounter various groups of people and none of them will hold still long enough to be asked.

The first circle is laughing at a joke that ended before you arrived. They open like a door when you approach. Someone presses a glass into your hand that is already empty. A woman wearing your mother’s earrings says, of course, you just missed them — they went toward the music. You turn. There is no music. There is only the suggestion of it, like a radio in another apartment.

The next group is arguing in low, reasonable voices about a night you almost remember. They have your coat, your city, a childhood detail that is almost right. In their version you left early. In another you never showed up at all. When you ask if they have seen the person you came with, they point in three directions at once.

You follow the middle one.

Hallways multiply. A ballroom becomes a kitchen becomes a rooftop garden with wet stone and no city underneath. A third cluster stands among the hedges wearing versions of the face you came here to find — almost, but not. The mouth is right on one, the walk on another, the laugh on a third. You say the name. They smile like hosts. None of them are the person.

Then you see them.

Across a crowded threshold: the one person you actually know. They are scanning faces with the same half-panic you feel in your own chest. You raise a hand. They raise a hand. The crowd thickens between you like water. When it thins, they are closer, tired in the specific way of someone who has been searching too.

“There you are,” they say, and then, after a pause that lasts either a second or a year: “I only knew one person here.”

You take a step. The floor holds. For a moment it is just a party, and you have found each other, and that is enough.

The light changes in the way it does near morning. Behind you the rooms keep rearranging, already preparing the next group, already laughing at something that has not happened yet.
