The canvas breathes.

As you step closer, the air around it ripples like water, and tiny fragments of color drift toward its surface—blue that hums like a distant bell, yellow that tastes faintly of sunlight, red that flickers with the memory of a heartbeat. You reach out, and the moment your fingers touch the canvas, the shifting world falls still.

Every shape you have ever seen begins to orbit you slowly: doors with no walls, staircases folding into flowers, birds made of glass and whisper. The canvas waits, patient and luminous, as if it knows you carry something that has not yet taken form.

Then, without paint or brush, an image begins to appear.

It is a path.

Not a path through the dream, but a path made of dreams—each brushstroke a moment you almost remember upon waking. At the end of the path stands a figure made of shifting colors, neither stranger nor familiar. It raises a hand, and the canvas opens like a doorway.

You understand then: the blank canvas was never meant to be filled.

It was meant to let you in.

You step through it, and the dream continues on the other side—only now, you are the one painting it with every step you take.
