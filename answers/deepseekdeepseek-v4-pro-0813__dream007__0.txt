You decide to stop fighting and say the thing you’ve been too proud to say. But when you open your mouth, no sound comes out—just a small flock of pale moths rising between you, silent and slow. Your friend watches them, and their face changes from anger to something older, softer. The room tilts, and the harsh words still hang in the air like glass shards, but now they catch the light instead of cutting.

You both sit down on the floor, no longer sure where the room ends. You understand, all at once, that you were both right and both wrong, and that the real argument was about being afraid to lose each other. Then the dream begins to dissolve, and you wake with your heart beating hard, the shape of an apology still warm in your mouth.
