You walk, barefoot, until you find stones. Flint and quartz, jagged and patient, lying in the dirt like forgotten tools. You strike them together once, twice, again—until a spark lands in the dry moss cupped in your palm. You blow gently. Smoke curls up like a half-remembered language.

When the flame catches, you feel something ancient bloom in your chest. You realize you don't just need to survive. You need to remember the things that were never written down—how to weave reeds into rope, how to read stars as a map, how to listen to the earth like an old friend.

You sit by your small fire as dusk settles. The sky deepens into colors you've never seen without light pollution. And for the first time since waking, you're not afraid.

You are here. You are awake. And you are beginning to remember what it means to belong to a world that has always been waiting for you.

Then you hear footsteps in the brush. And a voice—familiar, impossible—whispers your name.

But before you turn to see who it is, you realize:

You're still dreaming. And in this dream, you can choose to wake up… or you can choose to stay.
