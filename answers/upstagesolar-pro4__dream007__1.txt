You are in the middle of a heated argument with a close friend. The disagreement is about something important to both of you, and you decide to stop.

The words hang in the air between you, sharp and unfinished. For a moment, neither of you moves, the tension thick enough to feel like static electricity against your skin. Then, you realize the logic of the argument has shifted; you aren't speaking to understand anymore, you're speaking to win. And you can't afford to win if it means losing them.

You lower your voice, letting the anger drain out of your shoulders. "Wait," you say, and the word sounds fragile in the space between you. You reach out, not to grab, but to offer a hand. Your friend looks at it, then up at your face, searching for the truce hidden in your expression.

Slowly, the anger in their eyes softens into something tired, something human. They take your hand. The dream doesn't fade on the resolution of the fight, but on the feeling of the handshake—steady, warm, and real. You breathe out together, and the room stops spinning.
