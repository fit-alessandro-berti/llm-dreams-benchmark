...forget every single word.

The speech was in your hand a second ago — you're sure of it. You look down, but the pages are blank except for one line, written over and over in your own handwriting:

*Don't look at their faces.*

You look up anyway.

The auditorium is much larger than it was when you walked in. The rows stretch back so far they curve upward like the inside of a bowl, thousands of faces all turned toward you, silent and expectant. The spotlight is warm on your neck. Somewhere high above, a clock ticks, but far too slowly.

You clear your throat and the sound echoes for a long time.

"Good evening," you say.

Your voice doesn't come out of your mouth. It comes from somewhere in the middle of the crowd, delayed by a second, deeper than it should be.

The audience nods politely, all at once.

You try again. "I've come here tonight to talk about—"

This time your words leave your mouth as small, pale moths. They flutter uncertainly over the podium, bump against the microphone, and drift out over the audience. People reach up and catch them gently in cupped hands, examine them, then let them go.

No one seems disappointed. If anything, they look relieved.

That's when you notice them properly. The woman in the front row is you at eight years old, swinging her legs because her feet can't touch the floor. Next to her is you as a teenager, slouched down and pretending not to care. Further back is an older you, with grey in your hair, smiling like they already know how this ends.

Every seat is filled with you, at every age you've been and every age you might become.

The eight-year-old you raises her hand.

"Yes?" you say.

"Are we nervous?" she asks.

The whole auditorium leans forward to hear your answer.

You laugh, and this time the sound is your own, right where it should be. The moths turn, mid-air, into tiny paper airplanes and sail in lazy loops toward the ceiling, which is no longer a ceiling but a dark summer sky.

"Not anymore," you say.

And the oldest version of you, right at the very back where the rows disappear into the stars, stands up and begins to applaud.
