You are at your workplace, and suddenly, you are given a project with an impossible deadline. Your boss is waiting for you to start, and you cannot remember what the project is.

The brief is already in your hands. The paper is warm. The cover says DUE: NOW. Inside, a single line rewrites itself every time you blink: *make it perfect / make it yesterday / make it exist.*

Your boss smiles with too many teeth. “Whenever you’re ready,” they say, which means now, which means last week.

You sit. The keyboard has no letters, only hours. Every key you press subtracts ten minutes from a clock that is no longer on the wall — the clock *is* the wall. The open-plan office stretches like taffy. Desks recede into a fluorescent horizon. A coworker you almost recognize slides a finished deck across your desk and whispers, “Just need your sign-off.” You open it. It is blank. You open the next one and it is your own face, looping a presentation you have not written.

You try to ask what the deliverable is. Your voice comes out as a calendar invite: *Quick sync? Should be quick.*

There are three bosses now, all checking watches with no hands. Someone in the next cubicle is stapling shadows to a calendar-wall. Every square already says DONE except today’s, which is blank and getting larger. The Slack ping becomes a fire alarm becomes a heartbeat. You reach for the mouse and it turns into a small, exhausted version of you, already typing.

The countdown hits zero, then keeps going, into negatives, into a date that has not been invented yet.

Your boss leans in. The room tilts toward their watch.

“Did you start yet?”

You look down. Your hands are already moving. The document is empty. You keep typing into the blank, because in the dream that is the only door that still opens — and the door is just another hallway with another desk and another brief that has already begun to rewrite itself.
