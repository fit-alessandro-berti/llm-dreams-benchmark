You are in the middle of a heated argument with a close friend. The disagreement is about something important to both of you, and you decide to stop speaking and take a step back.

As you do, the air between you begins to shimmer, like heat rising from summer pavement. Your friend’s face softens, not because they agree with you, but because they can see how much the argument hurts you. Around you, the dream shifts: the room turns into a place made of glass walls, and every unkind word either of you has said is floating inside it like a trapped bird.

You realize that if you keep arguing only to win, the birds will break the glass. But if you speak honestly, without trying to wound them, the birds will turn into light.

So you say the thing you were afraid to say:

“I don’t want to lose you. I just want you to understand me.”

Your friend is quiet for a moment. Then they answer, not with agreement, but with truth.

“I’m afraid that if I change my mind, I’ll disappear.”

The glass around you begins to dissolve. The birds lift into the air, glowing. You and your friend are still in disagreement, but the fight becomes something else—a doorway instead of a wall. You both step through it together, not because everything is resolved, but because you choose the relationship more than the victory.

When you wake, the feeling stays with you: that sometimes the most important thing in an argument is not proving yourself, but making room for the other person to exist beside you.
