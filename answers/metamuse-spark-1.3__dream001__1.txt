Of course — let's slip back into it:

---

You find yourself in a vast, unfamiliar city, and you realize you have an important meeting in an hour but no idea where it is. You start to

check your pockets. Phone, wallet, a crumpled badge with your name on it but the company logo is smeared, like wet ink. Your phone says 2:47pm. Meeting at 3:30. And the maps app just spins.

You start to walk. Fast, at first, trying to look like you *do* know where you're going. The streets are impossibly wide, lined with buildings that are too tall to see the tops of. The sky between them is a late-afternoon purple, though you’re sure it was morning a minute ago.

You try to ask someone for directions. A woman with groceries smiles and points three different ways at once. “Oh, you’re close! Just down past the Blue Tower, take the under-stairs, then ask again.” Everyone you pass seems to know about your meeting. A bus driver nods at you: “Good luck in there.”

You start to run. Your shoes - wait, you’re wearing one dress shoe and one slipper - slap against wet pavement, though it isn’t raining.

You find a subway entrance that wasn’t there before. You duck inside because it feels like progress. The station is huge and humming and smells like popcorn and rain. The departure board flickers: all trains are going to YOUR STOP, arriving in 1 MINUTE, arriving NOW, arriving YESTERDAY.

You get on one. Inside, everyone is dressed for your meeting, holding coffees and laptops. You feel a spike of relief - you must be going the right way! You lean over to a man and whisper, “Do you know where this meeting is?”

He smiles without looking up. “You’ll know when you need to get off. You always almost miss it.”

The train never stops, but the city outside the windows starts to rearrange itself. Buildings lean in to whisper. The street signs change every time you blink: 7th Ave. 7th Ave? 7th Ave!

Your phone buzzes. A calendar reminder: *Important Meeting - The Blue Room, 3rd floor. Bring yourself.*

You look up. The train doors are open onto a lobby. There’s a staircase made entirely of light going up. You take the stairs two at a time and your slippers don’t make a sound anymore and you aren’t even out of breath and suddenly —

your alarm goes off.

You’re one floor away.
