...and as the competition progresses, you realize the other contestants are fragments of who you might have been. There’s the version of you who played it safe, wearing a gray suit that keeps trying to reshape itself into something comfortable; the one who gave up too early, clutching a blank canvas like a shield; and a tall, silent stranger whose face is your own, but older, kinder, and unafraid.

The first challenge takes place in a library where the books are breathing. Your task is to find your life’s work among the infinite shelves. You pull down volumes titled *The Art of Pausing*, *How to Hold a Door Open for Someone Else*, and *An Atlas of Places That Don’t Exist Yet*. Only one is real. You know it instantly because it’s warm, like a mug of tea left on a counter on a winter morning.

The second challenge is an interview with a committee of nocturnal animals. An owl in spectacles asks why you want the job. You open your mouth to give the rehearsed answer—the one about ambition and five-year plans—but instead you find yourself describing the time you stayed up all night helping someone move, and how the empty apartment at dawn looked like a cathedral. The fox at the end of the table marks something on their clipboard. The moth, who is clearly in charge, nods.

For the final round, you’re led to a plain wooden door at the end of a hallway that stretches a little too far. There are no keys and no final test of skill. The door simply bears a brass plate: *Enter if you are ready to be changed by the work.* You turn the handle.

Inside is a small room with a single desk facing a window that looks out not on a city or a sea, but on the sleeping world. Soft, glowing threads drift past the glass like lazy fireflies. The title card on the desk explains your position: **Keeper of the Threshold**. Your job is to guide lost ideas home, to find the right word when someone is stuck, and to keep the light on for travelers moving between worlds. The salary is *enough time*. The benefits include *afternoons when nothing is required.*

You sit down. The chair fits perfectly, as if it has been waiting. Outside, in the dream-dark, you can already hear the first soft knock of a seeker who doesn’t yet know they are lost. You reach for the pen—it leaks starlight—and begin.
