The air smells sharply of crushed pine, ozone, and cold hearth-smoke. Towering monoliths of pale, hand-carved stone rise into a dawn sky washed in sea-green and gold, hung with creeping moss and banners dyed in indigo. Around you, people wrapped in unspun wool and hammered copper bracelets move with a rhythmic, purposeful grace, their bare feet soundless against the damp flagstones. 

You look down at yourself. Your modern clothes are gone, replaced by a simple linen tunic stained with dried earth, and wrapped around your right wrist is a braided reed cord that thrums with a faint, physical pulse—like a heartbeat that isn't yours. 

From the center of the settlement, a massive bronze horn sounds, low and resonant, shaking the morning mist from the eaves of the timber houses. With every chime, the surrounding landscape wavers slightly at the edges, like ink bleeding into wet paper. 

In the logic of the dream, the truth becomes instantly clear: this isn't just an ancient era; it is a world built entirely out of things humanity forgot to remember. To wake up—or to survive long enough to understand why you were brought here—you must reach the central stone circle before the fourth horn sounds, following the gentle, pulling vibration of the reed cord at your wrist.
