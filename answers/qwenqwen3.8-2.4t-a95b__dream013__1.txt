Each turn of the maze presents a new riddle: locks made of light, bridges that appear only when you solve a sequence of symbols, doors that whisper questions in voices you almost recognize. The deeper you go, the more the maze seems to respond to your mind, shifting whenever you doubt yourself and stabilizing whenever you think clearly.

At last, you reach a circular chamber at the heart of the maze. In its center stands a mirror, but instead of your reflection, it shows every choice you have made—every hesitation, every insight, every moment of focus. You realize the final puzzle is not a lock or a code, but understanding why you entered the maze in the first place.

You place your hand against the mirror, and it ripples like water. The maze grows quiet. Then, softly, it begins to dissolve, not into darkness, but into morning light.

You wake with the strange, calm feeling that you have solved something important—even if you can no longer remember exactly what it was.
