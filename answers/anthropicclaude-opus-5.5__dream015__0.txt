...plant a garden.

It makes no sense, and yet in the dream it makes perfect sense. You carry a single seed in your pocket. You don't remember where you got it, only that it's warm, like something alive and waiting. The sky above is the color of old bruises, and the wind carries grit that tastes like rust and burnt paper, but you kneel anyway in the cracked parking lot of what used to be a supermarket.

You dig with your hands. The asphalt gives way like soft bread, which should frighten you but doesn't. Beneath it, the soil is black and rich and smells like rain, though it hasn't rained in years. You know this the way you know things in dreams, without being told.

You press the seed into the earth.

Nothing happens, so you sit and wait. Hours pass, or maybe minutes. The sun doesn't move. A figure appears at the edge of the lot, a woman in a coat stitched together from road signs, *YIELD* and *STOP* and *DEAD END* patched across her shoulders. She watches you with suspicion, then curiosity. She has a canteen. Without a word, she walks over and pours a little water onto the spot where the seed is buried.

A green thread pushes up through the dirt.

More people come. A boy with a dog made of wire and scrap metal that wags its tail with a sound like wind chimes. An old man carrying a birdcage with no bird, only a small glowing light that hums lullabies. They each bring something: a handful of ash, a cracked teacup of rainwater collected from a rooftop, a song. The sprout drinks it all in and grows. First a stem, then leaves, then branches spreading wide over the parking lot, casting a shade that feels cool and forgiving.

You realize, suddenly, that the tree is growing fruit, and each fruit is a small, glowing window. When you look inside one, you see a kitchen from your childhood. Another shows a library, its shelves perfectly intact. Another holds a city full of lights at night, people laughing on balconies. Not the past, you understand, and not quite the future either. Just the *possible*.

The woman in the road-sign coat picks one and hands it to you. "You don't eat them," she says, her voice sounding like it comes from far away and very close at once. "You plant them."

So you begin walking. The wasteland stretches out in every direction, gray and endless, but now your pockets are full of glowing fruit, and behind you the others are following, each carrying their own. Every so often, someone stops, kneels, and digs.

Behind you, the horizon begins to turn green.

Somewhere in the distance, a sound rises that you haven't heard in a very long time, and it takes you a moment to recognize it:

Birdsong.

And just as the first drops of real rain touch your face, warm and impossible, you begin to wake up, still holding the feeling of the seed in your palm.
