For a breathless moment, the silence stretches across the auditorium like a vast, shimmering sea. The faces before you are pale lanterns in the dark, waiting. Then you notice something strange: every person in the audience has the same eyes—your eyes.

Panic rises, but with it comes a sudden, dreamlike calm. You realize that this is not a test. It is a mirror.

You open your mouth again, and this time your voice emerges soft at first, then stronger. You are not giving the speech you prepared. You are saying things you did not know you knew: truths you have carried quietly for years, fears you have never named, hopes you have almost forgotten. The words float upward like sparks.

The crowd listens, perfectly still.

As you continue, the stage beneath your feet begins to change. It becomes warm, then soft, then grassy. Roots spread from the microphone stand. Leaves unfurl from the podium. The ceiling above dissolves into a sky filled with slow-moving stars.

The audience begins to glow, one by one, as if your words are lighting them from within.

You understand now: you are not speaking to them. You are speaking to every part of yourself you have left unheard.

When you finish, there is no applause. Instead, the entire dream exhales.

The crowd rises, not as strangers, but as fragments of you—childhood wonder, old grief, buried courage, future possibility. They walk toward the stage and merge into you, one by one, like returning light.

You stand alone beneath the stars, whole.

And then, gently, the dream opens like a door.
