...find myself staring at the clouds above, where they are streaked with the colors of a dying sunset—orange, violet, and a strange, luminous green. The wind howls like a living thing, but then I notice the crew isn't panicking. They're gathered in a tight circle around me, their faces calm and unnervingly familiar. One by one, they lean in and whisper, "You already know the way." And I do. I look up, and there, impossibly high above the churning clouds, is a coastline glowing like a second moon. I don't have a map, but my hands move on their own, drawing a great spiral in the air—and the ship follows it without a single shiver. As we approach the shore, the crew begins to sing, and the storm softens into a lullaby. I reach out to take my hand from the wheel, to grab the shoulder of the youngest sailor beside me, but the moment touches me, the sea falls silent. We're no longer on a ship at all. We're standing in a kitchen that smells of rain and fresh bread, and someone I've never seen but completely know is setting a bowl of soup in front of me. "You're dreaming," they say, smiling. "And it's time to wake up." But before I can, the soup begins to float, rising toward the ceiling in slow, glowing ribbons. I laugh, and I think: this is the part where I wake up. But I don't. I know that if I do, I'll forget this spiral, this coastline, this kitchen. So instead, I take a deep breath, and I pull the bowl of floating soup onto my tongue—and the dream spills out of me like light through a window, carrying with it the image of a storm-tossed ship, a crew of kind strangers, and a strange green light that says: *you have been dreaming your whole life. Now wake up and live.*
