You wake up in a completely different era, with no modern technology around. As you explore your new surroundings, you realize you need to **learn the dialect of the river stones before the tidal bell tolls at dusk**, or the current will carry you far from the memory of your own name.

The air tastes of rain and crushed sage. Above, two moons hang low and uneven, casting silver blades across a village of thatch and timber where people move in wool and leather, their faces lit by hearthfire and curiosity. A child points at you and whispers something that sounds like your name, but older, softer. You follow a muddy track toward the longhouse at the hill’s base, knowing that in this dream, the words you learn here might be the only way back—or the only way to truly stay.

The fire crackles. Somewhere beyond the hill, something moves. You settle in, ready to listen.
