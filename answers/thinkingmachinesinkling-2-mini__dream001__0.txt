You start to follow the light that keeps slipping up ahead—not toward the sky, but down an alley where the windows show the same street from five different hours at once. The pavement is warm under your feet, as if the city is breathing. Clocks hang from lampposts with no hands; your phone shows 10:03, then 3:47, then an endless minute. Somewhere above you, a siren loops like a held breath.

You pass a booth selling umbrellas that rain sideways, vendors arguing in dialects that almost sound like your childhood language. A doorway opens onto a crowded café where everyone is saying your name, but never quite clearly. You ask where the meeting is. The server smiles at you strangely and says, “You’re already inside it.”

With twenty minutes left, the city rearranges itself around your anxiety. The streets tilt so that running feels like climbing, stairs descend into walls, and every reflective surface shows a version of you looking back with slightly different certainty. You nearly walk into a fountain of black glass that changes color depending on whether you want to be remembered, late, loved, or forgiven. A figure shaped like a receptionist holds a sign: “Meeting room: the place you keep almost.”

Then the clocks agree. Not on the time—they all show the same symbol: a small door. You follow the sound of a key turning inside your own thoughts, the city dimming until the only thing left is a marble hall with no doors, just a table set for exactly one stranger and you. On the table is a note in your handwriting: “Come as the version of you who is not late.” You reach out—and the dream stretches, pulls you in, then opens a corridor behind you where the unfamiliar city continues, endless, waiting for its next hour.

And that’s where the dream waits for you: the meeting begins, but the answer depends on whether you want to get there or realize you already am.
