...find water. Not to drink—though your throat is terribly dry—but because something beneath thought knows that the well at the heart of the village is where you're meant to be. In dreams, knowledge arrives without explanation.

The village smells of woodsmoke, wet wool, and pigs. Bells ring somewhere, irregular and insistent, and you can't tell whether it's an alarm, a celebration, or simply the hour. People pass you leading oxen, hauling baskets of eels, trading copper coins stamped with faces you almost recognize. No one seems surprised to see you. That unsettles you more than anything else.

You follow a lane downhill, toward the sound of running water you can't yet see. The buildings lean in overhead, thatch nearly touching, and for a few steps the lane becomes the corridor of a ship, then a hallway lit by lights you can't quite name, then the lane again. You keep walking. In dreams, you've learned, the trick is to keep walking.

At the bottom of the hill stands the well. An old woman is there drawing up a bucket, and when she sees you she nods, as if you'd arranged this meeting years ago.

"You came for your reflection," she says.

"I did?"

"Everyone does, eventually." She steps aside.

You lean over the low stone lip. Far down, the water is black and perfectly still, and your reflection floats there—but it isn't your face. It's older. Weathered. A face that has lived whole decades in this place, sunburned cheeks, eyes lined with the same worries. The reflection smiles at you with such familiarity that your chest aches.

"You've been here before," the woman says gently. "Many times. You keep forgetting, and you keep coming back to remember."

The bucket rises, water sloshing silver in the gray light. The bells ring faster now, urgent, joyful. You reach down toward the water—

—and wake.

Sunlight through a familiar window. The hum of the refrigerator. A phone glowing on the nightstand with the ordinary hour displayed in plain digits. Everything is exactly where you left it.

Except your hands smell faintly of rope and well water. And somewhere beneath everything, faint as a memory of a memory, the bells are still ringing—keeping an hour that belongs to no clock you will ever own.
