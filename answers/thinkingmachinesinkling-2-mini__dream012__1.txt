...you find your voice not in commands but in a warning that is also a prayer: *We don’t have to look away.* The deck tilts, the lanterns gutter, and the storm starts to sound like a voice that wants to be answered. You listen past the wind and the shouting—until a rhythm emerges beneath it: swell, pause, swell. The crew doesn’t need heroic words. They need you to know what you’re afraid of and still move. So you choose the smallest, truest direction: not away from the rock at the edge of the dark, but along its edge, where the water turns from blind terror into readable danger. You tell them the storm is not trying to kill the ship; it is trying to strip away the part of them that pretends storms are only physics. Your hands are shaking. That’s okay. You guide them not by force but by being honest with them—your doubts included—and the moment you admit uncertainty, the crew’s hands steady. By dawn, the sea is still violent, but it has changed from an enemy into a language. You are not untouched. The storm leaves its marks. Still, the ship holds—not because you were perfect, but because they moved with you through the terror.
