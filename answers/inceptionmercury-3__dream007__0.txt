You laugh — a muffled, watery laugh — and the anger dissolves like ink in the sea. You swim toward each other, reach out, and hold hands.

Then the water drains, and you’re both sitting in a quiet kitchen at dawn. The tea is still warm. Neither of you can remember what the fight was about. All that’s left is the strange, weightless feeling of having been heard, and the odd certainty that you were both arguing with reflections in a broken mirror.

You reach across the table. Your friend squeezes your hand. The dream folds into a soft, golden light, and you wake up not knowing why you’re smiling — only that you are.
