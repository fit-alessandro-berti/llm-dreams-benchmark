You start to run, but your feet don’t quite strike the pavement—they sink and rebound like you’re moving through thick water. The street signs blur into cascading glyphs, shifting between languages you don’t know and alphabets that seem to rearrange when you blink. Your phone is dead, your pockets are empty, and the address you’re supposed to reach refuses to surface in your mind. Instead, it echoes as a room temperature, a faint scent of pine and old paper, the exact weight of a brass key in your palm.

The city breathes around you. Traffic flows in neat, silent ribbons. Streetlamps ignite in sequence, pointing down a route that feels less like navigation and more like wandering through a memory you haven’t had yet. You turn down an alley and the walls ripple, peeling back to reveal a corridor lined with doors. Each one bears a handwritten label: *The space between two thoughts. The name you forgot on Tuesday. Where you left your umbrella in 2008.* None of them are yours. But the seventh door down hums at a frequency that matches the rhythm of your pulse.

You push it open.

Inside is a quiet room with tall windows overlooking a city that moves in reverse. On a low table sits a single hourglass, its sand falling upward. Beside it rests a slip of paper with a time, a floor number, and a street name you suddenly recognize—not because it’s real, but because it’s the exact shape your urgency has been carving into the dream. You straighten your coat. The panic dissolves into a calm, dream-logic certainty: if you just walk forward, the meeting will already be waiting. You step through the doorway, and the floor beneath you becomes a staircase that folds upward into the sky.

*(The dream leans in. Do you take the stairs, follow the hum of another door, or try to read the shifting street name before it changes again?)*
