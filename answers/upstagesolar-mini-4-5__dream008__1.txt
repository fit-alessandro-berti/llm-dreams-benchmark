I'm standing in a hallway made of stacked spreadsheets, each tile labeled with a different stakeholder's name. Some tiles are soggy from a meeting I never attended. I walk carefully, avoiding a puddle that smells faintly of a deck I was asked to build three times.

At the end of the hall, a door marked "Q3." Beyond it, the impossible deadline has been built into a clock: the hour hand is a stapled pile of coffee cups, and the minute hand is a cursor blinking in a document that will never save. I step forward, and the floor becomes a long table covered in sticky notes. Each note is a task, and every task is a question I've been avoiding. "When will you test the login flow?" reads one. "Did you loop in design?" reads another. One note in the corner is written in my own handwriting: *"You'll figure it out."*

The note is the only thing in the room with a deadline written next to it, and the date is today. I reach for it, but before I touch it, the wall behind me cracks open, and I see my boss—not as a person now, but as a glowing number: **27 minutes**. The number is counting down. I turn and run back toward the spreadsheets hallway, and this time I don't step carefully. I leap over the puddle, kick through the sticky notes, and slide under the door marked "Q3." Inside is an empty meeting room with a single chair, a single blank whiteboard, and on the whiteboard, a diagram I've never seen before but somehow know by heart: a flowchart with no end point, just a question mark at the top of the page.

I pick up the marker and write, in my own handwriting, the only honest answer I have: *"We'll start by asking what's actually impossible."* The clock in the corner of the room stops at 3:47. The number next to my boss changes from 27 to 28. Somewhere, a stapler clicks shut. And I wake up—fully awake, alarm blaring, 6:02 AM, coffee already gone cold in a mug I don't remember making.
