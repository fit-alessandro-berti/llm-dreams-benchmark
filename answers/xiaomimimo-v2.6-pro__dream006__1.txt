You are about to give a speech to a large audience. As you step onto the stage, you see the crowd looking expectantly at you, and you begin to...

...walk to the microphone. But the stage feels strangely elastic under your feet — like taffy stretching with every step. The podium is there, but your notes have become watercolor paintings instead of words, and they're bleeding together in your hands.

You open your mouth to speak, and the first sentence comes out perfectly — except the audience responds not with applause but with a single, unified nod. Every face in the crowd is someone you recognize: your third-grade teacher, the barista from this morning, an old neighbor who moved away. They all nod at the exact same time.

Your notes dissolve into mist. You glance down and realize you're wearing pajamas. *When did that happen?* No one seems to notice. You keep speaking — though you can't remember the words — and the lights above the stage begin to drift slowly upward like helium balloons, taking the ceiling with them. Beyond it is a sky full of unfamiliar constellations.

The microphone in your hand grows warm, then sprouts a green leaf. You try to set it down, but it's rooted to your palm now. The crowd murmurs approvingly.

A voice from the back — *your own voice* — says, "You're almost done." But you've only been here for a moment. Or maybe hours. The clock on the wall has no numbers, only melting arrows pointing in every direction.

You look out at the audience one more time, and every seat is empty now. The silence is enormous. The microphone tree drops a small fruit into your hand. You bite into it.

You wake up with the taste of sunlight on your tongue and the absolute certainty that you said exactly the right thing.
