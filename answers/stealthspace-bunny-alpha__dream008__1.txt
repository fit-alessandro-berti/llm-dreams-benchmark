You open your mouth to protest, but your boss places a folder on your desk before you can speak.

The first page reads:

**PROJECT: Construct a bridge from this morning to this afternoon.**  
**DEADLINE: 8:45 a.m.**  
**STATUS: Already late.**

You look at the clock. It says 8:44.

“That can’t be right,” you say.

Your boss taps the second hand. It retreats toward 12.

“We should have started yesterday,” they say, as if this explains everything.

The elevator won’t descend. Your computer displays only one document: a half-finished bridge drawn in disappearing ink. When you sharpen your pencil, it grows legs and walks toward the window. The window opens onto the city, but every building is leaning backward, trying to return to yesterday.

You steal the boss’s car keys and drive to the storage room on nineteen. You find rolls of paper, abandoned plans, a jar of yesterday’s coffee, and a ladder with an impossible number of rungs.

It turns out the ladder works perfectly.

You climb until the clouds begin, then lay plans across the morning mist. The bridge takes shape beneath your hands: sturdy boards made from appointments, little milestones, and ordinary mornings stacked carefully together.

By the time you reach the far side, the clock strikes nine.

The bridge lands in your office—but tomorrow’s office. The plants are greener. Your coffee is steaming. Your boss is waiting, holding a completed project folder.

“There you are,” they say. “We’ve been wondering where you went.”

“I’ve delivered the bridge,” you say.

Their smile is quiet and satisfied.

“Good. Now we need to build a bridge back to the deadline.”

Behind them, the clock begins ticking backward again.
