…and no one seems to know why they’re there.

The first group is gathered around a grand piano, taking turns playing songs you almost recognize. You ask if they’ve seen the person you’re looking for.

The pianist stops mid-chord. “Seen them?” They laugh, but it sounds like a cough. “They’ve been here all night.”

You glance around the room. Everyone is still talking. No one is the person you mean.

The house stretches farther than it should. Every corridor leads to another gathering: people comparing versions of the same childhood, a woman handing out names from an old photograph, two children in grown-up clothes insisting they’ve already met you.

One group falls silent when you enter.

“Sorry,” whispers someone. “We were just saying how well we know you.”

They know nothing about you. You can tell because none of them remembers anything you’ve done.

You continue through the crowd. Sometimes you hear your friend’s laugh behind you. Sometimes you catch a flash of their face in a mirror, already turning away. The rooms multiply. The conversations blur together until they sound like rain.

At the end of a long hallway, you find a window overlooking a city suspended above dark water.

Your friend stands on the other side of the glass, wearing their coat as though they’ve been waiting to leave.

When you tap the window, they smile.

“About time,” they say.

The glass becomes a door.

You step through together, and behind you, every voice rises at once—not in conversation, but in song. The gathering dissolves into light. The floor tilts beneath your feet.

Then you’re lying in bed.

Morning has arrived, pale and quiet through your curtains. Your friend is gone.

But their hand is still warm in yours.
