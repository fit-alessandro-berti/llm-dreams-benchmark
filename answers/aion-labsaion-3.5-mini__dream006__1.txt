...speak, but no words come out. Your mouth moves, and what emerges instead is the sound of ocean waves. The crowd doesn't seem to mind—they begin nodding in unison, like a field of wheat swaying.

You look down and notice you're holding a microphone made of ice. It's melting, dripping through your fingers, pooling at your feet. Strange—the puddle is rising, not spreading. The stage floor is filling with water like a glass.

You try again to speak, and this time a single word comes out: "orange." The audience gasps appreciatively. Someone in the front row stands and begins applauding with their hands made of clock faces. You realize you know this person—you went to school with them, maybe, or they were your neighbor in a house you've never lived in. Their face keeps shifting, the way faces do.

The water is at your ankles now. You look up and the ceiling of the auditorium has become a night sky, stars wheeling slowly. A voice from the back row calls out, "You've forgotten the third thing!" and you feel a jolt of panic, because you know exactly what the third thing is, but you can't remember what "the third thing" means.

You begin walking toward the back of the hall, but the stage stretches with each step, the audience receding further away. The podium is still beside you somehow, no matter how far you walk. On it sits a folded piece of paper. You pick it up. It's blank except for one line, written in your own handwriting:

*"The dream ends when you stop running."*

You look up. The exits are gone. The crowd is standing now, all of them, silent, waiting. The water reaches your knees, and you feel an overwhelming urge to wake up—but you can't remember how.
