The first puzzle is a chamber filled with glass spheres suspended in midair. To pass, you must align them so that their reflections spell a word you cannot remember but somehow feel on the tip of your tongue. You concentrate until your temples ache. As you do, your heartbeat becomes the sound of the maze itself, a slow pulse beneath the floor.

Deeper still, the corridors begin to change shape when you look directly at them. If you stare too long at a door, it becomes a mirror, and the reflection shows not you as you are, but you as you might become: older, wearier, sharper-eyed, carrying a truth too heavy to speak aloud.

Each new puzzle is harder than the last, and yet each one seems to have been waiting for you. A labyrinth of moving tiles. A clock whose hands run backward and must be set to a forgotten hour. A room full of questions written in your own handwriting.

At the heart of the maze, you find a small door at the end of a spiral staircase. Above it are carved the words:

**“To exit, answer what you are.”**

You pause, and the maze falls silent.

And then, just before you can speak, you realize the answer has been changing every time you reached for it.
