You sit down at your desk, fingers hovering over the keyboard. The project file opens, but instead of documents, it's a nesting doll of screens—each one showing you sitting at a desk, opening a file. Your boss leans closer, but their face is a mirror reflecting your own tired eyes.

The deadline clock on the wall ticks backward, then sideways, pooling into a puddle of liquid time on the floor. You start typing, but the letters rise off the screen as blackbirds, flocking toward the ceiling where the fluorescent lights have bloomed into actual suns.

You realize the project isn't impossible—it's infinite. You are writing this dream as it happens, each word appearing in the margin of reality. The boss smiles, nods, and begins to dissolve into the wallpaper, which is actually a window looking out onto the bedroom where you are actually sleeping, chest rising and falling in the dark.

Your fingers keep moving across the keyboard that has softened into warm velvet, typing the final sentence: *And then you woke up, holding the completed project in your hands, though you could no longer remember what it was about, only that it felt finished, and your pillow was damp with ink.*

The screen goes dark. You are still at your desk. The sun has set. Somewhere, a clock strikes thirteen, and you realize with a start that you are still typing—but now your keyboard is your own chest, and your heartbeat is the only rhythm left in the room.
