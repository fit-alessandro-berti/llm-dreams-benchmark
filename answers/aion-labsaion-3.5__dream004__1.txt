You pick up the brush, and it hums against your palm like a sleeping bird. When you touch the canvas, the color doesn't spread — it *sings*. A stroke of blue tastes of cold mountain air. A sweep of gold sounds like bells underwater.

You paint a door. Of course you paint a door — in dreams, we always paint doors.

The doorknob turns before you even reach for it, and the canvas gives way like warm water. You step through, and suddenly you're inside your own painting: a meadow where the grass shifts from green to violet to a color you've never seen while awake, a color that only exists in the space between sleeping and waking.

Above you, the sky is folding and unfolding like origami. Clouds rearrange themselves into shapes from your childhood — a grandmother's kitchen, a dog you once loved, a house you moved away from when you were six. Each shape lingers for a heartbeat, then dissolves into birds that scatter into geometric confetti.

You walk, and the ground remembers your footsteps a few seconds after you've made them — glowing prints that bloom and fade like flowers.

Somewhere ahead, someone is singing. The melody is familiar, but you can't place it. It might be a lullaby your mother sang, or it might be a song you'll write someday and haven't yet. The dream doesn't distinguish between memory and prophecy.

You follow the song toward a tree whose branches hold lanterns instead of fruit. Each lantern flickers with a tiny scene: moments of your life, some real, some possible, some impossible. One shows you laughing with friends you don't recognize yet. One shows the ocean, though you've never seen the ocean.

You reach up to touch one of the lanterns—

—and the world ripples like the surface of a pond. The colors begin running, bleeding into each other, the shapes melting like wax. The dream is ending, or maybe just changing, and you can't tell which.

You try to hold onto the meadow, the tree, the song, but they slip through your fingers like water. The last thing you see before everything goes soft and white is the blank canvas, waiting patiently, as if to say: *you can always come back and paint it again.*

Then you're awake, the morning light ordinary and still, with the ghost of an impossible color still fading behind your eyes.
