I feel a storm approaching and the air grows heavy with the promise of rain. The trees around me suddenly seem to hold their breath, their leaves turning a bruised, silver color. The birds have gone silent.

A gust of wind slams into my chest, almost knocking me off balance. The ground trembles faintly beneath my feet. I reach out instinctively to steady myself against the nearest trunk, and the bark is warm and pulsing faintly beneath my palm, as if the forest itself is alive and afraid.

Then the first drops of rain come—not soft and gentle, but fat and cold, like handfuls of water flung from the sky. They strike my face and taste of iron and old stone.

I pull my collar tight and turn to run, but the path ahead has dissolved into a muddy river. The storm swells overhead, the sky boiling with lightning that cracks white across the horizon. Each flash reveals the forest in jagged, frozen relief—trees leaning toward me, branches twisting like grasping hands.

Somewhere beneath the roar of the thunder, I hear something. A sound that isn't thunder.

A voice. Low and calm, cutting through the chaos.

*"You've walked this path before, even if you don't remember."*

I freeze, mud soaking my boots. The rain doesn't fall on me anymore. It falls everywhere else.

*"The storm is not here to hurt you. It's here to cleanse you. But you must choose—run from the voice, or listen to it."*

A second flash of lightning. And in that flash, I see the path ahead of me—or rather, I see myself, standing far ahead, waiting, perfectly still. Watching.

The storm is fading. The voice is gone.

And I am standing in the mud, surrounded by trees, waiting to remember which of me is the real one.
