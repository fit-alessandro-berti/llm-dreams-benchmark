...and at its foot, a paintbrush no one left there.

You pick it up. The handle is warm, as though someone set it down only moments ago — though there are no hands in this place, no people, only hills that ripple like poured honey and a sun that keeps forgetting whether it's a circle or a square.

The moment the bristles touch the canvas, everything goes still. All that churning color holds its breath, waiting to see what you'll do.

You paint a door.

You don't decide to. Your hand knows before you do, pulling the brush in long, certain strokes — and the paint doesn't sit on the canvas. It sinks. And where it sinks, the canvas stops being a surface and becomes an opening. Light spills around the edges of your painted door. Warm light. Gold. Nothing like the cold, restless glow of this place.

Through the doorway: a kitchen. Yours, or almost yours. A kettle on the stove, just beginning to whistle. Everything still and ordinary and utterly impossible.

And you understand — the way you understand things in dreams, without evidence, without doubt — that the canvas was waiting. That this whole shifting world is what happens when nothing gets finished. Colors changing because they have no shape to hold them.

You step toward the door.

Behind you, a voice — not heard so much as felt, like someone standing close enough to touch: *"Not yet. There are others waiting to be finished."*

You turn. Across the landscape, hundreds of canvases have risen from the ground like pale flowers. All blank. All humming.

The door is still open. The kettle is still whistling. You can only finish one.

You reach out—

—and wake.

Some mornings after, waiting for your own kettle to boil, you'd swear you can hear it: a world of endless color, humming somewhere behind the light, waiting for you to come back and choose.
