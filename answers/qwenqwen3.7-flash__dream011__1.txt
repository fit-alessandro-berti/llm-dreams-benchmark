...written in ink that breathes. Each letter rises off the page like pale smoke, curling into silhouettes that match your own quiet hesitations: *“What if you chose wrong?” “Who are you when no one is watching?” “Which path lets you breathe?”*

You reach for the provided pen, but it settles into your palm like a river stone. When you finally press tip to paper, no answer appears. Instead, tiny brass latches click open along the margins, each engraved with a year you thought you’d outgrown. You realize, with a slow sinking sensation, that this isn’t an exam testing what you’ve studied. It’s measuring what you’ve kept locked away.

The proctor hasn’t shifted from the front of the room. Or perhaps they’ve always been here—impossibly tall, draped in a coat you recognize, their features smudged like charcoal left in the rain. On their wrist hangs a watch with no hands, only faint constellations rotating in silence. Outside the classroom window, snow falls upward, drifting toward a sky that looks almost like glass.

You turn to Question Two. It asks you to describe the weight of your own expectations in something tangible. You stare until the lines begin to swim. Then, slowly, you draw a key. As soon as your pencil lifts, the line sinks into the paper and warms beneath your fingers. From wherever you drew it, a thin vine curls outward, threading between other students’ desks. Leaves unfurl, each one holding a different version of you: one speaking on a stage they never thought they’d stand on, one sitting quietly in a sunlit kitchen, one walking away from a door they once begged to enter. None are perfect. All feel true.

A bell tolls, though bells don’t exist in this building. Around you, the other test-takers begin to dissolve—not vanishing, but lightening. Their chairs empty into folded paper cranes that lift through the ceiling tiles into that same impossible sky. No one panics. No one runs. There’s only the quiet rustle of wings and the smell of ozone after rain.

You turn in your sheet. It doesn’t go into a tray. The moment your fingers release it, the page turns translucent, then granular, then damp. It becomes morning condensation on a windowsill you didn’t know was there. Outside, a single sparrow taps twice against the glass, then flies.

You wake not with a gasp, but with the steady hum of daylight on your eyelids. The exam never had a score. It only asked if you were ready to stop waiting for permission to begin.
