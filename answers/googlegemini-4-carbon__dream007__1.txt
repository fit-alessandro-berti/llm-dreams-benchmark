You reach into your pocket for a pen, pull out a bright green asparagus spear instead, and furiously scribble on the table between you. Strangely, it works, leaving glowing violet ink across the wood. You look up triumphantly to see their reaction, only to realize you aren't in a living room anymore—you are both sitting in a wooden rowboat drifting through the flooded aisles of your childhood grocery store.

Your friend opens their mouth to yell back, to tell you why you’re completely wrong, and you brace yourself for the impact of their words. Instead, a cloud of iridescent soap bubbles floats out from between their lips. They look just as shocked as you do. Every time they try to shout, more bubbles emerge, drifting upward toward the fluorescent lights flickering high above the cereal aisle. 

Suddenly, you can't remember what you were fighting about. Was it money? A broken promise? The secret to keeping houseplants alive? The urgency of the anger is still humming in your chest, heavy and hot, yet the reason for it has dissolved like sugar in water. 

You try to ask them, *"Wait, what were we saying?"*—only to find your own voice sounds like a distant cello. 

Before you can panic, the water beneath the boat begins to drain rapidly, swirling down a massive sinkhole near the frozen foods section. Your friend grabs your hand—the anger entirely forgotten now, replaced by a shared, silent awe—as the rowboat tips forward. Instead of crashing onto the linoleum floor, the boat catches an invisible updraft. You burst right through the drop-ceiling tiles and out into a velvet, midnight sky, rowing effortlessly past a moon that looks suspiciously like a giant, glowing clock face with no hands. 

Just as you turn to your friend to laugh about the absurdity of it all, the boat gently bumps against the edge of your mattress, and you wake up.
