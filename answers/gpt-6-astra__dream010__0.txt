unexpectedly, each one takes place in a room from your childhood.

In your old kitchen, you must prepare a feast using ingredients labeled *patience*, *curiosity*, and *rent due*. In your school library, you search for a book that rewrites itself whenever you think you’ve found the answer. The other contestants move quickly, carrying trophies they seem to have won before the competition even began.

You keep stopping to help someone.

A woman has lost her voice before the interview round, so you lend her yours. A boy cannot reach the door to the next challenge, so you let him stand on your shoulders. By the time you reach the final room, you are late, barefoot, and unable to explain yourself.

Behind a vast desk sits someone wearing your face.

“Why should we choose you?” they ask.

Without your voice, you can only empty your pockets.

Out comes a library card. A bent spoon. A shoelace belonging to the boy. Finally, a small brass key you don’t remember finding.

The person behind the desk looks at the key.

“Oh,” they say. “You’re applying for *that* job.”

The wall behind them folds outward, revealing a greenhouse suspended above the sea. Inside, people tend strange plants: a tree bearing unfinished novels, vines heavy with inventions, a bed of pale flowers that hum forgotten songs.

At a workbench near the door, a silver-haired gardener is trying to coax a tiny sun out of a cracked flowerpot.

“Hold this a moment,” she says, handing you the sun.

It is warm and surprisingly heavy. You shift it carefully between your palms while she packs fresh soil around its roots.

From the other room comes the muffled sound of applause. Someone has won.

The gardener clears a space beside her.

“Have you eaten?” she asks.

And somehow, this is the question you’ve been preparing to answer all night.
