You begin to open your mouth, and the sound that comes out isn’t yours. It’s thin, high-pitched, like a child’s, and it doesn’t carry past the first row.

You try again. This time the words are there in your head — the opening line you rehearsed in the shower, the joke you were sure would land — but as soon as you try to shape them your tongue feels swollen and wet. You force a sentence out and it comes out in another language you don’t recognize, then in no language at all.

The crowd is still watching, perfectly still, expectantly. You look down to steady yourself and your notes are gone. Your suit jacket is gone. You are standing on the stage in your underwear, or maybe nothing at all, and the spotlight is so hot you can feel the sweat running down your back.

You try to laugh it off. “Sorry, let me just…” You reach for the podium and it slides away on the slick stage. The floor tilts. The audience is suddenly closer, rows folding in on themselves, and you realize they are all people you know — your old teachers, your boss, your mother — all with their mouths open, waiting.

You wake up just as you’re about to fall.
