...walk, because walking feels like the right thing to do, and the street agrees. It tilts gently downhill in whatever direction you face.

The buildings here are tall and slightly wrong. Their windows are arranged like sentences, and you can almost read them. You check your phone for the address, but the screen shows only a photograph of your childhood kitchen, and the clock on it says the meeting is in an hour. It has said that for a while now.

You ask a woman at a bus stop for directions. She nods as if she's been expecting you and says, "It's past the library, where the river used to be." This makes perfect sense. You thank her in a language you don't speak.

The library turns out to be your old elementary school, except the hallways are lined with filing cabinets, and each drawer is labeled with the name of someone you've forgotten. You open one. Inside is a staircase.

You climb down, which somehow takes you up, and emerge onto a rooftop above the whole city. From here you can see that the streets spell something, a word you know, right on the tip of your tongue. The meeting is in an hour.

There's a door on the rooftop, standing alone in its frame. You recognize it as the front door of a house you lived in once, or will. Through the frosted glass you can see figures seated around a long table, and one empty chair.

You realize, with the calm certainty that only comes in dreams, that you were never late. The meeting couldn't start without you. It has been waiting an hour for a very long time.

You reach for the handle. It's warm, like someone has just let go of it from the other side.

The door opens, and everyone at the table turns to look at you. Every one of them has your face, at different ages. The oldest one smiles, gestures to the empty chair, and says:

"Good. Now we can finally begin. Do you remember what you came here to tell us?"

And the strange thing is, you do.
