Thunder rumbles overhead, though it sounds less like weather and more like heavy furniture being dragged across a wooden floor. You try to run for cover, your legs moving with a sudden, frustrating underwater heaviness, just as the rain begins to fall. Only, it isn't cold water—it’s warm, glowing droplets of liquid amber that chime like tiny glass bells wherever they strike the moss. 

Desperate for shelter, you spot a freestanding red wooden door resting against the trunk of a massive, ancient oak. With the unquestioning certainty of dream logic, you know this has always been your front door. You grab the brass knob, pull it open, and tumble inside.

The howling wind vanishes instantly. You are no longer in the woods, nor are you wet. Instead, you find yourself standing in a quiet, warmly lit vintage train car, swaying gently along invisible tracks. Looking out the window, you see the forest is gone; the train is gliding smoothly across the surface of a vast, mirror-like ocean. High above, giant whales made entirely of dark storm clouds swim lazily through the starry sky, their distant songs sounding suspiciously like your morning alarm.
