You step through a low archway, and the air grows still and heavy. The walls are carved with glowing symbols that shift as you look at them—not random, but a language your sleeping mind begins to understand.

The first puzzle is a door of interlocking gears. You trace the patterns, find the one missing tooth, and turn the smallest gear counterclockwise. The door opens with a soft hum.

Deeper in, the corridor branches into seven paths, each guarded by a riddle etched in stone. You read each one slowly, letting your thoughts settle. The riddles are mirrors of your own doubts—fear of getting lost, fear of failing, fear of never waking. You answer them not with boldness, but with quiet certainty: *I am here to find my own way.*

The walls open into a vast chamber. In the center stands a pedestal, and on it rests a single, glowing key. But the floor is a checkerboard of illusion and truth. Some tiles are solid, some are traps of memory. You close your eyes and feel the maze breathe. You take one deliberate step, then another. The key is yours.

As you lift it, the maze begins to dissolve into light. You realize the puzzles were never meant to trap you—they were meant to teach you how to listen. You wake up, still holding the warmth of that key in your hand.
