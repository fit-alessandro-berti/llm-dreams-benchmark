As your fingers trace a spiral near the seam, the wood gives way like warm wax. There is no keyhole, only a shallow, smooth depression shaped like a resting fingertip. It isn't waiting for a key made of brass or iron; it is waiting for recognition. 

You press your thumb against the hollow. The box exhales—a faint, cool breeze that smells of rain on dry stone and old library pages. 

Slowly, the box begins to unfold. It doesn't just pop open; the panels glide outward into impossible angles, defying geometry, turning itself inside out like an origami blossom. 

Inside, there is no velvet lining, no gemstone or parchment. Instead, you find yourself staring into a miniature, self-contained night sky. A sea of clouds drifts beneath you, illuminated by a pale, silver sliver of moon. From within the tiny abyss, a quiet, melodic chime echoes—not from the box, but from somewhere just behind your own ribs.

You reach out to touch the miniature clouds, and the moment your fingers break the surface, gravity shifts. The box expands, or perhaps you shrink, until the horizon rushes upward to meet you, and you are falling upward through the cool, fragrant night, carrying the secret of the box in your open hands as the dream begins to dissolve into morning.
