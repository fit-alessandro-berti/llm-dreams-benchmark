You decide to stop arguing and instead reach into your chest and pull out the exact thing you’ve been fighting about—a small, glowing weight that hums like a struck bell. The moment it’s in the air between you, the room tilts sideways, and your friend’s face keeps sliding into other faces you’ve loved, then snapping back to theirs, as if the dream can’t settle on one version of them.

The walls melt into a sky the color of old photographs, and you realize the floor is made of every conversation you’ve ever had with this person, thousands of them, stacked in transparent layers. You decide to drop the glowing thing. When it falls, it doesn’t hit the ground—it sinks through the layers of words, and each one it passes through turns into a bird, a book, a blue flame, a childhood secret. Your friend dives after it, not in anger anymore, but in something like longing, and you follow.

You’re falling now, not down a stairwell but through a series of doorways that open onto the same argument from different angles: in a kitchen, in a forest, in the backseat of a car, on the moon. Just before you hit the ground—which, in the dream, is simply the other side of the ceiling—you hear your friend say one perfect sentence that explains everything, but the words are made of water and slip through your fingers. You wake up with your hand still open, trying to catch them.
