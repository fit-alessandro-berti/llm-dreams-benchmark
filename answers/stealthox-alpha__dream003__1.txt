I press my palm flat against the lid.

The patterns begin to move, swirling beneath the wood like fish beneath ice. The box grows lighter, or perhaps I do—I can no longer tell where my hands end and the box begins. Somewhere in the dream-fog, I understand that this box was never meant to be opened with a key. It opens the way a flower opens: slowly, and only for something patient.

So I wait. The dream waits with me.

And then, without any hinge creaking or lock clicking, the lid simply isn't closed anymore. Inside there is no treasure, no gold, no letter. Inside is a small, perfect sky—clouds drifting in a space far too vast to fit within the box, yet somehow it does. I lean closer, and the clouds part to reveal a reflection: not my face, but the face I had as a child, before I learned what boxes were for.

The child-me looks up and mouths something. No sound comes, but I understand: *"You already have everything you were looking for. You just keep putting it in boxes."*

The box begins to dissolve into moths—hundreds of them, pale as paper, spiraling upward into a ceiling that was never there. I want to follow them. I try to lift my arms, but they're made of the same intricate patterns now, the same shifting symbols, and I realize with a strange, calm terror that I was the box all along.

The dream loosens its grip. The moths become streetlights. The streetlights become morning.

I wake with my hand pressed flat against the mattress, fingers curled around nothing, still holding the shape of a key that never existed.
