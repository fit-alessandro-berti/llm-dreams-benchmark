You are at a large social gathering where you only know one person. As you try to find them, you encounter various groups of people and none of them quite notice you until you try to leave.

Near the drinks, a circle is toasting with glasses that are empty until you look away. Someone laughs at a joke that has not been told yet. You ask if they have seen the person you came with. They all nod. They all have the same first name as that person. They point toward a doorway that was not there a moment ago.

Through it, the kitchen has no walls. Three people are arguing over a recipe written on the insides of their wrists. They swear your person was just here, tasting the sauce, and they point in different directions at once. A woman offers you a glass that is already empty. You drink anyway. It is warm, then cold, then gone.

A hallway lengthens as you walk. Each doorway opens onto the same party in slightly different light. Someone is wearing their coat. You reach for a sleeve and the coat is empty, hanging on a chair that was not there a second ago. A figure turns—same stance, same way of standing—and your chest lifts. They face you. The face is almost right, then it isn’t; it keeps choosing features from other people at the party, as if trying them on.

Outside—or what claims to be outside—long tables are set for a dinner that has already been eaten. Plates are clean. Candles are unlit and still dripping. At the far end a hand rises in greeting. From this distance it is them. You walk the length of the table and the greeting becomes a wave goodbye. By the time you arrive the chair is pushed in.

You start checking reflections. In a dark window you see the gathering behind you, and in the middle of it, the one person you know, looking for you. You spin around. The room has rearranged. New groups have formed in the exact places the old ones stood, having the same conversations a half-step out of phase.

A child you do not remember knowing tugs your sleeve and says they are in the other house now. There is no other house. There is only this one, and it keeps growing rooms to hide a single familiar face.

You keep walking. Every group is almost the right one. Every back of a head is almost theirs. The party never thins. Somewhere you can hear your own name said fondly, by a voice you would know anywhere, always in the next room.
