A ripple of polite, tinkling laughter moves through the audience. You decide to roll with it. "I see we have some questions," you say, gesturing grandly to the frog. 

The audience erupts into applause, but the sound is thick and slow, like honey pouring over your ears. You step toward the edge of the stage to take a bow, but the floor tilts, and you find yourself sliding gently down a spiraling slide that has mysteriously replaced the steps. You land in a pile of giant, squeaking pillows, and just as you look up to see the entire audience peering down at you from the top of the slide—still clapping, still smiling—the whole world dissolves into a blinding white light. 

You wake up with the distinct taste of toothpaste in your mouth and your alarm clock blaring a very convincing impression of a standing ovation.
