You start to walk.

Or try to. Your feet feel heavy, like the sidewalk is made of warm wax. You look down and realize you're wearing two different shoes - one dress shoe, polished to a shine, and one soft, worn-out sneaker. Your bag is suddenly too heavy on your shoulder.

You pull out your phone. No signal. The clock says 9:07, but the numbers flicker. When you blink, it's 9:06 again. You have an hour, but time doesn't seem to be moving right.

You try to remember - who is the meeting with? Where? The harder you try to grasp it, the smoother the memory becomes, like trying to hold water in your hands. You know it's *important*. Vital, even. If you miss it, something terrible will happen. Or maybe something wonderful will never happen.

You stop a woman passing by. She's walking a dog that looks exactly like a cloud.

"Excuse me, do you know where--" you start, and realize you don't have an address to ask for.

She smiles like she knows you. "You're going the right way," she says, but she points in three directions at once. "Just keep going downhill. You can't miss it."

The city isn't just unfamiliar - it's *impossible*. The buildings are too tall, curving slightly inward to watch you. Streets rearrange themselves when you're not looking. You turn a corner and find yourself back in the same plaza with the same dry fountain, only now the fountain is full of clear water and overflowing.

You start to run.

Your dress shoe clicks, your sneaker squeaks. The city blurs. Storefronts flicker like old film: a bakery, a library, a shop selling nothing but keys, all closed. The people on the street move too smoothly, like they're on a conveyor belt. None of them seem in a hurry.

You find a crumpled card in your pocket you don't remember putting there. Thick paper. Gold lettering that shifts when you move it.

It reads:

**MEETING ROOM: 42nd Floor**
**Building With No Doors**
**Come As You Are**

The ink is still wet.

Above you, a skyscraper you've never noticed before pierces the clouds. It has no doors on the ground floor, only endless, smooth glass windows reflecting a sky that isn't the same sky you're standing under. In the reflection, you're wearing a perfect suit. In reality, you're still mismatched.

You look at your phone again. 9:59.

You have one minute. The building is right in front of you, but you have no way in.

And then - with that perfect, illogical clarity that only happens in dreams - you understand. You don't need a door. You place your palm against the cool glass, and it ripples like water.

Do you step through?
