You are given a mysterious locked box with no key in sight. As you examine it closely, you notice intricate patterns and symbols that refuse to stay still. They rearrange whenever you blink: a river becoming a vein, a constellation becoming a lock, a keyhole becoming an eye that looks back. The lines are not carved. They have grown there — vines, doors the size of fingernails, a maze that tightens if you try to memorize it.

When you tilt the box toward the light, the patterns lift from the wood like wet ink and hang in the air. They are not decoration. They are a lock made of memory. Each swirl matches something you almost forgot: a hallway in a house that was sold, the exact temperature of a hand you once held, a name you stopped saying.

One tiny door is already open. Behind it sits another box, identical and smaller. You follow the pattern inward with your eyes. The room tilts. Your hands look farther away than they should. Then you notice the largest box is no longer on the table. It is around you. The walls have the same grain, the same miniature doors. One of them, just behind your left shoulder, clicks.

There is still no key in sight. There is only the sound of something on the other side of the wood, breathing the same way you do. When you match its breath, the patterns settle. The box does not open. The room does. Everything folds inward like paper, and you understand you were never outside it. You were the thing it was keeping safe.
