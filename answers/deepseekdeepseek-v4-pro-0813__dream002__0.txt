The air sharpens with the smell of ozone and wet bark. The forest darkens to a deep indigo, and the trees lean in as though listening. You start to hurry, but the path loops back to the same moss-covered stone. Then the first gust hits, not with cold, but with a warm whisper that says your name. Rain begins to fall upward from the ground, glinting like needles of light.

A door appears in the trunk of the oldest oak, slightly open. Inside, you can hear your own heartbeat echoing like thunder. You step through—and the storm is gone. You are standing in a room made of rain and moonlight, where every lost thing you ever loved is laid out on a long wooden table. You reach for one, and just as your fingers close around it, you wake.
