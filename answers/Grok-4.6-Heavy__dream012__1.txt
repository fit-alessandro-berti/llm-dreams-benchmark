Here is the dream, completed.

You are the captain of a ship in the middle of a turbulent sea. Your crew looks to you for guidance as the storm worsens, and you do not shout. You put both hands on the wheel. The wood is warm, like a pulse.

Rain comes in sideways. Lightning writes the names of people you have not thought of in years across the water, then erases them. Your first mate calls something about the sails; when you look at him his face is your father’s. A boy with your childhood face is lashed to the mast. Beside them an oilskin stands empty, holding only wind.

“Hold,” you say, and your voice comes out older than you remember.

The ship heels until the deck is almost a wall. In the next flash you see what the waves are carrying: doors, upright in the foam, half-open. Through one, a kitchen light. Through another, a bed you used to wake in. The storm is not trying to sink you. It is trying to deliver you.

You give the only order that works in a dream. You turn toward the tallest door.

The crew cries out, some in warning, some in relief. The ship does not crash. It passes through. The roar drops away like a coat shrugged off. You are standing in a hallway that smells of rain and salt, still captain, still gripping a wheel that is now only a brass doorknob.

You do not look back. Behind you the sea keeps shouting, but the door has already begun to close.

When you wake, your hands will still remember the grain of that wheel.
The sea is still shouting. The door is still closing. Your hands already know the rest.
