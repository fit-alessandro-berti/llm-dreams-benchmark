You gently slip into the warm, golden light of a small courtyard tucked between towering skyscrapers. The air smells faintly of roasted nuts and rain-washed pavement. The strangers linger near you, not with urgency, but with quiet presence. One hands you a weathered wooden frame map, their eyes kind and steady. “You don’t have to carry this alone,” the youngest one says softly. “Follow that narrow alley until the clocktower shows the sun, and we’ll walk with you for the first few blocks.”

You take the map. The alley curves past flower stalls and flickering shutters, and for the first time in what feels like hours, your shoulders ease. You don’t know your destination yet, but you’re no longer moving through the dark alone. When the group finally reaches a quiet corner and turns to go their own way, one hand lingers on your shoulder, leaving a small stone in your pocket that feels warm. You nod, take a deep breath, and step forward into the city. The rhythm of footsteps around you begins to settle into a familiar, steady beat. You know you’ll still get lost sometimes, but you also know you’re never truly without a way home.
