In the waking world, walking toward an unknown sound in the wastelands would be foolish—especially with jagged silhouettes prowling behind rusted billboards and your canteen nearly empty. Yet in the quiet logic of the dream, the melody feels more vital than water.

You step over shattered glass that chimes softly beneath your boots like thin ice. As you enter the skeleton of the ruined city, the skyscrapers lean inward, their broken windows reflecting a bruised violet sky where two pale moons hang side by side. You reach into your pocket and your fingers brush against a heavy brass key. You have no memory of finding it, only the absolute certainty that you have carried it for miles.

The music grows clearer, pulling you toward a half-collapsed conservatory. As you climb its crumbling marble stairs, the rubble beneath your feet seamlessly turns into smooth, warm driftwood. The air loses its metallic bite of smoke and rust, suddenly smelling of ozone, wet earth, and crushed mint.

When you push open the heavy doors at the top, the roof is gone, opening directly onto a swirling expanse of twilight clouds. In the center of the floor, bioluminescent moss and pale wildflowers have overtaken the broken stone. Sitting among the blooms is a figure in a patchwork cloak, drawing a bow across the strings of a cello carved from driftwood. 

The musician pauses and looks up. Their face shifts like ripples on dark water—familiar as an old friend, yet impossible to name. Without a word, they tilt their head toward a small ironbound box nestled in the moss beside them.

You kneel and slide the brass key into the lock. It turns effortlessly. As you lift the lid, there is no treasure inside—only a swell of warm, golden light and the sudden, overwhelming rush of a summer breeze. The light spills out over the rim, washing across the jagged ruins below, softening the sharp edges of the broken world until the wasteland, the music, and the sky dissolve into quiet, weightless warmth.
