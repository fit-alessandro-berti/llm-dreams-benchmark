“The meeting,” she says, as if answering a question you haven’t asked, “has been waiting for you.”

You hurry down a street lined with clocks, each showing a different hour. Shop windows reflect places you almost recognize: your childhood kitchen, a train station from a forgotten trip, a room where someone is laughing just out of sight.

At the end of the street stands a narrow blue door. The key fits, though you’re certain it was too large a moment ago.

Inside is a long table set for dozens of guests. Every chair is empty except one. Sitting in it is a younger version of you, drawing a map with a green crayon.

“You’re late,” they say.

“I didn’t know where to go.”

“I know.” They slide the map across the table. It shows the city, the blue door, and a tiny figure standing at the head of the table. Then they add another figure beside it and hand you the crayon.

Outside, the clocks begin to chime. You sit down and start drawing.
