You are about to take the final exam for a course that determines your future career. As you sit down to start the exam, you notice the questions are written in your own handwriting, but in a language you only speak when you’re honest.

The first one asks, “What did you love before you learned to call it impractical?”  
The second: “Who did you become to make everyone else feel comfortable?”  
The third is blank except for a tiny mirror pressed into the paper.

Around you, everyone else is scribbling furiously, but their answers keep turning into birds and flying out the window. The proctor is an older version of you, tired and kind, holding a red pen like a lit match. “You don’t have to pass,” they whisper. “You have to choose.”

You raise your hand, but the exam hall has already become a train station. The train is leaving. You don’t know where it’s going, but somehow you know it’s yours. You step on just as the doors close—and wake up with your heart pounding like a pencil tapping a desk.
