You shout orders into the wind, but your voice comes back to you changed—low and strange, as if the sea itself is speaking for you. One by one, the crew follows your command, hauling the sails and lashing the rigging, though you notice they are no longer quite human: their eyes are full of foam, their hands are wrapped in seaweed, and when they smile, they show teeth like pearls.

Then, as the next wave rises taller than the horizon, you realize the ship is not sinking at all. It is being lifted upward, slowly, like a bird learning flight. The storm breaks open above you into a sky full of silver lanterns, and you understand, with the calm certainty of dreams, that your crew has been waiting for you to let go of the wheel.

So you do. The sea releases you. The ship sails on without a captain, and you fall gently into the dark water—but you do not drown. You rise instead, carrying the storm inside you like a heartbeat, and the ship, now smaller than a memory, disappears into the light.
