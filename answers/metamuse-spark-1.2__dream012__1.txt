The wind screams through the rigging, tearing at the sails. A wall of black water rises higher than the mast, higher than anything you thought water could do, and your crew - their faces wet with rain and sea spray - are all looking at *you*. Not with panic, but with waiting. With trust.

You open your mouth to shout an order, to tell them to pull the ropes, to brace, to fight - but no sound comes out.

And in that silence, you understand.

This storm isn't trying to sink you.

You stop fighting the wheel.

Instead of turning *away* from the massive wave, you breathe in - deep, salty, cold - and you turn the bow directly *into* it. Straight into the heart of the black wall.

Your crew gasps, but then they move. Not because you ordered them, but because you showed them. They lean into the wind with you.

The ship climbs.

For a second, you are completely vertical, suspended on the face of the water like you're climbing a mountain made of midnight glass. The rain stops in mid-air around you. The thunder holds its breath. Everything is silent except the creak of the wood and your own heartbeat.

Then you crest the top.

And the other side is not water at all.

The storm clouds have parted, and the sea below you is perfectly still - a flawless, starry mirror. The sky above and the sea below are the same endless night, full of constellations you've never seen. The ship is no longer sailing on water, but gliding across the stars themselves, weightless and silent. The wind is now warm against your face.

Your crew begins to laugh, soft and awestruck. Someone lets go of the ropes and just watches their hands trail through starlight.

You loosen your grip on the wheel. You finally understand you were never meant to control the storm. You were only meant to steer through it.

And just as you feel the ship begin to gently fly, you wake up - with the feeling of that warm wind still on your skin, and the strange, calm certainty that you can handle whatever is waiting for you today.
