...intricate patterns and they aren't carved at all.

They're *moving*.

At first you think it's a trick of the light, but as you hold the box closer, the thin golden lines shift and swirl under the dark wood like liquid light. They pulse faintly, in rhythm with your breathing. Or maybe you're breathing in rhythm with *them*.

The box is warm. Warmer than wood should be. It has no keyhole, no latch, no hinges that you can see — just that seamless surface covered in what looks like a labyrinth. Constellations. Veins. A map of a city you've never been to but somehow recognize.

And then you realize: the pattern matches the lines on your palm.

Curious, you press your palm against the lid.

The golden lines flare. They rush to meet your skin, aligning perfectly with your lifeline, your heartline. The wood softens under your touch like wax, like skin.

*Click.*

There was never a lock. You just hadn't touched it the right way.

The lid unfolds — not opens, *unfolds* — peeling back in four perfect petals that shouldn't have been able to fit inside that shape. Inside, there is no velvet lining. No treasure.

Inside is a deep, quiet ocean of midnight blue, contained impossibly within the small box. A tiny moon hangs over tiny waves. And floating in the middle, perfectly dry, is a folded slip of paper.

You reach in. Your hand doesn't get wet, but you feel a cool breeze.

You unfold the paper. The handwriting is unmistakably your own, though you have no memory of writing it:

> *You were supposed to find this later. Are you ready to remember?*

Behind you in the dream, you suddenly hear the faint sound of a key turning in a door —

— and you realize the box wasn't locked to keep something *in*.

It was locked to keep you *out* until you were ready to open it.

Do you want to read what’s on the other side of the paper, or put the lid back on and wake up?
