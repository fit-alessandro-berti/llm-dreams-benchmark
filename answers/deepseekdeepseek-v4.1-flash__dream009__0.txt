You realize you need to find the one thing that doesn’t belong. It isn’t you—not yet. You can feel it like a splinter under the skin of the world: a faint electric hum beneath the chanting in the square, a blue glow leaking from a monastery window, a stranger whose boots are too clean for the mud. You follow it through alleys that rearrange themselves when you look away. The stranger waits at a stone arch, holding a small brass key. “You left this behind,” he says. You take it, though you’ve never seen it before. The arch opens onto a corridor of doors, each one a different century. You choose the one with your name on it. Inside, your alarm clock is ringing. You wake in your own bed, but the key is still warm in your hand—and for a moment, you hear the blacksmith’s hammer, keeping time.
