At first, the puzzles were tangible—interlocking brass gears, cipher wheels, and corridors shadowed by towering, geometric monoliths. Move a mirror here, align a beam of starlight there, and a heavy stone door would grind open. 

But here, near the center, the walls are no longer made of stone; they are woven from murmuring voices and translucent glass. You approach a pedestal holding what looks like a chessboard, yet the pieces are carved from frozen memories. To checkmate the king, you don't calculate moves ahead—you have to remember the exact sound of rain hitting your childhood bedroom window. As soon as you recall it, the board melts into liquid silver, pooling on the floor and unlocking a staircase that spirals *upward* into nothingness.

Gravity shifts. You are walking on the ceiling of the maze now, looking down into an infinite fractal of pathways you’ve already solved. The intense concentration in your chest begins to feel heavy, like an anchor. Ahead lies the final gate, guarded by a massive clockwork astrolabe spinning wildly out of sync. 

You reach out, your mind racing to calculate the ratios, the angles, the sheer mathematics needed to halt the spinning rings. Your fingers brush the cold metal, and suddenly, a profound realization washes over you: *the maze is feeding on your effort.* Every logical deduction builds another wall. Every calculated step extends the corridor. 

You pull your hands back. You close your eyes, take a deep breath, and intentionally let go of the math, the logic, and the need to solve it. 

The deafening clicking of the astrolabe stops instantly. When you open your eyes, the maze is gone. The walls have dissolved into millions of glowing paper moths fluttering upward into a vast, indigo sky. You take one step forward onto empty air, weightless, and begin to float—waking up just as the wind catches you.
