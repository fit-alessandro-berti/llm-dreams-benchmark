All around it, the horizon is melting from electric violet into a warm, humming gold. Mountains fold inward like origami cranes only to bloom into sprawling fractal trees, and the ground beneath your feet ripples like velvet water. Yet the canvas stands anchored on an easel of bleached driftwood, stark white, silent, and entirely immune to the chaos.

There is no brush, no palette. Acting on pure instinct, you reach up and pluck a ribbon of crimson light straight out of the shifting sky. It feels cool and heavy between your fingers, smelling faintly of rain and ozone. 

You press it to the canvas. 

The moment the color touches the weave, a deep, resonant cello note echoes across the landscape. Where you drag your fingers, the crimson doesn't mutate or vanish—it stays, solidifying into the sharp, clear outline of a doorway. Intrigued, you scoop a handful of velvet indigo from the ground, splashing it beside the door, where it instantly freezes into a quiet, starry night. 

With every stroke, the frantic kaleidoscope around you begins to slow, leaning in as if curious to see what it looks like when it finally rests. You lose track of time, pulling shapes from the madness—a spiral of silver wind, a geometric sun, the emerald curve of a half-forgotten memory—anchoring them onto the linen until the canvas is no longer a painting, but a window into a calm, breathtaking world of your own making.

Drawn by a sudden, gentle gravity, you reach out to trace the crimson doorway one last time. Instead of rough fabric, your fingertips meet cool air. The painted stars twinkle, genuinely distant, and a soft breeze drifts out from the frame, brushing against your face. 

Without hesitation, you step over the wooden lip of the easel and walk inside. Behind you, the ever-changing world sighs and dissolves into mist, leaving you suspended in the quiet, starry warmth of your creation—floating weightlessly until the dark indigo slowly brightens into the soft, familiar glow of morning light filtering through your closed eyelids.
