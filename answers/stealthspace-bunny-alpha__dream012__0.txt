You take one breath, then another, and make yourself steady enough to think.

“All hands to stations!” you shout. “Secure the rigging! Light every lantern! Get the boats aboard!”

Your voice barely reaches the stern before the wind steals it, but your crew moves. They always do when the world begins to unravel. Men and women pull ropes until their arms tremble, lash down crates, lash down themselves. One young sailor freezes at the mainmast, staring into the darkness.

You catch her eye and point to the nearest rope.

“Hold on!” she screams—or perhaps you do. She reaches for it.

The ship climbs a wave so high the masts seem to brush the clouds. Then the crest folds beneath you, and for one suspended moment the sea falls away.

There is no water below the hull.

Only darkness, deep and bottomless, watching.

The compass in your hand begins to spin. North, south, east, west—then directions with no names, no meanings, only forgotten doors. At the bow, your first mate calls, “Captain! There’s something in the water!”

Lightning flashes.

Between the waves stands a lighthouse, impossibly tall, its lantern burning with a pale, steady flame. Yet its light reaches not upward, but down—illuminating a staircase descending into the black.

You could turn away. You could order another heading, promise your crew that you know the way home.

Instead, you walk to the rail.

The wind dies.

The sea becomes glass.

In that perfect stillness, you hear every heartbeat aboard the ship—and one more, vast and patient, somewhere beneath the keel.

You wrap your fingers around the wheel and say, “We follow the light.”

The ship slides toward the staircase. As the first stair shatters beneath the hull, driving the vessel impossibly downward, the darkness opens its eye.

And you understand that you were never meant to escape the storm.

You were meant to discover what waited at its center.
