…you start to run, but the streets keep rearranging themselves like shuffled cards. Each corner you turn leads to the same café with the same clock, its hands spinning backward. You ask a passerby for directions, but their face is blank, featureless—a smooth porcelain mask. They point upward, and you notice the buildings are made of stacked papers, each one a memo you forgot to read.

You pull out your phone, but the map is a maze of your own memories. The meeting's address is written in a language you almost understand. You walk into a lobby, and the elevator opens onto a hallway that smells like rain and old books. A door with your name on it appears. You push it open, and inside, everyone is already seated. They smile. The meeting was never about the location—it was about remembering that you were already here all along.

Then the alarm rings. You wake up, still not sure where you are, but with the strange certainty that you were on time.
