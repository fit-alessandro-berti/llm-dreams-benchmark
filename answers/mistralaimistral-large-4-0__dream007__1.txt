But as the word leaves your lips, it transforms into a flock of white birds that burst from your mouth and circle the room, rearranging themselves into the shape of every kindness you've ever failed to offer, every silence you've filled with anger instead of understanding. Your friend reaches out, and one bird lands on their fingertip, dissolving into silver dust that smells like rain and old books.

The argument begins to dissolve too, not because you've reached an agreement, but because you realize you are both standing in the middle of an ocean that has no surface—only depths. The water is warm, filled with the weight of everything unsaid, and you are sinking together, hand in hand, down through layers of years: the night you met, the night you almost lost each other, the night you first realized that love and fear wear the same face.

You decide to keep sinking.

Your friend's eyes are open, but they are dreaming you back, and in the dream logic of this place, that is the only apology that matters—the willingness to drown in the truth of each other, to let the argument become the current that carries you both toward some shore you cannot yet see, where the water turns to light, and the fighting finally, finally stops.
