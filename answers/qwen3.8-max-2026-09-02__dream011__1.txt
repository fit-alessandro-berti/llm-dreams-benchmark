…not written in words at all, but in shifting images: a door slightly ajar, a map with no roads, a clock melting into the desk like warm wax.

You look around the exam hall, but the other students are calm, as if this is normal. Their pencils move across the paper with soft scratching sounds, and each sound becomes a tiny bird that flies up to the ceiling and disappears.

You look down at your own paper again. The first question changes. Now it says:

**“What are you afraid of becoming?”**

Your chest tightens. You try to answer, but your pencil turns into a feather. The moment it touches the page, ink blooms out like watercolor, forming a picture of yourself—not as you are now, but as someone older, standing at the edge of a life you don’t recognize.

Then the second question appears:

**“What would you choose if no one was watching?”**

The room grows quieter. The walls breathe slowly, like the whole building is asleep. You realize, suddenly and clearly, that the exam is not testing what you know. It is testing what you are willing to admit.

You begin to write.

As you do, the dream shifts. The exam hall dissolves into a long corridor lined with doors. Each one bears a version of your future: one where you succeed brilliantly, one where you fail publicly, one where you walk away entirely and become someone gentler, stranger, freer.

At the end of the corridor stands a figure with your face, holding a blank diploma.

They say, “The course never determined your future. It only revealed how much you believed it could.”

You reach for the diploma, and the moment your fingers touch it, it becomes a mirror.

You wake up with the strange, quiet feeling that the real exam has not begun yet—and that maybe, this time, you are allowed to write your own questions.
