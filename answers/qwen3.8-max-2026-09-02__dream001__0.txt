You start to walk faster, your shoes clicking against wet cobblestones that gleam like dark mirrors. The city rises around you in impossible layers—bridges crossing between rooftops, stairways curling into the sky, windows glowing with amber light even though it feels like both midnight and late afternoon at once.

You clutch your coat tighter, heart pounding. Every street sign is written in a language you almost understand, the letters shifting whenever you look directly at them. People pass you in blurred motion, carrying briefcases, lanterns, bundles of flowers that whisper as they sway.

Then you see it: a clock tower at the center of a circular plaza, its face blank except for a single hand counting down. Beneath it stands a door—freestanding, attached to no building, painted deep blue. Somehow, you know that is where the meeting is.

You hurry toward it, but with every step the plaza stretches farther away. The hour is shrinking. The clock hand trembles.

Just as panic rises, a child appears beside you and holds out a silver key. “You’re not late,” they say. “You just haven’t remembered how to arrive.”

You take the key. The moment your fingers close around it, the city folds gently around you like paper, and suddenly you are standing directly in front of the blue door.

You turn the key.

Inside is not an office or a conference room, but a quiet garden beneath a glass ceiling filled with stars. At a round table sit several figures you recognize but cannot name. One of them smiles and slides an empty chair toward you.

“You’re right on time,” they say.

You sit down, and the dream settles into stillness.
