…and the challenges begin to blur into something stranger than any reality show.

The first challenge is simple: *Build a bridge.* But the bridge is made of your memories. You pull old school days, first heartbreaks, half-remembered song lyrics from your hands, and they harden into planks. It holds.

The second challenge: *Speak to your past self.* You stand across a mirror that shows you at eight years old. The interviewer says, "Convince them you made it." You try. The eight-year-old just laughs and says, "You're not even wearing the cape yet." So you tell them about the things you'll build, the people you'll help, the nights you'll cry and still show up. They nod, hand you a tiny paper star, and vanish.

The third challenge: *The elevator pitch.* But the elevator goes down, not up. Each floor is a year you almost gave up. The door opens at the year you lost everything. The judges are there, but they're wearing the faces of your fears. You still pitch. Your voice shakes, but you don't stop. The elevator reaches the basement, where there's a single door labeled "DREAM JOB." You open it.

Inside is not an office. It's a room full of people you've helped, projects you've loved, and versions of yourself that kept going. A woman with kind eyes steps forward and says, "You've been doing this job your whole life. We just wanted to see if you'd notice."

You wake up. The alarm is ringing. But for the first time, you smile—because you know the dream was right. You already have the job. You just need to keep showing up.
