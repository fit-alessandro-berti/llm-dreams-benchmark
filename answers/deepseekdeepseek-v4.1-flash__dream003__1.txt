...and thin, silvered glyphs that rearrange themselves whenever you blink. They aren’t carved into the wood; they’re breathing. You trace one with a fingertip, and the box hums—a low note you feel in your teeth. A seam opens along an edge, not to reveal a lock but to show you another layer of patterns inside, spiraling down like a staircase. You follow them with your eyes until you’re somewhere else: a moonlit room filled with a hundred locked doors, each one shaped like a promise you’ve forgotten.

In the center stands a pedestal. On it rests a key made of shadow and salt. When you pick it up, it melts into your palm and becomes a memory: you, very small, hiding a secret inside a music box. The secret was not a thing. It was a name. You whisper it, and every door opens at once.

Beyond them is not a corridor but the sky, folded like paper. The mysterious box floats there too, now open and empty. Inside its lid, one last pattern appears: a single line that reads, *This was never a box. It was a question.*

You step through the open doors, and the dream folds shut behind you. You wake with the taste of a key on your tongue.
