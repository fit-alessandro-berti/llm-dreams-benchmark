You are participating in a competition where the grand prize is your dream job. As the competition progresses, you face various challenges and you realize you don't remember signing up for it.

The first challenge is a hallway of office doors. Each one is labeled with a job title you have ever wanted: "Senior Architect," "Marine Biologist," "Novelist." When you open one, the room inside is empty except for a chair facing a blank white wall. A voice over a loudspeaker says "Interview in 3...2..." and the wall starts filling with your own handwriting, listing every small failure you've ever had. You try to talk but your mouth is full of paperclips.

The next challenge is an obstacle course in what looks like your childhood kitchen. The floor is sticky with spilled coffee that never dries. You have to balance on a beam made of résumés while a panel of your former bosses, all wearing the same expressionless smile, watch from folding chairs. They start throwing questions at you like soft balls: "Where do you see yourself in five years?" One hits you in the chest and you sink a few inches into the floor.

You try to run and the hallway stretches. The walls are now made of computer screens showing a live feed of you running. In the feed, you are already at the finish line, holding a trophy shaped like a golden briefcase. In real life, you are still running.

Finally you arrive at a giant conference table. At the head sits you, but an older version of you, wearing the exact outfit you hoped you'd be wearing in your dream job. He slides a contract across the table. The ink is still wet and smells like rain. As you reach for a pen, you realize the pen is your own hand, and your hand is getting smaller.

You wake up just as you sign your name, and the signature turns into a door.

The alarm is going off.
