You start to move, but the streets feel softer than they should, like ink spreading under your shoes. Every building you pass has your name written on it in languages you almost understand. A stranger hands you a map that is only blank paper, and when you look up, the sky has become a clock face with too many hands.

You ask for directions, but the people’s voices arrive out of order, answering questions you haven’t thought yet. “You’re almost there,” one says. “You left too late,” says another. “The meeting is with yourself,” says a third, before dissolving into a flock of pale pigeons.

Then a tram glides through a wall beside you, its windows glowing warm gold. Its sign reads: DESTINATION UNKNOWN, BOARDING NOW. You realize the doors will close in exactly one hour, so you step aboard. The seat is still warm, and in the pocket of your coat is a folder containing every place you’ve ever been afraid to find. The tram begins to move, and somewhere ahead, a voice over the speaker says, calmly:

“Please prepare for your introduction. You are expected.”
