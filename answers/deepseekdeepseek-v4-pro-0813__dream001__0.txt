You start to run, but the streets rearrange themselves like a shuffled deck. Every corner you turn reveals a different version of the city—a marble plaza full of fountains one moment, a crooked alley of blue-brick houses the next. Your phone screen melts into a swirl of unreadable glyphs, and the meeting invitation in your pocket keeps rewriting its address whenever you blink.

A passing stranger stops and smiles, their face shifting through dozens of familiar features—none of which you can name. They point toward a tower that wasn’t there a second ago, a slender black spire with a clock face showing both all twelve hours and only one. “You’re already late,” they say kindly, and then they dissolve into a flock of paper cranes.

You run toward the tower, but the streets stretch like taffy. The clock’s ticking grows louder, not from above but from inside your chest, each second a soft, steady pulse. Just as you reach the tower’s glowing doors, they swing open on their own, revealing a long table filled with people you somehow know—though you’ve never met them. At the head sits a version of you, wearing exactly what you’re wearing, leaning forward to whisper: “The meeting was always about arriving here.”

The doors begin to close. The city folds like paper. You step through, the floor drops away, and you wake with a start—still standing, still uncertain, and still holding a small paper crane in your hand.
