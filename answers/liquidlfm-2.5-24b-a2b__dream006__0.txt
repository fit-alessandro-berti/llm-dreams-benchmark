You are about to give a speech to a large audience. As you step onto the stage, you see the crowd looking expectantly at you, and you begin to feel the air thicken with a soft, humming glow. The words you intended to utter start as a whisper, then swell into a chorus of silver threads that drift upward, weaving themselves into the rafters of the hall. Each thread carries a fragment of a story: a child’s laughter, a distant ocean’s sigh, the rustle of pages turning in a forgotten book.

Your voice, though unsteady at first, finds its rhythm. It does not echo off walls but ripples outward like water over smooth stones. The audience watches, their eyes widening as the syllables transform into tiny lanterns that float above their heads, casting warm, amber light on the faces around you. In the flickering glow, the faces shift—each person’s features dissolve into the silhouettes of the things they love most: a baker kneading dough, a painter splashing color, a scientist peering through a microscope, a child chasing fireflies.

You notice that the expectations you sensed earlier are not a weight but a gentle pressure, like the gentle push of wind against a sail. The crowd’s anticipation becomes a current that lifts you, and you realize that the speech you were meant to give is not a lecture on a subject, but an invitation to remember the wonder that lives inside every dream.

As you continue, the stage itself begins to change. The polished wood beneath your feet turns to a field of midnight‑blue grass, and the podium melts into a river of ink that flows gently across the floor. The ink rises, forming letters that swirl around you, spelling out the very essence of your message: *“We are the storytellers of our own night‑time voyages.”* The letters dissolve into fireflies that settle on the shoulders of the listeners, each one a tiny beacon of hope.

A sudden hush falls over the hall, but it is not silence—it is the sound of a heartbeat, steady and ancient, resonating through the collective unconscious of everyone present. You feel the pulse sync with your own, and for a moment you are both speaker and audience, the speaker and the spoken, the dreamer and the dream.

Then, just as the last lantern flickers out, a soft, golden light spills through the ceiling, breaking the illusion like sunrise through a veil. The audience’s forms solidify once more, their eyes meeting yours with a new understanding. You step down from the stage, the floor beneath you turning back into polished wood, the river of ink receding into the darkness of the night.

You awaken, the taste of the dream lingering on your tongue—a reminder that the stage of life is ever‑changing, and that the true speech we give is the one we write in the language of our own dreams. The expectation you once felt has transformed into a quiet confidence, a promise that whenever you step into the spotlight again, the audience will always be ready to listen, because they, too, are dreaming.
