You decide to stop trying to win. Instead, you take a breath and say the small, tender truth underneath the anger. As you speak, the room softens—the walls turn to curtains, the floor to grass. Your friend’s face flickers, and for a moment you see them as they were when you first met. They say, “I know,” but their voice comes from behind you, even though their lips don’t move. You turn and see a younger version of both of you sitting under a table, sharing a secret. Then the dream thins, and you wake with the argument unresolved but with the quiet, urgent feeling that you should call them before the day ends.
