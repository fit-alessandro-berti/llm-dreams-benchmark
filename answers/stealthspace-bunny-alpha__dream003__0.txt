The box has no keyhole—only a shallow impression shaped like your palm.

When you press your hand against it, the symbols flare with warmth. Something clicks inside, softly, like a memory being put back where it belongs. You hear laughter from a summer you cannot quite remember, the hush of waves at night, and a voice saying, “You’ve been looking for the wrong thing.”

The lid opens by itself.

Inside is a pool of perfectly still darkness. When you look into it, you see a room filled with afternoon sunlight. Someone sits beside the window, reading a book. Their face is hidden, but you know, with absolute certainty, that they have been expecting you.

They turn a page and speak without looking up.

“You can’t open what you are still holding.”

You try to ask what they mean, but the sunlight folds inward. The room disappears. The figures, the stars, and the roads drawn across the patterns all begin to spin slowly, circling the empty space where your reflection should be.

Then you understand: the box has always been open.

You have simply been afraid to look inside.

You wake with your hands cupped around something small and warm. For a moment, you could swear it is humming—and that the sound is your own breathing, echoing from somewhere deep within.
