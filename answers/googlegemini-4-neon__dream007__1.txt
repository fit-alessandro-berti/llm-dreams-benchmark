You know you’re right. The argument is about something deeply, urgently important—something about a glass jar containing last October, and which one of you left it on the train—and the frustration in your chest is so thick it feels like trying to breathe underwater. You spin on your heel and march toward the front door of the apartment, intending to slam it hard enough to rattle the windows.

Except when you shove it open, it’s the heavy metal exit door of your old high school, and instead of a hallway, you step straight out onto a narrow wooden pier stretching over an endless, ink-black lake. 

Your friend follows you out, still talking fast and gesturing sharply, though their voice sounds strangely muffled, as if it’s traveling through a wall. You whip around to deliver the final, undeniable point that will settle everything once and for all. You draw a sharp breath and open your mouth to shout—but no sound comes out. Instead, a rush of dry autumn leaves spills from your lips, fluttering down and landing silently on the glassy water.

Panic flares right alongside your anger. You try again, pointing frantically at the floating leaves, desperate to make them understand: *Look! It’s right there! How can you not see it?*

Your friend stops mid-sentence. For a flickering second, their face blurs into someone you haven't spoken to in years before snapping back into focus. All the heat drains out of them at once, replaced by a sudden, quiet sadness. They look down at the leaves drifting across the black water, then reach into the pocket of an oversized winter coat they definitely weren’t wearing a moment ago.

They pull out a small, softly glowing brass key and press it into your palm.

The moment your fingers close around it, neither of you can remember what you were fighting about—only that it felt unbearable a second ago, and now the weight is completely gone. Above you, the sky shifts into a bright, impossible shade of violet. Without saying another word, you both sit down on the edge of the pier, letting your sneakers dangle just above the warm water, waiting together for a city bus that you somehow both know is about to drive right across the lake to pick you up.
