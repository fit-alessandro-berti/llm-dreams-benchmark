You are at a large social gathering where you only know one person. As you try to find them, you encounter various groups of people and—

...the room keeps stretching. The ceilings rise higher and higher with every step you take, and the music from the party starts to slow down, becoming deeper, more distant. You pass a cluster of people laughing around a table, but their laughter is delayed, echoing back at you in slow ripples like stones dropped in honey.



You call your friend's name — Sarah, was it Sarah? — and the sound comes out as a long, humming vowel that the room swallows entirely. A woman in a red dress turns to you and says, *"She just went into the kitchen,"* but you've already seen the kitchen, and it leads to another room, and that room has no ceiling at all — just stars.

The people in this new room are all wearing masks, but you can tell they're smiling underneath them. One of them offers you a drink that tastes like your childhood — wet grass and sunlight and the feeling of being called home for dinner.

You ask again: *"Have you seen Sarah?"*

Everyone points in a different direction.

You follow the first hallway and find yourself in your own bedroom. Everything is exactly where you left it, except the mirror on the wall is showing the party — and there, surrounded by strangers, is your friend. She's looking for you too. She's holding a drink and her eyes are wide with the same searching panic.

You knock on the mirror. She knocks back.

But now the hallway behind you is gone. You're standing in the bedroom and the mirror is just a mirror — your face staring back at you, slightly too late, as if it's trying to catch up.

Your phone buzzes. A text from Sarah: *"Where did you go?"*

And you wake up, still holding a glass you didn't bring to bed. It's warm. It tastes like sunlight and wet grass.

The alarm goes off.
