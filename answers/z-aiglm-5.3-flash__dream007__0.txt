…walk away before you say something you can't take back.

You turn toward the door — but the door is no longer behind you. It stands in the middle of the room, freestanding, glowing faintly at its edges. You open it anyway, because in dreams you always open the door, and step out into your childhood kitchen.

Your friend is already there, sitting at the table, still arguing — but their voice comes out of the refrigerator's hum, out of the faucet's drip, out of the neighbor's dog barking on the lawn. The words scatter. You catch fragments: *after everything*, *you always do this*, *I was there*.

You walk. The kitchen becomes a street becomes a shoreline. The argument follows you, performed by strangers: a woman at a bus stop says your name with your friend's inflection; the radio in a passing car finishes your sentence for you.

Then — with the sudden, terrible clarity that only comes in dreams — you realize you cannot remember what the argument was about. You stop. You turn around to say so, to laugh about it maybe, to make it right — but your friend is gone. On the sand where they stood lies a photograph of the two of you at sixteen, soaked through, the ink beginning to bleed. You kneel to pick it up. The faces in the photograph are still arguing. Their mouths are moving.

You wake with your jaw aching, clenched for hours, and for one long moment you lie still in the dark, trying to remember whether the two of you ever made up — or whether you're still standing on that shore, holding the photograph, watching the ink run.
