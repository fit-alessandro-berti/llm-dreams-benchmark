You are at a large social gathering where you only know one person. As you try to find them, you encounter various groups of people and each group seems to be waiting for you to recognize them.

One circle is laughing softly, their faces lit by a lamp that has no cord. When you step closer, their laughter turns into the sound of rain against a window. Someone hands you a note that says, “They were just here.”

You move on. Another group is speaking in languages you almost understand—one word sounds like your childhood nickname, another like a song you forgot on purpose. They part politely to let you pass, and behind them is a hallway that shouldn’t exist in the house.

At the end of the hall, you see the person you came looking for. They are standing near a doorway, talking to someone whose face keeps changing like a page being turned. You call their name, but your voice comes out as music. They turn toward you and smile, not with surprise, but relief.

“I’ve been trying to find you too,” they say. “But every time I find someone familiar, they become a memory instead.”

Then the gathering begins to dissolve—not violently, but gently, like breath fogging glass. The guests fold into coats, coats fold into shadows, shadows fold into silence. Only you and the one person remain, standing in an empty room that now feels like the inside of a thought.

They look at you and ask, “Do you think we found each other, or did the dream finally let us go?”

Before you can answer, you wake up with the strange, tender feeling that you were the person you were searching for all along.
