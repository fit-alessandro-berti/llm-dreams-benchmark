You feel a storm approaching and the air grows thick and electric, tasting of metal and of rain that hasn't fallen yet. Every bird goes silent at once, as though the forest is holding its breath.

The first drop that touches your skin is warm — strangely warm, like a tear that belongs to someone else. Then the sky flashes violet and you realize something impossible: the rain is falling *upward*. Droplets lift from the forest floor and drift past your face, carrying the scent of pine and old books into the clouds.

You try to run, but your legs push through air turned to honey. Behind you, the trees begin to whisper — not your name exactly, but something close to it, something that makes you think of being called home for dinner as a child. You turn, and there between two ancient oaks stands a door. Just an ordinary wooden door, alone in the clearing, slightly ajar, with warm golden light spilling out of its frame.

You know — with that unshakable certainty that only exists in dreams — that someone you've been missing is on the other side.

The thunder rolls in, but as it travels the sound bends and stretches until it isn't thunder at all. It's a lullaby, half-remembered, hummed in a voice you can't place. Lightning splits the tallest tree, and in the flash of white you see them: silhouettes of everyone you've ever loved, standing just inside the doorway, waiting the way dream-people wait — patient, flickering, unbothered by time.

You cross the clearing. Your hand finds the cold brass handle.

The moment you step through, everything is quiet and green again. Sunlight falls gently through the leaves. The peaceful forest, exactly as it was — except now a single gray feather rests on the path in front of you, and you understand somehow that you carried it through the storm.

Then you're awake. Your ceiling. Your room. Rain against the window — real rain this time, falling the proper direction — and on your nightstand, where you're certain you left nothing, a single gray feather.

Somewhere, you're almost sure, the rain is still falling upward.
