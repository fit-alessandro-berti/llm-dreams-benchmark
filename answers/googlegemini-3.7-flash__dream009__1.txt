The air smells intensely of damp cedar, crushed sage, and the sharp tang of woodsmoke. Above you, the sky isn't masked by city glow or satellite trails, but stretched wide and obsidian, dusted with a terrifying, breathtaking density of stars. There is no background hum of servers, no electric current vibrating in the ground—only the profound, heavy silence of the earth, punctuated by the rhythmic chirping of unseen insects and the distant clatter of iron against stone.

You look down at your hands, coated in fine river dust, and realize that your first task is survival in its most elemental form. You need to find clean water before the sun rises fully, decipher the constellations overhead to know where you stand, and, most importantly, find a way to communicate. 

Following a narrow dirt trail worn smooth by barefoot travelers and wagon wheels, you crest a low hill. In the valley below, torches flicker outside a cluster of timber-framed dwellings beside a winding river. You step forward into the cool grass, realizing that without data, maps, or machines, your only guide through this forgotten world will be human curiosity, intuition, and the ancient art of listening.
