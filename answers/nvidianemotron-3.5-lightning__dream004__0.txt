The canvas pulsed in time with the world’s restless rhythm, its surface a void that held the echo of every color and shape that had flickered across the landscape. You didn’t reach for a brush—the dream refused tools—but placed a hand upon the white, and immediately the swirling vortexes of magenta, ochre, and indigo that surrounded you coiled inward, drawn by an invisible current. They didn’t paint a picture so much as they organized: the chaos folded into a luminent spiral staircase rising into a sky of impossible geometry, each step a memory of a color you’d only glimpsed in passing. As the final hue locked into place, the ever-changing landscape stilled. The shapes solidified into floating islands of glass, the colors settling into a warm, perpetual twilight. The canvas, now alight with a single, self-sustaining pattern, reflected not the world, but a version of you standing at the top of the staircase, arms open, smiling as the dream shifted from creation to quiet. You took a step forward—and woke up, the taste of cold morning air on your tongue, the image of that spiral still humming behind your eyelids.
