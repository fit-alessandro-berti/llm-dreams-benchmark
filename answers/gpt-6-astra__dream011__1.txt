Not the handwriting you have now, but the crooked, oversized letters you used when you were six.

**1. What did you want to be before you knew what a career was?**

You glance around. Everyone else is writing furiously. The person beside you has filled three pages with tiny drawings of ladders.

**2. Show your working for the last time you said yes when you meant no.**

Your pencil feels warm. A small green leaf has sprouted near the eraser.

You raise your hand.

The examiner approaches wearing your coat. They look older than you, and very tired.

“Is there a mistake?” you whisper.

“Usually,” they say. “Which one have you found?”

You point at the final question.

**3. Describe your future without using the names of any jobs.**

“I didn’t study this.”

“No,” the examiner says. “You studied everything else.”

The clock above the door begins ticking backward. Around you, the other students turn their papers over, revealing maps, grocery lists, letters they never sent. Someone at the back laughs, then quickly covers their mouth.

You look down at your pencil. Roots are curling between your fingers.

On the blank page, you write:

*A kitchen with sunlight in it.*

Then:

*Work I can leave at the end of the day.*

You pause before adding:

*Someone I’m glad to see.*

The examiner reads over your shoulder.

“Will that be enough?” you ask.

They pull up a chair beside you.

“I was hoping you’d know.”

For a while, neither of you writes. Through a crack in the desk, your pencil sends up another leaf. The examiner touches it carefully, then takes off your coat and hands it back.
