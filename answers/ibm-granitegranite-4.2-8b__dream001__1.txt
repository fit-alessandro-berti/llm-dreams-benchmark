You start to walk—not with panic, but with that quiet, automatic stride you have in dreams when urgency feels oddly calm. The city hums in low, shifting tones: distant chimes that sound like glass bottles tapping, the faint smell of wet stone and burnt sugar, streets that seem to ripple slightly underfoot as if the pavement breathes. You glance down. Your watch face is blank—no numbers, just a smooth, cool curve. You frown, touch it; it feels warm now, pulsing faintly like a slow heartbeat.

You pass a café with no sign, just a single door made of frosted glass. Inside, through the glass, you see people sitting at tables, but their faces are softly blurred, as if viewed through water. One person lifts a cup; steam rises in perfect, slow spirals that don’t curl downward—they rise straight up into the ceiling of the café, vanishing. No one notices you.

You keep walking. A streetlamp flickers, but instead of light, it emits a single, clear note—a single, low C—every few seconds. The note lingers in the air, vibrating just enough to make the nearby leaves on a tiny, impossible tree tremble. The tree has no leaves yet; it holds only thin, glowing threads that shift color with each note—blue when the C sounds, gold when it fades.

You realize: *you don’t need to find the building.* The meeting isn’t *in* a place you locate. It’s the moment you notice the streetlamp’s note. It’s the way the café’s blurred faces aren’t looking at anything specific. It’s the feeling in your chest—not fear, but a small, clear sense of *arrival*, like remembering a song you hummed as a child.

You stop. You don’t search. You take one slow breath. The blank watch on your wrist glows faintly, just for a second, the color of the café’s threads when they shift to gold. Then it returns to smooth white. You smile, not because you know where, but because you finally understand: the meeting was the act of noticing the note, the ripple underfoot, the quiet hum. It’s happening *now*, here, in this step.

You take another step—not toward a sign or a landmark, but simply forward, listening to the next soft C-note rise from the lamp ahead. The city feels vast still, but no longer unfamiliar. It feels *held*, like a room you’ve always known you might step into. The hour doesn’t feel tight; it feels exactly right.
