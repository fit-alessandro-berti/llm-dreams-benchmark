...written in your own handwriting.

Not just a similar hand, but yours, with the lopsided loops and the sevens you cross out of habit. You flip through the booklet, and every question is something you once asked yourself and never answered.

*Question 1: What did you mean to say on the porch that evening, before the screen door closed?*

*Question 2: Show your work: how did you get from there to here?*

You look up. The exam hall has stretched while you weren't watching. The rows of desks run back until they vanish into fog, and the other students are all bent over their papers, writing furiously. The proctor at the front is your third-grade teacher, except she is also somehow a heron, and this strikes you as perfectly reasonable. The clock above her has hands, but no numbers, and the hands are moving in different directions.

You try to write, but your pencil has become a sprig of rosemary. You press it to the page anyway, and it leaves a faint green line that smells like your grandmother's kitchen.

*Question 3: The window is open. Why are you still sitting down?*

You hadn't noticed the window. It's right beside your desk, tall and bright, and through it there's no parking lot or campus, only a field of tall grass under a sky the color of early morning. A warm wind lifts the corner of your exam booklet.

The heron-teacher looks at you. She doesn't seem angry. She tilts her head, as if she's been waiting a long time for you to read that far.

You stand. Your chair makes no sound. None of the other students look up, and as you pass them you see that their pages are blank and their pencils aren't touching the paper. They've been waiting too.

You put one hand on the windowsill. The rosemary is still in your other hand. Behind you, very softly, you hear the heron-teacher mark something on her clipboard, and you know, the way you know things in dreams, that it's a passing grade.

You climb through. The grass comes up to your waist, and somewhere ahead, someone is calling your name as if they're glad you finally came.
