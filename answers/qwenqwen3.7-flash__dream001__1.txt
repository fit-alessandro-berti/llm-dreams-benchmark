...open the compass on your wrist, but there is no dial—only a single, pulsating blue vein of light embedded in your skin. It doesn't point north; it points *down*, vibrating against your pulse with a rhythm that matches a song you haven't heard since childhood.

Panic spikes, sharp and electric, but as soon as you focus on it, the emotion dissolves into curiosity. You try to run toward the nearest skyscraper, a monolith of black glass, but your body moves with fluid, underwater grace. You take a step and glide effortlessly over the cobblestones, which ripple like the surface of a pond with every stride.

A crowd gathers around you, but they have no faces—only mirrors where features should be. They don't speak; instead, they reflect fragments of your own memories back at you: a broken toy, a locked diary, a sunset over a ocean that shouldn't exist. Their reflections whisper in unison, *"The meeting is already here."*

You check the time again. Your phone screen is gone. In its place, a living moth made of parchment wings flutters out of the device, spelling out letters in its flight pattern before landing on your finger. It whispers in a voice that sounds exactly like yours: *"Look for the door without walls."*

The moths swarm upward, parting the thick, violet fog that clings to the streetlamps. Through the haze, you see it: a freestanding frame of twisted iron rising from the middle of the intersection. Beyond it, space folds inward, revealing a corridor lined with floating staircases that ascend into a sky filled with drifting islands of rock and lush jungle.

The vibration in your wrist intensifies, warm now, almost comforting. You realize the destination isn't a place you have to travel to; it's a choice you have to make. The doors of the frame creak open, invitingly ajar.

From the darkness beyond, a figure steps forward. It wears your coat, your boots, and your face, but its eyes are galaxies spinning in silent loops. It extends a hand, holding a key that glows with the same blue light as the vein on your arm.

*"You're late,"* the figure says, smiling sadly. *"But you brought what we needed."*

The figure offers the key. Around you, the mirror-people lean closer, their reflections showing you versions of yourself who never took this path, who stayed safe and still. The galaxy-eyes blink, waiting.

You realize waking up isn't possible anymore. The dream demands entry.

Do you take the key?
