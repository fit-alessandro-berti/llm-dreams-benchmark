As you step closer, the surface begins to breathe—softly, like fog lifted by a hidden wind. Colors rise from within it, not painted but remembered: the blue of a voice you once loved, the gold of an afternoon you never lived, the green of a door that opened only in dreams.

You reach out, and your fingertips do not touch canvas. Instead, they sink into warm light. The world around you folds inward, shapes melting into music, colors becoming words you can almost understand. A brush appears in your hand, though it is made of feathers, glass, or perhaps nothing at all.

When you make the first stroke, the sky answers.

A line of silver sweeps across the dreaming landscape, and wherever it touches, new paths appear—staircases curling into clouds, rivers flowing upward, flowers opening into tiny glowing moons. You understand, without being told, that the canvas is not asking you to create a picture. It is asking you to choose what is real.

So you paint.

You paint a doorway. You paint a heartbeat. You paint the feeling of waking just before the dream disappears. The canvas trembles, and the world begins to dissolve around it, colors swirling down into your hand like liquid stars.

When the dream is almost gone, only the canvas remains.

And on it, in shimmering, ever-changing letters, are the words:

**“Begin again.”**
