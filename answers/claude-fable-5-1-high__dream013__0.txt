You are in a maze filled with complex puzzles. Each puzzle requires intense concentration and logical thinking to solve, and as you move deeper into the maze, you begin to notice that the puzzles are getting easier, not harder, which is somehow much worse.

The first one took you what felt like hours. A wall of brass dials, each stamped with a letter, and you had to turn them until the letters spelled the name of a river you had never heard of but somehow knew. The second was a floor of tiles that sank when you stepped wrong, and you had to hum the right interval to make them hold. The third was a door that asked you a riddle, and the riddle was about your mother's hands, and you answered it correctly and felt sick.

Now the puzzles are things like: a table with two cups, one empty, one full. A sign that says CHOOSE. You pick the full one and the wall slides open. You are almost disappointed. You wanted to be tested. You realize you have been walking for a long time and the corridors have stopped turning. They go straight now, and the light is the color of old paper, and the puzzles come every twenty steps or so, laid out on little tables like refreshments.

A lock with the key already in it.

A scale with a feather on one side and a feather on the other.

A knot that is not tied.

You solve each one anyway, because that is what the maze is for, and each time a wall recedes with a sound like someone exhaling through their nose. Somewhere behind you, faintly, you hear other footsteps, and you understand without turning around that they are your own footsteps arriving late.

Then a puzzle that is just a mirror. Nothing else. No sign, no instructions. You stand in front of it and wait for the trick. You look at your reflection and it is looking slightly to the left of you, as if there is someone standing there. You check. There is no one. You look back and the reflection is now looking directly at you, and it looks tired, and you understand that it has solved this puzzle already, from the other side, and is waiting for you to catch up.

You put your hand on the glass. It is warm, like a forehead.

The wall does not slide open. Instead the floor behind you does, quietly, and you turn to see that the corridor you came down is gone. There is only the mirror, and a small table beside it with a pencil and a piece of paper on it. The paper says, in your own handwriting: YOU ARE DESIGNING THE NEXT ONE.

You pick up the pencil. You do not remember deciding to. You begin to draw a maze, and the maze you draw is this one, and you are careful, very careful, to make the early puzzles difficult, because you remember how good that felt, how alive, and you make the later ones easy because you are tired now and you cannot think of anything hard anymore, and you leave a mirror near the end because you know someone will need to be told.

When you are finished you look up and the corridor has returned, but it is running in the other direction now, back toward the brass dials and the sinking tiles and the door that knows about your mother. You can hear footsteps coming down it. Your own footsteps, arriving on time at last.

You fold the paper. You put it in your pocket. You sit down at the little table beside the mirror, and you pour the full cup into the empty one, and you wait, with the patience of someone who has already been solved.
