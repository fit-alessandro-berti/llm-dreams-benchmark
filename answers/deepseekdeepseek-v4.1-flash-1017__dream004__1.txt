…and as you touch it, the canvas ripples like water. Your fingertips leave trails of impossible colors—colors that have no names, only feelings. The blank whiteness begins to bloom: first a spiral of violet light, then a forest of glass trees growing upward through the paint, their leaves chiming softly whenever the sky changes shape.

You realize the canvas is not waiting for you to paint it. It is painting you. Each brushstroke appears from your memories: a half-remembered face, a staircase from a childhood dream, a door you were always afraid to open. The shapes drift off the canvas and into the landscape, becoming hills, rivers, birds made of folded paper and flickering lightning.

Then the canvas turns transparent. Behind it is another canvas, and behind that one, another, each holding a slightly different version of you. One is laughing. One is made of rain. One is still waiting for a train that will never arrive. They all raise a brush at once.

You reach forward to choose a color, but the color chooses you first. It floods your hands, your arms, your chest, until you are part of the painting—bright, unfinished, and free. The last thing you hear is the soft scratch of a brush against the sky, signing your name in a language you almost understand.

Then you wake with paint beneath your fingernails and the faint feeling that somewhere, a blank canvas is still dreaming of you.
