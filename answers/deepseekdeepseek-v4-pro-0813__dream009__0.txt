You realize you need to find shelter before the light fails. The air is thick with woodsmoke and damp hay, and the path under your bare feet is packed earth, still warm from a sun that feels smaller and older than the one you know. Your clothes are not your own: a coarse wool tunic, a leather cord at your waist, a small pouch containing only dried lavender and a smooth black stone.

You follow the sound of a river, then a bell tolling somewhere beyond the trees. It leads you to a village of low stone houses with thatched roofs, where people glance at you without surprise. An old woman at a well hands you a clay cup of water and says, “You’re just in time. The dream is thinning.” You don’t understand until you look at your hands and see the edges of your fingers beginning to blur, like ink in water.

You try to speak, but your voice comes out as the bell, ringing once across the valley. The village shivers, the sky folds, and you wake with the taste of woodsmoke in your mouth and a smooth black stone on your pillow.
