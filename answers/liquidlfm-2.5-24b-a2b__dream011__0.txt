You are about to take the final exam for a course that determines your future career. As you notice the questions are written in a luminous, ever‑shifting script that seems to breathe under the fluorescent lights, each line rearranging itself the moment you try to read it. The first query glows on the page: “If you could rewrite the rules of any profession, which would you choose and why?” Beneath it, a second line flickers: “Describe the taste of a memory you have never lived.” A third, more mathematical, appears: “Find the value of x such that the sum of its digits equals the number of letters in your name.”

The exam hall is not a typical classroom. The walls are made of translucent glass that reflects not your face but a dozen alternate versions of yourself—some older, some younger, some wearing robes of scholars, others clad in work gear. The desk beneath you feels warm, as if it were alive, and the pen you pick up writes in silver ink that leaves faint constellations on the paper. A soft hum fills the air, like distant wind chimes, and the clock on the wall ticks backward, each second echoing a different future you might have had.

As you begin to write, the ink forms tiny shapes—birds, gears, and tiny trees—that flutter off the page and settle on the margins. One bird lands on the question about the taste of an unseen memory, and you realize the answer is not a word but a feeling: the sweetness of a sunrise you never saw. The equation about your name resolves into a number that matches the number of times you have ever doubted yourself.

When the final question appears, it is simply: “What do you truly want to become?” The ink swirls, forming a mirror that shows you not the person you think you are, but the person you are becoming. You feel a surge of clarity, then a gentle tug, as if the dream is pulling you back to the present.

You blink, and the exam disappears, replaced by the familiar cover of your textbook. The hallway outside the exam room is quiet, the fluorescent lights steady, and you step out, wondering whether the answers you gave—or the ones you didn’t—will shape the path ahead.
