You start to drift down a narrow, cobblestone lane that seems to stretch farther than any map could chart. The air is thick with the scent of rain‑kissed stone and distant incense, and the streetlights above flicker in a rhythm that feels almost like a heartbeat. Your mind races—an hour is slipping away, and the meeting you’re supposed to attend is still a mystery. 

A sudden gust pushes a cascade of silver‑white leaves across your path, and as they settle, a small, weathered sign emerges on a cracked wall: “Lost & Found – Inquiries Welcome.” You pause, half‑expecting the sign to dissolve into mist, but it stays, its letters glowing faintly. Beneath it, a tiny brass compass lies half‑buried in the gutter, its needle spinning wildly before finally pointing toward a distant tower that wasn’t there a moment ago. 

You pick up the compass, feeling its cold metal pulse against your palm. The city seems to respond: the buildings shift subtly, their windows reflecting fragments of conversations you’ve never heard, and the sound of distant chimes grows louder, marking the passage of time. A street vendor, draped in a coat woven from midnight sky, calls out, “If you seek the place where ideas meet, follow the echo of the bell that never rings.” 

Curiosity outweighs panic. You follow the echo—a low, resonant hum that seems to emanate from the heart of the city’s oldest district. The streets narrow into a courtyard where a massive clock tower looms, its face cracked and half‑covered in ivy. At the top, a single golden door stands ajar, and from within drifts a warm, amber light that feels oddly familiar. 

As you step closer, the compass needle steadies, now pointing directly at the door. You push it open and find yourself standing in a grand hall lined with towering bookshelves that reach up to a vaulted ceiling. In the center, a round table is set with a single chair facing a window that looks out onto the same city you just left—only now the skyline is a tapestry of both old stone and shimmering glass, as if the metropolis itself is a living document of your thoughts. 

A figure seated at the table turns—a version of yourself, older, eyes bright with knowing. “You’ve been looking for the meeting,” the double says, “but the meeting was always the space between the questions you ask.” The room hums with a quiet certainty, and the ticking of the unseen clock syncs with the steady beat of your own heart. You realize the hour has passed, yet the urgency has dissolved into a calm clarity: the true gathering was the moment you chose to navigate the unknown, and the answer was already within you. 

You take a seat, the chair warm beneath you, and the city outside continues its endless dance, while inside the hall, the meeting begins—not with a presentation, but with the quiet conversation you’ve always been having with yourself.
