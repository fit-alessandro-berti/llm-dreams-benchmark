As you try to find them, you encounter various groups of people and catch only fragments of conversations that seem to bend backward the longer you listen. A circle near the balcony discusses libraries that haven't been built yet, their words dissolving into the scent of old paper and ozone. Nearby, someone passes around glasses that refill themselves with a liquid that looks like liquid twilight, and every time it touches your lips, you briefly remember a room you've never seen but somehow recognize. 

You move through the crowd like a needle finding a seam, guided by the feeling of a familiar laugh you heard just seconds ago—or maybe hours. Time here doesn't march; it pools. Balloon animals drift past like slow jellyfish. A string quartet plays a melody that sounds exactly like rain against a window you once slept beside. You keep turning corners, but the architecture keeps folding back on itself: a coat check that's now a bookshelf, a snack table that's now a small reflecting pool, all of it shimmering with the quiet logic of a dream.

Then, across a sudden opening in the crowd, you catch the exact cadence of their voice. You follow it past a doorway that shouldn't be there, through a hall lined with watches all ticking at different speeds, until you find them standing where the floor turns to warm sand. They look up, exactly as they did the moment before you fell asleep, and smile like they've been expecting you. 

*"You took the long way,"* they say, handing you a paper cup that steams with something that smells like your favorite season. You take it. The room steadies. Someone calls your name from very far away, or maybe from right beside you. The lights soften. And just as you're about to ask how you got here, the dream begins to unspool at the edges, leaving you with the quiet certainty that, for once, you weren't lost at all.
