You wake up in a completely different era, with no modern technology around. As you explore your new surroundings, you realize you need to **remember who you are before the dream forgets you.**

Here's how the dream continues:

---

You wake up on a hard straw mattress, not your bed. The air is cold and smells of woodsmoke and damp earth. Sunlight cuts through a small, warped window with no glass - just wooden shutters creaking in the wind.

You sit up. You're in a small cottage with a thatched roof. Your clothes are wrong. Heavy, rough linen. Wool that itches at your neck. Your hands look like your hands, but there's dirt under the nails that wasn't there when you fell asleep.

No hum of an AC. No glow of a phone. No cars outside. Just the distant sound of a rooster, a bell tolling, and horses' hooves on packed dirt.

You push the wooden door open and step outside.

It's a village. Not one from a movie - one that feels *real*. Muddy lanes, timber-framed houses leaning together, smoke curling from every roof. People in simple tunics and dresses stare as you pass. No one looks at a screen. A blacksmith hammers iron, the clang echoing through your chest. A child herds geese past you.

And then the strange part hits you - no one seems surprised to see you. In fact, an old woman by the well looks right at you and says:

"You're late. The letter still needs to go to the hill."

You look down, and only now do you notice you're clutching something tight in your fist. A sealed letter, yellowed parchment, closed with dark red wax. Your name isn't on it. No address either. Just a single symbol pressed into the wax: a spiral, like a sleeping eye.

You *know*, with that absolute dream-logic certainty, that you need to deliver this letter. If you don't, something will go wrong. The bell keeps tolling, but it never seems to count the right hour. The sky is a strange, too-vivid blue, like a painting.

You start walking toward the hill at the edge of the village, where a lone stone tower stands against the clouds. With every step, the details get sharper and stranger:

* The people you pass have no shadows.
* Your own footsteps make no sound in the mud.
* And when you try to remember your phone number, your address, your last name - it starts to slip away, like trying to hold water.

You realize the true task isn't just delivering the letter.

You need to deliver it *before you forget why you came here*. Before you forget that you don't belong in this era. Before the dream convinces you that you've always lived in this cottage, with these clothes, in this time.

The tower door is open. Inside, it's dark. Someone is waiting at the top.

You hold the letter tighter and start to climb, your heart pounding, not sure if you're about to wake up or fall even deeper into the dream.

---

Do you want to open the letter before you deliver it, or deliver it sealed and see who is waiting at the top of the tower?
