...you realize you need to find water before the sun sets, though you have no idea where you are.

The ground beneath you is soft with moss, and the air smells of woodsmoke and wet stone. Towering oaks rise around you, their branches so thick they hide the sky. Somewhere ahead, you hear a bell ringing, slow and deep, and you follow it through a tangle of ferns until the trees thin out.

Below you lies a village of timber houses with thatched roofs, their chimneys breathing gray smoke. Women in long wool dresses carry baskets along a dirt path. A boy herds geese past a well, and an old man sits on a bench carving a piece of wood, pausing to watch you with mild curiosity, as if strangers arrive every morning.

Your throat is dry, so you walk toward the well. The rope is worn smooth, and the bucket hangs heavy with cold water. When you lift it, your reflection shimmers up at you, and for a moment it doesn't look like your face. It looks like someone who has lived here all along.

The old man speaks in a language you shouldn't understand, yet you do. "You came through the old stones at the crossroads," he says. "Everyone who does has a task to finish before the bell rings again. Yours is written on the paper in your pocket."

You reach into your coat, though you are not sure you were wearing a coat a moment ago. Inside is a folded parchment, its ink faded to brown. The first line reads: *Light the lantern in the tower before the bell tolls thirteen times.*

Far above, the church bell begins to ring, and you count the first stroke.
