…written in a language you’ve never learned, yet somehow you can understand every word.

The first question says:

**1. If you had to choose between being remembered and being happy, which door would you walk through, and why does the handle feel like your childhood?**

You look up, but the classroom is no longer a classroom. The walls are made of soft blue light, and the other students are faceless figures calmly writing with pens that drip stars instead of ink. The proctor stands at the front, wearing your own face, smiling gently.

You look back at the exam.

**2. Describe the moment you became who you are. You may not use words, only weather.**

Your hand begins to move on its own. You don’t write an answer. Instead, a small cloud forms above the page and starts to rain quietly onto your desk. The rain smells like a street you lived on years ago.

The proctor nods and says, “Good. You’re finally ready.”

Then the final question appears, larger than the page:

**3. What are you most afraid of becoming?**

As you stare at it, the blank space beneath the question begins to fill with a moving image: you, older, standing in a life you have not chosen yet. That version of you turns and whispers something without sound.

You lean closer.

And just before you understand, the bell rings, the paper folds itself into a bird, and it flies out the window toward the life it just showed you.
