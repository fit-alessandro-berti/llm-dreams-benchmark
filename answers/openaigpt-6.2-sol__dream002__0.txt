You feel a storm approaching and quicken your steps, though you can’t remember where the path is supposed to lead.

The first drop lands on the back of your hand. It’s warm. The second hangs in the air at eye level, trembling, and in it you see the kitchen of the house where you grew up. Someone has left the window open. The curtain breathes in and out.

Then the rain begins in earnest. You run until you come to a door standing by itself between two pines. There’s a brass key in the lock and a folded towel over the handle.

Inside, the kettle has just boiled.

You dry your hair and sit at the kitchen table. Beyond the open window, the trees sway and the ground darkens, but not a single drop comes in. You remember being small enough to think the thunder was furniture being moved upstairs. For a while, you listen to it that way again.
