You are walking through a peaceful forest when suddenly the weather changes dramatically. You feel a storm approaching and—

The air turns heavy, sweet, and electric, like the moment before a kiss or a scream. The leaves above you stop their gentle rustling and freeze in place, as if the entire forest has been holding its breath. When you look up, the canopy has become a cathedral of shadows, the sunlight filtering through in shafts of impossible gold that tremble and bend.

Then the wind arrives—not a breeze, but a voice. It moves through the trunks like a long exhalation, and the temperature drops so suddenly you can see your breath clouding in the suddenly dim air. The birds have gone utterly silent. Even the insects have vanished.

You begin to run, though you don't know why. Your feet barely seem to touch the moss. The path beneath you shifts and changes; stones become smooth river-wet pebbles, then sheets of bark, then something like skin that feels warm and alive. 

Ahead, lightning doesn't split the sky—it *unzips* it. A seam of white light tears open between the clouds, and instead of thunder, you hear music: a deep, resonant chord that vibrates in your sternum, in your teeth, in the marrow of your bones. Rain begins to fall, but it doesn't get you wet. The droplets hang suspended in the air like tiny prisms, each one containing a single, perfect image—a version of yourself standing in this same forest, but younger, older, reaching for something just out of frame.

You find shelter beneath the arch of an ancient tree whose bark spirals upward like a helix. Inside the hollow, the world quiets. Through the opening, you watch the storm become solid: great translucent walls of blue and violet that advance through the forest without breaking anything. Trees bend around them like grass in a current. Time moves strangely here—each raindrop that lands near your feet takes minutes to dissolve into the moss.

In the hollow, you are not afraid. The storm feels less like weather and more like an arrival. On the inner wall of the tree, light from the prismatic rain has begun to spell words that shift and rearrange before you can read them: *You are already inside,* they say, then, *Look up.*

You look. The canopy has parted. Above you hangs not a moon, but an eye—vast, luminous, and gentle, made of the same white light that unzipped the sky. It blinks. And as it does, you realize the eye is yours, and the forest is inside it, and the storm is the world you've been dreaming into being all along. 

The light expands, warm and total, until you are no longer watching the dream. You *are* the dream, waking.
