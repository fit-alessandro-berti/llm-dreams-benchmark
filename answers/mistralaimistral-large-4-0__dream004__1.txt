When your fingertip brushes the fabric, it doesn't feel like cloth but like warm liquid, and the canvas begins to pulse in rhythm with your heartbeat.

Suddenly, a brush materializes in your other hand, heavy with paint that shifts through every hue imaginable. You don't choose the color; the color chooses you. As you stroke the surface, the paint doesn't stay where you put it—it flows across the canvas, becoming a river that spills over the edges, dissolving the frame entirely. The world around you begins to match what you're painting: the sky turns the exact shade of the violet you just laid down, and the ground ripples like wet pigment, swallowing your footsteps.

Then you realize you are standing *inside* the painting, not before it. The "blank" canvas was never a surface to be filled, but a threshold that only opens when you stop trying to control what appears on it. The colors stop changing because you have finally become one of them—a shape shifting into another shape, your edges blurring into the aurora, dissolving into the dream, becoming the dream itself.
