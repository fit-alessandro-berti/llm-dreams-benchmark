open your laptop. The screen is blank except for a blinking cursor. With each blink, the office grows a little taller.

“I’ll need an extension,” you tell your boss.

She checks her watch. Its hands are spinning backward.

“You’ve already had one,” she says.

You begin typing anyway. Words spill across the screen, then lift off it like moths. Your coworkers gather around, holding out coffee cups to catch them. Someone whispers that the deadline is tomorrow. Someone else says tomorrow was yesterday.

At last, you type the final sentence. The office goes still. Your boss leans in to read it, and her expression softens.

“Perfect,” she says. “Now we just need it in a different format.”

Your laptop folds itself into a paper airplane and sails out the window. You chase it down the hall, through a door, and into your childhood bedroom, where the airplane lands on your pillow.

Your alarm clock reads 8:59.

You close your eyes for one second.
