You are given a mysterious locked box with no key in sight. As you examine it closely, you notice intricate patterns and a tiny figure etched into one corner. It takes you a moment to recognize yourself. Your pockets are turned inside out. The figure’s are, too.

“Have you tried knocking?” asks a voice behind you.

You knock twice. Someone inside knocks three times.

“That’s my room,” says a child’s voice. “You can come in, but don’t turn on the light.”

The lid opens onto your childhood bedroom. Your feet are already on the carpet, though you can still feel the box in your hands. Outside the window, rain hangs in the air, each drop perfectly still. The child in bed is you, but younger than you know how to remember.

“I was having a dream,” they say. “You were in it, but I couldn’t see your face.”

You sit on the edge of the bed. The child reaches for your hand and feels along your knuckles until they find the little scar you got falling off your bike.

“Oh,” they say, relieved. “You learned to ride it, then.”

You want to tell them about all the places you rode after that. But the rain begins to fall, and from somewhere downstairs comes the sound of your father, alive and looking for his keys.
