You are at your workplace, and suddenly, you are given a project with an impossible deadline. Your boss is waiting for you to start, and you glance at the clock—only to see that it has no hands.

You open the project folder. Inside, there are hundreds of blank pages, except for the first one, which reads: **“Finish before yesterday.”**

Your coworkers begin typing frantically, but their keyboards make the sound of ticking clocks. Each time you try to ask someone for help, their desk slides farther away. Your boss stands silently behind you, growing taller with every passing second, until their head disappears into the ceiling.

Panicking, you write the first sentence that comes to mind. The moment your pen touches the paper, the office walls peel away, revealing a vast railway station. Every train is labeled with a different deadline: **Five Minutes Ago**, **Right Now**, **Too Late**, and **Never**.

A conductor waves urgently from the last train. You grab the unfinished project and run, but the pages scatter behind you, transforming into white birds. Instead of chasing them, you suddenly stop.

You realize the project was never yours.

The towering boss shrinks back to normal size and asks, almost nervously, “Well? Aren’t you going to begin?”

You hand them the empty folder and say, “I already finished the most important part.”

“What part?”

“Deciding what is possible.”

The clocks stop ticking. The train marked **Right Now** opens its doors, and you step inside. As it begins to move, you look out the window and see your workplace becoming smaller and smaller—until it is nothing more than a tiny model sitting on your bedside table.

Then your alarm rings.
