You raise your voice above the wind and point, not at the waves, but at the one clear gap between the clouds.

“Helm, two points to starboard—keep her bow into the swells. No heroics, just steady. Mainsail reefed, staysails only. Everyone else: lines, not panic. If you can’t hear me, watch my hands.”

The deck pitches. Salt stings your eyes. For a moment the ship feels smaller than the dark, and the crew’s faces turn toward you as if you might invent a calmer ocean. You don’t. You walk the rail, check the lashings yourself, and set a watch rotation so no one stands alone too long.

“We don’t outrun this,” you say, quieter now, close enough that the nearest hands can carry it aft. “We ride it. Dawn is on the other side of this water. Hold the course. Hold each other. I’ll be on deck until the wind drops.”

Lightning splits the horizon. The ship climbs the next wave, and for the length of one breath the bow stays true—and that is enough to begin the rest of the night.
