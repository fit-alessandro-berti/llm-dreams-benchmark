**...plant a garden.**

It sounds foolish, even in the dream. The ground is gray and brittle, and the sky hangs low, the color of old dishwater. But in the pocket of your coat you find a single seed, small and dry and warm, as if it had been waiting for you.

You walk for what feels like days, though the sun never moves. You pass the skeletons of cars draped in vines that have turned to rust-colored lace. You pass a playground where the swings still sway, though there is no wind. Somewhere far off, something howls. You don't look back.

At the edge of a collapsed overpass, you find a cracked porcelain bathtub with a thin skin of rainwater in the bottom. You scrape together soil from ash and broken concrete, mixing in what you can, including coffee grounds from a tin, a handful of moss, and the last crumbs of your bread. You press the seed into the dirt and whisper something you can't remember afterward.

Then the dream does what dreams do. Time folds.

A green thread breaks the surface. Then two leaves. Then a stem as thick as your wrist, climbing the overpass, unfurling leaves as wide as doors. Pale blue flowers open along it, glowing faintly, and where their light touches the ground, the gray softens to brown.

Figures begin to appear at the edges of the light: a woman with a limp, a boy carrying a dented kettle, an old man leading a goat that has no business being alive. They don't speak. They kneel and begin to dig.

You realize the howling has stopped.

The last thing you see before waking is the vine's shadow stretching long across the ground. For the first time in that world, the sun has begun to set.

And you wake up with dirt under your fingernails.
