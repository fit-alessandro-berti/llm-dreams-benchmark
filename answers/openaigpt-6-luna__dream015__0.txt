Despite the hardships, you decide to plant a garden.

You find a cracked tin can, fill it with rainwater, and tuck three seeds into the dust behind a fallen wall. Each night, you guard them from the wind and the things that move beyond the firelight. Each morning, you expect nothing.

Then, one dawn, a green shoot breaks through the soil.

Word spreads. People come from the ruins carrying whatever they can spare: a handful of beans, a chipped cup, a story from before the world ended. Together, you build a fence, then a shelter, then a small town around the garden.

Years later, when someone asks how civilization began again, you point to the first sprout. But in the dream, it keeps growing—higher and higher—until its leaves fill the sky and cast a cool green shade over everything.
