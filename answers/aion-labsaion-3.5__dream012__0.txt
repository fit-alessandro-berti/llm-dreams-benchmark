...you grip the wheel with both hands, salt spray stinging your eyes, and shout orders into the howling wind. "Reef the mainsail! Secure the rigging!" Your voice sounds strange—too calm, as if it's coming from somewhere outside you.

The crew scrambles across the deck, but as you watch them, something odd happens. Their faces begin to change. The boatswain becomes your old school teacher. The young sailor climbing the rigging is your younger brother, though he's been grown for years. Dreams are like this—everyone you've ever known is somehow aboard.

The waves rise higher than any wave should, black mountains of water that pause impossibly at their crest, as if deciding whether to fall. Lightning splits the sky, and in that frozen white instant, you see the eye of the storm directly ahead—not a weather pattern, but an actual eye, vast and gray, watching you with something like curiosity.

"Captain," your first mate says, suddenly beside you though you didn't see him approach. He's dripping wet but his voice is perfectly clear. "The crew wants to know if we're turning back."

You look behind you. The sea behind is calm and golden, sunset-lit, dotted with islands. Ahead is only the eye and the darkness.

"We keep going," you hear yourself say, and the words feel less like a decision and more like a memory of one you made long ago.

The wheel grows warm under your hands now, almost alive. The ship surges forward into the heart of the storm, and instead of the crash you expect, the sea opens beneath you like a door. The water isn't water anymore—it's sky. The ship is sailing downward through clouds, the crew laughing now, pointing at stars that drift past like lanterns.

And just as the bow touches something bright at the bottom of it all—

—you wake, sheets tangled around your legs like rigging, the sound of rain against your window, and the faint, fading certainty that you left something important unfinished out there on the water.
