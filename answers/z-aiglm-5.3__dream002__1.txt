...the sky deepens to a green you've never seen while awake — the color of light through old bottle glass.

The birds stop singing all at once. In the sudden silence you can hear your own heartbeat, slow and unbothered, and you realize the strange thing about this storm: you're not afraid of it. Somehow you know it isn't coming for you. It's coming *for* the forest, which has been waiting for it.

The wind arrives first, moving through the canopy like something searching. The leaves begin to murmur — not words, exactly, but almost-words, a language you knew once and have since forgotten.

Then the rain breaks. For one impossible moment it falls upward, silver threads rising from the moss to meet the clouds, before remembering which way rain should go and crashing back down, warm as bathwater. Where the drops land, the ground glows faintly blue-white, as if something beneath the soil is waking up.

At the edge of your vision, a great tree splits open with a sound like a held breath released. Inside, where the wood should be, there's something else — but every time you turn to look, it slides just out of focus.

You lift your face to the sky and open your mouth, and the rain tastes like—

You wake up. Of course you wake up. Right at the taste.

You lie still in the dark for a long while afterward, lips parted, still trying to remember.
