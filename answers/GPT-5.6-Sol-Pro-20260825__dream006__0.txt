You begin to speak, but instead of words, a flock of tiny glowing birds flies from your mouth. They circle above the stage, arranging themselves into sentences you cannot read. The audience watches in complete silence, their faces shifting whenever you blink—first strangers, then childhood friends, former teachers, and people you have not seen in years.

You look down for your notes, but the pages are blank. Then a single sentence slowly appears in your handwriting:

**“Tell them what you have been afraid to say.”**

Your heart pounds. You step away from the podium and admit that you do not feel prepared, that you are terrified of disappointing everyone, and that sometimes you pretend to be certain when you are not. As you speak honestly, the glowing birds descend and settle along the edge of the stage. The enormous auditorium seems to shrink, and the crowd no longer feels intimidating.

Someone in the front row begins to applaud. Then another person joins, and soon the entire audience rises—not because your speech was perfect, but because it was true.

Suddenly, the stage floor becomes transparent. Beneath it, you see a vast night sky filled with stars. The audience fades away, leaving only one empty chair in the front row. Sitting on it is a younger version of you, smiling proudly.

You try to speak to them, but the microphone turns into an alarm clock, ringing louder and louder until you wake up—with the strange feeling that you have finally said something important.
