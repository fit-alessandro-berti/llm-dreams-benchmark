You dip it into the blue of a passing cloud and paint a door. Before you can add a handle, someone knocks from the other side.

“Come in,” you say, because you can’t think what else to say to a door you’ve just made.

It opens onto your childhood kitchen. Rain falls gently over the table, but the toast on your plate stays dry. At the far end sits an orange you remember refusing to eat. It has been waiting all these years, and when you peel it, the room fills with the smell of summer.

You carry a segment back through the door. Outside, the landscape has gone still. A hill is halfway through becoming a horse. The sky has settled on the pale green of a school corridor.

You set the orange segment on the blank canvas. The canvas folds around it like a hand.

Then, from somewhere inside the hill, a horse takes its first breath.
