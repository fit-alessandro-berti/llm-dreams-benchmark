...push yourself to stand. The last time I remember falling asleep, I was staring at a glowing screen, tapping a rhythm into a pocket-size rectangle. Now, the air is cool, humid, and smelling of damp earth and crushed mint. I’m lying on what feels like burlap rather than a mattress, my chest pressed against something firm and uneven.

I roll onto my side and try to ease myself up, my muscles protesting. When I sit, my head aches—not from exhaustion, but from a quiet, unfamiliar pressure. I am in a narrow stone room. A high arched window lets in golden light. Outside, there are no towers of glass and steel, no hum of engines or satellites. There are charred wooden beams, a low stone hearth, and a dense forest pressed right against the rock face.

I push myself up, my palms slipping on rough brick. My breath catches. I know this place. It is not exactly what I remember from history books, but it is close enough to make my stomach twist. I am standing in a workshop not far from a coastal town. There is a hearth, a workbench with worn planks, and a stack of paper that looks nothing like the digital world I left behind. I sit heavily on the floor, staring at the same object I used to stare at in dreams: my phone. It is still there. But it is dark. The screen is cracked. The battery is dead. It is just an object now, a heavy, silent stone in my hand.

“If I can’t reach them,” I whisper, “what do I do?”

A door exclaims a light, not from a bulb, but from a twisted piece of metal burning in the hearth. I push myself up. The air is thick with the scent of salt and iron. The forest outside hums with a low, insect-like sound, but I don’t hear engines. To the east, I see a patch of land where no one was before: a field of dried grain, a stone wall, a signpost carved with symbols that look like sun and river. In the distance, smoke rises in a steady, languid column. It is not the breath of fire, but of wood and patience.

I realize then that this is not a post-apocalyptic wasteland. The sun still rises. The crops still grow. But the rhythm has changed. There is no wireless signal. No cloud. No distant server waiting to answer. There is only what is here, in this room, in this forest, in this moment. And what’s here is not a void, but a question:

*What did I carry with me that doesn’t need a battery to keep working?*

I look toward the window. The day is far from done. I smile, slowly. I stand. And I walk toward the hearth. I pick up the nearest piece of paper. It’s folded, thick, and heavy with ink. I don’t know what it says. But I don’t need to. I know how to read it. And more importantly, I know how to live by it.

If you need to rebuild, start small. Light a fire. Find a mark. Listen to the wind. The world will not hand you a map. You will have to draw your own. And if you carry a seed, a thread, and a question that won’t let go, you are already grounded.

The dream does not end. It continues. And I begin.
