You are about to take the final exam for a course that determines your future career. As you sit down to start the exam, you notice the questions are...

...already answered.

Every line is filled in with your handwriting. Messy, hurried, looping cursive that you recognize instantly as your own — but from when you were much younger. Eight years old, maybe.

You flip through the pages, heart pounding.

*1. What did you want to be before they told you to be realistic?*
*2. List every time you said "sorry" when you meant "help me."*
*3. Draw the shape of your mother's worry.*
*4. If a clock has no hands, is it still telling the truth?*

You look up. The exam hall isn't an exam hall anymore. It's your childhood classroom. The desks are too small. Your knees are jammed up against the underside. The person in front of you is you, at 10 years old, swinging your legs, humming.

The professor at the front is no one you recognize, but everyone seems to know them. Their face keeps shifting — your first teacher, your boss, your future self. They're smiling, patiently.

You whisper, "I didn't study for this."

The person next to you — a stranger in a perfectly pressed suit — leans over and says, "No one did. That's the point."

You look back at the paper. The ink is starting to bleed. The questions are dissolving and reforming:

*5. What color is the feeling you get right before you fall asleep?*
*6. Describe the job that doesn't have a name yet.*
*7. You have one minute to remember why you started. Go.*

The clock on the wall has no numbers, only words. *SOON. NOW. NEVER. LATER.* Its single hand spins lazily backward.

Panic rises in your throat — this is supposed to determine your entire future! You don't know the answers! You were supposed to memorize formulas!

And then you notice the last question, at the very bottom of the last page, barely legible:

*8. What if there is no wrong answer? What will you write anyway?*

Your pencil is trembling. You press it to the paper.

And just as you begin to write — really write, for the first time — you feel the desk wobble, the classroom tilt, like water.

You are waking up.
