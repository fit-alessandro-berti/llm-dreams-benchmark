Nobody asked me to. There's no one left to ask. But I've been going house to house with a shopping cart whose one bad wheel only turns left, so I walk in slow circles down the center of the road, and the sound of it carries for miles in the quiet.

I take every bulb I find. I wrap them in newspaper from a month that no longer exists — every kitchen has the same calendar, every calendar is open to the same page, and I never look at the date because in dreams you know better than to look. The bulbs go into a jar. The jar is somehow also a boat. I don't question this; I only worry about the glass.

My hands are full of it and never cut.

The wire I string between the buildings, except the buildings have become trees somewhere along the way, tall black ones with windows in them, and I climb by the fire escapes which are also branches. I am not afraid of the height. I am afraid of the thing past the overpass, the one that breathes like a house settling. It has been following me since the beginning of the dream in the way things do, always exactly one street behind, patient as weather. I don't look at it. Looking is how you give it a face.

At the substation there's a bicycle bolted to the floor. I pedal. It's also a piano — I can feel the keys under the balls of my feet, and what comes out isn't electricity, it's the sound, a low chord that goes up the wire like water finding its level.

And the street comes on.

Not all at once. Bulb by bulb, a slow amber stitching, the light running out ahead of me faster than I can ride, past the collapsed pharmacy, past the cars with their doors open like people who left in a hurry, past the tree that grew through the bus. The dark pulls back maybe forty feet on either side. That's all. That's the whole amount of world I can hold.

Then, far off — across the river, from a hill I've never seen before — three blinks.

Pause. Three more.

I know, with the flat total certainty you only get while asleep, that this is someone I haven't met yet. That they've been signaling into nothing for a long time. That I'm late.

I get off the bicycle and start walking. The street stretches the way streets do, the hill never getting closer, and I'm not worried, because behind me the lights stay on the entire way, and somewhere back in the dark I can hear a second bicycle starting up.
