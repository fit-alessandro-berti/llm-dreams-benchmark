...each group seems to be having the same conversation, just in a different key.

The first cluster stands near a fountain that pours out warm light instead of water. They're discussing a movie you've definitely seen, though none of them can remember the title, and when they turn to you for help, you realize you can't either. You open your mouth and say, "It's the one with the lighthouse," and they all nod solemnly, as if you've said something profound. One woman presses a small brass key into your hand. "You'll need this for later," she says, and you pocket it without asking.

You move on. The room is bigger than it was a moment ago. The ceiling has drifted upward, and chandeliers hang like jellyfish, pulsing slowly. You catch a glimpse of your friend's jacket, that particular shade of green, disappearing around a corner. You call their name, but it comes out as a different name, one you haven't said since childhood, and a man in a paper crown turns around instead.

"They went that way," he says, pointing at a door that wasn't there before. "But you have to bring something to the potluck first."

You look down. You're holding a casserole dish. You don't remember making it, but you know, with dream-certainty, that it's your grandmother's recipe and that it's very important.

Beyond the door is a garden party happening at night, lit by strings of bulbs that hum softly in harmony. The guests here are all people you've half-known: a teacher from third grade, the barista who always spelled your name wrong, a coworker from a job you left years ago. They greet you warmly, as though you've been expected for hours. "You're late," they say, but kindly. "We saved you a seat." There's an empty chair at a long table, but when you go to sit, the table stretches, and the chair slides farther and farther away, polite but unreachable.

You set the casserole down on the grass. A dog you've never met but somehow love trots over and sits beside it, guarding it.

Then you see them.

Your friend is standing at the edge of the garden, near a wooden gate, looking out at something you can't see yet. You walk toward them, and the walk takes both a second and a very long time. The noise of the party fades behind you like a radio being turned down.

"There you are," they say, not turning around. "I've been looking for you."

"I've been looking for *you*," you say.

They laugh, and it's exactly their laugh, the one detail the dream gets perfectly right. "Did you get the key?"

You reach into your pocket. The brass key is warm. The gate has a lock you didn't notice before, and the key fits it perfectly. When you turn it, the gate swings open onto a quiet beach at dawn. There's a lighthouse in the distance, the one from the movie no one could name, its beam sweeping slowly over the water.

"Oh," you say. "*That's* the one."

Your friend smiles and steps through. You follow, and the sand is cool under your feet, and the gathering behind you is gone, and for a moment everything makes complete and total sense...

...and then you wake up, the feeling of it still in your chest, already forgetting the title again.
