Not your handwriting now—the crooked letters you made when you were seven.

**1. What did you want to be before you knew what a career was?**

You glance around. Everyone else is writing furiously. The person beside you has already filled three pages with tiny drawings of doors.

You turn back to your exam.

**2. Show your work. Include all the times you changed your mind.**

Your pencil is suddenly the yellow one you lost in primary school, its eraser chewed down to the metal. When you touch it to the paper, you hear rain falling inside your childhood kitchen.

The clock above the board has no hands. Instead, behind its glass, a small bird is building a nest.

You raise your hand.

“Will this be graded?”

The examiner looks up. You can’t quite see their face, but you recognize their coat: you have been wearing it every winter for years.

“Only the questions you answer.”

You look down at the last question.

**3. What would you do if you were not afraid of getting this wrong?**

You know the answer. It is embarrassingly simple. You cover the page with your arm so no one else can see, and begin to write.

Before you finish, the bell rings.

Chairs scrape. The other students carry their pages to the front. You try to write faster, but your pencil has sprouted leaves.

The examiner stops beside your desk.

“I’m not finished,” you say.

They take a small watering can from their coat pocket and set it beside you.

Outside the window, someone calls your name. You turn toward the sound, and for a moment you remember exactly what you had wanted to write.

Then the bird begins tapping on the glass.
