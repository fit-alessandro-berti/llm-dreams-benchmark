You feel a storm approaching and the forest seems to hold its breath. The sky, once a gentle blue, darkens in a heartbeat, bruised purple and gray, as heavy clouds roll in like a tide. A cold wind snakes through the trunks, rattling the leaves into a frantic chorus. You sense the weight of the impending rain pressing against your skin, each drop a tiny drumbeat announcing the storm’s arrival.

As the first gust hits, a hidden path—once invisible—shimmers into view, a narrow trail of silver‑lit moss that winds deeper into the woods. The trees lean toward you, their branches reaching out like arms, and the scent of wet earth rises, mingling with the sweet perfume of pine. Lightning cracks across the horizon, splitting the darkness and revealing a stone altar half‑covered in vines, its surface reflecting the fleeting light like a mirror.

Your pulse quickens, but you are drawn to the altar. When you step closer, the wind seems to pause, as if the storm itself is listening. A low hum resonates from within the stone, and a soft voice—neither male nor female—whispers your name, urging you to place your hand upon the cool surface. As your palm touches the altar, the rain that had been falling elsewhere begins to rise, forming a cascade of luminous droplets that swirl around you, each one a tiny lantern of memory.

The storm reaches its crescendo, thunder rolling like distant drums, yet the forest remains calm, cradling you in its ancient embrace. In that moment, you feel both the chaos of the tempest and the serenity of the woods intertwined, as if the dream is teaching you that turmoil and peace can coexist. When the last flash of lightning fades, the rain slows, and the clouds part, revealing a moonlit clearing where a single, glowing flower blooms—its petals pulsing with the rhythm of your own heartbeat.

You stand there, the forest alive with the echo of the storm, and you know that whatever lies beyond this clearing is waiting, just out of sight, ready to be discovered.
