The deeper you go, the quieter the maze becomes, until the only sound is the steady rhythm of your own concentration.

At last you reach a circular chamber at the center. There is no door, no monster, no final lock—only a mirror made of polished obsidian. When you look into it, you see not your face, but every puzzle you have solved along the way, arranged into a single luminous pattern.

And then you understand.

The maze was never meant to keep you lost. It was meant to show you how clearly you can think when you stop running from complexity. Each puzzle was a fragment of your own mind, asking to be understood.

You place your hand against the mirror. It ripples like water.

Beyond it, a path opens—not outward, but inward—and as you step through, the maze dissolves into dawn, leaving you awake with the strange, calm certainty that whatever problem awaits you in waking life, you already know how to begin.
