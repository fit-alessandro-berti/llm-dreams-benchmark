You are at a large social gathering where you only know one person. As you try to find them, you encounter various groups of people and—none of them are quite solid. A circle of women in vintage dresses discusses a wedding that hasn’t happened yet. A man with a face like a faded photograph offers you a glass of something that tastes like the color blue. Children run past carrying a cake shaped like your childhood home.

Every time you ask, “Have you seen my friend?” they smile and point in a different direction: through the French doors, down the hall, into the painting on the wall. You follow. The music changes depending on which room you enter—sometimes it’s a song you almost remember, sometimes it’s just the hum of fluorescent lights. You pass a mirror and your reflection is already walking away.

At last you find a quiet balcony overlooking a city that is also the party. There, with their back to you, stands the one person you know. You touch their shoulder. They turn, and they have your face, only older and kinder.

“You took long enough,” they say. “I’ve been here all along.”

You wake with the taste of blue still on your tongue.
