The party sprawls across a house that keeps growing new rooms. Music drifts from somewhere underneath the floorboards, and the ceiling is so high it has become a night sky, with a few constellations I almost recognize. I'm looking for Maren, the only person here I know, though I can't remember how we met or what she looks like right now. I only know that when I see her, I'll know.

Near the staircase, a group of people in silver coats are debating whether rain falls up or down. They turn to me before I can pass, and each of them has the same face, smiling politely, like a rehearsed portrait. "Down," one says. "Up," says another. "Only if you're listening," says a third, and they all laugh at once, a sound like wind chimes. I thank them and keep moving.

In the kitchen, a crowd gathers around a table where a woman is slowly unspooling a ribbon from her sleeve. It's longer than her arm, then longer than the room. People hold the ribbon and pass it along, telling stories as it goes. A man with a pocket watch tells me he's been at this party for eleven years, though it only feels like the length of a song. "Everyone here is looking for someone," he says kindly. "That's the only rule." I nod, feeling oddly comforted, and take a small glass of something warm and golden that tastes like the first day of summer.

I drift toward a balcony, but the doors open onto a hallway lined with mirrors. In each one I catch a glimpse of myself at a different age, all of them looking slightly lost. The youngest one waves. The oldest one gives me a look of quiet reassurance, as if to say I'm closer than I think. I wave back to all of them.

Back in the main room, a band made of furniture plays a waltz. A chair taps its legs in time, and a lamp hums a melody I almost know. Couples spin past me, and for a moment the crowd parts, and I see a figure standing alone by the window, her back to the room, watching the impossible sky.

I know her shoulders. I know the way she tilts her head, as though listening for a sound only she can hear.

"Maren," I say, and my voice comes out clear, carrying over the music, over the chatter, over the whole endless house.

She turns around slowly, and she's smiling, as if she had been waiting for me all along. Behind her, the stars begin to fall upward, one by one, like lanterns being released into the sky.
