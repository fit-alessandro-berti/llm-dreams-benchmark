

You start to run.

The streets blur into ribbons of pale gold, and the buildings around you lean inward like curious giants. Somewhere overhead, the clock tower ticks, but each second lasts longer than the last. You pass a woman hanging laundry from the sky; her sheets are full of maps, and one of them shows the city folded up, tiny, like a letter never sent.

You ask a boy with bicycle wheels for glasses, “Where is the meeting?”

He points.

You look, but the city has turned.

There is a long corridor now, stretching between two towers, its floor made of glass. Beneath it, rivers of memory flow: your childhood kitchen, a school hallway, a door you once left open. You keep running, but with every step, the meeting grows less certain. The invitation in your pocket becomes a seed, then a feather, then a name you cannot quite remember.

At the corner of the fourth street, a sign reads: **You Are Already Late.**

You stop.

The city waits.

And in the distance, the meeting begins.
