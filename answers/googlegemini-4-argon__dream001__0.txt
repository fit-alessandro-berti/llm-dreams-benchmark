You start to run, reaching into your pocket for your phone, only to pull out a smooth, heavy river stone. You look at it, confused, and when you look back up, the street has changed. The towering glass skyscrapers have been replaced by crooked buildings made of stacked, leather-bound books, their pages fluttering in a sudden, warm breeze.

"Excuse me!" you call out to a passerby—a tall figure in a sharp pinstripe suit. As they turn around, you see they have the head of a clock, its brass hands spinning frantically backward. 

"You're late for the yesterday," the clock-person chimes, their voice sounding like a struck tuning fork. They point a gloved finger toward a neon-lit subway entrance that wasn't there a second ago. 

Panic rising in your chest, you sprint toward the stairs. You try to run faster, but the pavement suddenly turns into thick, golden honey, trapping your shoes. Every step takes immense effort. You glance at your wrist; you aren't wearing a watch, yet glowing numbers are written directly on your skin, counting down: *45 minutes. 12 minutes. 3 minutes.*

With a final, desperate lunge, you pull your feet free from the honey and tumble down the subway stairs—only there are no stairs. You are falling through open sky, plummeting through a layer of pink clouds. 

Just as the ground rushes up to meet you, you land softly in a plush leather swivel chair. 

You blink. You are sitting at the head of a miles-long mahogany boardroom table. On either side of you sit dozens of penguins, all wearing reading glasses and staring at you expectantly. At the far end of the table, your third-grade teacher stands in front of a chalkboard, holding a pointer. 

"Well?" she asks, tapping the board, which is covered in glowing constellations instead of words. "We're waiting for your presentation."

You look down at your hands. The river stone is gone, replaced by a single, glowing dandelion. You take a deep breath to blow the seeds away, and as you exhale, you wake up.
