...plant a garden.

It makes no sense, and I know it even as I kneel in the cracked asphalt of what used to be a parking lot. The sky is the color of old bruises, and somewhere beyond the skeleton of the shopping mall, something howls that isn't quite a dog. But my hands are already moving, prying up chunks of pavement and piling them into a low wall, as if they know something I don't.

The seeds come from my coat pocket. I don't remember putting them there. They're warm, like they've been waiting.

In the way of dreams, time folds. One moment I'm pressing seeds into ash-gray soil with my thumb; the next, there are green shoots, impossibly bright against all that rust and concrete. A woman appears at the edge of the lot. Her face is wrapped in cloth, and she carries a rifle made mostly of pipe and wire. I should be afraid. Instead I hand her a tomato, round and red as a stoplight, and she takes off her mask to eat it. She's crying, or laughing. In dreams those are often the same thing.

She comes back the next day with two others. Then five. A boy who used to be a mechanic's apprentice rigs a rain catcher from a car hood. An old man who never speaks teaches us which weeds are edible by eating them in front of us and nodding. Someone paints the words **STILL HERE** on the mall's collapsed sign, and nobody asks who.

The dangers don't disappear. Raiders circle at night, their engines coughing like sick lungs. The howling thing gets closer. But the strangest part of the dream is this: the garden keeps growing *outward*, through the cracks, faster than anyone can pull it up. Vines climb the burned-out buses. Sunflowers split the highway. The raiders stop one evening at the edge of our wall, stare at a squash blossom the size of a dinner plate, and one of them slowly gets off his bike and asks if we need help digging.

At the very end, I climb to the roof of the mall to see how far the green has spread. It goes all the way to the horizon, soft and humming with bees that shouldn't exist anymore. The bruised sky is breaking open, showing a strip of pale blue like the inside of a shell.

I turn to tell someone, but the roof is crumbling into petals beneath my feet, and I'm falling, gently, into the smell of wet earth.

And I wake up with dirt under my fingernails.
