It doesn’t make sense—there shouldn't be electricity out here in the rust-choked wasteland, let alone a grand piano—but every night when the violet dust settles over the shattered highway, you hear the same three notes drifting on the wind. 

You wrap a frayed scarf tighter around your face and step out from behind the crumbling concrete overpass that has been your shelter. The sky above is bruised with swirling colors, shifting from ash-gray to a deep, bruised amber, the way skies only do in dreams. Your boots crunch over shards of sun-bleached glass and dry riverbeds. Somehow, your backpack feels lighter than it did a moment ago, though you don't stop to check what you’ve lost.

Shadows detach themselves from the skeletal remains of skyscrapers, watching you pass. You know there are scavengers out there, and worse things that sleep beneath the cracked asphalt, yet a strange, quiet certainty pulls you forward. 

You climb a hill of tangled rebar and dead vines, and at the top, the wasteland abruptly gives way to an overgrown botanical conservatory. Most of its glass dome has shattered, leaving jagged teeth pointing at the clouds, yet inside, impossible green ferns and luminescent moss thrive in the damp shadows. 

At the center of the tangled foliage sits a weathered wooden piano, its keys yellowed and half-swallowed by creeping ivy. No one is sitting on the bench, yet as you step closer, the keys depress on their own, ringing out a clear, warm chord that vibrates right through the soles of your boots. 

You reach out and rest your fingers on the cool wood of the piano's lid. Suddenly, the heavy scent of ash and rust vanishes, replaced by the smell of rain on warm pavement. Looking down, you realize your tattered coat is clean, and tiny green shoots are beginning to curl up through the cracks in the conservatory floor, spreading outward toward the ruined city beyond—until the sound of the final note stretches out, echoing softer and softer, pulling you gently back toward waking.
