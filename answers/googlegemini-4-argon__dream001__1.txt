You start to run, your shoes making a strange, hollow *clack-clack* sound against cobblestones that seem to ripple like water with every step. You reach into your pocket to check your phone for the address, only to pull out a heavy, ticking brass compass and a handful of dry autumn leaves. 

Panic flutters in your chest. You know the meeting is crucial—everything depends on it—though you can't quite remember *what* it is about, or who you are meeting. You look down and realize with a jolt that you are wearing a tuxedo jacket over pajama bottoms. 

Surrounding you are towering skyscrapers made of shifting, iridescent glass and terracotta, their peaks lost in a lavender-colored fog. Street signs are everywhere, spinning slowly like weathervanes, but the letters on them rearrange themselves the moment you try to read them. 

Desperate, you stop a passerby—a tall figure in a yellow trench coat walking a bicycle that has clock gears instead of wheels. 

"Excuse me," you gasp, your voice sounding distant, as if coming from underwater. "Where is the... the building? I’m going to be late."

The figure turns. They don't have a face, just a smooth expanse of mirror that reflects your own anxious eyes back at you. They point a gloved finger straight up. "The third door past the chime," they say, though their voice comes from the bicycle's bell. "Hurry. They're already pouring the tea."

You look up. A stone staircase is unfolding itself from the side of a brick building, spiraling upward into the clouds with no railings. You check the brass compass in your hand; the needle is spinning wildly, and you realize you only have five minutes left. Somehow, fifty-five minutes have vanished in a heartbeat.

You begin to climb the stairs. Strangely, your legs now feel as heavy as lead, as if you are wading through deep molasses. You pull yourself up step by agonizing step. Just as the fog swallows the city below you, the stairs end abruptly at a freestanding, mahogany door floating in the middle of the sky. 

A neon sign above it buzzes softly: *NOW ENTERING.*

Heart pounding, you reach out, turn the freezing brass doorknob, and push the door open. 

Instead of a boardroom, you step out onto a quiet, sunlit beach. In the center of the sand is a single wooden desk. Sitting behind it is your third-grade teacher, smiling warmly, holding a glowing teacup. 

"Right on time," she says, and as the waves crash against the shore, the sound turns into the shrill ring of your morning alarm.
