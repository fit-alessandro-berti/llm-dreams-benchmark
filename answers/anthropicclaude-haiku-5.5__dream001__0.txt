...start walking, though the streets seem to rearrange themselves each time you glance away. The buildings lean toward one another like gossiping neighbors, and their windows are lit with a warm amber that flickers like candlelight. You check your wrist for the time, but there is no watch there, only a faint pale band of skin where one used to be. Somewhere a clock is chiming, though you can't tell which direction it's coming from.

You ask a passerby for directions. They're a tall woman carrying an umbrella, though the sky is cloudless and full of slowly drifting paper boats. She smiles without quite looking at you and says, "The meeting is always on the floor above where you last left your shoes." Then she opens her umbrella and walks into a wall, which seems to swallow her without a sound.

Panic tightens in your chest, but you notice a staircase you're certain wasn't there before, spiraling up the side of a narrow tower made of stacked suitcases. Each step is a different color, and when you touch the railing it hums softly, like it's remembering a song. You climb. The city falls away beneath you, and you can see the whole sprawling map of it now, its streets forming a pattern that looks almost like a face, one you feel you should recognize.

At the top, instead of a conference room, there's a small round table set for a meeting. Several people are already seated, their faces blurred like photographs taken in motion. One of them looks up, checks a pocket watch, and says warmly, "You're right on time. We were just about to begin. Have you prepared your presentation?"

You reach for your bag and find, to your relief, that it's full of papers. But when you look down at the first page, the words are written in a language you somehow understand perfectly, and they describe the very dream you are in.
