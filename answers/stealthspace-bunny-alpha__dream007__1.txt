You decide to stop arguing.

Not because you’ve run out of things to say. You’ve rehearsed every sentence for days, maybe years, waiting for the moment you’d finally make them understand.

But the moment is here, and your friend looks less like someone you need to convince and more like someone you’re about to lose.

So instead of delivering your best speech, you say their name.

Just their name.

It falls into the silence between you like a stone into deep water.

Your friend goes still. Their anger doesn’t vanish, but it cracks. Beneath it, you glimpse something you haven’t allowed yourself to see: they’re hurting in exactly the same way you are.

“You really believe I’m trying to hurt you,” they whisper.

It isn’t a question.

You nod, because any excuse would become another wall between you.

“I don’t know how to do this without losing you,” you say.

The room begins to brighten, though no sun has risen. Somewhere behind you, a clock starts ticking backward.

Your friend takes your hand—not forgiveness, not the end of the argument, but a choice to remain in the same room.

“Then don’t,” they say. “Stay. Just stay.”

When you wake, the disagreement is still there. It will still demand an answer.

But for once, you know the first thing you will not do:

You will not let it teach you how to lose them.
