...stop talking mid-sentence, because you've just noticed that the words leaving your mouth are turning into small gray moths.

Your friend doesn't seem to notice. They're still arguing, gesturing at the kitchen table between you, except it isn't your kitchen anymore. It's the classroom from third grade, and the thing you were fighting about, which felt so urgent a moment ago, has slipped just out of reach. You know it mattered. You can feel the shape of it, like a word on the tip of your tongue.

"You always do this," your friend says, but their voice sounds like it's coming through water.

Instead of answering, you reach out and catch one of the moths. It's warm in your cupped hands. When you open them, it's a tiny folded note, and in your own handwriting it says: *I'm scared of losing you.*

You look up. Your friend is holding a note too. They're younger now, the age they were when you first met, and the classroom has no ceiling, just a deep violet sky with slow-moving stars.

"What does yours say?" you ask.

They turn it around. It's the same sentence.

The argument is still there somewhere. You can hear it faintly, like a television left on in another room. But the two of you are already walking out through the wall, which has become a field of tall grass, and your friend is laughing about something that happened years ago. You can't remember which of you was supposed to be right.

The moths follow overhead, a soft gray ribbon, heading wherever you're going.
