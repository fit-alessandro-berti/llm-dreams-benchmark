…written in a language you’ve never seen, but somehow you understand every word.

The first question reads: *“Describe the shape of your fear, using only the color of your name.”*

You look around. The other students are writing furiously, their pencils moving like metronomes. The clock has no hands—only a small bird sleeping on the hour.

You pick up your pencil. It’s made of glass. It writes in light.

You write: *My fear is a hallway that ends where it begins, and my name is the echo that forgot to arrive.*

The paper hums. The bird opens one eye and whispers: *Good. Now turn the page.*

But when you turn the page, the next question is blank. And the page after that is blank. And the whole exam is blank except the first question.

You raise your hand. The professor appears beside you, though you didn’t see them walk over. They are wearing your face.

They say: *The exam isn’t about answers. It’s about noticing when the questions disappear.*

You look down. The first question is now gone too.

The dream begins to fade, but you finally understand: you were never supposed to take the exam. You were supposed to give it.

You wake up holding a blank sheet of paper. On it, in your own handwriting: *Pass.*
