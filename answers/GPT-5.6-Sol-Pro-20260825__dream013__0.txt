…notice that the walls are slowly shifting behind you, erasing every path you have taken.

At first, you hurry. You solve riddles carved into stone, arrange glowing symbols into perfect sequences, and cross a floor where only prime-numbered tiles are safe. Each correct answer opens another passage—but the deeper you go, the puzzles become stranger. They no longer test mathematics or memory. One asks you to choose between two doors that lead to identical rooms. Another presents a mirror and demands that you identify the one thought you have been trying not to think.

You stand before it for what feels like hours. Then you realize the maze is not responding to your intelligence; it is responding to your fear of making a mistake. Every time you doubt yourself, the corridors multiply. Every time you trust your first instinct, a wall quietly disappears.

You close your eyes and begin walking without solving anything.

The maze trembles. Gears grind beneath the floor, puzzles collapse into dust, and the walls fold outward like the pages of an enormous book. When you open your eyes, you are standing beneath a clear night sky. At the centre of the empty field is a final pedestal bearing a single question:

**“Now that there is no correct path, where will you go?”**

You smile, choose a direction for no reason at all, and the moment you take your first step, you wake up.
