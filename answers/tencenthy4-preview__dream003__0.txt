…tiny doors, each no larger than a fingernail. They shiver open one by one, and through them you glimpse other dreams: a forest wearing the color of your grandmother’s voice, a train made of rain, a city where every window is a memory you misplaced.

The box is warm, as though it has been holding its breath for years. You realize there is no keyhole because the lock was never mechanical. It is waiting for you to remember something.

The patterns rearrange themselves—first into a spiral, then into a hand, then into your name written in soft silver light. You speak it, not aloud but the way dreams let you speak, with thought alone.

The lid unfolds like petals.

Inside is a small paper boat sailing on a sea of stars, carrying the one thing you came into the dream to find: a feeling you had lost, still alive, still waiting. The moment you touch it, the walls of the dream dissolve into morning.

You wake with salt on your lips and the strange certainty that somewhere, just out of sight, the box is still open.
