...they all seem to know you.

A woman in a yellow dress waves as if you're old friends, but her face keeps changing every time you blink—first someone from childhood, then your third-grade teacher, then someone you're certain you've never met but who somehow feels important. You wave back anyway, because in dreams this seems like the right thing to do.

The room stretches impossibly long. The ceiling lowers and rises with your breathing. You try to remember who you're looking for—their name sits right at the edge of your mind, warm and familiar, but every time you reach for it, it dissolves like sugar in water.

Someone hands you a drink. You don't drink it, but you keep holding it, and it becomes heavier and heavier until it's too heavy to hold, and when you set it down, it's a small sleeping cat. Nobody notices this.

You find a hallway lined with doors, each one slightly ajar. Behind one, you hear laughter you recognize. Your heart lifts—you're close now. You push the door open and step through and you're outside, suddenly, in the backyard of a house you lived in when you were eight. It's nighttime but the sky is bright as noon. The garden is full of people, all of them mid-conversation, all of them turning toward you at once.

"You made it," they say, in unison.

And then you remember—the person you were looking for was never at the party. They were the reason you left the house tonight. They're somewhere else entirely, and you feel a flash of that familiar dream-panic, the kind where you know you're late for something but can't remember what.

You start running through the garden. The grass is soft and your feet barely seem to touch it. Ahead, past the fence that wasn't there a moment ago, you see a single porch light, and a figure standing beneath it, waiting, and you know without seeing their face exactly who it is—

And then you wake up, with the strange, aching feeling that you were about to remember something important.
