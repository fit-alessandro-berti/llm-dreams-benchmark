When the thunder finally rolls, it doesn’t boom—it hums like the low, resonant bow of a giant cello, vibrating right up through the soles of your shoes. You try to run for cover, and at first, your legs feel as though they are wading through chest-deep honey. Panic flickers in your chest until you remember how this place works: you lean forward, push off a mossy stone, and begin to glide effortlessly, skimming three feet above the ferns in long, weightless bounds.

The rain begins to fall, though it isn't water. Luminous droplets of liquid silver drift down in slow motion, stopping just inches above the forest floor to hover like a million tiny lanterns. Passing through a cluster of them, you hear faint, overlapping whispers—snippets of old conversations and half-forgotten songs.

Ahead, the widest oak tree in the woods features a tarnished brass doorknob right in the center of its bark. Without questioning how it got there, you turn it and pull. 

Warm amber light spills out. Stepping inside and shutting the bark door behind you, the howl of the wind vanishes instantly. You are no longer in a tree, nor in the forest, but standing in the quiet, velvet-lined aisle of an empty vintage train car. Rain streaks horizontally across the windows now, blurring the dark woods outside into ribbons of emerald and violet. You sink into a plush seat as the train sways gently along invisible tracks, listening to the rhythmic clatter beneath the floorboards slowly morph into the steady, peaceful sound of ocean waves, pulling you deeper into the quiet dark.
