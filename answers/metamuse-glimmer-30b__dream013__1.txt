As you move deeper into the maze, you realize the walls are no longer stone.

They are made of paper. Thick, yellowed pages covered in handwriting that is unmistakably yours. The puzzles shift when you look away. A logic grid becomes a grocery list from ten years ago. A sliding tile puzzle resolves itself into a phone number you haven't called in years.

The air gets warmer and quieter. Your concentration is no longer a struggle; it feels familiar, like remembering something in the middle of a conversation. Each solution you find doesn't open a door so much as it erases a wall, and the maze gets smaller, not larger.

You stop solving to get out. You start solving to see what comes next.

In the center there is no exit, only a small wooden table and a single chair. On the table is a copy of the first puzzle you solved at the entrance. You sit down and pick up the pencil. You can feel the weight of it, the faint smell of graphite.

And then you understand: the maze isn't testing you. It's remembering you. Every corridor you walked was a question you once asked yourself, and the deeper you go the more honest the answers become.

You are about to write the solution down when the paper starts to curl at the edges, the walls begin to breathe in with you, and the whole maze folds in on itself like a book closing.

You wake up with your fingers still curled around an invisible pencil.
