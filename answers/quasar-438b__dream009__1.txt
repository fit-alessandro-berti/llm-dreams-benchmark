complete basic tasks just to survive. The air smells of woodsmoke and animal sweat. The ground beneath your bare feet is a packed dirt path, trodden smooth by countless others before you.

You realize you need to find water before nightfall.

The sun is low and amber-hot, maybe three hours from the horizon. Your mouth is dry. Your lips feel cracked. You lick them and the taste is stale, salty, wrong. Someone — a man, older, with worn hands and a clay jug slung against his hip — walks past you on the path without stopping. He does not see you as a person yet. You are just another body on the trail.

You follow him.

The path threads between lean-to poles holding roofs made of stitched hide, and past a clearing where hides are being scraped clean with flat stones. The smells are liver and sweat. A woman kneels near the far edge, working a grindstone against pigment. She does not look up.

The man with the clay jug descends toward a bank of mud, dry-looking, but you watch him press his jug down and crouch. He waits. Water rises — slow, then steady — soaked up from somewhere below. The bank is not what it appears. He knew where to look before arriving.

You wait for him to pass. Then you kneel at the same place. Mud packs around your hands, dense and wet. He used the jug to collect. You don't have one.

You press a hollowed reed-stalk down and wait. Your tongue feels numb at the edges. Altered. Dreaming. But the grime beneath your nails is specific enough. Your pulse is high. Your stomach is tight.

Water enters the reed. Pale, cool. You taste it. This is survival calculus. You need more volume and you need it before dark.

Behind you, a soft thud. A small waterskin has been dropped — or discarded — near the leaning poles. Someone left it. You take it. Fill it. Tie it shut with a strip of dried sinew from the windbreak wall. One full waterskin, yours, taken from nothing.

The sky dims. The heat is dropping. You carry water, a scraping stone, a reed.

Your hands are already stained. You do not know how long you have been here.
