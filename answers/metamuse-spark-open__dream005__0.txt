...and nothing quite stays still.

The music - a low string quartet you could have sworn was a DJ just a moment ago - stretches every time you turn your head. The room itself can't decide what it is. One moment it's a grand ballroom with chandeliers, the next it's somehow also a backyard barbecue, with string lights tangled in the rafters overhead and the smell of cut grass under polished shoes.

You press forward, scanning faces for the one you know.

**First, a tight circle of old women in cardigans**, all knitting furiously with yarn that glows faintly in the dim light. They don't look up, but they speak in perfect unison without moving their lips:

*"He was here just a minute ago, dear. Or maybe you're early."*

Inside their circle, the half-finished scarf they're making seems to map the party - tiny knitted figures moving through tiny knitted rooms.

**Next, a cluster of children by what is now inexplicably an indoor pool.** They're blowing bubbles the size of beach balls that don't pop. Inside each bubble, a different version of the party plays out on loop. In one, you see the back of your friend's head - that familiar jacket, that laugh - and you lunge for it. Your hand passes straight through the bubble and comes away dry. The kids giggle, not unkindly.

**Then, a group of people in sharp business suits,** frozen mid-handshake around a table of untouched champagne flutes that will not spill no matter how much the floor tilts. All their faces are turned toward you, smiling politely, waiting for you to say something. When you apologize and step past them, they resume their conversation mid-word as if you'd never been there.

With each group, the dream lags a little more. You catch glimpses of your person everywhere - a laugh from behind a curtain, their shoes under a tablecloth, their hand reaching for the same doorway you are - but when you turn, it's always someone else wearing a piece of them for a second before melting back into a stranger.

Frustration turns soft and strange, the way it does in dreams. You stop looking so hard. You take a glass of champagne someone offers, even though you're sure you don't drink champagne.

And then the room goes quiet.

At the very center of the gathering, where there had definitely been a fountain / stage / kitchen island before, there's a small, round table. And there they are. Your person. Sitting alone, perfectly calm, stirring a cup of tea that somehow exists despite everyone else drinking champagne.

They've saved you a seat.

"You took the long way," they say, smiling like you've both known how this would end.

You sit down, and all the overlapping noise of the party finally resolves into one clear, warm song. You realize you weren't trying to *find* them to stay with them - you were trying to find them to feel anchored.

You ask, "How long have you been here?"

They look out at the swirling, shifting crowd and then back at you. "The whole time," they say. "I was just waiting for you to notice the party was moving around *us*."

You feel the profound, illogical relief that only happens in dreams - and just as you lean in to tell them something important, something you can only remember while asleep, the alarm-string-lights-chandelier flickers...

And you wake up, still feeling the warmth of that table, and the faint echo that you were exactly where you needed to be.
