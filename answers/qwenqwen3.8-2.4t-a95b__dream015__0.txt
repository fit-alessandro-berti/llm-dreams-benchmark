

Despite the hardships, you decide to keep moving toward the thin column of smoke rising beyond the ruined city, because something in you insists it is not destruction—it is a signal.

Your boots crunch over broken glass and blackened leaves. The sky is the color of old bandages, and the wind carries whispers that almost sound like names. You pass hollow buildings with windows like empty eye sockets, and everywhere there are signs of people who left in a hurry: a child’s shoe, a rusted bicycle, a photograph fading into white.

At last you reach the source of the smoke. It is not a fire, but a small iron stove standing in the middle of a cracked highway, and beside it sits an old woman stirring a pot of clear water. She looks up as if she has been expecting you.

“You’re late,” she says gently.

You want to ask late for what, but she hands you a cup. When you look into the water, you do not see your reflection. You see a green shoot pushing up through ash. You see birds returning to a dead tree. You see hands—many hands—building something from the ruins.

“Dreams survive even when the world doesn’t,” she says. “That is why we keep them warm.”

She lifts the lid of the pot, and inside are not food, but seeds, glowing faintly like embers. She places one in your palm. It is warm, almost alive.

“Plant it where you are most afraid,” she says.

You wake within the dream, standing in the center of the ruined city, holding the seed. The dangers are still there. The scarcity is still there. But now you understand what you must do. You kneel in the dust, dig a small hole beside the broken road, and plant the seed.

For a moment, nothing happens.

Then the ground trembles softly, and a green vine rises from the earth, twisting upward around the skeleton of a fallen streetlamp. It blooms with pale blue flowers that open like eyes. Where their light touches, the ash turns to soil. Other seeds begin to stir beneath the surface. Doors creak open in the distance. Footsteps answer yours.

The post-apocalyptic world does not vanish, but it changes. It becomes a place waiting to be rebuilt.

And you realize: you were not wandering through the ruins to escape the end of the world.

You were dreaming the beginning of the next one.
