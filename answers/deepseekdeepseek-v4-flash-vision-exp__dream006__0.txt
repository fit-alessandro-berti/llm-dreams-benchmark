...and you begin to speak.

At first, your voice comes out a little shaky, but then it warms into something steady and clear. The words seem to form themselves, rising from some deep, comfortable place you didn’t know you had. The audience leans in, and you see faces softening, nodding, even smiling.

You tell them a story—not the one you prepared, but a better one. It’s about a time you were lost in a strange city and found your way home by following the sound of laughter. You say that’s what this moment is: everyone wandering through dark rooms, searching for something that sounds like home.

A warm light falls over the stage. The audience glows like lanterns. You realize you’re no longer nervous—you’re *awake inside the dream*, and that makes you feel braver than you ever have been.

You finish with a simple sentence: “You are already the thing you are looking for.”

And as the applause begins, it sounds like rain on a tin roof, then wind through leaves, then waves. The whole hall transforms into an ocean of quiet gold. You step forward, not off the stage, but *into* the audience, dissolving into the dream itself, until there’s no speaker and no crowd—only a single note of sound, humming softly in the dark.

Then you wake up, with the echo of that sound still in your chest.
