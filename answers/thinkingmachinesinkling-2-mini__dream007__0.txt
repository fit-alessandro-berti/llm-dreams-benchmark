In the dream, the argument rattles like a storm inside a locked room. You’re both standing by the same doorway, the words slicing through the air too sharp even for a dream. Something important—trust, timing, what you owe each other—has calcified into a shout. And then, suddenly, you notice the wall behind your friend is made of ripples, like the world is being held together by your choices. You decide to stop—not to win, but to name the fear under the insult: “I feel abandoned, and I’m angry because I don’t want you to leave me behind.” Your friend feels the same ache, then laughs strangely, apologizes, and the room softens into a corridor of amber light where the fight can’t take root. You decide to finish the dream by telling the truth—not as proof, but as a doorway—and letting the argument end not with a resolution, but with a promise to keep trying.
