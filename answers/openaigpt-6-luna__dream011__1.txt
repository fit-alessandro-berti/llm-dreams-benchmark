...written in a language you almost understand.

The first asks for the name of the person you’ll become. The second is a map of a city you’ve never visited, though somehow you know your way around. You turn the page, and every question is about something you meant to say years ago.

The clock has no hands. Around you, the other students quietly fold their exams into paper boats and sail them down the aisles. You look up at the proctor, but it’s your childhood self, standing at the front of the room with a red pencil.

“Just answer honestly,” they say.

So you write your name. Then, beneath it, you write: **I don’t know yet.**

The pencil rolls from your hand. When it reaches the edge of the desk, it becomes a key. Somewhere beyond the classroom door, the future is waiting—not as a career, not as a grade, but as a place you can finally walk into.
