And as you move deeper into the maze, you begin to realize the puzzles are not just placed along the path—they *are* the path. The walls are made of half-written equations, the floor tiles are chess moves, and the air smells of oz and burnt sugar. You solve a door by balancing a scale made of pure light: on one side, all the lies you have ever told; on the other, a single feather. The feather is heavier. The door opens.

The next chamber is a spiral library where every book is missing its last chapter. You must find the story that has no ending, because it is the only one that can be continued. You run your fingers along the spines until you feel a book that is warm, like a sleeping animal. When you open it, the words rearrange themselves into a map—but the map shows not the maze, but you. You follow a red line drawn through your own ribs, down to your heart, and at the center of your heart there is a door.

You step through, and the maze turns inside out. Now you are standing on the ceiling, looking down at the corridors you just solved. Some of the puzzles are still ticking like clocks; others are blooming like flowers. A voice comes from everywhere and nowhere: *“Every puzzle you solved was a lock. You are the key.”* You look at your hands, and they are made of glass, and inside each finger is a small, glowing answer to a question you haven’t been asked yet.

You walk on, but the maze no longer resists. It unfolds before you like a flower opening at dawn. You reach a final room with a single mirror. In the mirror, you see yourself sleeping in your bed, tangled in sheets, frowning slightly. You reach out and touch the glass. It ripples like water, and your reflection whispers: *“Don’t wake up yet. We haven’t finished the last puzzle.”*

You lower your hand, and the mirror becomes a doorway. You step through it into a vast garden where every flower is a memory and every tree is a decision you haven't made yet. You understand now: the maze is not a place. It is the shape of a thought that is still thinking you. And you are not lost. You are being remembered.

Then the garden begins to hum, and the hum turns into the sound of your own alarm clock. But just before you open your eyes, you realize the alarm is not ringing in your bedroom. It is ringing inside the maze, and the maze is still inside you. You smile, close your eyes again, and continue deeper.
