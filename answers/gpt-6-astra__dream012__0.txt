“Keep her pointed into the waves,” you call, though the wind tears your words into ribbons. The crew moves anyway, trusting the steadiness of your voice more than the orders themselves.

Beyond the bow, a wall of water rises. For a moment, lightning shines through it, and you see something impossible inside: a staircase descending into a warmly lit house.

Your childhood home.

At the bottom of the stairs, someone is setting the table.

“Captain?” asks the first mate.

You look back. Rain streams from his coat, but his face is dry. He is holding a small brass key.

You recognize it without knowing why.

The wave does not break. It waits.

You turn the wheel, and the ship turns with it—not left or right, but downward, slipping beneath the surface as easily as a needle through cloth. Above you, the storm becomes a trembling silver ceiling. The sails fill with moonlight.

No one needs to breathe.

Ahead, a lighthouse stands on the ocean floor. Its beam sweeps across drowned forests, sleeping whales, a bicycle leaning against a gate. Each time the light passes over your crew, they look younger.

By the time you reach the lighthouse, they are children in oversized coats.

You lead them up the spiral stairs. At the top is no lantern, only a kitchen window glowing over a sink. You can smell bread.

The brass key fits.

Inside, the table has been set for everyone. Your crew hurries toward it, suddenly loud and hungry, dragging their sleeves through the butter. You count them as they sit. All accounted for.

Behind you, the first mate closes the door. He has to stand on tiptoe to reach the latch.

You sit down last. You are still wearing your captain’s coat, and the water dripping from its hem gathers around your boots. Under the table, a tiny wave breaks against one chair leg.

No one seems to mind.

Someone passes you the bread.
