You notice that certain symbols seem to glow faintly when you press them, as if responding to your touch. You try combinations — press a spiral, then a crescent, then a downward arrow — and somewhere inside, gears turn with a sound like laughter echoing down a corridor.

The lid shifts. Not fully, but enough for a pale, golden light to spill through the gap, casting patterns on the floor that move and rearrange themselves like living ink. You push harder, and the light grows warmer, tingling against your skin. The patterns in the air begin to form shapes — a bird, a door, your own face smiling back at you in a way you don't remember ever smiling.

You hesitate. In dreams, you know, opening things changes everything. But the humming grows into a song — not quite a melody, not quite a whisper — and it tells you, in a language you've always understood but never heard: *What waits inside is not a treasure. It is a memory you gave yourself.*

You lift the lid.

Inside, resting on a bed of soft, cloud-like moss, is a small glass bottle. Inside the bottle, a firefly — but no, not quite. It shifts between being a firefly and being a letter, and then your name, written in your own handwriting.

The box is empty now, and the song has stopped. You close the lid gently. When you do, the patterns on the box have changed — they now form a picture of your hands holding it, right here, right now.

You wake up. The light is still on the back of your eyelids. The humming still lingers in your fingertips.

And for reasons you can't explain, you feel *found*.
