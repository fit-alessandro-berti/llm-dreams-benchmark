...written in a language you almost recognize. The letters look like English, but when you try to read them, they slide around like fish in water. You squint, and one question briefly comes into focus: *"Explain everything you have ever learned, with citations."*

You look down at your desk. Your pen has turned into a small, nervous bird. It flutters up to your shoulder and whispers, "You didn't study, did you?"

Around you, the other students are writing with impossible speed, their hands blurring. One of them turns to you and smiles—you realize it's your third-grade teacher, except she has the face of your boss, except she has *your* face.

You glance at the clock above the blackboard. The hour hand is spinning counterclockwise, and the minute hand is dripping onto the floor like warm honey. Somewhere, faintly, you hear the exam proctor say, "Time is a suggestion."

Panic rises in your chest—that familiar exam-day dread—until you notice something strange: the walls of the classroom are softening, breathing gently in and out. The ceiling opens like an eye, and beyond it there isn't sky but the feeling of being seven years old on the first day of summer vacation.

And then you remember. That calm, certain knowing that only comes in dreams: *none of this is real. There was never an exam.*

The papers in front of you dissolve into white moths. The bird on your shoulder bows politely and flies away through the open ceiling. You stand up from your desk, and the classroom applauds—not mockingly, but warmly, as if you've just done something brave.

You walk toward the light, and just before you wake, a voice that sounds suspiciously like your own says:

*"You always knew the answers. You were just afraid of the questions."*

You wake up. Your alarm is playing the song that was stuck in your head yesterday. Outside, it's raining—but somehow, it feels like summer.
