You are about to give a speech to a large audience. As you step onto the stage, you see the crowd looking expectantly at you, and you begin to speak—but no sound comes out.

You try again. Your lips move, yet the enormous hall remains perfectly silent. A wave of panic rises in your chest. Then you notice something strange: every person in the audience is holding a small glowing lantern, and each lantern grows dimmer whenever you struggle to remember your prepared words.

At last, you stop trying to recall the speech. You take a breath and say the first honest thing that comes to mind.

“I’m afraid.”

This time, your voice echoes clearly through the hall.

One by one, the lanterns brighten. A woman in the front row smiles. Someone farther back whispers, “So am I.” Soon, voices begin rising from every corner of the room—people admitting their own fears, hopes, regrets, and dreams. The stage beneath your feet slowly dissolves, and you find yourself standing among them rather than above them.

The roof of the hall opens to reveal a sky filled with unfamiliar constellations. The lanterns float upward from everyone’s hands, becoming new stars. As the final light leaves your palm, you realize that the speech was never meant to impress the audience. It was meant to remind them—and you—that nobody has to stand alone.

Then the entire crowd turns toward you and begins to applaud, not loudly, but with the soft sound of rain falling through leaves.
