...and you realize you can’t move. Your feet are glued to the carpet, your fingers have turned to stone, and your monitor is glowing a color that doesn’t exist in any real spectrum.

Your boss smiles—too wide, too many teeth—and hands you a single sheet of paper with a due date that has already passed. The letters are crawling like ants.

“It’s simple,” she says, her voice echoing through a hallway that goes both directions at once. “Just finish it by yesterday.”

You look down at the paper. The project is written in a language you almost remember, full of diagrams that rearrange themselves when you blink. Somewhere in the distance, deadlines clock is ticking, but each tick is a heartbeat, and the heartbeat is yours.

You try to speak, but your words come out as sticky notes, fluttering around the room. One lands on your boss’s shoulder. It says *I need more time.* Another lands on the desk: *I don’t know how.*

Your boss picks up the second one and reads it aloud. Her voice is kind now, softer, and the hallway bends into a cozy office with warm light.

“Wake up,” she says gently. “You’re dreaming. The project can wait.”

But when you look at the paper again, it’s blank—and the deadline is now a doorway. You step through it, into a hallway of identical doors, each labeled with a different impossible date. You realize you can choose any one. You choose the door labeled *Tomorrow*, open it, and find yourself back at your desk with a fresh cup of coffee.

Your boss is waiting. She hands you the project again, but this time the deadline is realistic. You smile. You know what to do.

And then the alarm clock rings.
