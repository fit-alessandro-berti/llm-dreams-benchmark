“Keep her into the waves. One hand for the ship, one for yourselves.”

The crew moves, steadied less by the words than by the certainty with which you speak them. You take the wheel. It bucks beneath your palms like something alive.

Then lightning splits the sky, and for one impossible instant you see that the sea is not water at all.

It is a vast field of dark blue grass, bending beneath the storm. The waves are hills. The foam is a scattering of white flowers.

“Captain?” your first mate asks.

You look back. Every sailor is holding a lantern, though you never ordered them lit. Inside each lantern swims a tiny golden fish.

Far ahead, where the horizon ought to be, stands a door.

You know that door. Its paint is chipped near the handle. Behind it is the kitchen of the house where you grew up, and someone has left a light on.

“Steady,” you tell the crew, though your hands are trembling now.

The ship climbs one last enormous wave. At its crest, the thunder stops. You hear a spoon stirring in a cup.

The door opens.

Warm light spills across the sea, laying a narrow path beneath your bow. You ease the wheel toward it.

Behind you, the crew begins to sing—not a sailor’s song, but a lullaby you have not heard in years.

And as the ship glides forward, the storm folds itself away like a blanket.
