You are in a world where colors and shapes are constantly changing. As you explore this surreal landscape, you come across a blank canvas and—though it hangs on nothing—it feels heavier than the sky. A brush waits beside it, made from a single beam of moonlight. The canvas is not empty. It is full of whiteness, a silence so deep it hums.

You touch the brush to it, and the first stroke becomes a door. The second becomes a garden where flowers whisper in arithmetic. The third becomes your own face, younger and older at once, watching you from inside the paint. The landscape leans closer. Every color you invent spills outward and turns into terrain: a river of violet glass, a forest of soft clocks, a sky that tastes like oranges.

Then the canvas begins to paint you. Your fingers loosen into brushstrokes. Your breath becomes a thin wash of blue. You try to step back, but the dream has no edges. The brush moves on its own, signing your name in a language you almost remember. When the last stroke lands, the canvas turns transparent, and you see the waking world on the other side—your room, your bed, your hand twitching as if it still held the brush.

You step through. The dream folds shut behind you, leaving one wet mark on your palm: a tiny, unfinished sun.
