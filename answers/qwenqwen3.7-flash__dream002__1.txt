...and the sky doesn't cloud over so much as it *unfolds*, like a heavy book slowly opening to a page you've bookmarked but never read. The wind arrives first, but it doesn't push—it pulls, threading through the pines in a rhythm that matches your heartbeat until you realize it's been syncing all along. 

The air smells of petrichor and burnt sugar. Lightning flickers, but instead of white, it pulses in deep indigo, illuminating the forest floor in brief, strobing glimpses of footprints you didn't make but somehow recognize. They lead ahead, glistening with a moisture that rises instead of pools. You follow them without deciding to. 

Rain begins not as drops, but as threads. Silken and weightless, they drape between branches, catching the indigo light and humming when brushed by passing moths with wings like stained glass. The deeper you walk, the quieter the storm becomes. It isn't threatening anything; it's rearranging things. Moss carpets crack open to reveal smooth, warm stones. Fallen logs curl their ends upward, forming natural benches that invite you to sit even though you keep moving. 

Then the ground slopes gently downward, and you're standing at the edge of a basin where the rain collects in perfect concentric rings. Each ripple shows a different hour of your day: coffee steaming, keys clicked shut, sunlight cutting across a crowded street. At the center, the water is still. Clear. Waiting.

You kneel. The storm parts above you like a curtain drawn by invisible hands, leaving only a single shaft of pale light that settles on your shoulders like a known weight. You understand now that dreams don't always run ahead to warn you. Sometimes they run alongside, just to remind you what it feels like to walk through uncertainty without rushing to the end.

A distant sound bleeds through the canopy: muffled, rhythmic, impossibly familiar. You don't open your eyes. Not yet. Instead, you let the last thread of rain slide over your knuckles, feel the warmth return to your fingers, and let the forest hold you until you're ready to remember how to wake.
