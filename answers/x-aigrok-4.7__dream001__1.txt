You start to walk, and the street signs keep rearranging themselves into times instead of names. One reads 10:47, another 11:02, and none of them point toward a building you recognize.

A tram glides past without rails. Its destination board says MEETING, but when you step toward the stop the tram has already become a staircase going down into a station that smells like rain on hot stone. Underground, every platform is labeled with a different version of your own handwriting. You pick the one that looks most hurried.

The train that arrives has no doors, only windows that open like eyelids. Inside, the other passengers are all holding folders and none of them will meet your eyes. A conductor made of coat hangers asks for your ticket. You pat your pockets and find a key, a receipt for something you don’t remember buying, and a note that says: *You’re already late, which means you’re early enough.*

When you look up, the city has folded. The station is now a lobby with too many elevators, and every button is the same floor. You press one anyway. The doors close on a soft chime that sounds exactly like your name said from another room.

You wake, or you arrive—it's hard to tell which—with the sense that the meeting was never in a place at all, only in the hour you were trying not to miss.
