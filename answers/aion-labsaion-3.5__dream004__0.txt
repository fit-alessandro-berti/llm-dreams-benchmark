The shapes around you respond. The floating geometry of the landscape pauses mid-transformation, as if waiting to see what you'll paint next. You realize, with the strange certainty that only dreams allow, that the canvas doesn't just record this world — it creates it.

You drag your hand across the surface and a river of silver light pours into the landscape behind you, carving a path through crystalline hills. You paint a door, and somewhere in the distance, you hear it creak open. You paint a bird with wings of shifting geometry, and it lifts off the canvas, scattering droplets of color that fall upward into a sky made of slowly breathing darkness.

But then you notice something unsettling: there's a small smudge on the canvas you didn't make. A fingerprint. Someone else has been here, painting this world before you. And as you lean closer, you realize the smudge is still wet — and it's moving, spreading, forming itself into words:

*"Don't forget to wake up."*

The colors around you begin to drain, the shapes losing their edges, everything folding inward like a letter being sealed.
