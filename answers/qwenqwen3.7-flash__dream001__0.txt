...walk faster, but your steps sink into cobblestones that ripple like shallow water. With each stride, the pavement warms beneath you, and the city begins to breathe. The streetlamps bend toward you like tall grass in a wind you can't feel. Signs above shop awnings flicker, swapping languages mid-syllable until they settle into words you've only ever seen in margins of old notebooks: *Remember, Return, Rise.*

You press the button for an elevator in a building whose entrance you didn't notice before. The brass panel responds by unfolding into a map that smells like rain on hot asphalt. The dots don't show streets; they pulse with heartbeats. Yours thumps loudest near the center, but when you try to trace it, your finger slips sideways, and the ink bleeds into a river that flows uphill.

A train arrives without tracks, rolling on a ribbon of polished obsidian. The conductor hands you a ticket printed on what feels like pressed leaves. The destination reads: *Where You Left Off*. You don't question it. In dreams, questioning only makes the floor drop away.

Inside, the windows aren't glass. They're looking glasses. You catch glimpses of other versions of yourself: taking the wrong turn, sleeping through the alarm, laughing while walking backward, standing perfectly still in the center of a storm. One waves. Another shakes their head. All of them know the way.

Your pocket grows heavy. You reach in and pull out a compass, but its needle spins lazily, pointing not north, but toward warmth. Toward a sound you haven't heard in years: a kettle whistling, a chair scraping, someone calling your name from another room.

The platform materializes under your shoes before the doors even fully open. No skyscraper. No lobby. Just a narrow stairway winding down into golden light, flanked by potted plants whose leaves drip starlight instead of water. At the bottom stands a door slightly ajar, held open by a sliver of afternoon.

Inside isn't a boardroom or a courthouse. It's a sun-flooded hallway lined with photographs that shift when you blink. On the table sits two mugs, steam curling in twin spirals. A chair is pulled out, exactly your size.

You sit. The meeting was never about coordinates or schedules. It was about coming home to yourself.

And in the dream, that's always enough.
