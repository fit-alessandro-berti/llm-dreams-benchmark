You keep walking, though your feet feel heavier with every step. The path ahead begins to shimmer, like heat rising off summer pavement. The storm is not above you anymore. It is all around you, breathing.

Then you hear it—a voice, soft and familiar, carried on the wind. It speaks your name, not as a question, but as an echo of something you once forgot. You stop. The forest goes silent. Even the wind holds its breath.

You realize then: you are not walking *through* the forest. The forest is walking through you. The storm is not approaching. It is arriving—from the inside out. And you are not afraid. You are remembering.

The first drop of rain touches your cheek, and it is warm, like a tear you didn't know you were shedding. The sky opens, and the light pours down in ribbons of gold and silver. You close your eyes, and when you open them again, you are standing at the edge of a field you have never seen—but somehow, you have always known it. The storm is gone. The peace is back. And you are still dreaming.
