

You are about to take the final exam for a course that determines your future career. As you sit down to start the exam, you notice the questions are written in your own handwriting — but you don’t remember writing them.

The first question says:  
**“What have you been pretending not to know?”**

You blink, and the ink shifts. The second question appears:  
**“If you pass, what part of yourself will you leave behind?”**

The room grows quiet, except for the sound of pencils scratching around you. Everyone else seems to understand what’s happening. You look down again, and now there is only one question left, centered on the page like a door:

**“Are you sure this dream belongs to you?”**

When you look up, the exam hall is gone. You are standing in a long corridor of mirrors, each one showing a different version of you taking the same exam in a different life. In one, you are confident and smiling. In another, you walk away from the desk. In another, you never arrive at all.

A voice, soft but close, says:  
“Choose the answer, and the dream ends. Refuse the question, and it begins.”

You look back down at the paper. A pencil appears in your hand. Beneath the final question, a blank line waits — not for an answer, but for a name.

Yours.
