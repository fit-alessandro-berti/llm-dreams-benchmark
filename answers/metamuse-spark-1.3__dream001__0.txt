You start to walk faster, hoping movement itself will jog your memory.

Your shoes click too loudly on the wet pavement. Every building around you is impossibly tall — glass towers that lean in slightly, as if listening to you. You check your phone: 58 minutes left. No address, no contact name, just a calendar notification that simply says **IMPORTANT - DO NOT BE LATE.**

You start to ask for directions. A woman selling flowers from a cart that seems to stretch down the whole block smiles and points in three directions at once. "Oh, it's just over there," she says. "Past the clock that runs backwards."

You thank her and run "over there." The streets don't stay still. A familiar-looking cafe on the corner becomes a laundromat when you blink. The street signs are in a language you *almost* understand — the letters swim if you stare too long. You ask a bus driver, but he just laughs: "Everyone has that meeting today."

Panic rises in your throat. You check your pockets and find: a key you've never seen, a crumpled ticket stub for a movie you don't remember watching, and a business card with YOUR name on it, and an address that changes every time you look away.

47 minutes. Then 23 minutes. Then 58 minutes again. Time is skipping.

Suddenly you turn a corner and the city goes quiet. The crowd thins. In front of you is a low, warm-lit building that feels completely out of place — wood and soft yellow windows among all that steel. The door is slightly open, like it's been waiting for you.

You push it open, and inside is...
