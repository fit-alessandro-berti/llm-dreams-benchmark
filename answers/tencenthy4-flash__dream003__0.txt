The designs spiral into constellations you almost recognize—stars that arrange themselves into the shape of a forgotten childhood hallway, the cadence of a lullaby hummed in a language you can’t quite place, the exact shade of twilight from a evening you thought you’d forgotten.

The lock isn’t a keyhole at all, but a small, engraved eye that slowly blinks back at you.

You reach out, and the moment your fingertip grazes the metal, the box sighs. It’s a soft, wooden sound, like someone letting go of a long-held breath. The lid creaks open—not with a click, but with a yawn of warm, golden light that spills across your hands. The scent of rain on hot pavement and old library books wafts out.

Inside, there is no gold, no jewels. Resting on a bed of velvet is a single, glass key. It isn’t meant for the box. Tiny letters etched into its shaft read: *“For the door you left slightly ajar.”*

As you close your fingers around it, the room around you begins to stretch and warp. The walls dissolve into a sky of drifting paper cranes, and the floor turns into a path of floating stepping-stones. Somewhere ahead, a door stands alone in the mist, glowing with the same light as the box. You know, with the absolute, unshakable certainty of dreams, that this is exactly where you are supposed to be. 

Do you turn the key? Or do you wake up?
