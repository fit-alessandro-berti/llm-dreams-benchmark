...speak, but the words that leave your mouth aren't the ones you rehearsed. They come out as a soft, silvery mist that curls upward and hangs in the air above the crowd. Nobody seems alarmed. A few people lean forward and breathe it in, and their eyes go wide with understanding.

You look down at your notes. The pages are blank except for a single sentence in handwriting that is somehow both yours and not yours: *"Tell them what you were afraid to tell yourself."*

The room has grown larger. The ceiling dissolves into a dusky sky, and the rows of seats stretch back into a hazy horizon of faces. In the front row you notice a childhood friend you haven't seen in twenty years, your third-grade teacher, and a very serious-looking owl wearing reading glasses.

You clear your throat, and this time your voice comes out steady and warm.

"I had a whole speech prepared," you say, and the audience laughs gently, as if they already knew. "But I think I've forgotten it on purpose."

The mist gathers into shapes, a staircase, a door, a small lantern, and they drift down and settle into the open hands of the people below. Each person's gift is different. The teacher's lantern flickers with a golden light. The owl receives a key and nods gravely.

"What I wanted to say," you continue, surprised by how true it feels, "is that none of us were ever given the script. We've all been improvising the entire time, and look how far we've come."

The applause begins as a low rumble, like distant thunder, then swells until the stage trembles. The floor turns soft and starts to ripple like water. You realize you aren't nervous anymore. You're floating a few inches above the boards, and the crowd is floating with you, all of you rising together toward the open sky.

Just before the stars swallow the scene, you notice a small clock on the far wall. Its hands are spinning slowly backward, and somewhere far away, you can faintly hear an alarm beginning to ring...
