...reach for your keyboard, only to realize that all the keys have been replaced with smooth, blank river stones. 

You try to type anyway, hoping muscle memory will save you, clicking the stones together frantically. On the monitor, instead of a document, there is just a live video feed of an empty hallway, and someone is slowly walking toward the camera carrying a very large potted fern. 

"We really need this alphabetized by yesterday," your boss says, leaning heavily over your shoulder. You glance up to explain that you don't even have the files, only to realize your boss is now your fifth-grade gym teacher, wearing a tuxedo and holding a stopwatch that is ticking *way* too loudly. 

Panic starts to bubble in your chest. You frantically open your desk drawers looking for a pen, a binder, anything to show you're making progress, but the drawers are completely filled with loose, dry autumn leaves. When you try to push the drawer shut, the leaves spill out onto the carpet and immediately fold themselves into tiny paper cranes, flapping lazily up toward the fluorescent lights.

You stand up to go to the printer—maybe the project is waiting there—only to find that the office walls have quietly vanished. Your cubicle is now sitting in the middle of an endless, dimly lit swimming pool deck. The floor is slick with water, and you suddenly realize you aren't wearing shoes, just two mismatched winter mittens on your feet. 

Trying to walk feels like wading through waist-deep honey. You push forward with all your might, gasping for air, while your coworkers casually glide past you on office chairs fitted with bicycle pedals, smiling and nodding as if everything is completely normal. 

"Two minutes!" your boss shouts from miles away, though their voice sounds like it's right inside your ear. 

Desperate, you grab a clipboard floating nearby. Written on it in thick red marker is a single math equation made entirely of geometric shapes you've never seen before. You stare at it, waiting for it to make sense, when suddenly the overhead lights flicker and buzz, sounding suspiciously like an alarm clock. The floor tilts gently sideways, the pool water turns into a swirling purple sky, and you realize with immense relief that you don't actually have to finish the project—you're supposed to be catching a train that leaves from the roof. You drop the clipboard and start swimming upward.
