You wake up in a completely different era, with no modern technology around. As you explore your new surroundings, you realize you need to **find water before the sun climbs higher**.

The air smells like damp earth and wild herbs. Beneath your bare feet, the ground is cool and uneven—mud, stone, roots. You’re standing in a narrow valley, and above you the sky is a strange, endless blue, unmarked by planes or wires. The only sounds are birds you don’t recognize, calling to each other in long, liquid notes.

You follow a worn path toward a grove of ancient oaks. There, a spring spills out of a mossy rock. You cup your hands and drink, and the water tastes like the beginning of the world—cold, mineral, alive. When you lift your head, you notice a small settlement in the distance: round huts with smoke rising from their roofs. People in rough-spun clothes are moving among them.

You realize you need to **approach them without fear, because your brain is the only tool you have left.**

You take a step forward. The grass whispers. A dog barks, then goes quiet. An old woman at the edge of the village turns to look at you, her eyes bright and knowing.

She smiles. And you realize you have been dreaming all along—but the water was real. You feel it still, cool in your throat, as the dream begins to fold into waking.

But when you open your eyes, you are no longer in your bed.

You are standing in the valley again, the sun a little higher, the path waiting.

And the dream is only beginning.
