Question One asks: *"Describe the exact weight of a Tuesday you can almost remember."*

You reach for your No. 2 pencil, but it has softened into a stalk of celery. Panic starts to bubble in your chest, but the proctor glides over—a figure in a sensible cardigan wearing the face of your third-grade librarian—and whispers, "Don't mind the celery, dear. Suddenly, the fluorescent lights melt into warm, buttery sunlight. The desks sprout moss and begin to float. The whole classroom tilts upward, revealing itself to be the basket of a hot air balloon. The answer sheet flutters off like a startled moth, and instead of chasing it, you grab a passing cloud, take a bite (it tastes like vanilla and static electricity), and realize the only remaining requirement of the course is to enjoy the view.
