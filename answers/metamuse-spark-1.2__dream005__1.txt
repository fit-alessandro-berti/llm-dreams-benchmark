You are at a large social gathering where you only know one person. As you try to find them, you encounter various groups of people and —

the house keeps getting bigger.

It was supposed to be an apartment, but every doorway leads to another room you weren't expecting. You squeeze past the first group by the entrance.

They are all laughing at a joke that was told a few seconds before you arrived. Their laughter is a little too loud and a little too long, and none of them seem to need to breathe. When you try to smile and pass, they all turn to look at you at exactly the same time, smiling with the exact same smile, before turning back as if you were never there.

You mumble your friend's name under your breath like a compass and keep walking.

In the kitchen, a second group stands in a tight circle around the island, not talking at all. They're just watching a pot of water that will never boil. The light above them buzzes too brightly. One woman makes eye contact with you, and you think for a second she’s going to help you, but then she just holds out a plastic cup to you. It's empty. You take it so you don't seem rude, and when you look down again, you're already in the next room.

The living room is sunken and carpeted in a color that doesn't exist - a deep, warm green that feels like late summer. Here, everyone is talking at once, but you can't understand a single word. The conversations sound like they're being played underwater. A man in a paper crown tries to pull you into a conversation, his mouth moving around words that slide right off your ears. You nod. You say, "Sorry, have you seen —" but you've already forgotten your friend's name for a second. It flutters just out of reach, like trying to remember a word upon waking.

You push on. The hallway stretches.

It should have ended at the bathroom, but now it's a long hotel corridor lined with doors that are all slightly ajar. Behind each one, the same party is happening from a slightly different angle. You catch a glimpse of the back of your friend's head in one of them — that familiar jacket, that familiar hair — and your heart leaps. You call out.

They don't hear you.

You run, but the hallway stretches with every step, the carpet soft and swallowing the sound of your feet. The people lining the hallway aren't facing each other anymore. They're all facing you. Not threateningly. Just... watching. Patiently. As if they know you're dreaming and are waiting for you to realize it too.

Then, suddenly, the hallway is gone. You're outside.

It's quiet. The party is muffled behind the glass doors now, warm light spilling onto a huge, empty balcony. And there is your friend, leaning on the railing alone, looking out at a city that isn't any city you've ever been to. The sky is too many colors at once, like an aurora.

They turn and see you, and their face lights up with pure relief.

"There you are," they say. "I was looking for you everywhere."

And you realize the entire crowded house was just the space between you and them — and you had finally crossed it.

You open your mouth to answer, but the wind on the balcony is suddenly very loud, and the light behind you flickers like an old bulb...

And you wake up, still holding that empty plastic cup in your hand for just a half-second before it fades.
