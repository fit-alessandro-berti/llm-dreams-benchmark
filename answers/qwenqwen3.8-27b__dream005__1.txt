

…you find yourself moving through rooms that do not quite follow the rules of architecture. One doorway opens into a ballroom where the chandeliers hang from the ceiling like fruit, and people in evening dress are quietly discussing the weather as if it had happened to someone else. You pass a group laughing around a table made of stacked clocks, each one showing a different hour, and you have the sudden, dreamlike certainty that one of them knows your name but will not say it.

You ask for the one person you know, but the question comes out as a smell: lavender, rain, old paper. The woman you ask looks at you with too much sympathy and says, “They’re already looking for you,” before she becomes a coat rack, and then a door.

You slip through the door into a long hallway where everyone is holding something that does not fit their hands—glasses, umbrellas, letters, small broken birds. You follow a line of guests through a garden that has grown inside the banquet hall, its roses opening and closing like eyes. The music has changed. It sounds like your name being forgotten and remembered in turn.

Finally, you see them. They are standing near a window, looking out at a sky that is not connected to anything. They turn, and for a moment their face is not their face at all, but a familiar expression: the look someone wears when they have just woken up.

Then they say, “You came.”

And you do, though you have not moved.
