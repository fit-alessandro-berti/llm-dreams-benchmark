...you realize you need to find the bell tower before the light fails.

The cobblestones are warm under your bare feet, though you don't remember taking off your shoes. Smoke curls from chimneys with no wires or satellite dishes above them, only a sky so crowded with stars it looks spilled. A woman in a wool shawl passes with a basket of pale bread, and she nods at you as though you are expected. Her words come out in a language you shouldn't understand, yet you do: *"The lamplighter is late tonight. Mind the well."*

You don't know why the bell tower matters. You only know that somewhere above the rooftops, a bell has been waiting for someone to ring it, and that the sound it makes will tell you where you belong in this century.

So you walk. The streets fold into one another like the pages of a book. A dog barks in a language of whistles. A blacksmith's forge glows orange through an open door, and the man inside lifts his hammer and grins at you as if you're an old friend. Somewhere a fountain murmurs, and the water sounds like it's saying your name.

At the top of a narrow stair, the tower opens onto the night. The bell hangs still and enormous, its bronze dark with age. You reach for the rope, and the moment your hands close around it, you remember everything you forgot: who you were, why you came, and the reason the bell has been waiting for you all along.
