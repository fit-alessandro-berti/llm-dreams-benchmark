Rain lashes your face like handfuls of cold gravel, and the deck pitches so violently that the horizon tilts into a dizzying spiral of slate and foam. You open your mouth to bark an order—*“Reef the mainsail! Batten down the hatches!”*—but the wind tears the words from your throat, carrying them away like startled gulls.

Around you, the crew huddles near the base of the mast, their oilskins slick and dark, eyes wide with a terror they are trying hard to hide. The bosun, a man whose hands are usually as steady as iron, is clutching a life-line with trembling fingers. They wait. They need your certainty more than they need the sails trimmed.

But then, the thunder changes. It doesn't just roll—it *speaks*. A low, rhythmic vibration travels up through the soles of your boots, into your bones, and settles in your teeth. The sea, moments ago a churning nightmare, suddenly stills. Not calm, but *expectant*. The waves freeze in mid-curl like sculpted glass. Lightning forks down, but instead of a crack, it releases a sound like a thousand wind chimes.

You look up. The clouds have torn open, not to reveal stars or moon, but a vast, glowing doorway made of driftwood and pearl, hovering impossibly above the bowsprit. The crew follows your gaze, their fear melting into a wide-eyed, silent wonder. You realize, with a jolt that feels like stepping off a cliff, that the storm was never the enemy—it was the key.

Taking a breath of air that suddenly smells of rain, cinnamon, and old paper, you turn the wheel hard to starboard. "Hold fast," you say, and your voice is suddenly clear, cutting through the silence. "We sail through."

The frozen waves begin to move again, lifting the hull gently as if the ocean itself is cradling you. As the ship glides toward the luminous threshold, you glance at your crew and see their faces bathed in warm gold. And just before you cross over, you notice the one thing that makes your dream-heart hammer against your ribs: standing on the other side of the doorway, waiting with a lantern in hand, is a figure wearing your own face—only older, and smiling as if they've been expecting you all along.
