You lower your voice, though your hands are still clenched. Your friend keeps speaking, but the words blur — not because you can’t hear them, but because the room is suddenly filling with water. It rises around your ankles, then your knees, cold and silver under the kitchen light.

The thing you were arguing about changes shape every time you look at it. First it’s a letter on the table, then a photograph, then a locked door, then a small bird made of glass trembling between you.

Your friend says, “I thought you understood.”

You want to explain yourself, to build a careful defense, but instead you say, “I don’t. Not completely. Tell me.”

The water stops rising.

Your friend’s face softens, but only for a second — long enough for you to see that they’re not angry so much as afraid. Then the dream tilts. The walls fold inward like paper. The glass bird cracks, but no sound comes out.

You reach across the table anyway.

Before your fingers touch theirs, you wake up with your heart pounding, already reaching for your phone.
