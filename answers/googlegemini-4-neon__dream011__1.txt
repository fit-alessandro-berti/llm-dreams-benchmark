I blink hard, rubbing my eyes, and manage to pin down Question 1 before it can swim away: *Using only the scent of wet pavement, calculate the exact year your childhood dog learned how to read.*

A cold wave of dread washes over me. I didn't study this. In fact, as I stare at the booklet, a horrible realization settles in my stomach—I haven't been to this class since the first Tuesday of the semester. I forgot I was even enrolled. Yet somehow, my entire future as a Licensed Cloud Surveyor depends on passing this test.

I glance around the vast lecture hall, hoping to see someone else looking confused, but the other students are writing furiously. The scratching of their pencils is deafening, rising and falling in unison like a swarm of cicadas. When I look down to grab my own pen, I realize it’s just a stalk of limp celery. Worse, I am no longer sitting at a wooden desk; I’m sitting in a porcelain clawfoot bathtub filled with dry autumn leaves, parked right in the center aisle. Nobody seems to notice or care.

"Fifteen minutes remaining," the proctor announces. 

I look up at the front of the room. The professor is wearing a heavy velvet robe and has the face of my tenth-grade gym teacher, except he is slowly inflating like a balloon. He taps a wristwatch that is actually a tiny, glowing compass. "Remember to show your work," he says, his voice echoing as if we are underwater. "And do not forget to hand in your shoes with your scantron."

Panic makes my limbs feel like they are encased in wet cement. I grip the celery stalk and try to write an answer anyway, pressing hard into the paper, leaving only a faint, watery green streak. I flip to the next page, desperate for a multiple-choice question I can guess on, only to find the pages have fused together into a thick, warm mirror. 

In the reflection, the lecture hall ceiling is gone, replaced by an endless twilight sky. The paper begins to vibrate in my hands, folding itself rapidly into a massive origami bird. I lunge forward to grab it before it can fly off with my grade, tumbling over the edge of the bathtub—

—only there is no floor beneath me. I fall through a sudden layer of cool mist, the sound of scratching pencils fading into the rush of wind, and land softly in the backseat of a moving car with no one behind the wheel, still clutching the stalk of celery.
