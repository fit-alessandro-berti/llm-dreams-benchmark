Looking out across the rain-slicked deck, you notice the crew's faces flickering in the flashes of lightning. They aren't panicking; they are just waiting, utterly silent, staring at you through the pouring rain.

Ahead of the bow, a wave rises impossibly high, towering like a mountain of black ink. Instead of crashing down, it freezes at its peak, curving over the ship like a vaulted ceiling of dark water. Inside the suspended wave, faint shapes are moving—not fish, but glowing white birds and drifting paper lanterns.

A sudden, quiet certainty washes over you, the kind that only makes sense here: you aren't supposed to steer *around* the storm. 

You let go of the glass wheel. Walking to the bow, you raise your hand and point straight up into the eye of the clouds. 

The crew doesn't hesitate. They cut the rigging, and instead of falling, the torn sails catch an upward draft and billow like giant white wings. The ship groans, lifts cleanly off the churning surface, and begins to sail straight up the vertical wall of the frozen wave. 

Then, all at once, the thunder cuts off—as if someone in another room just closed a heavy door. The rain stops falling, hanging suspended in the air as thousands of silver beads that chime softly like wind chimes as the ship glides through them. 

You break through the crest and emerge into a vast, breathless calm. The sea is gone, replaced by an endless horizon of slow-moving lavender clouds lit by two pale moons. The deck is suddenly warm and dry beneath your bare feet. Your crew is leaning over the railings, smiling, reaching out to catch tiny stars drifting past like snow. 

You look down at the brass compass resting in your palm. The needle isn't pointing north anymore—it’s spinning gently, glowing faintly gold, pointing everywhere at once.
