...not written in standard ink, but printed in shifting, luminescent script that changes every time I blink. 

The first question reads: *“Calculate the exact weight of the choice you didn’t make five years ago. Show your work in lost seconds.”*

My heart races. I flip the page, desperate for something recognizable—an equation, an essay prompt, anything from the syllabus I spent weeks studying. But Question 2 asks: *“If a memory catches fire in an empty room, who inherits the heat? (Select all that apply.)”*

Question 3 is simply a mirror taped to the page, with a single instruction underneath: *“Identify the stranger looking back at you.”*

I look up to see if anyone else is confused, but the lecture hall has stretched into an impossibly vast, cathedral-like expanse. The other students are writing furiously, their pens humming like tuning forks, leaving trails of silver light across their desks. At the front of the room, the proctor slowly dissolves into a flight of pale white moths, yet their voice still echoes over the silence: *“Ten minutes remaining. Please ensure your identity is clearly marked.”*

I look down at my hands. The pencil between my fingers has softened into warm clay, molding to my grip. 

Suddenly, the cold panic in my chest gives way to a strange, weightless calm. I realize the exam isn't testing what I know about the world—it’s asking what kind of world I want to create next. 

I press the pencil to the paper. I don’t write words; I draw a door, and as the graphite line meets the edge of the page, the handle turns.
