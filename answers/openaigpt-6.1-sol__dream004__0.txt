When you reach for the brush, it turns into a small silver fish. It slips between your fingers and swims into the canvas, leaving behind a blue door.

You open it.

On the other side is the room where you slept as a child, except the ceiling is a slow-moving sea. Boats drift above your bed. Somewhere, someone is peeling an orange, and the scent makes you remember something that hasn’t happened yet.

A woman made of folded paper sits by the window.

“You’re late,” she says gently.

“For what?”

She points to the blank canvas, which is now standing behind you.

“For choosing.”

You look at its white surface. Shapes gather beneath it like animals beneath ice: a mountain, a face, a staircase descending into sunlight. Whenever you try to recognize one, it becomes another.

“What if I choose wrong?”

The paper woman unfolds one hand. In her palm is a tiny yellow square, warm as a patch of afternoon.

“Then you can paint over it.”

You take the square and press it to the canvas.

The room fills with yellow—not the color, exactly, but everything yellow has ever meant to you. Buttercups. Kitchen light. A coat you once lost. The brief warmth of someone’s hand.

Above you, the boats lower their anchors.

One catches gently on your sleeve.

You climb aboard, and as the sea carries you through the ceiling, you look back. The woman has become a sheet of paper again. On it, in your own handwriting, are the words:

*Leave the door open.*

So you do.
