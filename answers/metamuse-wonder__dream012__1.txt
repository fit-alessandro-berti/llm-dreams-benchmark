...and you realize, suddenly and with absolute dream-certainty, that the storm isn't listening to the wind at all. It's listening to *you*.

Every time your heart pounds faster, the waves rise higher. Every time you hold your breath, the rain hangs frozen in the air.

Your first mate grabs your sleeve, shouting over a thunder that sounds strangely like your own name being called from far away. "Captain! Orders?!"

You steady yourself against the railing. The compass in your hand isn't pointing north anymore — it's spinning slowly, and instead of letters, it has little doors around its edge. One is slightly ajar, spilling warm, golden light onto your wet hands.

You don't shout. You don't need to.

"Everyone," you say, and your voice comes out calm and much larger than it should, rolling across the deck like calm water, "let go of the ropes."

They stare at you.

"The ship knows," you tell them, though you're just as surprised by the words as they are. "We’ve been trying to fight the sea in the wrong direction."

One by one, your crew — who, you notice now, all have your eyes, your hands at different ages, one impossibly small version of you in an oversized captain's hat — let go.

The ship doesn't capsize. It *lifts*.

Not wrecked, not sinking — rising, gently peeling itself free from the crest of a wave as if the ocean was only ever holding it cupped in its palm. The rain turns upward. The lightning stops cracking and instead blooms silently in the clouds, in colors you don't have names for, like flowers opening at night.

The turbulent sea below you smooths itself into dark glass. In it, you can see a perfect reflection of your ship sailing through stars.

Your tiny double tugs at your coat. "Are we flying, Captain?"

You look toward the horizon, where the storm is parting — not breaking apart, but politely stepping aside to reveal a coastline made entirely of soft morning light, with a lighthouse whose beam is a staircase.

"No," you smile, turning the wheel not left, not right, but *inward*, toward that open little door in the compass. "We're finally sailing the right way."

And as the ship glides forward without wind, without fear, the whole crew begins to hum a song you haven't heard since childhood — but somehow, you remember every word.
