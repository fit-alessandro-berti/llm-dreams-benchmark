You start to wander the labyrinthine streets, the architecture shifting like wet clay—towers melting into alleys, clocks melting into doorways. A crumpled map materializes in your pocket, but the ink keeps rearranging itself into new languages. Strangers you pass all wear faces you almost recognize from childhood, yet they speak only in riddles about “the meeting that already happened yesterday.” You hail a taxi that turns out to be a giant mechanical bird; it deposits you on a rooftop garden where the important meeting is already in progress… and you are both the late arrival and the person everyone has been waiting for. The agenda is written in light on the sky, and as you sit down the entire city folds itself into a single quiet room that smells like rain and old books. You wake with the feeling that you finally understood something important, though the details dissolve the moment you try to hold them.
