You start to ask for directions, but everyone you approach points somewhere different. A woman with a glass umbrella tells you to follow the river; a boy carrying a ladder says the river has moved.

Then you notice the city’s clocks all show the same time: one hour from now.

You hurry through streets that rearrange themselves behind you. At last, you reach a station where trains arrive without tracks. One opens its doors, and inside you see the meeting room: a long table, a pitcher of water, and a chair with your name on it.

You step aboard. The doors close. The train begins to climb.

When it stops, you are standing in the lobby of a familiar building, though you have never been there before. Someone at the desk smiles and says, “They’re waiting for you.”

You enter the meeting room. Everyone turns toward you—not impatiently, but with the quiet relief of people who have been waiting a very long time.

Then you realize you’ve forgotten what you came to say. You open your mouth, and a flock of small, bright birds flies out. The others nod, as if this is exactly what they expected.
