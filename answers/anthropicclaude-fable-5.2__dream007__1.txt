...stop talking mid-sentence, because you've noticed that the words leaving your mouth have become small paper birds. They hang in the air between you, wings trembling, each one carrying a piece of what you were trying to say.

Your friend's words are there too, though theirs have taken the shape of moths. The two flocks circle each other warily above the kitchen table, which is also somehow the shore of a lake you visited as a child.

You realize you can't remember what the argument was about. You only know it mattered, the way things in dreams matter: completely, and without explanation.

So you reach up and catch one of your friend's moths. It doesn't struggle. When you open your hand, you can read what's written on its wings, not the angry thing they said but the thing underneath it: *I'm afraid you'll stop needing me.*

You look up. Your friend is holding one of your paper birds, reading it with an expression you haven't seen since you were both much younger. You don't know what it says. You're a little afraid to know.

The lake laps at the legs of the table. The sky has turned the deep orange of a late hour, though there's no sun anywhere.

"I didn't know that's what I meant," you say, and this time the words come out as words.

Your friend laughs, the real laugh, the one that makes their shoulders shake. They fold the paper bird gently into their pocket. "You never do," they say. "That's why I keep them."

Then the two of you are walking along the shoreline, the argument dissolving behind you like salt in water, while overhead the birds and moths fly together in one drifting constellation, heading somewhere warm.
