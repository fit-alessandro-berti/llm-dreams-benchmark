...you decide to leave. The argument matters too much to finish having here — though you notice, dimly, that "here" has become the parking lot of your elementary school, and neither of you remembers driving there.

Your friend calls your name, but it comes out in your mother's voice. When you turn around, they're holding the thing you were fighting about — except it isn't a thing at all. It's a handful of water, leaking through their fingers, and you understand with sudden dream-certainty that this is what the fight was really about: the water. The losing of it.

You want to apologize. You open your mouth and a train whistle comes out.

Then you're both on the train, sitting across from each other, not arguing anymore, watching the same window. Every house going past is a house you've lived in. Your friend turns to you and says something — you can see their mouth moving, you know it's important, you can feel that it's the answer to everything — but the wheels are too loud.

You lean closer.

You wake with the sentence still pressed against the edge of your ear, and all morning you keep almost remembering what they said.
