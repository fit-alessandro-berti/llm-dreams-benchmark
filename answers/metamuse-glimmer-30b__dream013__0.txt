You are in a maze filled with complex puzzles. Each puzzle requires intense concentration and logical thinking to solve, and as you move deeper into the maze, you realize the walls are getting closer.

The corridors narrow. The puzzles stop being mechanical. The first one was a simple lock with tumblers that clicked into place when you remembered your childhood phone number. The next was a sliding tile that only moved when you admitted something you hadn't told anyone. Now the puzzles are just questions written in light on the stone.

*What are you still trying to prove?*
*Who are you running from?*

You solve them without touching them. You just think the answer, and the wall sighs and opens.

The air grows warm and smells faintly of ozone and old paper. The maze floor becomes soft, like the carpet in your first apartment. The complex logic gives way to something simpler, something you can't solve with concentration at all.

You find a room at the center with no exit. In the middle is a table with a single chair, and you are already sitting in it. On the table is a smaller version of the maze you just walked through, made of glass and string.

And then you understand: you weren't solving the maze. The maze was solving you, stripping away every unnecessary corridor until only this one moment was left.

You open your eyes and you're still dreaming, and you wonder if you'll wake up before you finish the last puzzle.
