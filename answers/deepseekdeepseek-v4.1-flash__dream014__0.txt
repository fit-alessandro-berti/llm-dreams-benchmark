A man in a yellow raincoat buys you tea from a corner stall that smells like your grandmother’s kitchen. A child hands you a paper crane and whispers, “This one knows the way.”

Together you cross bridges that hum like cellos, pass markets where vendors sell forgotten names, and climb to a rooftop where the whole city glows beneath you like a circuit board of stars. “There,” they say, pointing not to a street but to a feeling in your chest. “That’s your direction.”

You try to thank them, but they are already fading into the crowd, each one becoming a stranger again. You wake with the paper crane still warm in your hand—or maybe it’s just your heart, finally oriented.
