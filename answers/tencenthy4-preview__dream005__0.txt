A group in feathered masks is waltzing on the ceiling; another huddles in the corner, solemnly planting seeds in the carpet and weeping when they instantly sprout into tiny silver spoons. You navigate through them, calling out the name of the one person you know, but your voice emerges as a swarm of monarch butterflies that flutter away before anyone can hear it.

You wander into a hall where a banquet is laid out, but all the food is made of memory—roasts that smell like your tenth birthday, soups that taste like your first heartbreak. At the head of the table sits the person you’ve been looking for, but as you approach, they stand and gracefully merge into the wallpaper, becoming just another pattern in the roses. 

A hand touches your shoulder. You turn, and it’s them—but they look different, or maybe you do. "You’ve been looking everywhere," they say, and their voice sounds like a key turning in a lock. "But I was the one holding the door open the whole time." 

Then the floor tilts, and the entire gathering slides sideways into the face of a large pocket watch, ticking loudly. The hands of the watch spin wildly, and just as you realize you are terribly late for something you cannot name, the ticking transforms into the sound of an alarm clock. It cuts through the dream like a knife, and you wake up, your hand still outstretched, grasping only the cool, empty edge of your pillow.
