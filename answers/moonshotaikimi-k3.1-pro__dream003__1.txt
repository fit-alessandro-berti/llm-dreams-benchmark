There was no keyhole, only a shallow groove around the lid. When you ran your finger along it, the box began to hum. You recognized the melody at once—not with your mind, but with the backs of your knees, the soles of your feet. It was the song the sea sings just before a storm.

You hummed the missing note.

The box opened without a sound.

Inside lay a small key made of blue glass, resting on a bed of salt. The moment you picked it up, the room around you filled with the smell of rain. A door appeared in the wall where there had been only shadow.

You understood, with the perfect certainty of dreams, that the key had never been meant for the box.

Beyond the door was a long beach beneath a colorless sky. Far out on the water, someone was waving. You couldn’t see their face, but you knew they had been waiting for you a very long time.

You walked toward them. With each step, the key grew warmer, until it melted between your fingers and became a handful of light. When you reached the water, the figure stopped waving.

“You took your time,” they said, in your own voice.

Then the tide came in—not over the sand, but across the sky—and you woke with the taste of salt on your lips. Somewhere, very faintly, you could still hear the box humming, as if it had never really closed.
