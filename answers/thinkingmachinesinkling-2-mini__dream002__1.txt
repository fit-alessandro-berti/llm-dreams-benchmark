The air goes tight and metallic, and the forest does not shrink around the storm; it waits for it. I hear a faraway bell under the roots, then the sound of water poured from the sky into my cupped hands.

I step onto the path’s edge, where the dirt shines like dark glass. A black-green wall of clouds comes rolling through the treetops, but it does not break against the trees—it bends around me. Light slices down in one cold stripe, and in it I see the forest become a staircase of mist: roots rising like whale ribs, ferns folding into wings, the river moving sideways between the trunks. The storm gathers not to punish but to reveal. For a moment I am inside the thunder—cold, bright, almost kind—and then it opens like a curtain. Not the darkness I feared, but a quiet, golden rain falling on a path I had almost forgotten I knew. I walk through it, and the forest exhales.
