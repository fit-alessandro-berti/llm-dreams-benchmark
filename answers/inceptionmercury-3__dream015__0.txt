…you decide to keep walking.

Not because you know where you’re going — you don’t. The compass in your pocket is cracked, and the maps you’ve scavenged are stained with rust and rain. But staying still feels worse, like letting the ash settle on your skin until you become part of the ruins.

So you walk.

Past overturned cars turned into gardens, their trunks bursting with wild tomatoes and weeds. Past the skeleton of a shopping mall where a clan of survivors has strung up wind chimes made of spoons and old keys. They wave at you from a balcony, and you wave back, but you don’t stop. The road is a question, and you’re determined to answer it.

You pass a radio tower that hums with static. You pause, listen. Sometimes you hear fragments of songs from the old world — a woman’s voice, a piano, the crackle of something that might have been a laugh. You wonder if someone is still broadcasting, or if it’s just memories tangled in the wires. You whisper your name into the wind, just in case.

At dusk, you find a library. Its roof has caved in, but the books are still there, scattered like fallen leaves. You light a small fire from the pages of a telephone directory — no one needs those anymore — and you read aloud to yourself from a novel about the sea. You’ve never seen the sea. You decide that tomorrow you will.

You fall asleep under a cracked dome of stars, and in your dream, you dream of water. Endless and blue and patient. When you wake, the sun is rising, and the ash has turned to gold. You stand up, brush the dust from your coat, and start walking again.

Because the world may have ended. But you haven’t. Not yet.
