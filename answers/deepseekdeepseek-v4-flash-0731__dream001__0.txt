...start to run, though the streets seem to shift every time you blink. The buildings lean in like curious giants, their windows glowing with wrong-colored light. You check your phone, but the map is written in a language your sleeping brain almost understands — arrows curling into spirals, street names that change when you look away.

You ask a passerby for directions, but they only smile and hand you a single shoe. “The meeting is at the place where the clocks forget your name,” they say, then vanish into a crowd of people walking backward.

You keep moving. The city hums with a sound like a memory of a song. You pass a café where your childhood dog is sitting alone at a table, wagging its tail. You want to stop, but the hour is thinning, and the meeting — you suddenly remember — is with yourself, from tomorrow, and they already know you’ll be late.

You turn a corner and the street opens into a vast library made of fog. A librarian floats toward you, holding an envelope sealed with a wax stamp that glows. “You made it,” she says, “right on time.” And you realize the hour never passed at all. You open the envelope. Inside is a mirror, and in it, you are already sitting at a long table, waiting patiently, smiling, gesturing for you to take the empty seat beside yourself.

You step through the mirror.

And you wake up, still dreaming, just as the meeting begins.
