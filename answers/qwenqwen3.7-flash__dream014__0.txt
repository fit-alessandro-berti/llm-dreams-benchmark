As you walk, the city breathes around you. Neon signs bleed into watercolor skies. A street vendor's cart rolls forward on silent wheels, offering cups of tea that steam into shapes: a childhood door, a half-forgotten song, the curve of a hand you've held twice. You sip, and warmth spreads outward until the edges of the world soften into gold.

They lead you to a plaza where the fountain flows upward. Droplets hang in midair like glass beads, catching the light of a sun you haven't seen since you entered this dream. The strangers stop at the edge of the water, turn to you, and bow slightly. In that gesture, you understand: they weren't leading you to a destination. They were escorting you back to yourself.

You reach out to thank them, but they're already dissolving into the mist rising from the fountain, their shapes turning into origami birds that lift into the sky. The cobblestones beneath you settle into a single, steady path. It doesn't end. It just opens.

You wake slowly, not with a start, but like a page turning in a well-loved book. Morning light filters through your blinds, painting stripes across the floor. Your room is ordinary again. But somewhere deep in your ribs, a paper lantern still burns, and you know, without doubt, that you will never be completely lost again.
