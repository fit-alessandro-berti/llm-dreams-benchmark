...and the rules keep changing every time you think you've figured them out.

At first, the competition looks normal - a bright stage, a panel of judges in sharp suits, and a line of contestants holding numbered badges. Your badge says 0. When you flip it over, it says *Everything*.

**Challenge One: The Interview of Mirrors.**

You're called into a room made entirely of mirrors. But none of them reflect you as you are now. One shows you at 8 years old, presenting a messy crayon drawing like it's the most important thing in the world. Another shows you exhausted at 2 a.m., working on something you loved even though no one asked you to. A third shows a future version of you, laughing in an office filled with sunlight and plants.

A judge with no face asks you only one question: "Why do you want this?"

When you try to give the perfect, rehearsed answer, your voice comes out as static. When you finally just speak honestly - nervous, rambling, real - the mirrors all light up at once.

You pass.

**Challenge Two: The Impossible Task.**

Next, you're led to an enormous library where the books are blank. The other contestants frantically start scribbling. The announcement booms: "Finish first to win."

But you notice a small door between the shelves that the others ignore. Behind it is a child sitting on the floor, trying to build a tower from scattered pages. He looks up and asks for help.

In dream-logic, you know: this *is* the challenge. You sit down and help him build, even though it means falling behind. As you place the last page on top, the tower glows and unfolds into a staircase made of light. The other contestants are still scribbling in their blank books, but your staircase leads upward, past the library entirely.

**Challenge Three: The Final Stage.**

At the top, there's only one chair. It’s exactly your dream job - not just a title, but a feeling. For you, it looks different than you expected. Maybe it's not a corner office or a stage with applause. Maybe it's a quiet workshop where you get to make things that help people, or a classroom, or a studio, or a lab where curiosity never runs out.

The final judge turns out to be you - the future version from the mirror. They smile and stand up from the chair and say, "I’ve been keeping this warm for you."

Just as you're about to sit down, your alarm begins to ring - but in the dream, it sounds like gentle chimes. You realize the grand prize was never about beating the other contestants. It was about remembering why you started competing in the first place.

You wake up with that warm, strange certainty that dreams give you - that you're already one step closer.
