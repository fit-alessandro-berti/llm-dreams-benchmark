…each challenge seems designed to test a different part of you.

First, you have to cross a library where the books whisper contradictory directions. You choose the one that sounds like your own voice, and the shelves swing open onto a moonlit bridge. Next, you’re asked to build something from a box of mismatched pieces. Everyone else makes something polished and perfect; you make a small, crooked boat that somehow begins to glow.

By the final round, you’re exhausted. The other contestants stand in a shining hall beneath a huge clock, waiting to be judged. Then the clock’s hands fall off and clatter across the floor. The judges look at you.

“What would you do if there were no rules?” one asks.

You glance at the glowing boat in your hands. “I’d find out where it can go.”

The boat grows until it’s large enough to climb aboard. The hall dissolves into a silver sea, and the other contestants step onto the shore as you set sail. Ahead, the horizon opens like a door.

When you reach it, you’re standing in the place you’ve always imagined working—not quite as it looked in your plans, but brighter, stranger, and full of possibility. Someone hands you a key. It fits a door you hadn’t noticed before.

Behind it is a desk, a window full of stars, and a small brass plaque with your name on it.

You wake just before you can read the title beneath your name, but the key is still warm in your palm.
