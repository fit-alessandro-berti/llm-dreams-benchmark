As the competition progresses, you face various challenges and find yourself moving through stages that feel less like tests and more like manifestations of your own inner landscape.

In the first round, you are asked to build a prototype of your dream job using only materials you have previously discarded—old ideas, fragments of past failures, and half-remembered conversations. You hesitate at first, then realize that your discarded pieces are actually the most authentic parts of you. You assemble them into something beautiful and strange: a bridge made of your own questions.

The second challenge collapses time. You must interview for the position inside a clock that only runs backward. The judges are silent, and the questions they ask are not tests of skill, but invitations to voice what you truly want. Your voice trembles as you speak, and when you finish, the clock stops—not because you failed, but because you finally told the truth.

In the final round, you enter a vast, empty office: your dream job’s headquarters. There is no trophy ceremony, only a window looking out onto a landscape of open doors. The prize is offered to you not as a title or salary, but as a condition: you must spend the first day not working, but simply sitting in the room and noticing what it feels like to belong there.

When you wake, the competition leaves no physical trophy, but the feeling of having already arrived remains in your chest like a key turned in a lock you never knew was yours.
