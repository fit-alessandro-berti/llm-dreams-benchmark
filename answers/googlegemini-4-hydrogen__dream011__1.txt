A cold knot of panic tightens in your stomach. *If I fail this, I’ll never get certified,* you think, though you can no longer remember what the certification is even for—only that your entire life depends on it. You shift in your seat and realize you aren’t wearing shoes, and the wooden desk beneath your elbows has quietly become the warm hood of an unfamiliar car.

You look back down at Question 1. The tiny crickets have settled back onto the page, rearranging themselves into fading gray letters: *"Explain, in your own words, the exact weight of a Tuesday. Show your work."*

Desperate to write *something*, you press your pencil to the paper, only to discover the graphite tip has turned into soft, pink eraser. The harder you press, the more the paper wears away—not tearing, just thinning out until a hole opens straight through the exam booklet, revealing a deep, starry night sky underneath.

The silver whistle finally blows, ringing out like a piercing alarm clock. As the sound echoes, the classroom floor tilts steeply sideways, and you begin sliding down toward the open window at the back of the room, still clutching your blank paper as the ceiling dissolves into clouds.
