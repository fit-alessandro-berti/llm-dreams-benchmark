...the conversations swirl around you like a wind you can't quite feel.

You pass a group huddled around a table, playing a card game where the cards are photographs of strangers' living rooms. "You have to play the kitchen," a woman insists, tapping a card showing floral wallpaper. "The bathroom is wild. You can't just lead with the bathroom." Everyone nods solemnly, though no one seems to be holding any cards at all.

You keep walking. The room seems to breathe — walls expanding and contracting gently, like the lungs of some enormous sleeping animal. The lighting is always late afternoon, no matter which direction you face.

In the next cluster, a man is explaining something passionately to three nodding listeners. "That's the thing about glaciers," he's saying, "they're just very patient rivers." One of the listeners turns to you and you realize with a start that she has your childhood dentist's face, but she's wearing a name tag with your own name on it. She doesn't acknowledge the strangeness. Neither do you. That's how dreams work — the impossible is simply furniture.

You drift toward the kitchen, because kitchens are where the truth of a party lives. But the hallway stretches as you walk it, the door at the end receding with each step, and when you finally arrive, the kitchen is your grandmother's kitchen, except it's standing in the middle of a field, and the field is indoors, and there are sheep at the counter drinking from coffee mugs.

"Have you seen—" you start to ask, but you realize you've forgotten the name. You only know one person here, and their name has dissolved like sugar in tea. You remember only the *feeling* of them: warm, familiar, the person-shaped absence you've been navigating toward all night.

So you start asking people, "Have you seen... you know...?" and everyone says, "Oh, absolutely, they were just here a second ago," but "a second ago" is always a different room, a different decade.

Finally, you climb a staircase that goes up and up and opens onto the rooftop, except the rooftop is a beach, and the beach is inside a snow globe that someone is holding — you can see the giant blurred face of a person peering in at you through the glass. And there, sitting on a beach towel, patting the sand beside them, is the person you were looking for.

You don't remember their name. It doesn't matter. You sit down, and they say exactly what they always say, and the ocean sounds like a crowd of people talking softly in the next room, and you think: *I'm dreaming. I'm definitely dreaming.*

And just as you turn to tell them, the snow globe tilts.
