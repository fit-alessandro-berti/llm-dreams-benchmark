One of the strangers, a tall woman with a silver scarf that never stops fluttering even though there's no wind, presses a small brass compass into your hand. "It doesn't point north," she says, smiling. "It points to where you're trying to go, even when you haven't figured that out yet."

The needle trembles, then swings toward a narrow alley you hadn't noticed. The group falls into step around you. A boy in an oversized raincoat hums a tune you somehow remember from childhood, and an older man tips his hat to pigeons that bow back to him.

At the end of the alley is a door painted the exact shade of blue from your earliest memory of home. It isn't any home you've actually lived in, but your chest loosens the moment you see it, the way it does when you hear your name spoken kindly.

"We can't go in with you," the woman says. "But we'll be here if you need to find the way back."

You turn the handle, and warm light spills over your shoes. Beyond it you can smell rain on summer pavement and something baking, and you hear a voice you love calling you by a name you'd forgotten you had.

You step through. Behind you, the city's hum softens into a lullaby, and as the door closes you realize you're no longer lost, and perhaps you never were...

...and then the dream begins to dissolve at the edges, leaving only the warmth, the way a good dream does when morning comes.
