...plant a garden.

It seems absurd, even in the dream. The sky is the color of weak tea, and the ground is a patchwork of ash and broken glass. But in your coat pocket you find a single seed, smooth and warm as if someone had only just set it there. You don't remember picking it up. In dreams, you don't have to.

You clear a square of earth beside the skeleton of an overpass. Rust flakes off the rebar and drifts down like red snow. You dig with a dented hubcap, and somewhere nearby a wind chime made of old spoons and keys begins to ring, though you never hung one.

You press the seed into the soil and pour out the last of your water. The dream lets you feel how much you're giving up, the dry tongue and the long road ahead, and you do it anyway.

Nothing happens at first. Then the dust around the spot darkens, and a green thread unspools upward, faster than any plant should grow. It climbs the concrete and splits into leaves, and the leaves open into pale blue flowers that glow faintly, like lanterns.

Out past the ruins, other lights flicker on. One, then three, then dozens. Someone else's garden. Someone else's seed. You realize you were never the only one who decided to try.

A figure steps out from behind a wall of twisted rebar and raises a hand, not in warning but in greeting. They're carrying a watering can, and somehow it's full.

The sky shifts from tea to honey, and as you walk toward them, the dream starts to soften at the edges. The last thing you notice is the smell of rain, real rain, coming from a long way off.

Then you wake, with dirt under your fingernails that you can't quite explain.
