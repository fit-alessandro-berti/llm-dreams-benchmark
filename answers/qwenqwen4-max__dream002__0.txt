...and the light goes the color of weak tea. All the leaves turn their pale undersides up at once, like a crowd showing you their palms.

You know, the way you know things in dreams, that you've left something on the stove. There is no stove. There has never been a stove. But the urgency is enormous and it lives in your teeth.

The first drops fall upward. They come out of the moss and rise past your face, warm, tasting faintly of pennies, and you think *of course, I should have brought the other umbrella* — and you're holding an umbrella, you've always been holding it, and it's made of bread.

The thunder isn't thunder. It's the sound of furniture being dragged across the floor of a room above you, and you understand that someone is rearranging the forest and you weren't consulted.

You start walking faster and the path becomes a hallway. Carpet, slightly damp. The trees are still there but they're indoors now, and their branches go up into a ceiling with a water stain shaped like your grandmother's profile. She's here too, actually, standing by a coat rack, except she's a deer and she's also your friend Marcus from years ago, and none of this requires explanation. She says, "You're early, they haven't finished the sky."

Behind her, through a doorway, the storm is being assembled — big grey panels of it leaning against a wall, waiting to be hung. Two men in overalls are arguing about where the lightning should go. One of them looks at you and you realize you're not wearing anything below the waist, and the shame is so total, so tidal, that the hallway tilts.

You run. The running is the wrong kind, thick, like pulling yourself hand over hand through the inside of a mattress. There's a door at the end that you know is your own front door, and your key is in your hand, and the key is a small live bird, and you're trying to be gentle with it while also fitting it into the lock, and the bird is saying something you almost understand, and the storm panels are going up behind you one by one, and you can hear the sky being nailed into place—
