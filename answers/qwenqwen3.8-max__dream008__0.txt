You are at your workplace, and suddenly, you are given a project with an impossible deadline. Your boss is waiting for you to start, and you realize your hands are full of papers that keep multiplying, sliding off the desk like white birds trying to escape.

You open your laptop, but the screen shows a countdown: **00:05:00**. Every time you blink, another minute disappears. Your boss stands behind you, silent, watching, though when you glance back, his face seems to blur like wet paint.

You begin typing anyway. With each keystroke, the office changes. The walls stretch upward. The ceiling becomes a night sky. Your coworkers turn into shadows moving in slow motion, their voices sounding like distant radio static.

Then you understand: the project isn’t a report or a presentation. It’s a door. You have to build it before the deadline, or you’ll be trapped in this moment forever.

You work faster. You gather fragments of things—an old email, a coffee-stained note, a line from a childhood song, the hum of the fluorescent lights—and somehow they fit together perfectly. The door appears in front of your desk, glowing faintly at the edges.

The countdown reaches **00:00:03**.

Your boss leans in and whispers, “Are you finished?”

You place your hand on the door and say, “Yes.”

The door opens into morning sunlight. Your office dissolves. You step through, and behind you the impossible deadline folds itself into a small paper crane and flies away.

You wake up with the strange, calm feeling that you have just solved something important—not the project, but the fear of it.
