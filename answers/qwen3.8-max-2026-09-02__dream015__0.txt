Despite the hardships, you decide to keep walking toward the faint green light flickering on the horizon.

At first, you tell yourself it is only a trick of the ruined sky — a reflection from old glass, a fire burning in some abandoned tower, or perhaps just the dream itself inventing hope where none should exist. But the light does not vanish. It breathes softly through the haze, steady as a heartbeat.

You pass through hollow cities where wind moves through broken buildings like a mournful song. You pass fields turned to ash and roads split open by time. Your throat is dry, your hands are raw, and every shadow seems to hide something hungry. Still, you walk.

Eventually, you reach the edge of a vast crater. There, at its center, stands a single tree.

It should not be possible. Its leaves glow with a quiet emerald light, and its roots spread across the broken earth like veins of silver. Beneath its branches sit a few survivors — tired, dirty, silent — but alive. They do not look frightened. They look as though they have been waiting.

One of them rises and offers you a cup of water. It is clearer than anything you have seen in this ruined world. When you drink, the taste reminds you of rain, of mornings before everything fell apart, of something forgotten but never truly lost.

Then the tree’s leaves stir, though there is no wind. From its branches fall seeds, shining softly in the dim air. The survivors gather them carefully and press them into the cracked ground. Where each seed lands, a small green shoot appears.

You understand then that the dream is not only about survival.

It is about what people choose to grow after everything has been taken from them.

You kneel and plant a seed yourself. The earth is warm beneath your fingers. For the first time since entering this broken world, you feel no fear.

When you look up again, the green light has spread across the horizon — not as one distant flame, but as a thousand small fires waking across the wasteland.

And somewhere inside the dream, you realize: the apocalypse did not end the world.

It only revealed what still wanted to live.
