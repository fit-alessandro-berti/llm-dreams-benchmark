You are feeling lost and alone in a bustling city. Suddenly, a group of kind strangers offers to help you find your way, and you almost don't believe them.

They don't crowd you. A woman in a yellow raincoat holds out a paper map that keeps folding and unfolding itself, the streets on it rewriting as you watch. A boy with too many pockets offers you a warm paper cup you didn't ask for, and the steam spells out your name in the air before dissolving. An old man with a soft voice just points.

You say yes, because the alternative is standing on the corner forever while the neon signs buzz too loud and everyone walks through you like you're glass.

They walk with you, talking gently about things that don't matter — the best time to buy oranges, a song their mother used to hum, where the light hits the river at 4 a.m. — and the city starts to change with them. The buildings lean in to listen. The crosswalks stretch longer, then shorter, then become a hallway of laundry lines strung between skyscrapers.

You start to recognize one of the streets they’re guiding you down. It’s the street from your childhood home, except it’s in the middle of this city, and the house is there with its door open and light on.

You realize you’re not following them to a place. You’re following them back to yourself. And just as you reach out to thank them, they all turn into pigeons, or into reflections in a shop window, or simply smile and keep walking without you — because in dreams, help is only ever offered long enough for you to remember you weren’t really alone.
