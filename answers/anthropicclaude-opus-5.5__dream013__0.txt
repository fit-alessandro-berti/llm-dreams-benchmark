...notice that the walls are changing. At first they were cold gray stone, but now they shimmer faintly, like the surface of water seen from below. Each time you solve a puzzle, the maze remembers it. You can feel it remembering.

The first puzzles were simple: a door that opened only when you arranged seven brass gears so their teeth told the time backward, a floor of tiles that had to be crossed in the order of prime numbers. But deeper in, the puzzles begin to know you. One asks you to balance a scale using only memories. You place the smell of your childhood kitchen on one side, and it takes three forgotten birthdays to even it out. The scale clicks, and a passage slides open with a sound like someone exhaling.

The corridors narrow. You hear footsteps that match yours exactly, a half-second late. When you stop, they stop. When you turn a corner, you catch a glimpse of someone disappearing around the next one, someone wearing your coat.

You reach a circular chamber with a ceiling of slowly rotating stars. In the center stands a pedestal holding a single sheet of paper. It is a puzzle written in your own handwriting, though you don't remember writing it. The question is simple:

*What are you looking for?*

Below it are dozens of crossed-out answers, all in your handwriting. *The exit. The center. The answer. Myself. Nothing.* Each one is struck through with a firm, certain line, as though you have stood here many times before and been wrong every time.

You pick up the pen lying beside the paper. It's warm, as if someone just set it down. The footsteps behind you have stopped, and you know without turning that the person in your coat is standing in the doorway, waiting to see what you'll write.

You think about all the puzzles behind you: the gears, the tiles, the scale of memories. You realize none of them were locks. They were questions. The maze was never trying to keep you out. It was trying to ask you something, over and over, in every language it knew.

So you don't write an answer. Instead, beneath all the crossed-out lines, you write:

*I'm not looking. I'm listening.*

The paper grows warm in your hands. The stars overhead stop rotating. The walls of the chamber soften like wax, then fold outward like the petals of a flower, and you see that the maze was never built of stone at all. It was built of pages, thousands of them, all the thoughts you've ever left half-finished, stacked into corridors.

The figure in your coat steps forward. Up close, they look tired but kind, and older than you. They take the pen gently from your hand.

"Took you long enough," they say, smiling. "Now it's my turn to wake up."

And the last thing you hear, as the pages flutter around you like birds taking flight, is the soft sound of someone, somewhere, beginning to write the next puzzle.
