...deliver the letter clutched in your left hand before the tide slips out of the harbor.

You don’t remember who gave it to you, or what is written inside, only the heavy, breathless certainty that it cannot be late. The parchment is thick and warm against your palm, folded shut with a dollop of dark crimson wax stamped with the shape of a crescent moon. 

Around you, the city breathes in a rhythm you have never heard before. There is no distant hum of traffic, no buzz of streetlights, no sharp glow of screens—only the clatter of iron-rimmed cartwheels over uneven cobblestones, the murmur of voices speaking a cadence you somehow understand without knowing a single word, and the thick, layered scent of woodsmoke, wet wool, and sea salt. Towering houses of plaster and dark timber lean toward one another across the narrow lane, nearly touching at the eaves, blotting out all but a ribbon of bruised violet sky.

You start walking downhill, guided by the cry of gulls and the pull of the ocean air. Merchants in coarse linen tunics call out over baskets of figs, dried herbs, and hammered copper pots. No one stops you, though a few glance down with mild curiosity—and looking down yourself, you realize you are barefoot, the ancient stones shockingly cold beneath your soles. Yet it doesn’t hurt. With every step, the lane stretches longer and steeper, bending in ways that shouldn't be possible, until the cobblestones seamlessly dissolve into weathered wooden planks slick with sea spray.

The harbor opens before you like a vast amphitheater of mist. Massive wooden ships creak softly in the dark water, their furled sails looming like sleeping ghosts. Hanging from iron hooks along the quay are glass lanterns burning with tallow candles, casting shaky pools of amber light across the fog. 

At the very edge of the longest pier stands a crooked wooden shack on stilts, crowded with rolled maps, brass astrolabes, and jars of shimmering ink. Inside, an old man with silver hair and fingers stained midnight blue is bent over a drafting table, tracing constellations onto sheepskin with a feather quill. 

Before you can even knock, he looks up, his eyes bright and unsurprised. "Just in time," he says softly, holding out a scarred hand. "The water is already turning."

You step forward and place the letter into his palm. The moment his fingers close over the parchment, a deep, resonant bell begins to toll somewhere out in the fog—slow at first, then faster, shifting in pitch until it sounds strangely like a distant chime. The old man cracks the wax seal, and instead of paper unfolding, a rush of cool, white light spills across the table, washing over the wooden planks, the dark ships, and the scent of salt, lifting you gently upward until the harbor fades entirely into the quiet gray of morning.
