You start to run, though your feet feel like they’re moving through honey while the streets tilt slightly upward, as if the city itself is trying to spill its secret. The buildings are all made of different eras—glass towers with Gothic arches, subway entrances carved into marble, windows that flicker with scenes you almost recognize but cannot place. Somewhere ahead, a clock chimes thirteen tones, and you realize the hour is counting down backward from your own heartbeat.

A woman in a postman’s uniform steps into your path and holds out a map that is blank except for a single glowing word: *Remember*. You try to ask where the meeting is, but your voice comes out as a train whistle. She nods as if this makes perfect sense, taps your chest with a wax stamp, and says, “The address is what you lost.” Before you can answer, she melts into the crowd.

The streets begin folding like paper. Bridges rise from sidewalks, staircases uncoil from lampposts, and doorways appear in the middle of roads. You choose the nearest door and step through, expecting an alley, but find yourself standing in the lobby of a hotel made entirely of mirrors. Each mirror reflects you at a different age—child, teenager, old man, version wearing a suit you’ve never owned. One reflection mouths the time: *Nine minutes left*.

You run toward an elevator whose doors open into a sky full of clocks instead of cars. You board, and the elevator descends upward through clouds shaped like office buildings, like waiting rooms, like your mother’s kitchen. The pressure in your ears sounds like a phone ringing. You answer it.

A familiar voice says, “You’re late,” and you shout, “I don’t even know where the meeting is!” The voice softens. “You do. You’ve always known. It’s the place you stopped believing.” The elevator stops. The doors open.

You step into a quiet room that looks exactly like a place you can never quite picture while awake—warm light, dust floating slowly, a table set with two cups. There are no notes, no agenda, no strangers. Only yourself, sitting opposite you, looking tired and calm and deeply familiar.

It says, “You made it.”

And just then, the city begins to wake you up, gently pulling the dream back into the sea of sleep.
