The first note doesn't just echo off the hollowed-out skyscrapers—it ripples visibly through the air like water. Where the soundwaves touch the gray ash coating the ground, tiny, bioluminescent blue flowers instantly sprout and bloom. 

From the shadows of an overturned subway car, the dangers of this world begin to emerge. Scavengers with eyes like shattered mirrors and jagged hounds made of rusted scrap metal and black smoke creep forward, drawn by the noise. My heart hammers, expecting an attack, wanting to run, knowing my pockets are empty of anything that could save me. 

Instead, Dream-logic takes over. I lean in and play a slow, sweeping melody—something I don't know in real life, a song that tastes like rain and old photographs. 

As the music swells, the scrap-metal hounds stop snarling. Their glowing red eyes dim to a warm, sleepy amber. They curl up at the base of the piano bench, their jagged edges softening into velvet, purring like distant, idling engines. The mirror-eyed scavengers lower their weapons, looking up as the toxic dust clouds above us begin to swirl in time with the tempo, parting to reveal three impossibly massive, silver moons.

I keep playing, realizing I am no longer starving or afraid. With every chord, the ruins around me stop decaying and start transforming—rebar twisting into brass trees, broken glass melting into clear, quiet pools. 

I reach for the final, highest note on the keyboard. When I press it, the key feels warm, like sunlight, and melts beneath my fingertip into liquid gold. The sound rings out, bright and endless, vibrating right through my chest until the highway, the moons, and the sleeping beasts dissolve into the soft, quiet dark behind my eyelids, just before I wake up.
