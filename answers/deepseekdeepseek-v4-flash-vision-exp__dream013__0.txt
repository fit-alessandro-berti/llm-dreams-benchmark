You begin to notice that the puzzles are not random at all. They are echoes of questions you’ve been avoiding—choices you buried, doors you left unopened. Each riddle you solve doesn’t just unlock a passage; it peels away a layer of forgetting. Your breath slows. The walls hum with a low, familiar music—your own heartbeat.

Deeper, the maze stops resisting you. The corridors curve like the inside of a shell, and the light softens to the color of late afternoon. You arrive at a room with no exits, only a mirror. In it, you see yourself as a child, holding a key that’s still warm from your pocket. You understand then: the final puzzle isn’t solved by logic. It’s solved by accepting that you already know the way out.

You take the key. The child smiles. The mirror ripples like water, and you step through—into a morning that smells of rain and coffee. And for just one breath, you remember everything: that the maze was never there. It was only the shape your fear made while you were asleep. Then the dream folds softly, and you wake up already reaching for the day.
