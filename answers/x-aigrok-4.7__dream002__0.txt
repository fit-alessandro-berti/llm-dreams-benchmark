You feel a storm approaching and the wind rises all at once, cool and clean, carrying the smell of rain and wet pine. The trees lean and whisper, and the first heavy drops strike the path like small stones. You keep walking, unhurried, as the light turns silver-green under the canopy. Thunder rolls somewhere far off, more like a distant drum than a threat, and the forest seems to open around you rather than close in. Rain finds the leaves, the moss, your shoulders; everything softens. Ahead, a break in the trees shows a pale clearing where the storm is already passing, and the air smells like earth after rain. You step toward it, the path still solid underfoot, the dream holding steady.
