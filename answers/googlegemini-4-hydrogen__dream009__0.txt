You don’t remember picking up the key, only that its metal is faintly warm against your palm, pulsing with the strange, urgent certainty that only exists in dreams. 

Around you, the street is alive with the sensory weight of a century long past. There is no hum of distant traffic, no buzz of streetlights, no weight of a phone against your thigh—just the sharp scent of woodsmoke, damp wool, and crushed herbs drifting from an apothecary’s open window. Timber-framed houses lean inward over the crooked cobblestones like whispering giants, their leaded-glass panes glowing with tallow candlelight.

People hurry past in cloaks and rough-spun tunics, their voices rising and falling in a clipped, archaic cadence. You shouldn't be able to understand them, yet their words settle into your mind as clearly as your own thoughts.

*"The fog's coming in off the river,"* a baker calls out as he pulls down wooden shutters. *"Best be behind locked oak before the bell tolls nine."*

You glance down at yourself and realize you're wearing a coarse linen shirt, sturdy leather boots, and a heavy cloak you’ve never seen before. Still, you feel conspicuously untethered—like a brushstroke painted onto the wrong canvas. Every shadow along the alleyways seems to stretch a little too far, and the sky overhead is a deep, bruised violet that looks painted rather than real.

A low, resonant chime echoes from a stone spire rising above the rooftops. *One.*

Your grip tightens on the brass key. Ahead, the street forks: to the left, a bustling market square is rapidly emptying into the mist; to the right, a narrow stone archway leads down toward the dark glint of a river—and standing just beneath the arch is a lantern-bearer staring directly at you, holding out an unlit candle.

*Two.*

With a sudden rush of adrenaline, you step toward the archway, knowing somehow that if you don't reach that door before the ninth chime fades, you'll wake up—and never find out what was waiting on the other side.
