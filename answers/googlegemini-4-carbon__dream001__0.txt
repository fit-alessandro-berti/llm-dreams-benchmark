The screen glows brightly, too brightly, and as you squint at it, the letters slide off the glass like raindrops on a windshield. You try to type, *Where am I going?* into the search bar, but your thumbs feel made of lead, and the keyboard only produces musical notes instead of letters. 

Looking up, the city seems to stretch vertically into infinity. Skyscrapers bend gently in the wind like blades of tall grass, their windows reflecting a sky that can’t decide if it’s dusk or dawn. You stop a passerby—a tall figure in a sharp trench coat—to ask for the time. When they turn around, they have no face, just a smooth mirror reflecting your own panicked expression. They point silently toward a subway entrance that is inexplicably floating three feet off the pavement.

Knowing you have no choice, you hoist yourself up and descend the stairs. Instantly, forty-five minutes vanish. You just *know* you only have fifteen minutes left. 

At the bottom, there is no train, only a long hallway lined with doors from your past: your childhood bedroom, your high school locker, the back door of a restaurant you visited once years ago. You start to run, though it feels like wading through chest-deep water. Every step takes monumental effort. 

*Five minutes.* 

You look down and realize with a sudden jolt of cold dread that you aren't wearing shoes, and instead of a briefcase or a laptop, you are clutching a warm loaf of sourdough bread to your chest. Somehow, in the undeniable logic of the moment, you know this bread *is* your presentation, and you haven't even sliced it yet.

At the end of the hallway stands a massive pair of mahogany double doors with a brass plaque that reads: **THE MEETING.** 

*One minute.*

Breathless, you push the heavy doors open. Instead of a boardroom, you step out onto a quiet, moonlit pier jutting into a vast, glass-still ocean. A long conference table sits right on the water's surface. Seated around it are people you haven't thought about in years—an old teacher, a childhood friend, a stranger you saw on the bus yesterday—all dressed in impeccable business attire, staring at you expectantly.

"We've been waiting," your third-grade teacher says, though her mouth doesn't move. "Tell us everything."

You step forward, place the loaf of bread on the table, and open your mouth to speak. Instead of your voice, a loud, rhythmic *beep-beep-beep* echoes out across the dark water, growing louder and sharper until the ocean, the pier, and the city dissolve into the ceiling of your bedroom.
