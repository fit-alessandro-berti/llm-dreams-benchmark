The patterns are not carved into the metal—they seem to move like liquid silver vines, rearranging themselves whenever you look away. When you touch them, tiny chimes sound from somewhere inside, though no bells exist in the dream. The box whispers a question you almost remember: “What did you lose before you slept?”

You search your memory and name the forgotten thing aloud. The lid does not unlock so much as remember how to open. Inside, there is no treasure, only a smaller locked box, a key made of moonlight, and a mirror reflecting not your face, but the back of your head—still dreaming.
