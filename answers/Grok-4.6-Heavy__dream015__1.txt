You find yourself in a post-apocalyptic world, with resources scarce and dangers everywhere. Despite the hardships, you decide to keep walking toward the thin green line on the horizon, even though the others in the bunker swore the horizon was a trick.

The city behind you is a ribcage of rust. Rain tastes like coins. At night the wind threads through broken glass and almost sounds like radio static, as if the old world is still trying to finish a sentence. Something hunts in the far streets—you hear it once, a dry metallic scrape—and you keep going anyway, a handful of scavenged seeds in one pocket and a water-stained page of star charts in the other.

On the third day you find it: not a forest, not a miracle, just a crack in the asphalt where something stubborn has pushed through. A crow wearing a bottle-cap crown watches from a melted traffic light. A child is already there, barefoot, talking to the weed as if it can answer. Behind her, a school with the roof torn off still holds a half-erased sentence on the chalkboard: *What do we owe each other?* You finish it in charcoal. You kneel. You share the last of your water. The soil drinks like it has been waiting for someone to remember how this works.

Night comes sideways. The stars are too close. You sleep with one hand in the dirt, as if the ground might answer—and you wake with soil under your fingernails that was never there when you fell asleep.
