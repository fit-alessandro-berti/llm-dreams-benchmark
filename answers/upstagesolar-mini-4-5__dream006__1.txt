The audience, a sea of expectant faces, begins to ripple in response. They aren't unblinking statues; they are shifting, breathing, turning to one another with low murmurs. One voice, clear and close, carries on the air, "Remember the day you found the hidden garden?" And another, softer, "You were never alone, you know. The roots are still holding you."

And then, I realize: the stage isn't a stage. The lights aren't spotlights; they are the filters of a thousand windows, and each face in the crowd is a reflection of a person I have loved, and a person I once was. The speech isn't about the future. It's a gentle telling of the past, and as I speak, the words dissolve into the warm, humming hum of the kettle again. And everyone in the crowd, without a single exception, closes their eyes and smiles.

I wake up to the sound of my own kettle, whistling softly, and the smell of tea.
