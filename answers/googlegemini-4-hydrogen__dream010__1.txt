At first, everything feels grounded. You’re in a cavernous, sunlit atrium packed with overly polished rivals in sharp blazers, all vying for the exact position you’ve spent your entire life wanting. The first challenge is a simple timed essay. Yet the moment the countdown starts, you look down and realize your pen has turned into a stick of celery, and the exam paper is slowly folding itself into an origami crane. Everyone else is scribbling furiously, completely unbothered, so you force yourself to write with the celery anyway—and somehow, pale green ink flows cleanly across the flapping paper wings.

You make the cut. The surviving finalists are ushered into a brass elevator that doesn't go up or down, but sideways, sliding smoothly through walls until the doors open onto a flooded subway station. 

The judges are waiting on a floating mahogany conference table. One is a severe-looking CEO you recognize from magazines; another is your fourth-grade gym teacher; the third is just a tall, impeccably tailored suit filled with swirling autumn leaves. 

"The semifinal," the gym teacher shouts through a megaphone, blowing a whistle that emits bubbles instead of sound. "Crisis management! Retrieve the quarterly projections before the tide comes in!"

You dive into the waist-deep water, which feels strangely warm, like bathwater, except your shoes keep getting heavier, sinking into thick velvet mud. Halfway across the platform, a glowing rotary phone begins to ring on a submerged podium. You lunge for it, water swirling up to your chin, and press the receiver to your ear. There is no voice on the other end—just the sound of distant wind chimes and a train rumbling underground. Acting on pure dream-logic certainty, you clear your throat and deliver a passionate three-minute pitch directly into the silence. When you hang up, the water drains instantly into the floor grates, leaving everyone completely dry.

Suddenly, you are standing alone on a narrow wooden plank suspended high above a starry, cavernous void. A blinding spotlight hits you. Your final rival has vanished, replaced by an enormous audience of faceless silhouettes leaning forward in velvet theater seats. 

"For the grand prize," echoes the voice of the autumn-leaf judge from everywhere at once, "present your portfolio."

Panic washes over you. You look down—you’re wearing mismatched socks and no shoes, and the sleek leather binder you were holding has shrunk to the size of a postage stamp. Your chest tightens with that familiar, sinking dread of being entirely unprepared. 

You pry open the tiny binder anyway. Inside isn't paper, but a single, impossibly bright spark of light. 

Following a sudden rush of instinct, you blow gently on the spark. It lifts off your palm, swirling upward into the dark void, splitting into thousands of glowing filaments that weave themselves together like a spiderweb of gold. Within seconds, an entire miniature city made of light floats suspended between you and the judges—every impossible idea you’ve ever had, shimmering in three dimensions.

The faceless audience erupts into silent applause; instead of clapping, waves of warm, golden air ripple across the plank, lifting you gently off your feet. 

You float smoothly over to the judges' balcony. The CEO steps forward and hands you a heavy brass key stamped with your own initials. 

"Congratulations," he says, though his mouth doesn't move. "The corner office is yours. You start yesterday."

Feeling a rush of pure, weightless relief, you walk over to the massive oak door behind him, slide the key into the lock, and push it open. Instead of a desk or a computer, you step out onto a quiet, sunlit beach where a hammock hangs between two pine trees—and just as you lean back to finally rest, a distant, rhythmic beeping begins to echo from somewhere high up in the clouds.
