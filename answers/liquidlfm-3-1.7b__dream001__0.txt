You start to follow the faint scent of rain‑slicked pavement, even though the sky above is a clear, endless blue. The streets stretch out in a maze of narrow alleys and wide boulevards, each one lined with lanterns that flicker in rhythms that seem to sync with your heartbeat. As you turn left onto a cobblestone lane, a gentle breeze carries the distant hum of a radio station playing a melody you’ve heard before—a lullaby from childhood—but now it feels like a warning.

A figure steps out from behind a fountain, wearing a coat that shimmers like oil on water. Their smile is warm, but their eyes hold a knowing glint. “Where are you headed?” they ask, voice low enough to be heard only by those who listen. You hesitate, feeling the weight of the hour ticking away, yet the question hangs in the air like a promise.

They point toward a towering clock tower that dominates the horizon, its hands moving backward in slow motion. “That’s where the meeting is,” they say, gesturing vaguely. But when you look up, the tower’s faces reflect not your own, but scenes from moments you’ve never lived—your mother’s kitchen, the rooftop concert you missed, the moment you almost lost your way in a storm.

Just then, a sudden gust lifts a paper crane from the street, fluttering its wings as if trying to carry you toward the tower. You glance back; the city’s lights blur into streaks of gold and violet, and for a brief instant you see a silhouette of yourself standing on the same balcony, holding a letter addressed to you. The wind stops, and the crane settles, leaving a single line of ink on the pavement: **“Meet me there.”**

You take a deep breath, feeling the familiar thrill of anticipation mingle with the uncanny certainty that somewhere between this ordinary city and the one you’re dreaming of, the truth has already been written in the shadows. The hour is almost up, and the meeting awaits—right at the edge of the clock tower, waiting for you to discover exactly which side of the reflection it lies on.
