You start to run, but the city gently refuses to be crossed. Streets unspool behind you. Traffic lights blink in colors you’ve never seen. You ask a man in a pinstripe suit for directions; he opens his mouth and a flock of paper cranes flies out, each carrying a tiny address. One lands on your shoulder: *The room where you forgot something.*

You follow a trolley that runs on rain. It takes you past a park where statues play chess with your childhood toys. The driver says, “You’re late, but you’re also early. That’s how dreams work.”

At last, a glass tower rises from the fog. Inside, every elevator opens onto a different year of your life. You step out on a floor that smells like pencil shavings and coffee. A conference room waits. Around the table sit everyone you’ve ever disappointed, including you. They don’t look angry. They look patient.

The empty chair at the head has your name on a card. You sit. The agenda has one item: *Decide what matters before the hour is up.* You reach for a pen, and the pen becomes a key. The key becomes a door. You open it.

Beyond is the ordinary morning of your bedroom, sunlight on the wall, your alarm about to ring. You wake with the strange certainty that the meeting went well—though you can’t remember a word of it—and that you were exactly where you needed to be.
