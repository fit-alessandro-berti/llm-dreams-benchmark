You are in a maze filled with complex puzzles. Each puzzle requires intense concentration and logical thinking to solve, and as you move deeper into the maze, you begin to notice that the walls are slowly closing in behind you.

The first rooms contain familiar challenges: number sequences, rotating tiles, riddles carved into stone. But soon the puzzles become stranger. One door asks you to arrange fragments of a forgotten conversation in the correct order. Another will open only when you choose which of two memories is real. Every wrong answer causes a corridor to vanish, leaving you with fewer paths forward.

At the center of the maze, you enter a circular chamber with no visible exit. In the middle stands a mirror, and beneath it is a single question:

**“What are you trying so hard to escape?”**

You search for clues, but there are none. The mirror shows you not as you are now, but at different moments in your life—hesitating before an important decision, hiding a mistake, walking away from something you wanted because you feared you would fail.

At last, you understand that the maze was never testing your intelligence. It was testing whether you could stop analyzing long enough to be honest.

You place your hand against the mirror and whisper, “I am afraid of choosing the wrong path.”

The glass becomes warm beneath your fingers. Cracks spread across its surface, flooding the chamber with golden light. The walls dissolve, revealing that the maze has been standing in the middle of an enormous open field all along.

Ahead of you are countless paths, none marked as correct.

For a moment, panic rises again. Then you notice that the ground appears beneath your feet only when you take a step.

So you choose a direction—not because you know where it leads, but because you finally understand that standing still was the only way to remain trapped.

As you walk forward, the field brightens into morning, and you wake with the strange certainty that somewhere, a door has opened.
