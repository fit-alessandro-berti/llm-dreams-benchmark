**The Dream, Continued**

*…as you move deeper into the maze, you notice that the puzzles are changing.*

At first they were honest puzzles: gears that jammed, latches that needed a sequence, a chessboard that reset itself every time you lost — and you lost a great deal, because the chessboard was always playing as your own reflection. But deeper down, the corridors shorten. The air thickens. And the puzzles begin to use your life as material.

A door that will not open until you name a memory you have spent years refusing to touch. A corridor whose length is measured in apologies. A fountain whose water rearranges itself into a map of somewhere you have never been but somehow recognize — the layout of your grandmother's kitchen, perhaps, or the inside of your own chest.

And then, at the center of the maze, where all the branching paths have quietly converged, there is a single door. It has no lock, only an inscription, and the inscription is a question:

> *I have cities, but no houses.*
> *I have forests, but no trees.*
> *I have water, but no fish.*
> *What am I?*

You answer, aloud, in the dream: *a map.*

And the door does not open.

It waits.

Because the maze has been listening to you for hours, and you have been answering everything else perfectly — every riddle, every gear, every remembered apology — and it knows now that you understand puzzles. It knows that you solved the whole maze to get here, and it wants one more thing from you. Not an answer. A truth.

So the inscription changes. It reads:

> *What do you have, but never possessed?*
> *What do you give, but never lose?*
> *What do you see, but never touch?*
> *What are you, when nothing is watching?*

And you understand, with the particular clarity that only dreams allow, that the maze is not a place you are trapped inside. It is a place you have been *building*, all this time, out of everything you have not said. Every corridor is a conversation you avoided. Every puzzle is a feeling you filed away and called *fine*.

You put your hand on the door.

It opens onto the same room you are standing in — but you are standing in it twice, and the second you is asleep.

You wake. You don't remember the answer. But your hands are warm.
