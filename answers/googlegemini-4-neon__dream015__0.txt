You don’t remember what song is on it, only that it matters more than water, more than shelter, and that if you don’t play it before the sky completely turns to ash, something vital will be lost forever. 

The city around you is a skeleton of rusted rebar and skyscrapers bent like melted wax. In the distance, shapes made of flickering television static prowl between the hollowed-out husks of cars, their footsteps sounding like crunching glass. You slip through a jagged doorway to avoid them, expecting a collapsed convenience store, and instead step straight into an endless, dimly lit subway station where the floor is ankle-deep in warm, glowing rainwater. 

Dream-logic pulls you forward. You know the turntable is at the end of the platform. 

Behind you, the static-creatures begin to pour down the tiled stairs, filling the cavernous tunnel with a deafening white noise that makes your teeth ache. You run, though your legs feel heavy, as if wading through thick syrup. The platform stretches impossibly far, the signs overhead blurring into gibberish, until you stumble onto a dry island of cracked marble.

Sitting in the center, bathed in a shaft of pale silver light from a hole in the ceiling, is an old brass gramophone. It has no wires, no power source, and its needle has been replaced by a sharp, dried rose thorn. 

The static is right behind you now, cold and suffocating, reaching out with jagged, pixelated hands. Your fingers tremble as you pull the record from your coat. It feels warm against your palms. You drop it onto the platter, give it a gentle spin with your fingertips, and lower the thorn onto the grooves.

For a breathless second, there is only a soft crackle. 

Then, the music begins—except it isn't just sound. Rich, golden light spills out of the brass horn, washing over the flooded station in rippling waves. Wherever the melody touches, the rusted iron blooms into tangled ivy, the ash turns to soft green moss, and the encroaching shadows of static unravel into flocks of shimmering, silent birds that spiral up through the ceiling.

You look up as the crumbling concrete above dissolves entirely into a clear, star-filled night. The floor beneath you softens, giving way like a cloud, and you begin to float weightlessly upward, carried by the warm, familiar hum of a song you almost recognize—until the melody slowly blurs into the steady, rhythmic beep of your alarm clock.
