You step onto the deck, the cold spray of the sea biting your cheeks, and raise your hand to the helm. “Listen up, men—hold fast!” you bellow, the words echoing off the weather‑swept walls. The waves now roar like a thousand drums, each crest threatening to tear the rigging from its moorings. I feel the tremor in the mast, the wind tugging at the sails, and I glance toward the horizon where a thin line of silver flickers—just a lighthouse, its beam cutting through the bruised clouds.

“Turn west,” I order, pointing to the glimmer. “Follow the light; it’s the way home.” My crew tightens their belts, eyes wide with fear and hope. One by one, they heave their anchors, brace themselves against the lashing, and chant an old hymn of courage. The storm rages, thunder cracking the sky, yet the ship holds its course, the hull steadied by the ironwork and the steadfast hearts within.

When the final surge rolls over the bow, the sea sighs back into calm. The lighthouse’s beam steadies, casting a warm circle around us. The crew erupts in cheers, their faces illuminated by the soft glow. I lower my gaze to the water, feeling the pulse of the ocean beneath me, and smile. In the midst of chaos, I have shown them that leadership isn’t about commanding the storm—it’s about guiding others through it, trusting the stars and the spirit of those who sail beside you.
