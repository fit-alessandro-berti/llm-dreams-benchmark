...each one seems to be having a conversation you almost understand.

The first group stands in a tight circle near the door, laughing at a joke whose punchline you just missed. When you ask if they've seen your friend, a woman in a green dress says, "Oh, they were just here," and points down a hallway you're sure wasn't there a moment ago.

The hallway opens into a room much larger than the house should allow. A group sits cross-legged on the floor, passing around a bowl of something that glows faintly. Soup, maybe, or moonlight. They offer you some. It tastes like the smell of your grandmother's kitchen. "Your friend went upstairs," one of them says, though you never asked.

The stairs go on longer than they should. Partway up, you pass a cluster of people speaking a language made entirely of names. You catch your own, said warmly, the way someone says it when they've been telling a fond story about you. They nod as you pass, as if you've met before. Maybe you have.

At the top there is a balcony, though you're somehow also still indoors, with stars scattered across the ceiling. A string quartet plays a song you knew as a child but can't place. Everyone here is dancing slowly, and you realize you're no longer anxious about being a stranger. Somewhere along the way, people began greeting you like an old friend.

Then you see them across the room, the one person you know. They turn and smile, completely unsurprised, as though you'd agreed to meet at exactly this spot.

"There you are," they say. "I've been telling everyone about you."

You open your mouth to answer, but the music swells, the stars on the ceiling begin to drift like snow, and you realize you can't remember which of you arrived first, or whether the party was ever for anyone else at all.
