You wake up in a completely different era, with no modern technology around. As you explore your surroundings, you realize you need to **find a way to blend in before the sun sets**, because the village elder has warned that strangers are not welcomed after dark.  

The room is a low‑ceilinged stone chamber, its walls rough and warm from a crackling hearth. The air smells of pine smoke, fresh bread, and the faint metallic tang of iron. A single torch flickers on a wooden beam, casting dancing shadows that make the simple wooden chest at the foot of the bed seem almost alive. Its lid is slightly ajar, revealing a bundle of faded parchment—perhaps a map or a journal left by someone who once walked these same stones.  

Outside, the narrow lane is lined with thatched roofs and the distant clang of a blacksmith’s hammer. Cloaked figures move toward the central square, their faces hidden in the gloom. The villagers speak in a dialect you barely recognize, their words a melodic mix of old tongues that hint at a culture far removed from the one you know.  

A chill runs down your spine as you notice a faint, rhythmic hum emanating from the far wall—a low vibration that seems to pulse in time with your own heartbeat. The legend your grandmother once whispered tells of a stone circle beyond the hills, where a forgotten altar once opened a doorway between worlds. If you can locate that circle, perhaps you could find a way back to your own time.  

But the night is falling fast. The elder’s warning echoes in your mind: “Strangers are not welcomed after dark.” You have two choices: follow the cryptic map toward the circle, hoping it will grant you passage home, or hide in the shadows and hope the villagers never discover you. The weight of the unknown presses upon you, and as you take a tentative step forward, the ground beneath your feet trembles ever so slightly, as if the world itself is waiting for your decision.  

The first star pierces the twilight sky, and a soft, otherworldly glow begins to seep from the cracks in the stone—an invitation, or perhaps a test. Whatever you choose, the dream is no longer just a fleeting vision; it has become a crossroads where the past, present, and the possibility of return converge.
