The audience doesn't gasp or laugh; instead, they rise slowly from their seats, their eyes wide with wonder, as if they've been waiting their whole lives for exactly this.

You step forward, heart pounding, and try again. This time, a chorus of soft music flows from you — a melody that makes the lights flicker and the walls seem to breathe. Some people close their eyes. Others begin to sway. A child in the front row reaches up, trying to catch the sound with her hands, and you realize the music has become visible — ripples of light drifting like water across the room.

You feel an unusual calm settle over you, as if your body knows something your mind hasn't figured out yet. You look out at the crowd and notice their faces are your own — people you recognize from your waking life, but wearing expressions of total openness, no walls, no pretense. And you understand, without knowing how, that this isn't really a speech at all.

It's a memory.

The butterflies dissolve into stars. The music hums into a low vibration you feel in your chest. And just as you open your mouth to ask what it all means, the stage tilts gently beneath your feet — not falling, just *shifting* — like the floor is turning into calm, warm ocean water. You float upward, and as you rise, you hear a voice from beneath you whisper:

*"Everything you say here, you'll remember when you wake up."*

The stage is gone now. You're drifting in a vast, dark space speckled with light — and you begin to form a sentence you've never thought before, words you've never used, shaped by something older than language. You don't hear yourself speak, but you feel it resonate through every cell of your body, and somewhere far below, you can tell that the audience has understood every single word.
