…each group seems to recognize you, though you can’t remember meeting any of them. They greet you by name and ask how you’ve been. You smile, answer vaguely, and keep moving.

The house is much larger inside than it looked from the street. Every hallway opens onto another room, each louder and brighter than the last. You pass a table where people are arranging tiny, glowing oranges into careful patterns. In another room, everyone is listening to a woman tell a story about a city that sank beneath the floorboards. They laugh at the ending before she reaches it.

You keep looking for the one person you know. You check the kitchen, the garden, and a balcony overlooking a dark ocean. At last, you spot them across the crowd. They’re standing beneath a clock with no hands.

You start toward them, but the party grows quiet. One by one, the guests turn to face you. Your friend lifts a glass—not to toast, exactly, but as if signaling that they’ve been waiting.

Then the clock begins to tick.

You hurry forward. The rooms rearrange themselves around you; the ocean balcony becomes a staircase, the staircase becomes a long, sunlit beach. Your friend is still just ahead, walking at an easy pace. You call their name, but the sound comes out as a flock of birds, scattering into the sky.

When you finally catch up, you find an open door where they were standing. Beyond it is your own bedroom, morning light on the floor. You step through—and wake with the strange feeling that you were expected somewhere.
