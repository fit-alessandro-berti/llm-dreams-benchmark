…and the wind steals the last birdsong from the trees. Leaves flip to show their pale undersides, and the sky folds low over the forest. A crack of thunder shakes loose a rain of needles, and the path beneath your feet vanishes beneath creeping darkness.

You run. Branches claw at your sleeves; the rain is warm, but the wind bites. Then the lightning flashes, and there—impossible, yet waiting—a narrow door stands open in an ancient oak.

Inside is a small room filled with amber light. A kettle hums on an invisible stove, and a dry chair waits beside a round window. You sit, heart pounding, watching the storm tear through the trees beyond the glass.

*It cannot touch you here.*

Slowly, the thunder softens to a rumble, the rumble to a whisper, the whisper to rain tapping against the window. Your eyes grow heavy. Just before you sleep, you see your own reflection looking back—not from the chair, but from somewhere far above, standing beneath a sky that is beginning to clear.

You wake to sunlight on your face and the scent of wet earth.

For a moment, you could swear you hear a kettle singing.
