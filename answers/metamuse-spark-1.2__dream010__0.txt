You face various challenges and each one makes a little less sense than the last.

**The first challenge is The Waiting Room.**

You are in a waiting room for your interview, except the waiting room is an entire office building turned inside-out. The desks are on the ceiling, the potted plants are growing sideways out of the walls, and all the other competitors are sitting patiently upside down, reading magazines that are completely blank.

A receptionist with kind eyes and a headset that whispers on its own calls your name. But it's not your real name - it's the name of your dream job. *"Is... Astronaut-Artist up next? Or Lighthouse Keeper for Lost Ideas?"*

When you stand up, your chair disappears with a soft *pop*. Your resume is in your hands, but it's not paper anymore. It's a small, warm pane of glass, and as you breathe on it, words fog into existence that you don't remember writing: *Curious. Kind. Tries again.*

The door to the interview room opens by itself.

**The second challenge is The Presentation.**

Inside, there are no judges. There are three versions of yourself sitting at a long table - you at 8 years old in a too-big blazer, you at 16 looking nervous, and you as you are now. They don't ask you any questions.

Instead, the 8-year-old you slides a huge box of crayons toward you and says, "Show us what you want to do every day without using any words."

You try to speak, but your voice comes out as music. So you draw.

You draw not a job title, but a feeling: A sunny workshop with open windows. People coming in with tangled problems and leaving a little lighter. Your hands being useful. The feeling of being so focused you forget what time it is. As you draw, the lines lift right off the paper and float in the air around you like glowing string, building the room around you in real-time.

The 16-year-old you smiles for the first time and says, "Oh, that's good."

**The final challenge is The Collaboration.**

Suddenly the walls dissolve and you are back with all the other competitors. But the announcer's voice, which now sounds like it's coming from the ceiling fan, says: *"Final Task: Build the ladder together. Only one prize, but everyone has to climb."*

You all look at each other, confused. In the center of the room is a pile of impossible materials - ladders made of cloud, hammers that giggle when you pick them up, nails that are actually just good ideas.

For a second, everyone tries to build their own ladder alone and they all wobble and melt. Then someone - you - laughs and says, "What if we just... make one big one?"

And you do. You all build together. Your glowing string-drawings weave with someone else's cloud-ladder and another person's giggling hammer. It shouldn't be structurally sound, but this is a dream, so of course it holds. It stretches up and up through the ceiling, through the sky, through the roof of the dream itself.

At the very top, there is no trophy. No certificate. No office.

There's just a door with your name on it - your real name this time - and it's already open. Inside is that sunny workshop you drew. The light is exactly the way you imagined it. And you understand, with the perfect, wordless clarity you can only have in a dream, that you didn't win the dream job.

You remembered that you were already qualified for it.

You step through the door, and the feeling of sunlight on your face is so warm and real that it wakes you up.
