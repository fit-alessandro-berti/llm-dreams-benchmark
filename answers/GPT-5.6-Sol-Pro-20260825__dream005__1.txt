…each group seems to be speaking a different language—not foreign languages exactly, but languages made of gestures, laughter, and half-finished stories.

At one table, everyone is dressed as if attending a wedding, yet they insist it is a business meeting. In another corner, a group of strangers greets you warmly and begins reminiscing about adventures you have no memory of. They show you photographs in which you are clearly present, smiling beside them in places you have never visited.

You ask whether anyone has seen the person you know. Each group points in a different direction.

“They just went upstairs.”

“They’re outside by the fountain.”

“They’ve been waiting for you all evening.”

The building seems to grow as you search. New corridors appear behind curtains, staircases lead back to the same crowded room, and every clock shows a different time. Gradually, you realize that the gathering has gone completely silent. The guests are still talking and laughing, but no sound reaches you.

Then, across the room, you finally see the familiar person standing alone beneath a chandelier. Relief washes over you. You hurry toward them, weaving through the motionless crowd, but the room stretches with every step. They remain impossibly far away.

At last, they lift a hand and point—not at you, but behind you.

You turn and find a small, ordinary door where there had been only a wall. Your name is written on it in your own handwriting.

When you look back, the familiar person is gone. The guests have all stopped moving and are now watching you.

You open the door.

On the other side is your own bedroom, dimly lit by morning light. Someone is asleep in your bed. As you approach, you realize it is you.

The sleeping version of you opens their eyes and whispers, “You found me.”

Then you wake up—but for several seconds, you can still hear the distant murmur of the gathering somewhere beyond your bedroom wall.
