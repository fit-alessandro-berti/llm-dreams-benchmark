…you need to remember how you got here. But the harder you try, the more the memories slip away, like water through your fingers.

You stand in a narrow cobblestone street. Half-timbered houses lean toward each other overhead, their shutters painted in faded colors. There’s no hum of electricity, no glow of screens. Just the smell of woodsmoke, damp earth, and something baking. A horse clops past, pulling a cart, and the driver glances at you—not with suspicion, but with a kind of tired familiarity, as if he’s seen strangers stumble out of time before.

You pat your pockets. No phone. No wallet. Just a smooth stone, warm as a living thing.

A woman in a linen dress steps out of a doorway and hands you a piece of bread without a word. You open your mouth to thank her, but the words that come out aren’t yours—they’re a language you’ve never spoken, yet somehow understand. She smiles. Nods toward the end of the street, where a torched archway leads into a forest that glows faintly, even though the sun is high.

You realize you need to go there. Not because you’re lost. Not because you’re being chased. But because a part of you knows—has always known—that this is where you left something. Something important. Something you forgot the moment you were born.

So you walk. The bread is warm in your hand. The stone pulses in your pocket like a second heartbeat. Behind you, the town fades. Ahead, the forest opens its arms.

And then you hear your own voice, whispering from the trees:

*“Finally. You came back.”*

You realize you need to wake up. But you also realize you don’t want to. Not yet. Not until you remember what you came for.

So you keep walking, deeper into the dream, where the light grows stranger and the path becomes a river, and the river becomes a door—and you step through, knowing that when you wake, you’ll forget it all.

But just before you do, the dream leans close and breathes:

*“I’ll be waiting.”*
