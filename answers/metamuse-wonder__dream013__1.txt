...realize the puzzles are changing with you.

The first rooms are exactly what you expect. A door with nine brass levers that must be pulled in an order hinted at by a riddle carved into the floor. A chessboard where every wrong step makes the tiles sigh and reset. A bridge of glass over black water, held up only as long as you keep counting prime numbers in your head without stopping.

You solve them. Your head aches with the effort. Your hands shake a little less each time a lock clicks open.

Deeper in, the maze stops making sense in the way it should.

You enter a library where all the books have the same title — your name — but each one is written in a slightly different life. To get through, you don't have to read them. You have to close the one you've been carrying the longest.

In the next room, there's no door at all. Just your younger self, sitting cross-legged on the floor, crying over a puzzle with missing pieces. You kneel down, ready to solve it for them, to think harder, faster — but the pieces only appear when you stop trying to fix the picture and just sit there with them until they're ready to laugh at something small and stupid.

The maze gets quieter after that. The walls, which were stone, are now made of hedges, then curtains, then warm morning light.

The final puzzle is a mirror in an empty, round room.

In it, you aren't alone. Behind your reflection are all the versions of you who got stuck in the earlier rooms — the frustrated one at the chessboard, the frightened one on the bridge, the tired one in the library. They're not trapped anymore. They're just waiting.

There's a single inscription underneath the mirror:

*You cannot think your way out until you bring everyone with you.*

So you don't solve it. You reach out your hand, and they reach back through the glass like it's water, one by one, until the room is crowded and noisy and wonderfully unsolvable.

Then the maze isn't a maze at all. It's a house you recognize. Your house. A kettle is whistling somewhere. Sunlight is coming through a window you forgot to close.

You open your eyes in your bed, heart still pounding from the last door, with the strange, certain feeling that you didn't escape the dream.

You brought it with you.
