The moment you close the door, the hallway stretches into an endless railway platform. Your friend follows, still speaking, but every angry sentence leaves their mouth as a paper bird. The birds flap wildly between you, colliding with the ceiling and scattering words like feathers: *betrayal*, *promise*, *afraid*, *listen*.

A train pulls in without making a sound. Its doors open, and inside you see scenes from your friendship instead of seats: the first time you met, the night you laughed until morning, the moment this disagreement truly began. You both step aboard, moving from memory to memory. Each scene looks slightly different depending on which side of the carriage you stand.

At last, you reach an empty compartment containing only two chairs and a small glass box. Inside the box is the thing you were arguing about—but now it changes shape whenever either of you tries to describe it. In your hands, it becomes something precious. In your friend’s hands, it becomes something fragile.

You realize neither of you has been fighting to win. You have both been fighting because you are terrified of losing the same thing.

You sit down across from each other. This time, when your friend speaks, no paper birds appear. Their voice is quiet and ordinary.

“I thought you didn’t care,” they say.

You reach across the space between you and answer, “I cared so much that I stopped listening.”

The train begins moving again. Outside the window, dawn spreads across a city made entirely of bridges—and when you wake, your hand is still outstretched, as though someone had been holding it.
