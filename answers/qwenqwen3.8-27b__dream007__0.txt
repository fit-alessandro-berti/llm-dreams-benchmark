You push past the chair, but the floor tilts gently, as if the argument has loosened it from the rest of the world. Each step carries your voice into the walls, where it becomes a low hum—no longer sharp, no longer accusing. Your friend calls your name, but the name arrives before their mouth moves, as if the dream has remembered it from somewhere else.

The room folds into a smaller version of itself. The table shrinks until it is only the space between two hands. You realize, in the way dreams know things, that neither of you is trying to win. You are both trying to be remembered correctly.

You turn back. Your friend is not angry anymore, just tired.

You sit. The chairs are soft as breath. On the table is the thing you were fighting over, wrapped in cloth. When you unwrap it, it is just a small, ordinary object: a key, a photograph, a button—something neither of you can name but both have always needed.

You look at your friend. Your friend looks at you.

The dream ends with the sentence that never has to be spoken:

*I was not trying to hurt you. I was trying to be here.*
