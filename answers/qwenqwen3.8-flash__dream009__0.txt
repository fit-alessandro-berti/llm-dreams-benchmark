You wake up in a completely different era, with no modern technology around. As you explore your new surroundings, you realize you need to find the old bell tower before the moon rises — because in this dream, every sound is a door, and your name has been left somewhere inside one of them.

The cobblestones glow faintly beneath your feet, though the sky is still blue with daylight. There are no phones, no screens, no engines — only horses, lanterns, and the smell of bread and wet stone. People pass you without speaking, their faces blurred like unfinished portraits. You try to call out, but your voice comes out as the chime of a distant train that hasn’t run in centuries.

When you reach the bell tower, you find stairs that spiral both upward and downward at once. At the top hangs a silver bell with no tongue, yet it rings as if it already remembers. Inside its hollow curve is a small brass key. On it is engraved your name — not the one you use now, but the one you had before you were born.

You take the key, and the dream shifts. The city turns into a forest made of clock hands. The sky becomes a page of unread letters. Somewhere, a child laughs in the voice of your grandmother, and you understand at last what you need to do.

You must ring the bell.

You pull the rope, and the sound breaks open a door in the air. Beyond it stands your own bed, morning light touching the sheets. For a moment, you are almost there — but on the nightstand sits the brass key, humming softly, still warm from the dream.
