You look down at the project brief and feel your stomach drop.

The deadline is **8:00 a.m. tomorrow**.

You check the time. **7:58 a.m.**

Across the desk, your boss folds their hands. “I knew you’d be excited.”

The office stretches until the walls are miles away. The fluorescent lights flicker. Every clock starts spinning backward, then stops.

You try to type, but the cursor moves faster than you can think. You open a spreadsheet; every cell fills with the word **URGENT**. You turn to ask for help, but the office is empty except for your boss, who now has your face and is waiting impatiently for you to begin.

“Show me,” you say, your voice echoing strangely, “exactly what needs to be finished.”

Your boss points to a paragraph of tiny, unreadable text.

You shake your head. “I can’t do this by tomorrow.”

For a moment, the dream holds its breath.

Then your boss sighs—not with anger, but with relief.

“I was afraid you’d say yes,” they admit. “I’ve been afraid to say this isn’t possible.”

The impossible deadline dissolves like ink in water. On the page, a new line appears:

**What can we finish together?**

You sit down. You ask questions. You sketch a plan. Somewhere in the building, other people wake up, remembered just when you need them. The work doesn’t become easy, but it becomes possible: one manageable piece, then another.

Your boss smiles.

“Don’t wait for the perfect moment,” they say. “Start with the first honest thing.”

You reach for the keyboard.

This time, your fingers move.
