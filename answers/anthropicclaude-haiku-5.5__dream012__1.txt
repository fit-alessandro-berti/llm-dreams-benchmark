...and you feel the wheel grow warm in your hands, though you have never touched it before. The storm does not roar so much as hum, a low note beneath the waves, and the sky above is the color of bruised plums, lit from within by lightning that falls upward.

"Hold her steady," you say, and your voice comes out calmer than you feel, and somehow the crew believes it.

Bosun Maren, whose face you cannot quite remember, is lashed to the mast with a rope that keeps growing longer. "Captain, the compass is spinning!"

"Let it spin. Watch the gulls instead."

There are no gulls. But when you look up, three pale birds are circling the masthead, and you know, the way one knows things in dreams, that they are pointing the way. You follow them.

The waves rise like the backs of sleeping whales. One of them opens its eye as the ship climbs its flank, and you hear a voice from the deep say your name, the name you had as a child, the one no one has used in years. You do not answer. You only tighten your grip and steer toward the dark seam where the clouds part.

Behind you, the crew begins to sing, a wordless melody that rises above the wind. It steadies them. It steadies the hull. The rain falls sideways, then stops mid-air, hanging like beads on invisible strings.

You reach the seam. Beyond it is not calm sea but a harbor you have never seen, lanterns strung between masts of drifting ships, and a stone pier where someone waits with a lantern held high.

The ship slides into still water. The crew turns to you, soaked and silent, waiting for you to speak. You open your mouth, and find that the words have already arrived, as if they had been sailing toward you all along:

"We're home."

And the lanterns on the pier all go out at once, gently, like eyes closing in sleep, and you wake with salt on your lips and the wheel still warm in your hands.
