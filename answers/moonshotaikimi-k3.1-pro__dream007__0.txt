“Tell me what you’re afraid of,” you say.

Your friend looks startled. The anger drains from their face, and suddenly the room begins to fill with water—not rising from the floor, but hanging in the air, clear and weightless. Chairs drift past. The clock floats off the wall, its hands turning backward.

“I’m afraid you’ll go somewhere I can’t follow,” they say.

You realize the argument was never really about the choice in front of you. It was about everything that choice might change.

Outside the window, the street has become an ocean. Far away, a lighthouse flashes once, twice. You want to promise that nothing will change, but even in the dream you know that would be a lie.

So you say, “Some things will change. But we don’t have to disappear from each other’s lives.”

Your friend reaches for you. For a moment, your hands pass through each other like smoke. Then the water collapses into rain, and you are both standing on the roof of your childhood school, soaked and laughing, though neither of you remembers why.

Below, hundreds of doors open in the grass. Each one leads somewhere different.

“Which one is yours?” your friend asks.

You look, but none of them has a handle.

“I don’t know yet.”

They nod. “Then don’t choose just to spite me.”

The sky folds inward like paper. Just before everything goes white, you feel their hand close around yours—not holding you back, not pulling you forward. Simply there.

When you wake, the disagreement is still waiting.

So is your phone.
