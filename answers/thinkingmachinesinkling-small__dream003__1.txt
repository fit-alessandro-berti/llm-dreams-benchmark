The patterns begin to glow with a blue-green luminescence that matches the color of your earliest, half-lost memories. You understand now: the box has no key because it was never locked by someone else. It was sealed by your own forgetting.

You place your palm against the center of the design, where the carvings spiral inward like a shell. The box sighs. The patterns rearrange themselves into the shape of your own face, but younger, unburdened, eyes wide with a question you have been avoiding for years. The lid doesn't open; it unfolds, like a flower made of shadow and gold.

Inside, there is no object. There is a sound—the precise frequency of your name spoken in a language you spoke in a dream years ago. Then, a small door. You step through not with your body but with your attention, and suddenly you are standing in a garden where the trees grow keys instead of fruit. You pick one, but it turns to smoke as you hold it, because in this dream, you were never meant to unlock the box with a key. You were meant to become the key.

You turn back to see the box now empty, its patterns resting peacefully, and you realize with a feeling of profound, floating clarity that the mystery wasn't inside the box at all—it was the version of you that believed you needed to carry it. You wake with the faint scent of wood and starlight, and your pockets are empty, though you keep reaching for something that was never there.
