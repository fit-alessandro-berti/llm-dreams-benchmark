“Reef the sails. Secure the lines. Stay together.”

Your voice sounds calmer than you feel. The crew moves, and you take the wheel. Each wave lifts the ship until its timbers groan, then drops it into a trough so deep you can see nothing beyond the next wall of water.

Through the rain, a light appears off the starboard bow.

Three slow flashes.

You look down at the compass. Its needle has vanished. Beneath the glass, a tiny silver fish swims in circles, then turns toward the light.

“Captain?” the first mate asks.

You turn the wheel.

The ship resists at first. Then its bow swings, and the crew hauls together on a line slick with rain. You steer toward the flashes, counting them aloud whenever they return.

The waves begin to lengthen. The wind falls behind you. Soon the ship is gliding through a narrow channel between cliffs covered in dark trees.

At the mouth of a sheltered harbor stands a lighthouse. Someone waits in its open doorway, holding a lantern. You recognize the yellow coat before you recognize the face: your mother, younger than you remember her.

The crew lowers the anchor. No one seems surprised.

You step ashore, though you cannot recall a boat being lowered. Your mother puts the lantern in your hand.

“I kept it lit,” she says.

Behind you, the ship rocks gently in the harbor. You want to ask how long she has been waiting, but she is already leading you up the stairs. Halfway to the top, you hear your crew calling from below.

You stop, one hand on the warm stone wall.

When you look back down, the staircase has become the sea.
