

You wake up in a completely different era, with no modern technology around. As you explore your new surroundings, you realize you need to find the well at the edge of the village before the third bell rings, because the water there will tell you whether you are lost in the past or the past is lost in you.

The air smells of woodsmoke, damp stone, and fresh bread. You are standing in a narrow lane lined with crooked timber houses, their roofs thick with moss. People pass you wearing rough cloaks and leather shoes, carrying baskets, buckets, and bundles of herbs. No one seems surprised to see you, though you know you do not belong here.

A woman selling candles by a wooden stall looks at you and says, softly, “You’re late.”

“Late for what?” you ask.

She smiles as if the answer is obvious. “For your own remembering.”

She places something in your hand: a small coin, warm as if it has been held by someone for a long time. When you look down, you see that the face on the coin is yours, but worn away, almost erased.

Far off, a bell sounds once.

You begin walking toward the edge of the village, guided by a feeling more than a direction. The road becomes dirt, then grass, then a path of pale stones that seem to glow faintly in the early light. Birds call from the trees, but their voices sound almost like whispered names.

At the second bell, you reach the well.

It is older than the village, older perhaps than the land itself. Its stones are carved with symbols you cannot read, yet somehow your fingers know the shape of them. You lean over the edge and look down, expecting darkness.

Instead, the water shows you a city of glass towers, bright streets, and moving lights. You see screens, wires, engines, and all the familiar things of the world you thought was real. For a moment, you feel homesick for a life you cannot fully remember.

Then the reflection changes.

You see yourself standing at this very well, but in many different eras at once. You are wearing robes, armor, simple linen, a traveler’s coat, a uniform, a child’s dress, a suit, a crown of leaves. In every reflection, you are holding the same coin.

The third bell begins to ring.

The water rises slowly until it hovers before your face like a mirror made of air. From within it, a voice speaks—not loud, not frightening, but deep enough to shake something inside you.

“You did not come here to escape your time,” it says. “You came here to remember that you are more than one.”

You understand then that the dream is not a prison. It is a doorway. The past is not behind you; it is layered beneath your feet, alive in every story, every ruin, every habit, every word you speak without knowing where it came from. The future is not ahead of you either; it is already dreaming itself through your hands.

You hold the coin up to the water. Your reflection presses its hand against yours from the other side of the shining surface.

When your palms meet, the world folds gently, like a page turning.

You wake again—but not into the same darkness.

You are lying beneath a sky full of fading stars, and in your hand, faintly warm, is the coin. Its face is no longer worn away.

It is waiting for you to decide what to become next.
