...speak, but the words that leave your mouth are not the ones you prepared. They come out as small paper birds, each one unfolding its wings as it passes your lips and drifting over the heads of the audience. The crowd doesn't seem surprised. They reach up to catch them, unfolding each bird to read what's written inside, nodding thoughtfully.

You look down at your notes for reassurance, but the pages have turned into a window. Through it you can see your childhood kitchen, where someone is humming and stirring a pot. The smell of it rises up through the podium.

"Go on," says a woman in the front row. She has your third-grade teacher's face but your grandmother's voice. "You were just getting to the good part."

You realize the auditorium has no ceiling. Above you, the night sky is crowded with stars, and they are leaning in to listen too. The stage beneath your feet has become the deck of a slow-moving ship, and the audience's seats bob gently on dark water, each person holding a small lantern.

You open your mouth again. This time, instead of birds, a single clear note comes out, a sound you've never made before but somehow always knew. The lanterns brighten. Everyone in the crowd begins to hum along in harmony, and you understand, with the certainty that exists only in dreams, that this was the speech all along. There was never anything to memorize.

The ship drifts toward a horizon that is just beginning to glow, and someone in the back row, someone you can't quite see but know you've been missing, starts to applaud.
