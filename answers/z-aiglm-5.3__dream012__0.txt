...grip the wheel with both hands. Rain comes sideways. The mast groans like something alive, and the faces of your crew are pale crescents in the dark, all of them waiting.

"Batten everything down!" Your voice surprises you — steadier than you feel. "We take her into the waves. Together."

And they move. Their fear becomes rope in their hands, becomes boots on a pitching deck, becomes purpose. You point the bow at the heart of the storm, because the only way through is into it.

Then the dream does what dreams do. The largest wave of all rises ahead, tall as a cathedral, and instead of breaking — it holds. Suspended, curling, lit from within. Drifting inside it: fish like paper lanterns, slow and unbothered, as if the sea has lifted a curtain to show you its quiet interior.

You steer straight in. No impact, only a hush — silver and total. When the ship passes out the other side, the water lies flat as a mirror. The clouds peel back around a low orange moon. Your crew stands silent, faces upturned, salt drying on their cheeks.

A certainty settles over you then, the kind that only visits in dreams: the storm was never against you. The sea was only clearing its throat.

Now, at last, it has something to say.
