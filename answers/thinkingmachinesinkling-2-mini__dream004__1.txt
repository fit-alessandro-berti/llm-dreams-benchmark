You reach the blank canvas and feel it breathe—not empty, but patient. At the edges, the world’s colors slide in like slow weather: a corridor of teal curves folds into a sun-colored arch, only to dissolve into a field of floating rectangles that pulse like distant heartbeats.

You touch the canvas with bare fingers. Instead of pigment, your skin prints a lattice of light—aleph-like loops, constellations, a spiral that looks almost like a thought. The surface responds by changing itself: the corners become stairs, the center becomes a doorway, and the white field grows into a soft valley where shadows are shaped like familiar things—a hand, a cup, a falling leaf—but each object is also different from what it appears to be.

Music arrives without sound: a rhythm felt in your throat. The changing world begins to arrange itself around the image you make. Blue blooms into pollen, yellow hardens into braided rope, deep violet tightens into a curtain that parts just enough to reveal a room you’ve never entered, furnished with the textures of other rooms.

And then—very gently—you realize the canvas is showing you the self you’d been trying to hold onto.

The dream asks one quiet question: *what do you want to paint when the world will meet you halfway?*

If this is your dream, the next line could be: “I reach into the canvas—not to paint, but to be painted.”
