…and their laughter goes quiet the moment you approach—then resumes behind you before you're even past, leaving you nothing but drifting fragments of sentences you weren't meant to hear.

You weave through rooms that shouldn't connect. A living room becomes a conservatory; the conservatory becomes the kitchen of your childhood home; the kitchen opens onto a hotel lobby, though the same party flows seamlessly through all of them. A man hands you a glass of wine that turns out to be filled with confetti. You accept it anyway, because in dreams you always do.

Then—you see it. The back of your friend's unmistakable green coat, by the doorway. You push through the crowd, sidestepping platters of food no one is eating, and just as your hand touches the shoulder—

—it's a stranger. He turns to apologize in a language you understood perfectly until this exact moment.

Behind him, everyone has stopped dancing to look at you. Not unkindly. Simply waiting.

You retreat up a staircase that didn't exist before and step out onto a balcony overlooking open ocean where the city should be. Your phone buzzes. It's her: *I'm here!! Where are you?* Relief surges up as you lift your hands to reply—and your fingers pass straight through the screen, the message never sent.

But strangely, the panic doesn't come. Standing in the warm dark, listening to the muffled pulse of the party below, you feel a calm, impossible certainty: she's in the next room. She was there the whole time. Any moment now you'll drift back down through the shifting rooms, the crowd will part, and the search will quietly begin again.

Unless—that sound? Is that the alarm?

It's hard to tell which. Either way, the party keeps humming somewhere behind your teeth.
