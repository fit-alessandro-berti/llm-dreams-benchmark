…written in a language you've never seen before—except you can somehow read it, and the first question is: *"Explain the summer you were nine. Be specific. Use diagrams."*

You look up. The classroom is somehow also your third-grade classroom, but the desks are too small now, your knees jammed against the underside. You realize you're wearing one shoe. This feels important.

The clock on the wall has thirteen hours on its face, and the hands are spinning backward. The proctor is your dentist. Or your old soccer coach. They trade places every time you blink.

"One question left," the proctor announces, though you haven't answered any.

You look down. The exam now reads: *"Why didn't you attend any of the classes?"*

But you did attend. You're almost sure you did. You try to remember the semester and get only a single image: a hallway, narrowing, which you have been walking down for years.

Your pen won't write. Neither will the backup. You raise your hand to ask for another and discover the room is underwater. Everyone else keeps working, unbothered, small fish threading between their pencils. You can breathe fine as long as you don't think about breathing.

The person beside you—were they always there?—leans over and whispers, "Relax. Everyone fails this one. Failing isn't the point."

"Then what is the point?"

They're gone. In their seat sits an old rotary phone, ringing. You lift the receiver, because in dreams you always do.

A voice says: "You're going to be late."

Late for what? You look down for your exam—

—and you're walking into a classroom, slightly out of breath, holding a pencil you're not sure works. Everyone is already seated. You find your desk. The final exam for a course that determines your future career lies face-down in front of you. You turn it over.
