Despite the hardships, you decide to stay human. You share water with a stranger even though your canteen is almost empty. You plant a handful of seeds in the cracked earth behind a collapsed gas station, not because you’ll be there to harvest them, but because something should grow. You leave messages on ruined walls: “Safe water two miles north,” “Medicine at the blue door,” “You are not alone.”

At night, you climb to the roof of an old school and watch the stars. For a moment the world doesn’t feel dead—it feels like it’s holding its breath. Then the ground shimmers, the stars smear into daylight, and you wake with your heart beating slow and a strange, stubborn hope in your chest.
