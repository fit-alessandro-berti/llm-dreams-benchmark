...each group seems to be mid-conversation about something you almost understand. A cluster of coworkers from a job you never had laughs at a joke that stops the moment you approach. Someone hands you a drink—warm, amber, faintly tasting of pennies—and asks how your mother is doing. You don't have an answer that makes sense, so you smile and drift on.

The room keeps rearranging itself. The hallway you walked down a minute ago now leads back to the coat check, where your jacket has become someone else's jacket, or maybe it was always theirs. Through the crowd you glimpse them—the one person you know—a flash of their familiar laugh across the room, near the bookshelf that wasn't there before. You push toward them, weaving between bodies that seem to multiply the closer you get.

"Wait," you call out, but your voice comes out as someone else's voice, deeper, older.

They turn. For half a second it's really them, their face lit with recognition—and then they say, "Oh, you made it! Everyone's been asking about you," and gesture to the entire room, which has gone silent, forty faces turning toward you at once, expectant, patient, waiting for you to say something you were apparently supposed to prepare.

You open your mouth. Nothing comes out except the sudden realization that you never finished a degree, or that you're standing in socks, or that the party is actually happening in your childhood home and the kitchen doorway leads to an ocean.

Someone starts clapping politely.

And just before the embarrassment becomes unbearable—just at the peak of it—you feel the specific weight of your own blanket, hear the hum of the refrigerator through the wall, and the whole gathering dissolves like sugar in warm water.

You wake with your heart going fast, still searching the dark bedroom for a face you can't quite remember anymore.
