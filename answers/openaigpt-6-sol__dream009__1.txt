…find out what year it is before you say something foolish.

You’re in a narrow street paved with uneven stones. A horse pulls a cart past you, and the driver calls out for you to move. Across the way, a woman hangs laundry from an upstairs window. Nothing looks familiar, but everyone else seems to know exactly where they’re going.

At a market stall, you pick up a bruised apple and ask the seller what day it is.

“Thursday,” she says.

“And the year?”

She studies your clothes, then names a year more than two centuries before you were born.

You put the apple down. For a moment, you consider telling her the truth. Instead, you ask what a person with no money might do for work.

She points toward the river. “They’re always looking for hands.”

You thank her and follow the sound of the water. You need food and somewhere to sleep. After that, you can begin looking for a way home.
