...decide to follow the sound of water.

You haven't heard running water in what feels like years. It's a thin, silvery trickle somewhere beyond the rusted skeletons of the city, and you're sure it's real, though nothing else here quite is. The sky is the color of weak tea. The buildings lean toward each other like old friends who've forgotten why they stopped speaking.

You walk for a long time. Your pack is light: a dented canteen, a knife with a cracked handle, a lighter that still sparks on the third try. Along the way you pass a playground where the swings move slowly in a wind you can't feel. A dog with three legs trots alongside you for a while, then stops at a line you can't see and sits, watching you go. You want to call it back, but you don't know its name, so you let it stay.

The sound grows louder. You climb over a collapsed overpass and find the source: a stream cutting through cracked asphalt, its water so clear it looks like glass with light moving underneath. Beside it stands a woman in a coat stitched from a dozen different fabrics. She's filling bottles, and she doesn't look surprised to see you.

"You came from the east," she says. "Everyone from the east eventually comes this way."

"Is it safe here?"

She considers the question seriously, as if it deserves more than a quick answer. "Safer," she says finally, and hands you a bottle.

The water is cold enough to ache in your teeth, and when you drink, something loosens in your chest that you didn't realize was clenched. Behind her, through the haze, you see faint lights: a cluster of windows glowing in a building that should have been dark for decades. People moving between them. Someone laughing.

You decide, then, that the world may be broken, but it isn't finished, and you start walking toward the lights.
