The air smells of rain and something sweeter—orange peel, perhaps, or a birthday cake cooling in a kitchen you once knew. You look for shelter, but the path has disappeared beneath a carpet of blue leaves.

Then lightning flashes.

For an instant, every tree is a doorway.

You hurry toward the nearest one, a crooked oak with a brass handle in its bark. Before you can touch it, the door opens, and a fox wearing your old red scarf steps aside.

“You’re nearly late,” it says.

Inside is a small railway station. Rain patters against the windows, though outside them you can see only deep water. Silver fish drift past the departure board.

You search your pockets for a ticket and find a warm stone. The fox nods toward the platform.

A train waits with its doors open. Through one window you see your childhood kitchen; through another, a beach at night. In the last carriage, someone has set a table for breakfast.

You climb aboard just as thunder rolls through the station.

The train moves so gently that you notice only when the fox begins to recede, one paw raised. Beyond the glass, the underwater lights give way to branches tossing in the rain.

At the breakfast table sits an older version of you, buttering toast.

“Did you get wet?” they ask.

You look down. Moss is growing from your sleeves, and a little rainwater spills from each cuff.

“A bit.”

They pass you a cup of tea and make room beside them. As you sit, you feel the warm stone stir in your pocket.

Outside, the storm lifts the whole forest into the sky.
