The air carries woodsmoke, damp earth, and the sharp green scent of bruised rosemary. Street lamps don’t exist; light pools in bronze braziers and spills from oil-paper windows. You walk without knowing why, but your feet know the rhythm. Shoes that weren’t yours click against cobbles smoothed by centuries of bare soles. A baker draws shapes into dough that bloom into birds when the oven’s heat hits them. They fly, dissolve into ash, and settle as crumbs at your feet. No one seems startled. Dreams, you’re learning, only astonish the parts of you that still think they’re awake.

You follow a ribbon of dried lavender tied between two iron gates. It frays as you walk, yet grows longer. Past it, a courtyard fountain pours upward. Droplets hang like glass beads, reflecting not your face, but a room you haven’t lived in since childhood: a rug worn thin near the hearth, a half-read book left spine-down, your own hands resting on a wooden table as if waiting for permission to begin again.

A woman sweeps the courtyard steps. Her broom makes no sound, but each stroke echoes in your ribs. “You keep arriving late,” she says, without looking up. “Not to this place. To yourself.”

She gestures to a low bench. Upon it rests a clay pot filled with water and floating seeds. You kneel. The surface doesn’t show the sky. It shows a path winding through tall grass, ending at a door you recognize—the one you walked past every day on your way to work, the one you never opened because you told yourself you’d go back when you had more time.

The dream leans closer. Gravity forgets its rules. Your reflection in the water reaches up. Your real hand moves down. Fingers brush. Not skin on skin, but moment on moment. A click. A breath held, then released.

In that exchange, the era around you softens into transparency. Looms become synapses. Mill wheels pulse like heartbeats. Market chatter braids into your own inner monologue, untangling decades of hurry into single, steady notes. You understand now: you weren’t dropped here by accident. The dream isn’t transporting you backward. It’s asking you to stop outrunning forward.

When you finally stand, the lavender ribbon lies coiled at your feet, whole again. The fountain flows downward. The baker’s birds have returned to dough. The woman is gone. Only the clay pot remains, and beside it, a small wool note tied with twine. Inside, pressed flat, is a ticket stub from a train you took ten years ago, along with your grandmother’s handwriting: *It’s okay to just be here.*

The horizon blurs. Dawn bleeds into dusk into morning. You don’t fight the pull. You let the edges of the dream fray at the corners, letting the scent of rosemary linger behind your eyelids, the weight of silence settle in your shoulders, the quiet certainty that you already knew how to stay.

You open your eyes. The ceiling is familiar. But beneath your palms, where sheets usually lie smooth, your fingertips find the faintest impression of thread. Over. Under. Over. You smile, close your hands gently, and don’t rush to stand.
