“Tell me what you heard me say,” you ask.

“That the shop is yours,” your friend says.

You look down at the counter between you. You’re twelve again, both of you, and the counter is a plank laid across two milk crates. There are six bruised apples on it and a sign in your friend’s crooked handwriting: OPEN FOR BIZNIS.

“That’s not what I meant,” you say. Then you remember the estate agent standing in this very spot yesterday, asking if anyone else needed to be consulted. You’d said no. It was simpler than explaining.

“I should have called you before I listed it.”

Your friend picks up an apple. “I know we can’t keep it. I’ve seen the bills too.” She polishes the apple on her sleeve until a patch of red shines through. “I wanted to be here the last time we closed.”

Outside, it’s already getting dark. The two of you carry in the milk crates. You sweep while she counts the till: four buttons, a Canadian penny, a tooth neither of you can account for. At the door you hand her the key, but she shakes her head and puts her hand over yours.

The lock is stiff. It takes both of you to turn it.
