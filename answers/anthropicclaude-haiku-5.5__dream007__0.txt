...you decide to stop talking and simply walk away. But the street you step onto isn't a street anymore. It's the hallway of the house you grew up in, and the floor tilts gently, like the deck of a ship. Your friend's voice follows you, but it's softer now, and it's coming from inside the walls.

"You always do this," they say. Their voice echoes, so you can't tell whether they're behind you or ahead.

You turn around to answer, and they're standing at the end of the hallway holding a small brass key. Neither of you is angry anymore. The argument has drained out of you both, and what's left looks almost like tenderness, or grief.

"What's the key for?" you ask.

They smile the way they used to before you knew each other well. "The door at the end. We left something in there, remember? We always said we'd come back for it."

You don't remember, but you want to. The hallway stretches, and every door you pass shows a different scene: a kitchen where you once cooked together, a rain-soaked bus stop, a field full of paper kites. Each one feels like a version of the fight you never had.

When you reach the last door, you take the key. It turns easily. Behind it is a room filled with light, and on a table sits a single, unfinished letter in your handwriting, addressed to your friend, with the first line reading: *"I was wrong about the thing we were fighting about, and I think you knew it all along."*

You wake up with the words still in your mouth.
