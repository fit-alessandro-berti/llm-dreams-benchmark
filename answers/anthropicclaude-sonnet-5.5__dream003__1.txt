...and as you trace them with your fingertip, the patterns begin to *move*. The silver filigree loosens and curls like smoke, rearranging itself into a spiral of tiny constellations. You realize you've seen this sky before, on a night you can't quite remember, from a window in a house you can't quite place.

The box grows warm, then hums a low note that you feel in your teeth.

There is no keyhole. There is only a small, smooth hollow on the lid, shaped like the pad of a thumb. Somehow you know, the way you know things in dreams, that the box doesn't want a key. It wants something you've been carrying.

You press your thumb into the hollow. Nothing happens.

You close your eyes and think of the first thing that comes to mind: the smell of rain on warm pavement, your grandmother's laugh, a door you never opened, a word you never said. The hollow grows hot, and the patterns flare gold.

*Click.*

The lid lifts without your touching it. Inside there is no treasure, only a pocket of deep blue twilight, and in it a small brass door, no taller than your hand. Faint music plays behind it. It sounds like a song you hummed as a child and forgot you'd invented.

The door swings open from the other side. A tiny figure steps out, and it has your face, though older, kinder, and a little amused. It looks up at you and says:

"There you are. We were wondering when you'd stop looking for the key."

The room begins to dissolve at the edges, walls unspooling into ribbons of light. The figure holds out a hand the size of a snowflake.

"Come on," it says. "You'll fit. You always do, in here."

And as you lean closer, the dream tilts, the floor becomes sky, and you begin to fall, gently, toward the music...

Would you like me to keep dreaming, to see what's behind the little door?
