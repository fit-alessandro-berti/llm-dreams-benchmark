You start to run, but every street sign bears a word you almost recognize. A tram glides past with your name glowing above its windshield. You climb aboard.

The passengers are all asleep except for a little girl holding a paper map. “You’re going the wrong way,” she tells you, without looking up.

“Where’s the meeting?”

She turns the map around. It shows only the tram you’re sitting in, drawn so carefully that you can see yourself leaning toward her. At the bottom, in tiny letters, is an address: **The Last Stop**.

The tram bells ring. Outside, the towers give way to a shoreline, and beyond it the sea rises in a sheer blue wall. Your watch says you have fifty-nine minutes left. You glance up at the girl to ask what happens at the last stop, but her seat is empty.

In her place lies a folded note, addressed in your handwriting.
