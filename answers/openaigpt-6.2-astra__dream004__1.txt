a brush with a single wet bristle. You draw a small blue square. For once, something stays put.

The hills go on folding themselves into green fans and the sky changes from apricot to tin, but the square is still blue, still square. A little wind comes out of it. You lean closer and smell toast.

On the other side is the kitchen of the house where you grew up. You can see only as much as the square allows: a corner of the table, the butter dish, your father's thumb working a stubborn bit of peel off an orange. His hand is younger than you remember. You'd forgotten that when he peeled an orange he tried to get the skin off in one piece.

You reach for the brush to make the opening bigger. By mistake your sleeve smears the bottom edge. Blue runs down the canvas. The kitchen tilts and an orange rolls out, landing softly in the grass at your feet.

“Did you get that?” your father calls.

You have a thousand things to tell him. Behind you the hills shake out their pleats. The sky is turning the pale pink of a sliced radish.

“Got it,” you say.

You sit below the canvas and start at the navel with your thumbnail, turning the fruit slowly in your hands. A curl of peel lengthens past your knee. When it comes free at last, all in one piece, you lift it to the little blue square. His hand reaches through and takes it.
