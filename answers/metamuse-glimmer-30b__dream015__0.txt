You find yourself in a post-apocalyptic world, with resources scarce and dangers everywhere. Despite the hardships, you decide to stop running.

You were already halfway down the cracked highway, scavenging for water from a rusted tanker, when you see it: a small concrete schoolhouse with its roof half gone, but its walls still standing. Inside, the chalkboard is still legible. Someone has been writing on it.

You decide to clean it out. Not to fortify it, not to hide in it. You sweep the broken glass into a pile, you drag in the fallen desks, you pull a tarp over the hole in the roof to catch rain. The first night you sleep on a pile of old library books and wake up to the sound of wind whistling through the broken windows.

On the second day a kid comes in. Barefoot, maybe eight, clutching a dead sparrow. He doesn't ask for food. He asks if you know how to read.

You decide to teach him. You find a dry textbook in the rubble and start from the alphabet, writing the letters in chalk. He copies them. Other people start coming too, drawn by the chalk dust in the air. A woman with a scar across her cheek brings canned beans. An old man brings a working radio, just to listen to static with you.

The dangers are still out there. The sky is still bruised purple. The sirens still wail sometimes in the distance. But inside the schoolhouse you have decided to make a library, a kitchen, a place where the lights are kept on. You are not rebuilding the world, you are just keeping one room warm.

And in the dream, that feels like enough. You fall asleep at the chalkboard with your hand still on the letter O, and when you wake up you are still writing it.
