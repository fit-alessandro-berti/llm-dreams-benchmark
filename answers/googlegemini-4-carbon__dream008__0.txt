You look up at your monitor to read the project brief, expecting spreadsheets or documents, but the screen is just a window looking into a washing machine tumbling glowing, neon numbers. "We need the Thursdays alphabetized in five minutes," your boss says. Somehow, he is now towering ten feet tall, wearing a tuxedo jacket over pajama pants, and tapping impatiently on a wristwatch that is melting down his wrist like warm Brie. 

"I'm right on it," you try to say, though your voice comes out as the muffled sound of a trombone. 

Desperate to look productive, you open a thick manila folder on your desk. Instead of paper, a flock of frantic origami birds bursts out, fluttering up toward the fluorescent ceiling tiles—which are no longer tiles at all, but a heavy, bruised thundercloud rolling through the accounting department. 

You try to chase the birds to gather the data, except your legs suddenly weigh a thousand pounds, as if you are wading through waist-deep peanut butter. Every coworker you pass is furiously typing on loaves of bread or whispering secrets into desk lamps, completely unfazed. Nobody will make eye contact with you.

"Time's almost up," your boss echoes, though he has suddenly morphed into your ninth-grade math teacher, holding a giant red pen. 

You lunge for your desk, realizing that if you just press *Enter*, everything will magically resolve itself. Your office chair begins rolling backward down an infinitely stretching hallway. You reach out, stretching your fingers impossibly long toward a glowing red button on the wall, when a deafening, rhythmic *BEEP-BEEP-BEEP* shakes the entire building. The walls dissolve into grey static, the floor drops away beneath your stomach, and—

—your eyes snap open in the dark, your heart racing, as your hand fumbles to turn off the alarm on your nightstand.
