...not questions at all. The first page is covered in handwriting you recognize as your own—except you've never written any of it. The words shift as you try to read them, letters rearranging like insects scattering when a rock is lifted.

You look up. The exam hall has become your childhood kitchen. The proctor is standing at the stove, back turned to you, and you realize with a jolt that it's your third-grade teacher, the one who told you that you'd never amount to anything. She's stirring something in a pot.

"You have one hour," she says without turning around. "The timer started when you were born."

You look down at the paper again. Now there's only one question, printed in enormous letters across the page:

**WHAT ARE YOU AFRAID OF?**

You pick up your pencil to write an answer, but the pencil is a thermometer. The mercury inside is rising fast. You try to write anyway, but the words come out as water, soaking through the paper, the desk, dripping onto the floor which is somehow also the ceiling.

The other students—you didn't notice them before—have all finished. They stand up in unison and file toward the door. As each one passes you, they lean down and whisper a number in your ear. You can't remember a single one.

The clock on the wall has no hands, but you can hear it ticking anyway.

The proctor turns around at last. She's holding out a second exam, this one blank.

"This is the real one," she says, smiling. "The first was just to see if you'd panic."

You take the paper. Your hands are someone else's hands. You write your name at the top, and the name is wrong—it's the name of a person you've never met, and yet you know, with the strange certainty of dreams, that it will be your name someday.

You begin to answer the first question.

Then the fire alarm goes off, except it isn't a fire alarm, it's your mother calling you down for breakfast, and you wake with your heart pounding, the ghost of an unanswerable question still pressed somewhere behind your eyes.
