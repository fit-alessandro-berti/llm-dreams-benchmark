You are at a large social gathering where you only know one person. As you try to find them, you encounter various groups of people, and each group seems certain that you belong with them.

At one table, everyone greets you by a different name. They laugh at jokes you do not remember making and show you photographs of trips you have never taken. In every picture, your face is turned away from the camera.

You excuse yourself and move into the next room, which is somehow much larger than the building appeared from outside. A string quartet plays in one corner, but instead of instruments, the musicians hold ordinary household objects—a broom, a lamp, a glass bowl, an old telephone. The music sounds strangely familiar, like a melody you once heard while half asleep.

Across the crowd, you finally catch sight of the person you know. They are standing beneath a chandelier, wearing a bright red coat. You call their name, but the music grows louder and everyone begins applauding. When the applause ends, the person has disappeared.

You push through the crowd toward the chandelier. Beneath it, you find only the red coat hanging over the back of an empty chair. Inside one pocket is a small folded note.

**Meet me where the gathering ends.**

You ask a nearby guest where that is. She smiles as though the answer is obvious and points toward a narrow hallway hidden behind a curtain.

The hallway is lined with doors. From behind each one comes the sound of another gathering: birthday songs, wedding speeches, arguments, laughter, glasses clinking. You open the first door and see the same party you just left, except everyone is much older. Through the second door, they are children. Behind the third, the room is empty, but hundreds of shadows continue dancing on the walls.

You keep walking.

The hallway gradually narrows until you must turn sideways. At the very end is a tiny blue door with no handle. When you touch it, it opens onto a quiet beach beneath a dark violet sky.

The person you have been searching for is sitting at the edge of the water.

“You took a long time,” they say.

“I couldn’t find where the gathering ended.”

They look back at the building. It now stands alone on the sand, every window glowing, though no sound comes from inside.

“It ends whenever you stop trying to belong to everyone in it.”

You sit beside them. The sea is perfectly still, reflecting stars that are not in the sky. For a while, neither of you speaks.

Then you ask, “Do I actually know you?”

The person smiles, but their face changes—first into someone from your childhood, then a stranger, then your own reflection.

“You used to,” they say.

A wave rolls toward you, slow and silent. Instead of water, it carries hundreds of forgotten objects: a lost key, an unopened letter, a broken toy, a photograph with your face turned away. The red coat drifts among them.

When the wave reaches your feet, the entire beach begins to tilt. The stars slide across the sky and fall into the sea one by one. Behind you, the building’s lights go dark, floor by floor.

You reach for the person beside you, but your hand closes around the folded note.

The words have changed.

**You are the person you came here to find.**

At that moment, all the doors in the distant building open at once. Every guest steps onto the sand and turns toward you in complete silence.

You expect them to call you by all those unfamiliar names again.

Instead, they begin to disappear—gently, like reflections disturbed by rain—until only the empty building remains.

The person beside you is gone too.

You stand and walk toward the sea. With every step, the water becomes shallower rather than deeper, until you realize you are no longer walking through an ocean but across a vast mirrored floor. Far ahead, a single red door waits beneath the stars.

You open it and find your bedroom on the other side.

Someone is asleep in your bed.

As you move closer, you see that it is you.

Your sleeping self opens its eyes and whispers, “Did you find me?”

Before you can answer, you wake up holding your hand tightly closed, as though there is still a note inside it.
