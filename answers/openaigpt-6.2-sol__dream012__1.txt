and you realize, just as the next wave blots out the sky, that you don’t know how to sail.

“Lantern,” you say, because it’s the first word that comes to you.

Your first mate takes a lantern from her coat. The flame inside is blue and bends toward the sea.

“Higher,” you tell her.

She climbs onto the rail and lifts it. For a moment, in the green face of the wave, you see a kitchen. Rain stipples the window. A kettle is coming to a boil. At the table, with his sleeves rolled up, sits your father, who has been dead for eleven years.

He sees you. He gets up and pulls out a chair.

Behind you, someone asks, “Captain?”

You could step straight through. There isn’t even glass in the window. You can smell the toast he’s burned, the way he always used to burn it on one side.

But your first mate is still on the rail, and she’s looking at you, not at the man in the kitchen.

“Give me your hand,” you say.

She does. The wave comes down.

You wake with your fist closed around a fold of the bedsheet. In the apartment upstairs, someone sets a kettle on the stove. You lie very still, listening for a chair to scrape back.
