…find a way home before nightfall.

The village appears between the hills as the sun begins to sink. Its houses are built from dark timber, their roofs weighted with stones. No machines rattle in the streets. No artificial lights shine in the windows. People move quietly, speaking in a language you somehow understand.

Every path away from the village ends in the forest.

At the edge of the settlement stands an old bell tower, its bronze bell covered in strange markings. A watchmaker peers at you through round spectacles.

“You came from the other side,” he says. It isn’t a question.

When you ask how to return home, he studies your empty hands.

“The gate opens at dawn,” he tells you. “But someone must ring the bell first.”

You climb the tower alone. From its highest window, the landscape stretches impossibly far—a patchwork of kingdoms, forests, and seas, all belonging to histories that never happened. Among them, you glimpse a glittering city that looks almost like home.

The bell rope is stiff and cold.

Then you hear a voice below.

“Don’t ring it,” the watchmaker calls. “If you open the gate, one of us will have to remain behind.”

You look at the impossible city, then at the sleeping village.

At dawn, you pull the rope.

The bell’s voice rolls across the hills. The tower walls dissolve into silver mist, and the gate appears before you—a tall archway filled with darkness.

You step through.

Warm air surrounds you. The hum of traffic rises from a distant road. After a moment, you recognize it: your own time.

Behind you, the watchmaker stands beneath the bell tower, smiling sadly.

“You can return,” he says, “but one of us will have to remain.”

The gate vanishes before you can answer.
