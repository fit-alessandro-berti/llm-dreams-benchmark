The crowd doesn’t seem confused—they lean forward, and each face flickers between familiar and strange, like people you’ve known in other lifetimes. Your notes turn to white feathers in your hands and scatter upward into the stage lights. Instead of panic, you feel calm. The microphone has become a seashell, cool against your palm, and when you lift it to your lips, you hear the ocean instead of your own voice. The audience sighs together, a wave rising and falling. You realize the speech has already been given, not in words, but in the strange, perfect silence between heartbeats. Just as you open your mouth to say the final line, the lights grow too bright, and you wake with the taste of salt and the fading echo of applause.
