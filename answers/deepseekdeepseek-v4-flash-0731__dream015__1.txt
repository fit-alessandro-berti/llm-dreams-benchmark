The sky is the color of old bruises, and the wind carries ash that tastes like forgetting. You don’t know how long you’ve been walking—days, weeks, maybe years—but the road keeps unraveling ahead of you, cracked and swallowed by rust-eaten cars and skeletal trees.

You decide to find the library.

It sounds absurd, even in your dream. But you remember something your mother once told you, back before the world ended: *When everything else is gone, stories are the last thing that survive.*

So you follow the broken highway past collapsed gas stations and empty towns. You ration the last of your water, counting each sip. You sleep curled in the hollow of a dead water tower, listening to distant howls—not all of them animals. You learn to step around the shadows that move too quickly, and you never, ever make eye contact with the figures standing just past the treeline.

On the third day—or maybe the thirtieth—you find it.

The library stands like a cathedral in the ruins. Its windows are shattered, its doors hanging off their hinges, but the walls are still strong. Inside, the air is cool and smells of old paper and dust. Moonlight spills through a hole in the roof onto rows upon rows of books, spines faded, pages brittle.

You walk slowly down the aisles, running your fingers over titles you no longer recognize. You pull one out. The cover is torn, but the words are still there.

*Once upon a time...*

You laugh—a dry, cracked sound. And then you whisper the story aloud, to no one. To everyone. To the ghosts of the world.

As you read, the shadows at the windows pause. The howling outside grows quieter. A small plant sprouts through a crack in the floor, reaching toward the light.

You realize: this is why you came. Not to escape. Not to hide. But to remember what the world was made of.

So you stay. You read. You rebuild the world one word at a time, and in your dream, that is enough.

Then you wake up, and the sun is warm on your face, and the dream fades like morning mist—but somewhere in your chest, the story remains.

And you decide to keep going.
