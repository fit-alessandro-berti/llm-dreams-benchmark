…vanishing one by one, replaced by blank sheets.

A soft chime echoes in the room, but there's no teacher, no classmates—just the hum of an old ventilation fan and the rustle of your own breath. You try to turn the page, but the paper is smooth, like it's made of smoke. A voice whispers from the back of your throat, low and familiar, even though no one is standing there:

*"The exam was never about the answers. It was about the questions you're afraid to ask."*

The room shifts. The desk in front of you multiplies into three, each holding a different kind of paper. One is covered in equations. One is blank. One has a single word written in your own handwriting: **"Who are you when no one is watching?"*

You realize the test isn't graded by a professor. It's graded by the quiet voice inside your head. The dream pauses, the air thickens, and you understand: the only way to finish is to stop waiting for permission.

You pick up the pen. It writes on its own. The questions melt into a single line:

*"What do you truly want, even if you're not allowed to have it?"*

The dream doesn't end. It pauses, waiting. What do you write?
