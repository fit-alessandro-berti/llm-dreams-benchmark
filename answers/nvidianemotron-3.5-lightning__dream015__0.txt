Despite the hardships, you decide to head toward the old lighthouse at the cliff's edge, where the beam is said to still cut through the smog some nights. It’s a long, dangerous trek through rusted highways and silent suburbs, each step weighing the risk of scavengers, collapsed structures, or the quiet that comes when the air runs out. You move with your coat pulled tight, a can of beans and a cracked map your only companions, but you walk with your eyes on the horizon—not because you’re certain the light is still working, but because deciding to move forward, even into the unknown, feels like the only way left to prove you’re still alive.
