You look down and realize your footsteps are no longer pressing into soft moss. The forest floor has shifted into dark, creaking floorboards, stretching endlessly between the towering pine trunks. The first drops begin to fall. They are heavy and luminous, and the moment they strike your shoulders, they don’t splash—they burst into tiny, glowing moths that flutter wildly against the gale.

Thunder cracks, sounding strangely like someone dragging heavy furniture across an attic ceiling. You try to run, but the air thickens around you, dragging at your limbs as if you are wading through chest-deep water. 

Through the sheets of silver rain, a shelter appears in the middle of the path: a single, freestanding wooden door with a tarnished brass knob. There are no walls attached to it and no roof—just the frame standing solidly against the howling wind.

You heave yourself forward, grab the freezing handle, and pull it open.

The roar of the storm vanishes instantly. You stumble through into a warm, sunlit conservatory smelling of cedar and dry earth. Above you, a vast glass dome reveals a bright, calm afternoon sky where giant, translucent jellyfish drift lazily among the clouds. 

In the center of the room sits a small wooden table holding a glass terrarium. Drawn to it, you step closer and peer inside. Beneath the glass, miniature pine trees bend furiously under a tiny, swirling violet tempest—and just beneath the canopy, a minuscule version of you is wading through the wind, reaching for a tiny wooden door.
