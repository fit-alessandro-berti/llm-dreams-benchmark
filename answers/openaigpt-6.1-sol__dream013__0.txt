notice that the walls are shifting—not when you look away, but whenever you become certain.

You solve a sequence of numbers carved into a door, and the corridor beyond folds back on itself. You fit seven brass shapes into a stone circle, and the ceiling lowers until you must crawl. Every correct answer makes the maze smaller.

At last you reach a room containing only a table, a candle, and a sheet of paper.

On the paper is a single question:

**What are you trying to escape?**

You begin working through possibilities. The maze. Fear. Failure. Each answer seems plausible, but as you reach for the pencil, another question appears beneath the first.

**And where would you go?**

The candle burns without growing shorter. Somewhere beyond the walls, water drips upward.

You sit down.

For the first time, you stop trying to solve anything. You listen to the water. You feel the cool edge of the table beneath your hands. After a while, you turn the paper over.

There is a drawing on the back: a child building a maze from fallen leaves. The child has left one side unfinished.

You recognize the garden before you recognize the child.

A breeze moves through the room. The candle leans toward it, and a leaf tumbles across the table. When you stand, the wall behind you is gone.

Outside, evening light lies across the grass. The child looks up at you, then points to the gap in the leaves.

“Can you hold this open?” they ask.

You kneel beside them and put your hand on the ground.
