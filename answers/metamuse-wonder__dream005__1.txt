and each one seems to be having a much better time than you are.

The room is bigger on the inside than it should be. It might have been a ballroom once, but now the walls keep politely moving back whenever you look at them, making room for more couches, more low tables covered in glasses, more people standing in perfect, closed circles.

You squeeze past the first group. They are all laughing at the same time, at nothing, their laughter rising and falling like they're following sheet music only they can see. One woman catches your sleeve as you pass.

"You made it!" she says, delighted.

"I'm just looking for—" you start, but you've already forgotten the name. Not forgotten, exactly. It's right there, behind your teeth, but it won't come out in this room.

"Of course you are," she says, and the whole circle laughs again.

The second group is dancing, though there is no music here. The music is always coming from the *next* room. They move slowly, shuffling in a ring, holding drinks that never spill. Their faces are turned inward, toward each other, and for a second you are absolutely certain that if you joined the circle, you would know the steps.

The third group is whispering. When you get close, they all stop and look at you with kind, expectant eyes, like you've just walked in halfway through a surprise party planning meeting — for you.

"Don't tell him," someone says.

"Don't tell who?" you ask.

"You," they say, and resume whispering.

Your phone is in your hand. It wasn't a moment ago. There's a single text on the screen, no contact name, just:

*I'm by the window.*

There are no windows. There are mirrors, tall and cloudy, and in each one the party looks slightly different — fewer people, older furniture, you in the background wearing clothes you don't own anymore. In one mirror, you see yourself still standing by the door, holding your coat, deciding whether to come in.

You walk faster now. The gathering thins out into a long hallway lined with doors, all of them open an inch, all of them playing that same distant music just a little louder behind the next one.

At the very end is a window.

And there they are.

You know them instantly, the way you know a song from the first two notes. Relief floods you so completely your knees feel weak. You don't even care that you still can't say their name.

"You took forever," they say, smiling. They are holding two drinks, and they hand you one you've apparently been drinking all night — it's half-empty and warm from your hand.

"I didn't know anyone," you say.

"You knew me."

"I was looking for you the whole time."

They look out the window. Outside isn't a street or a garden, it's the party again, from above, tiny and glittering, and you can see yourself — a small, lost figure — threading through the crowds, asking, searching.

"We've done this before," they say, not unkindly.

And then, very gently, they take your hand, and the music finally starts in *this* room, and you realize you were never late at all.

You were just early enough to have to find your way back.
