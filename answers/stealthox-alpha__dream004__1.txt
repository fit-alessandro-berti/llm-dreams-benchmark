...and it hums with a quiet kind of patience, as though it has been waiting for me since before the dream began.

I reach out, and my fingertips come away streaked with colors that don't have names — a blue that tastes like cold water on a summer afternoon, a gold that sounds like someone I love laughing in another room. The moment I touch the canvas, the shifting world around me slows. The melting spirals of the sky pause mid-drip. The staircases folding into themselves hold their breath.

I realize the canvas isn't blank at all. It's hungry.

I paint without brushes — the landscape lends me its own pigments. I drag a river across the surface and it becomes a river. I press my palm where I sense a door should be, and a door blooms there, wooden and warm, its brass handle remembering every hand that ever turned one.

Behind me, the world is changing faster now, as if jealous of my stillness. Mountains unspool into ribbons. The sun flickers between a lantern, an eye, a paper crane.

So I paint a sun that stays.

I paint a house with the porch light on. I paint a figure in the doorway — I don't look too closely at its face, because in dreams you know better than to check. I paint the smell of rain on hot pavement, the sound of a screen door, the feeling of being expected somewhere.

The canvas drinks it all in and gives it back, and slowly the chaos around me arranges itself around what I've made, like iron filings snapping toward a magnet.

Just as I sign my name — though the signature is less letters than a feeling, a warmth behind the sternum — the dream begins to thin at its edges. The canvas glows softly, satisfied, and I understand that I was never the painter here.

I was the paint.

And somewhere, in a room I'm just beginning to wake into, an easel stands by the window — its canvas blank, humming, patient as morning.
