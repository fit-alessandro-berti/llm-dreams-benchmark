...and the light turns strange — that heavy green the sky gets just before something big. The birds stop singing all at once, and the silence is somehow louder than the singing was.

You walk faster. Then run. But the path keeps rearranging itself — the twisted oak you just passed, you pass it again — and the storm follows at exactly your pace. Never closer. Never farther. Patient as a cat.

Lightning strikes somewhere ahead. Instead of thunder, there's a voice. Or something like a voice, the way things in dreams are things without quite being them. It says your name.

So you stop. You turn to face the storm, and it's enormous now — a slow gray wall of turning cloud — and inside it, perfectly visible in the way only dream-things are visible, there's a door. Old wood, brass handle, standing upright in the middle of the rain.

The storm reaches you. Wind tears straight through you without moving you an inch. Rain falls warm through your open hands, and every drop rings, faintly, like a small bell.

You walk to the door. The handle is cold and real. Just as you turn it — just as the latch gives —

You're back in the peaceful forest. Sunlight through the leaves. Birds singing. Everything exactly as it was before the storm.

Except in your hand, still warm, is a brass door handle.

And from somewhere deep in the trees, the voice says your name again.

I have a feeling this dream isn't finished with me yet.
