...not about the course at all.

You blink at the paper, sure you misread it. But no, it's still there, typed in plain, too-neat letters:

**1. What did you want to be when you were 7, before anyone told you what was practical?**
**2. Draw the exact shape of home.**
**3. Your best friend from childhood is calling from another room. What is she saying? Show your work.**
**4. Explain in 500 words or less why you keep running.**
**5. Bonus: Spell your own name correctly.**

Your pen feels too heavy. You look up and everyone else is already writing furiously, like they knew this was coming. The clock above the whiteboard is ticking backwards. The fluorescent lights are buzzing too loud.

You try to answer Question 1, but your hand writes something else on its own — in crayon, in *your* seven-year-old handwriting. The letters start to swim and rearrange every time you look away.

Then you realize with that cold, dream-stomach drop: you've never been to this class. Not once. You don't even know what the course *was*. You enrolled, and then you just... forgot to show up all semester.

The professor walks down the aisle toward you. As he gets closer, you see he has your face, just older and tired. He leans down, taps your blank page, and whispers, "Don't worry. Nobody passes this one. You just have to wake up."

And the paper goes warm and soft in your hands, like a blanket.
