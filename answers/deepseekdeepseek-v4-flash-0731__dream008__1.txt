...and you turn to your desk, but the keyboard is made of warm bread, the monitor is a window into a quiet ocean, and the project is just a single word floating in the air: *breathe*.

Your boss is still waiting, but now he’s wearing a penguin suit and holding a cupcake with a candle. The deadline is written in disappearing ink on the wall: **NOW** — except *now* keeps moving like a cat chasing its tail.

You open your mouth to say you can’t do it, but what comes out is a soft blue ribbon, and it ties itself into a knot that looks like a question mark. Your hands start typing anyway, except they’re not your hands — they’re small foxes dancing across the keys, leaving paw prints that spell out a poem about swimming through ceilings.

Your boss leans closer. His voice is honey and static. “Is it done yet?”

You look down. The project completed itself while you weren’t looking, and it’s made entirely of laughter and glass. The deadline dissolves into glitter. You try to feel stressed, but the stress is wearing a little hat, and it waves goodbye.

You smile.

“It was never impossible,” you say. “It was just a dream.”

And then the alarm clock rings, and you wake up with a half-finished sentence on your tongue: *...and you realized the deadline was only a door, and you had already walked through it.*
