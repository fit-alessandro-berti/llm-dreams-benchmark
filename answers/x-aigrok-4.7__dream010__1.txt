You step into the final challenge: a quiet room with one empty chair and a single question glowing on the wall.

“What would you actually do with the job?”

No timer. No judges visible. Just the hum of the lights and the sense that the answer has to be true, not impressive.

You sit. You don’t reach for the polished speech you’ve rehearsed. Instead you say the thing you’ve been circling all competition: you’d use the work to make something that didn’t exist before, something useful and a little strange, and you’d keep going even when it got hard and no one was watching.

The wall goes dark. The chair is just a chair again. Somewhere a door opens onto ordinary daylight, and your name is being called—not as a winner yet, just as someone who finished.

You stand up and walk toward it.
