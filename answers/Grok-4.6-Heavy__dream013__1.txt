You are in a maze filled with complex puzzles. Each puzzle requires intense concentration and logical thinking to solve, and as you move deeper into the maze, you find that the puzzles no longer sit on the walls. They sit in you.

The first ones were clean: doors that opened if you could hold a proof still in your head. Deeper in, concentration no longer just opens doors; it builds them. A gate appears when you prove it exists, but the gate is made of the proof, and walking through costs you the certainty you used to make it. Behind you the corridor forgets it was ever solved.

The riddles grow less mathematical and more intimate. A room whose floor is an unfinished sentence writes a word with every step, trying to become your name. Your hands are the same pale stone as the walls. You catch the maze solving itself in the corner of your eye—corridors rearranging to the shape of whatever you were just thinking. You understand then that the maze is not testing your logic. It is using it. Every deduction becomes another corridor. You are not escaping. You are being written.

At the center there is no exit. There is a table, and on it a small wooden maze, and inside that maze a figure pausing at a puzzle, concentrating so hard the air around them hums. You recognize the posture. You recognize the hands.

The figure looks up.
