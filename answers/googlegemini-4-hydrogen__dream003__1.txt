A low chime echoes—not from the box, but from somewhere deep inside your own chest—and the shifting patterns freeze into place with a quiet *click*.

The lid lifts on its own, folding backward until it vanishes completely.

Inside, there is no bottom. Peering into the box feels like looking down through a window in the clouds. Far below, tiny amber streetlamps glow along winding cobblestone paths, and a quiet, silver ocean laps against a shore of white glass. With the sudden, effortless certainty that only makes sense in sleep, you realize you are looking down at a place you used to live—even though you have never seen it before in your life.

From the depths of the box, a moth made of folded sheet music flutters upward, brushing against your cheek as it rises toward a ceiling that is no longer there. The scent of rain on hot pavement fills the air, sharp and vivid. As you reach your hand inside to catch the faint melody trailing behind it, the box softens into warm seafoam, slipping gently between your fingers just as the horizon begins to brighten into morning.
