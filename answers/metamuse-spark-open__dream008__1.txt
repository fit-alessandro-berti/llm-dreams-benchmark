Here is how your dream continues:

You are at your workplace, and suddenly, you are given a project with an impossible deadline. Your boss is waiting for you to start, and you

look down at your desk to open the file — but your desk is suddenly much, much longer than it should be. It stretches down a hallway that wasn't there before.

Your boss taps his watch impatiently, but when you glance at the clock on the wall, the hands are spinning backwards. It's 5 PM, then 9 AM, then 5 PM again.

"Due by end of day," he says, and his voice echoes like he's speaking from far away, even though he's right next to you.

You open the project folder and instead of papers, it's filled with sand. The more you try to scoop it into a pile, the faster it slips through your fingers and onto the floor — only the sand isn't falling, it's *floating* up toward the ceiling lights.

You try to type, but the keys on your keyboard are soft and warm, like piano keys made of wax. Your computer screen flickers and instead of a document, it shows you sitting at this exact desk, watching yourself.

Then you realize no one else in the office has noticed anything strange. Everyone is typing calmly, sipping coffee, as if their desks aren't also floating a few inches off the ground.

Your boss leans in again and whispers, not unkindly: "You don't have to finish it, you know. You just have to start."

And the moment he says that, the pressure in your chest lifts. The folder of sand becomes light as air. You take a breath, press one wobbly key, and the whole office gently begins to float — like you all decided, together, that gravity and deadlines were just suggestions.
