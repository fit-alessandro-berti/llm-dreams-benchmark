...a set of brushes that hum softly, as if each one holds a different note of music.

You reach for the nearest brush, and the moment your fingers close around it, the canvas ripples like the surface of a pond. You haven't dipped the brush in any paint, since there is none, but when you sweep it across the canvas, a ribbon of color pours out: the precise shade of a memory you'd forgotten, the warm amber of late afternoon light through a childhood window.

Each stroke paints something you feel rather than something you see. A curl of laughter becomes a spiral of gold. A quiet longing spreads into deep indigo, pooling at the edges. The shapes refuse to stay still. Triangles soften into birds, birds dissolve into rain, and the rain falls upward into a sky that is slowly becoming part of the painting.

Then you notice that the landscape around you is growing paler. The shifting colors of the world are draining toward the canvas, drawn in like breath. You realize you aren't painting a picture of this place. You are painting the place itself, and it has been waiting for someone to give it form.

You step back. The canvas is no longer blank, and no longer a canvas. It is a doorway, and through it you see a landscape of impossible beauty: mountains folded like origami, rivers of liquid light, a horizon that curves gently back toward you like an open hand.

The brush in your fingers hums one last note and falls silent.

You step through.

And just as your foot touches the painted ground, soft as the moment between sleeping and waking, you hear a voice that sounds remarkably like your own whisper, "Now, what will you make next?"
