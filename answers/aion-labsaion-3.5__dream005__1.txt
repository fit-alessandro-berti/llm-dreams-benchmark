...each conversation pulls you in like quicksand.

The first group stands in a tight circle near the refreshment table, speaking a language you almost recognize. You catch fragments—"the lighthouse," "before the thaw"—and when you ask where the bathroom is, they all turn in unison and point in different directions. You smile and back away.

The room stretches. It was a banquet hall a moment ago, but now the ceiling has receded into darkness and the far wall keeps retreating as you walk toward it. Somewhere across the space, you spot your friend's yellow jacket. You're certain it's them. But the crowd flows against you like a river, and every time you get close, they've moved to the opposite side of the room.

You pass a group of people all wearing your grandmother's face. They're laughing warmly at something one of them said, and you have the sinking feeling the joke was about you. One of them pats your shoulder as you pass. "You'll find them," she says. "Or you won't. Either way, the cake is being served soon."

There is no cake.

A woman materializes beside you and asks if you've seen her dog. You haven't. She shows you a photograph of your childhood home anyway. You tell her you're looking for your friend, and she says, "Oh, they left twenty minutes ago," but you can still see the yellow jacket, farther away now, near a door you're certain wasn't there before.

You try to shout their name but your voice comes out as static, like a radio between stations. The music—which you hadn't noticed until now—speeds up. Everyone begins swaying slightly off-beat. The lights dim to the exact warmth of a summer evening when you were nine.

The yellow jacket is right in front of you now. They turn around.

It's you. Of course it's you. They smile with your mouth and say, "You found me. I was here the whole time."

You wake with the sensation of falling upward, the dream already dissolving, holding onto nothing but the certainty that the cake—wherever it was—was probably very good.
