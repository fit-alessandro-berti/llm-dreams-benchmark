You realize you need to find your way back before the sun crosses the meridian stone, though you have no memory of ever having been here, only the certainty that the path lies through the market where merchants trade in languages made of light. The cobblestones beneath your bare feet are warm, almost breathing, and when you look down, you see that you are wearing garments you don't recognize—heavy wool dyed the color of dried blood, with clasps shaped like sleeping birds.

Around you, the city rises in tiers of timber and thatch, smoke curling from hearths that burn with a blue flame that doesn't quite look like fire. People move through the square with purpose, their faces blurred as though seen through rain on glass, and you understand with a clarity that defies the strangeness of it all that you are not merely visiting this place—you have always been here, and the *waking* was the dream.

You need to reach the well before the bells toll, for that is where the keeper of the threshold waits, and only she can tell you whether you have stumbled into the past, or if the world you left behind was the illusion all along.
