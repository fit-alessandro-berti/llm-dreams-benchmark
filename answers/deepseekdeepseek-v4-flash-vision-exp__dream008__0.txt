So you smile back at your boss and say, “No problem.”

You snap your fingers, and the project folder turns into a bouquet of paper flowers. The conference table becomes a river of warm tea. The deadline on the wall melts and drips into a puddle that says “never.” Your boss laughs, and it sounds like wind chimes.

You sit down, pull out a laptop made of moonlight, and start working in a language that doesn’t exist. The work feels like play. The project completes itself before you even think about it, and then you and your boss are floating above the office, looking down at all the desks like little toys.

“Good job,” says the boss, turning into a friendly owl.

“I know,” you say, and you wake up just before the alarm, feeling weirdly proud, with no memory of any real work at all.
