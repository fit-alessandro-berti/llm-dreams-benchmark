…find the one thing that doesn’t belong. The village around you is all thatched roofs, mud roads, and candlelit windows—no wires, no engines, no screens—yet everyone stops speaking when you pass, as if they’ve been expecting you. A child presses a small brass key into your palm and whispers, “You’re late.” At dusk, a bell begins to toll, and the streets empty. You follow the sound to a tower with a door that has no handle, only a keyhole shaped like your own silhouette. You slide the key in, and the door becomes a mirror. In it, you see your bedroom exactly as you left it—but behind your sleeping body stands a figure in old-fashioned clothes, smiling. They mouth the words: “Don’t wake up yet.”
