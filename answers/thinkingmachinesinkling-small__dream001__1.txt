You start to ask a passerby for directions, but their mouth unspools into a map made of moth wings, and suddenly you’re not walking *toward* a destination but through the very idea of one. The city rearranges itself like a deck of cards: the skyscraper with your meeting is now the tree you climbed at age six, and the conference room is a kitchen where someone you lost is stirring something that smells like regret.

The streets don’t loop—they *remember*. You turn a corner and find yourself in a subway station that is also a cathedral, where the announcement boards don’t list stops but feelings: *Longing*, *Almost There*, *The Meeting You Were Afraid Of*. You check your watch, but your watch is a sunflower, and its face is turning toward a sun that is also the clock tower in the distance.

You realize the hour hasn’t been shrinking; it’s been stretching like taffy, elastic and impossible. The meeting isn’t in a building at all—it’s happening inside every window you pass, in every reflection of rain you haven’t noticed falling upward. You stop running. The crowd becomes birds, then paper lanterns, then the sound of your own breath agreeing with you.

When you finally arrive—at a door made of warm glass—you open it not with a key but with a thought. Inside, the table is empty except for a chair that looks exactly like yours, and a single cup of tea cooling in the center, where the steam spells your own name backward.

You sit down. The meeting begins not with words, but with the understanding that you were never late, because in dreams, the destination is only ever a mirror for the self that finally agreed to show up.
