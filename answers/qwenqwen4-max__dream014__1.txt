...and you follow them, though you never quite catch their names. There are five of them, or maybe six — every time you count, someone is laughing at the edge of the group and the number slips.

One carries a map folded into the shape of a bird. When she opens it, the streets on the paper are moving, slowly, like something breathing in its sleep. *You're here,* she says, and her finger rests on a small blue square that is unmistakably the room you slept in as a child, though that room is a thousand miles from this city.

You walk. The buildings lean in to listen. A traffic light turns a color you have no word for, and everyone crosses, so you cross too.

Someone asks where you're trying to go, and you open your mouth to say *home,* but what comes out is the sound of a screen door in summer, and they all nod as though this is a perfectly ordinary address. *Oh, that's just past the fountain,* says the tall one. *We know it well.*

At the fountain there is no water, only warm light pooling in the basin, and the strangers begin to wade into it, coats and shoes and all, holding their hands out to you. You are afraid for one clean moment — the fear of being helped, of being seen all the way down — and then you step in, and it is like stepping into a bath your mother drew for you before you were born.

When you come up on the other side, the city has gone quiet and small, the size of a model village, and you can see the whole shape of it: every street you took wrong was a street that led here.

You turn to thank them.

They are gone — no, not gone. You look down and there are five sets of footprints leading out of the light, and yours are the sixth, and ahead of you, at the far end of the square, someone is standing very still with a suitcase, looking the way you looked an hour ago.

You start walking toward them. You find you already know the way.
