Not your handwriting now, but the uneven, oversized letters you used as a child.

**Question 1: What did you want to become before anyone told you what you were good at?**

You look around. Everyone else is writing quickly. Their pens make the sound of rain.

You turn the page.

**Question 2: Describe a mistake you would be willing to make again.**

The clock above the door has no numbers. Instead, its hands point to words: *Soon. Already. Never.*

You raise your hand, but the examiner is standing outside the window, three floors above the ground. She motions for you to keep going.

On the next page is a photograph of a kitchen you almost remember. Someone has left a chair pulled out for you.

**Question 3: Where would you go if no one asked you to explain?**

You search your pockets for another pen and find a small brass key. As you place it on the desk, a keyhole appears in the paper.

Around you, the other students stop writing.

“Is this part of the exam?” you whisper.

The student beside you looks up. He is very old. His exam booklet is still blank.

“I’ve been waiting for them to clarify that,” he says.

You put the key into the page and turn it.

The desk opens onto a narrow staircase. Warm air rises from below, carrying the smell of toast. You can hear someone moving dishes in a sink.

Behind you, the examiner taps on the window.

You leave your pen across the unfinished answer and begin to climb down.
