“Behind you,” they say.

You turn. The crowd parts, revealing a long hallway lined with clocks. Every clock is counting backward, and each time one reaches zero, a stranger steps through its face and joins the gathering behind you.

You hurry onward.

In the next room, a circle of people dances without music. Their feet trace patterns on the floor, and with every step, the walls change color. One dancer catches your arm.

“We know who you’re looking for,” they whisper. “They’re kind. They’re funny. They forget to eat when they’re nervous. They’re standing right beside you.”

You look around. The circle has turned its head toward someone just outside the room.

Beyond them, you find another group seated in darkness, watching a film made of smoke. They wave you through without looking away from the screen. The smoke shows flashes of your life: a kitchen filled with morning light, a hand waving goodbye, a voice saying your name in a room you can’t quite remember.

The film begins skipping.

The same image appears again and again: a small table at the center of the gathering, two chairs, and someone sitting beneath a lamp, waiting.

You push through the final doorway.

The room is empty except for the table. A cup of tea steams gently in one chair. In the other, the person you know looks up and smiles.

“You took a long time,” they say.

Behind you, the laughter of all the groups rises like rain.

You sit down, and they slide the cup toward you. Its surface reflects a familiar room: warm light, familiar faces, a place you realize you are not seeing for the first time.

“I thought you were looking for me,” they say.

You start to explain, but the room fills again with everyone you have encountered—the dancers, the clock-faced strangers, the people beneath the rain. They sit together in perfect silence, watching you take the first sip.

And as the tea tastes faintly of childhood, you understand that you were never looking for just one person.

You were remembering how to find your way back to them.
