and it is the only thing in the entire world that stays still.

Everything else around you is melting and reforming — trees unfolding into flights of paper birds, mountains breathing in and out like sleeping animals, a river of liquid gold flowing uphill and turning, mid-air, into a flock of silver fish. The sky cycles through colors that don't have names, colors you can hear like low musical notes, colors that taste faintly of mint and rain.

But the canvas floats in front of you, perfectly white. Perfectly silent. Perfectly blank.

A paintbrush hovers beside it, dripping a drop of blue that never falls.

When you reach for it, the brush is warm, like it's alive. You don't dip it in paint. Instead, you sweep it through the air and it comes back coated in the swirling purple of the sky. You touch it to the canvas.

The stroke doesn't sit *on* the canvas. It opens.

Where you painted purple, there is now a real window into a purple sky, calm and still. You paint a green hill beneath it, and you can smell fresh grass. You paint a small yellow sun, and warmth spills out onto your face.

Excited, you paint faster.

You paint a door and step through it into the world you just created — except inside, everything holds its shape. The hill doesn't melt. The sun doesn't turn into a flock of clocks. For the first time since the dream began, your feet feel solid ground.

You paint a chair and sit down. You paint a table, a cup of tea that stays tea. Then, because the stillness feels lonely, you paint a companion: a fox made of soft orange triangles, with eyes like polished amber.

The fox blinks. It steps down off the nothing and shakes paint from its fur.

"You've been running from the changing," it says, in a voice made of rustling leaves. "So you made a place that doesn't."

"Is that wrong?" you ask.

The fox looks back at the door, where the outside dream is pressing against your painted sky, turning your sun wobbly at the edges. "Nothing in a dream can stay still forever. Not even this."

As if on cue, your painted hill begins to ripple. The tea in your cup rises up in a spiral and becomes a tiny, steaming dragon. The fox's triangles soften into feathers, then into autumn leaves, then back into fur.

But it's gentler here. Slower. The changes don't sweep you away — they dance around you.

You pick up the brush again and, instead of trying to hold the world still, you begin to paint *with* the changes. You paint a tree and let it become a staircase. You paint a staircase and let it become a waterfall. You paint a boat on the waterfall and sail down through clouds that turn into whales, singing you onward.

The fox, now a bird, now a fox again, laughs beside you.

At last, the canvas appears in front of you again, floating on the water. But this time, it's not blank. On it is a painting of a person sleeping in a quiet, dark room, dreaming of colors and shapes and a small orange fox.

The person in the painting looks exactly like you.

You reach out to touch your own sleeping face —

— and wake up gasping in the dark, heart pounding, the dream already dissolving like paint in water.

Everything in your room is still. Chair. Table. Cup left from last night.

You sit up and look down at your hands.

Across your fingertips, faint and impossible, are smears of purple, gold, and orange paint.
