You spin on your heel, grab the brass doorknob of their apartment, and yank it open. You expect the dim, carpeted hallway and the faint smell of someone else's dinner. Instead, a rush of cool, salty wind hits your face. You step through the threshold and find yourself on a narrow wooden pier stretching endlessly into a dark twilight ocean, the water completely still and reflecting a sky crowded with three pale moons.

"Wait!" your friend calls out.

You whip back around, ready to deliver the final, undeniable point that will finally make them understand—though the moment you try to grab hold of what the argument was actually about, the details slip through your mind like wet sand. All that remains is the sharp, burning tightness in your chest and the absolute certainty that you are *right*.

Your friend is standing in the freestanding wooden doorframe, which sits awkwardly on the damp planks of the pier with no apartment behind it. In their arms, they are clutching the exact thing you were fighting over. A second ago it was a broken promise, or maybe a crucial decision about the future, though now it looks unmistakably like an antique, ticking brass clock with no hands.

You open your mouth to shout, to tell them they’re being stubborn and impossible. You pull in a deep breath and force the words out—but only a dry, papery whisper escapes your throat. You try again, straining until your jaw aches, yet the ocean breeze swallows your voice whole.

Your friend doesn't look angry anymore. Their face flickers briefly, shifting into the features of a teacher you haven't thought about in years before settling back into their own familiar expression. They step forward through the doorway, and the wooden frame dissolves behind them like mist.

"You forgot these," they say softly, their voice echoing as if you’re both standing inside a vast cathedral.

They hold out their hands, which are overflowing with keys—hundreds of them, rusted iron and bright silver, tiny and impossibly huge. You glance down and notice your pockets are torn open, steadily leaking fine white sand onto the pier. 

All at once, the anger evaporates, replaced by a dizzying wave of relief and quiet absurdity. You aren't furious with them anymore; you are only suddenly, urgently worried because the tide is rising and neither of you is wearing shoes. 

You reach out to take a handful of the keys. As your fingers brush theirs, the wooden pier shudders and begins to tilt gently upward, lifting off the black water like a quiet elevator rising toward the moons.
