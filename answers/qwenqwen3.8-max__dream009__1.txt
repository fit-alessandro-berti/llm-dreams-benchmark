You wake up in a completely different era, with no modern technology around. As you explore your new surroundings, you realize you need to find water before nightfall.

The air smells of woodsmoke and damp earth. The path beneath your feet is worn smooth by sandals and hooves, not tires. Somewhere nearby, a bell rings slowly, marking the hour in a language you almost understand.

You follow the sound of distant laughter and come upon a village built from stone and timber. People glance at you with curiosity but not fear, as if strangers are rare but welcome. An old woman gestures toward a well at the center of the square and says, “If you’re lost, begin by drawing water. The well remembers everyone.”

When you lower the bucket, the rope feels heavy, real, more real than anything you have touched in years. As the water rises, you see not your own reflection but a map shimmering on the surface—roads, rivers, and a single glowing path leading beyond the hills.

You realize this dream is not about escaping the past, but about discovering what remains when everything familiar is taken away: instinct, courage, and the quiet knowledge that even in another time, you are still searching for a place to belong.
