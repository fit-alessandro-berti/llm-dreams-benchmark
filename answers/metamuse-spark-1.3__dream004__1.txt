You come across a blank canvas and —

as soon as you look at it, it stops changing.

Everything else around you keeps shifting — the sky melts from turquoise to violet, the trees fold themselves from triangles into spirals, the ground ripples like water under your feet — but the canvas stays perfectly still, perfectly white. Waiting.

Next to it, floating in mid-air, is a paintbrush made of light.

You reach for it without thinking, and the moment your fingers close around it, you *understand*: whatever you paint here becomes real.

You stroke soft blue across the top, and above you the wild sky calms into a clear, endless morning. You paint a golden sun, and warmth spreads across your skin. You paint a winding path of deep green leading to distant purple hills, and you feel the ground steady itself beneath you into solid earth.

You paint birds, and they lift off the canvas, singing. You paint a door, and when you open it, soft white light pours out.

Curious and not afraid at all, you step through —

and you wake up, with the faint smell of fresh paint still in the air, and the feeling that you brought a little bit of that color back with you.
