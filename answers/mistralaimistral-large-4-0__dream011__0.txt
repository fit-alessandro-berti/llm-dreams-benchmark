You reach for your pencil, but your hand passes through it. The invigilator—a woman with your mother's eyes but a stranger's posture—leans over your desk and whispers, "You don't need to write the answers, dear. You just need to remember why you chose to forget them."

The clock on the wall begins to melt, counting down not in minutes, but in years. You realize, with a clarity that burns like ice water, that you have already passed this exam. You have failed it. You have aced it. You are still sitting here, and the only question that matters is the one you haven't asked yet: *What happens when you wake up?*

The paper curls at the edges, transforming into autumn leaves. Somewhere outside the dream, an alarm clock is ringing, but you cannot move to answer it. You are, finally, exactly where your career needs you to be: suspended between the person you were and the person you are still becoming, holding a pencil that writes only in dreams.
