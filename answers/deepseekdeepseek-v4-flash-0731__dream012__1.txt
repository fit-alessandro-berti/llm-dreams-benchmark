...you grip the worn wooden wheel, feel the spray sting your face, and hear your own voice cut through the roar—steady, certain, alive.

“Hold the course! Reef the mainsail! Batten down every hatch—we’ve weathered worse than this, and we’ll weather this too!”

The crew hesitates for only a heartbeat. Then they move as one, ropes flying, voices shouting over the wind. The ship groans, tilts, climbs a mountain of black water—and for a moment the world is nothing but salt and thunder.

But you smile.

Because in the eye of the storm, you remember: this is not just water and wind. This is the dream-test. And the ship? The ship is your own heart, learning to sail through fear.

You take a deep breath, set your jaw, and whisper to the storm:

“Is that all you’ve got?”

And the sea, somehow, laughs with you.
