You step closer. The air hums. The shifting shapes around you slow down, almost holding their breath.

The canvas is perfectly white—too white, a white that glows like a memory not yet made. You reach out, hesitant. The moment your fingertips brush the surface, the dream pours through you.

You don’t paint with your hands. You paint with your longing.

A sweep of your sadness turns the canvas into a field of blue rain. A flicker of joy spills golden spirals across the sky. A forgotten thought curls into silver vines, blooming with impossible flowers that open and close like breathing hearts.

You keep painting—laughing, crying, remembering—until the canvas is no longer blank. It is the world you are standing in. The colors and shapes stop shifting. They settle into a single image: you, standing on the edge of a dream, looking back at yourself.

And then the canvas smiles.

You wake up with wet paint on your fingers—and a quiet voice whispering, *“Don’t forget me.”*
