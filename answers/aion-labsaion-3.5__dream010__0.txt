...each one is stranger than the last.

The first challenge is a written exam, except the questions rewrite themselves while I'm reading them. Question 7 asks, "What did you want to be before you wanted anything at all?" I don't know the answer, but my hand does — it writes in a script I've never learned, and the judges nod as though it's correct.

The second challenge takes place in a hallway made entirely of doors. Behind each one, someone is already doing my dream job. Every one of them is me — different ages, wearing clothes I outgrew decades ago. One of them looks up from the desk and says, "You're late," and I want to explain that I've been busy, that I got lost, that I almost came so many times, but the door closes on its own.

By the third challenge, the other competitors have started to look like mirrors of me too. We're asked to give a presentation, but the stage shrinks a little every time someone speaks a sentence that isn't true. I'm the only one left standing on a platform the size of a postage stamp, and the strangest thing is that I feel fine there. Steady. The judges' faces keep shifting — my grandmother, an old teacher, a stranger from a train station I passed through once — and they all have the same patient expression.

The final challenge is simple, they say. Just one question: *Name the job.*

I open my mouth and nothing comes. I panic — how did I make it this far without knowing? But the head judge leans forward and whispers, "Correct. That's the answer. It doesn't have a name yet. You're the one who's supposed to give it one."

They hand me the prize. It's warm, like something alive, and when I look down to read the engraved title, the letters rearrange themselves into a single sentence: *You fill this in every morning. That's the job.*

The audience applauds, and it sounds exactly like rain beginning. Somewhere behind me, all the doors in the hallway swing open at once, and light pours out of every one of them.

I wake with the ghost of paper in my hand and the smell of fresh ink in the air, and for a moment I can still feel the answer — right there, on the tip of my tongue, waiting to be written down.
