You are about to give a speech to a large audience. As you step onto the stage, you see the crowd looking expectantly at you, and you begin to speak.

The first word leaves your mouth as a moth — pale, certain, almost polite. Then another. Then the rest follow, and they do not land like a speech so much as weather. The crowd does not applaud. They tilt their faces up the way people do in snow. You keep going because the lights seem to depend on it.

Halfway through a sentence you cannot finish, you notice the front row is everyone you have ever needed to tell something to. Their programs say only *you are dreaming*, in a font that changes when you blink. No one seems surprised. They are not judging. They catch the moths in their open hands as if the words were always meant to be held, not heard.

When you run out of language, one moth lands on the microphone and says thank you in your voice. The hall goes dark except for that small white wing, still beating.
