You decide to stop talking.

Not because you've given up, or because they won - but because suddenly, in the middle of your sentence, you notice how strange everything looks.

The room you two are arguing in isn't your apartment, or theirs. It's your childhood living room, but the walls are breathing. The wallpaper flickers like it's made of paper lanterns. Outside the window, it's snowing and sunny at the same time.

Your friend is still talking, their voice loud and hurt, but their words are coming out as little clouds of colored smoke - yours are too. Your angry red clouds and their sharp blue ones are colliding in the middle of the room, tangling together and popping.

And you realize you can't even remember what the argument was *about* anymore. Something important, yes, but the details are dissolving like sugar in water. Was it about moving away? Money? A broken promise?

All you can remember is that you don't want to lose them.

So you decide to walk across the room, right through the cloud of tangled words, and sit down next to them on the floor. The floor is suddenly warm sand.

You say, "Wait. Can we just start over for a second? Tell me what you're actually afraid of."

Your friend blinks. The blue smoke clears. Their face softens, and for a moment they look much younger, like when you first met.

They say, "I'm afraid if we don't agree on this, we won't be us anymore."

You take their hand. It's oddly light, like it might float away.

You decide to tell the truth back: "I'm afraid of the same thing."

And the second you both say it, the house stops breathing so hard. The snow outside slows down and hangs suspended in the air, sparkling. The argument isn't solved, but the pressure in the room drops, like someone finally opened a window that had been sealed shut for years.

You both just sit there in the quiet, sand-floor living room, watching the word-clouds fade into nothing, knowing you'll figure it out together when you wake up.
