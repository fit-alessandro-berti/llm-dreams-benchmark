As you move deeper into the maze, you realize the puzzles are no longer just puzzles.

At first, they were mechanical - sliding tiles that clicked into place, brass locks with spinning dials, doors that only opened if you arranged glowing symbols in the right order. The walls were high hedges at first, then cold stone, then something stranger - smooth, dark glass that rippled like water when you touched it.

But now, the deeper you go, the more personal they become.

One room has no door at all, just a wall covered in thousands of tiny clocks, all ticking out of sync. The puzzle isn't to stop them, but to find the one clock that ticks in time with your own heartbeat. When you press your hand to it, all the others fall silent at once, and a new path opens.

The next room is filled with photographs scattered on the floor - faces you know, faces you almost remember. You have to arrange them not by date or name, but by how they made you *feel*. As soon as you place the last one, the photos dissolve into light and the floor beneath you shifts.

The maze is breathing now. You can feel it. The walls aren't trying to trap you anymore - they're trying to *learn* you. The air gets warmer, softer. The puzzles stop requiring intense concentration and start requiring surrender. Logic gives way to intuition.

And then, at the very center, there is no final puzzle at all.

There is just a small, circular garden under an open sky that shouldn't exist. The stars above are impossibly bright. In the middle of the garden is a simple wooden chair, and on the chair is a version of you - sleeping peacefully, a faint smile on your face.

You understand suddenly: You weren't trying to solve your way *out* of the maze. You were solving your way *back* to yourself. Every locked door was a doubt you had let go, every solved riddle was a memory you had made peace with.

As you step into the garden, the hedges and stone walls behind you fold down silently like paper, revealing that there never were any walls at all - just an endless, quiet field.

You sit down next to yourself, close your eyes, and for the first time in the dream, you stop thinking altogether.

And just as you feel yourself waking up, a soft voice - your own voice - whispers:

*"You know the way out now."*
