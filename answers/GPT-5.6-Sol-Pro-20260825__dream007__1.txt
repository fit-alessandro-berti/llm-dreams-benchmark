…you decide to stop trying to win.

The words you were about to shout remain trapped behind your teeth. Instead, you ask your friend to explain what they are truly afraid of losing. At first, they stare at you suspiciously. Then the anger drains from their face, and the room around you begins to change.

The walls stretch upward into dark trees, the ceiling dissolves into a stormy sky, and the floor becomes a narrow bridge suspended over a deep canyon. You realize that throughout the argument, you have both been standing at opposite ends of this bridge, pulling on the same fraying rope—each convinced the other intends to cut it.

Your friend admits that the disagreement was never only about the decision itself. They were afraid that choosing differently would mean the friendship no longer mattered to you. You confess that you had mistaken their fear for an attempt to control you.

As you speak honestly, the bridge widens beneath your feet. The broken planks repair themselves, one by one, until you can meet in the middle. Neither of you completely changes your mind, but you agree to stop treating disagreement as betrayal.

Then your friend opens their hand. Resting in their palm is a tiny, glowing compass. Its needle points not north, but toward you.

“You were never supposed to follow me,” they say. “I only wanted to know we could still find each other.”

The storm clears. Just before you wake, you notice that you are holding an identical compass—and its needle points toward them.
