The lightning splits the sky into pale, impossible shapes—faces, doors, maps of places you have never been. The crew’s shouts blur into one voice, rising and falling with the waves, while beneath the deck you hear a deep knocking, patient and deliberate, like something old trying to find its way out.

You turn to face the storm, rain stinging your eyes, and suddenly the sea goes still. The clouds part to reveal a moon reflected in the water—not above you, but below, as if the ship has drifted into the sky instead of the deep. The wheel goes slack in your hands. You understand, without knowing how, that the voyage was never about escaping the storm.

It was about arriving.

And as the first pale light touches the horizon, you wake with the taste of salt on your lips and the sound of waves still moving somewhere far away.
