... and each challenge feels like a surreal version of the work you’d actually do.

First, you’re dropped into an ocean of unread emails that rise like tidal waves. You have to swim through them, opening the ones that glow golden and ignoring the ones that whisper lies. You make it, exhausted but lighter.

Then, a maze of endless meetings. Every door is labeled “Important” and every room smells like stale coffee. You learn to walk past the loudest voices and open the only door that’s slightly ajar—behind it is a quiet room with a single chair, where someone says, “We’ve been waiting for you.”

Then, a stage. The judges all wear your own face. They ask: *“Why do you want this job?”* You answer honestly, and your reflection in their glasses begins to smile.

The final room is empty except for a desk. On the desk: a blank contract and a pen. The host says, *“Describe your dream job in one sentence. If it’s true, it will sign itself.”*

You pick up the pen. Your hand moves before you think.

You write: *“A place where I can build what I love, with people who care, and never stop learning.”*

The paper glows. The signature appears—yours. The room fills with applause. You turn to thank everyone, but the world is dissolving.

You wake up in your own bed, still smiling. For a moment, you can’t remember if that was a dream or a memory of something your future left behind. Then your alarm rings—but instead of a beep, it sounds like clapping.

And you realize: you already have the job. You just haven’t started yet.
