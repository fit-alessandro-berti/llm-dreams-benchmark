The strangers—who somehow all have your eyes—lead you to a courtyard you’ve never noticed before, tucked between two buildings that lean together like old friends. In the center is a compass the size of a door, its needle spinning lazily. One of them takes your hand and places it on the glass.

“You weren’t lost,” they say in unison, their voices like pages turning. “You were just walking the long way home.”

The needle stops, pointing not north, but inward. And suddenly, the city exhales. The streets rearrange themselves into a map of your own making, each alleyway a memory, each intersection a choice you now feel calm enough to make. When you turn to thank them, the strangers are gone, but their warmth lingers on your skin like sunlight.

You begin to walk. For the first time, the crowd doesn’t feel like a flood pulling you under—it feels like a current carrying you exactly where you need to be.
