As you move deeper into the maze, you notice that the walls are rearranging themselves—not randomly, but in the order of the thoughts you’re trying to finish.

The first chamber contains three boxes labeled **ALL APPLES**, **ALL ORANGES**, and **MIXED FRUIT**. Only one label is telling the truth.

You reach into the first and find only oranges. The second contains only apples. The third holds a mixture of both. The final label is true, and the door unlocks with a sound like a page turning.

With every solution, the maze changes. A silent corridor becomes a library. The library becomes a staircase rising through stars. The puzzles grow more intricate, but your mind grows clearer. You begin to understand that every locked door is a question waiting for the right shape of thought.

At the final chamber, there is no lock—only a mirror.

Written across its surface are the words:

**What remains when there is nothing left to discover?**

You realize the answer is not a thing, but a quality: your attention. The looking. The questioning. The part of you that refuses to accept the first obvious answer.

You touch the glass.

The maze falls silent.

Then the mirror opens like an eye, and light pours through you.

You wake—not lost, but carrying the feeling that the whole world is a puzzle you have only just begun to solve.
