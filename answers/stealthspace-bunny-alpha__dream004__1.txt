and a paintbrush is already waiting in your hand.

It feels cool as moonlight and warm as a childhood summer. The canvas stands in a meadow where triangles drift past like silver fish and the horizon keeps folding itself into new colors. Its surface is perfectly white—then softly blue, then almost gold, though none of those colors seem to belong to it.

You raise the brush.

The first stroke is a curve. It shivers, unfolds into a ribbon, and becomes a road winding toward a sun with too many edges. The second stroke is a triangle; it sprouts windows and doors and begins to hum. A careless drip of blue becomes an ocean. A trembling dot rises into the sky and hangs there, bright as a secret.

The landscape catches every color. Mountains soften into waves. Clouds stretch into wings. Flowers open in colors that have no names, each petal a different shape, as if the world is remembering how to dream.

You paint until the canvas is full—except for one small square left untouched.

You paint a tiny door inside it.

Beyond the door is a room you know but cannot name. A blank canvas rests on an easel. A brush lies beside it, still warm.

You understand, suddenly, that the painting was never the dream.

It was an invitation.

You pick up the brush, and the colors begin to change all over again.
