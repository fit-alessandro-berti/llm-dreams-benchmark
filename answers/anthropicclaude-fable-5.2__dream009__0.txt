You walk through tall grass that whispers in a language you almost recognize. In the distance, smoke rises from a cluster of thatched roofs. Your clothes have changed without your noticing: rough wool, a leather belt, boots that seem to have always fit your feet.

At the edge of the village, an old woman is drawing water from a stone well. She looks up as if she's been expecting you. "You're late," she says, handing you the bucket. "The clockmaker has been asking for you."

This makes sense to you, the way things do in dreams.

The clockmaker's shop sits at the end of a crooked lane, and inside, hundreds of clocks tick out of rhythm with each other. He's bent over a workbench, and when he turns, he has your grandfather's eyes, though he's no one you've ever met.

"There you are," he says. He presses something small and warm into your palm: a brass key, humming faintly. "One of these clocks is yours. Wind it, and you go back. But choose carefully. The others belong to other sleepers."

You move along the shelves. One clock has no hands. One runs backward. One shows a tiny painted moon that winks at you. Then, in the corner, you see a plain wooden clock that ticks in time with your own heartbeat.

You lift the key. The clockmaker smiles. Through the window, the village is already dissolving into gold light, the grass and the well and the old woman all fading.

You turn the key once, twice...

And somewhere far away, you hear a familiar sound, growing louder. It might be an alarm. It might be birdsong. You can't quite tell yet.
