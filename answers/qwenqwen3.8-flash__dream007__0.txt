…you decide to stop shouting and listen—not just to what your friend is saying, but to the fear beneath it. The moment you do, the room begins to change. The heated argument, which had felt like a courtroom full of accusations, softens into a quiet kitchen at night. The table between you is no longer covered in sharp words, but in old photos that move when you’re not looking at them.

Your friend is crying, though their voice stays steady. They say, “You always make me feel like I’m too much.”

You want to defend yourself, but the dream turns your words into a small bird that flutters up between you. Instead of speaking, you reach across the table and touch their hand. As soon as your fingers meet, the air fills with remembered moments: laughing in the rain, staying up too late, the way your friend once sat with you in silence when no explanation was possible.

The disagreement was never really about the thing you were fighting about. It was about wanting to be understood without being fixed.

You take a breath and say, “You are not too much. I’m just afraid of losing you.”

Your friend looks at you for a long moment. Then the kitchen walls turn to glass, and beyond them is a vast field under two moons. The argument becomes quiet. The tension becomes a door. You both open it together, not as enemies, but as people who care enough to risk being honest.

You wake up feeling like the friendship has survived the night, though you’re not sure whether you were the one who changed—or the dream.
