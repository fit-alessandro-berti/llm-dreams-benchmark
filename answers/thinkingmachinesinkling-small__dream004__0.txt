You reach out to touch it, but your fingers don’t land on fabric. Instead, they sink into something cool and liquid, and as you pull back, your hand leaves behind a trail of cobalt and silver. The canvas responds. Not with a brush, but with light. A shape blooms: a square that is also a sphere, turning slowly, bleeding droplets of emerald that become leaves, then become birds, then become wing-shaped clouds.

The colors don’t stay still—they migrate. The crimson beside your knee becomes a low, humming chord; the jagged yellow line across the top sounds like distant thunder. You realize the landscape around you—the shifting hills, the trees that are sometimes spirals and sometimes flat planes of magenta—are not separate from this canvas. They are its reflections, its unfinished edges.

You step closer. The canvas expands, or perhaps the world contracts, and you find yourself inside the painting. Your body becomes a brushstroke of liquid gold, your thoughts turning into geometric patterns that float and dissolve. You are both the observer and the color, both the dreamer and the dream.

Then, from a corner of the canvas that seems to open like a mouth, a new shape emerges: a door made of shifting shades of violet, with a handle that is just a single, perfect circle of white. You know, with the certainty of dreams, that stepping through it will make you not the artist, but the first stroke of an entirely new reality—one where the sky is a spiral, and every shape you become is only temporary, beautiful, and endlessly unfinished.

You reach for the handle. The world around you dissolves into a single, brilliant, changing color... and the canvas whispers your new shape into being.
