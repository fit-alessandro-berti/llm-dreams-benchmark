...reach for your keyboard, only to find the keys have rearranged themselves into an alphabet you almost recognize. Your boss stands at the edge of your desk, tapping a wristwatch that has no hands. "It's due yesterday," she says, and somehow this makes perfect sense.

You open the project folder. Inside is a single sheet of paper, and on it, a drawing of the folder you're holding. You nod as though this clarifies everything.

"I'll need more time," you try to say, but the words come out as a small flock of paper birds. They flutter up toward the ceiling, which is much higher than you remember, and nest among the fluorescent lights. Your coworkers don't look up. They are all typing in unison, and you realize the sound of their keys is rain.

The office is filling with water, slowly and gently. Your desk begins to drift. Your boss is now standing in a rowboat, still tapping her watch, but she's getting smaller as the current carries you past the copy room, past the break room where the coffee maker is brewing something that glows, and out through the big window that has always, you now understand, been a door.

Outside, the deadline is waiting for you. It's an actual line, drawn in chalk across an enormous field at dusk. You step off your floating desk and walk toward it, the project folder warm in your hands like fresh bread.

You open it one more time. The drawing has changed. Now it shows you, standing in a field, looking at a drawing.

You step over the line.

Nothing happens. The wind moves through the grass. Somewhere far behind you, a phone is ringing, and it sounds remarkably like your morning alarm...
