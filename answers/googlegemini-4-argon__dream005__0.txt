...every time you try to squeeze past a circle of guests, the layout of the house seems to shift. You are holding a warm, heavy glass filled with a glowing blue liquid, though you can't remember who handed it to you. 

You spot a familiar jacket at the far end of the room—it’s your friend! Relief washes over you, and you hurry toward them. However, the plush velvet carpet beneath your feet suddenly feels like deep, wet sand, making every step agonizingly slow. 

As you push through the crowd, you pass a group of people dressed in vintage masquerade attire. They are laughing hysterically, though no sound is coming out of their mouths; instead, the sound of crashing ocean waves fills your ears. You ask a tall man in a tuxedo if he has seen your friend. He turns to you, revealing he has a vintage clock where his face should be, and politely hands you a single autumn leaf as an answer. You accept it, because in the logic of the dream, it makes complete sense.

The parlor stretches into an endless hallway lined with towering bookshelves. Halfway down, a group of partygoers is floating a few inches off the floor, casually sipping champagne in mid-air. 

Finally, you reach the end of the hall and tap the shoulder of the person in the familiar jacket. They turn around, smiling warmly. It isn't your friend at all—it’s your third-grade teacher, who rapidly morphs into a childhood pet, and then into a mirror reflection of yourself. 

"You're late," your reflection says, their voice echoing like you're inside a vast cave. "Everyone is waiting for your speech."

Suddenly, the walls fall away, and the hallway becomes a massive, brightly lit stage. The murmuring crowd goes dead silent, thousands of faceless silhouettes turning to stare up at you under the blinding spotlight. You open your mouth to speak, realize you aren't wearing any shoes, and—just as the microphone begins to melt like candle wax in your hand—you wake up.
