...open your mouth to tell him you'll try, but no sound comes out. Instead, a small paper airplane flies out and lands on his desk.

He doesn't seem surprised. He just folds his arms and points at the clock on the wall.

The clock says 5:00 PM. The deadline is 5:01 PM.

Then it says 4:59 PM. Then 5:02 PM. The numbers are melting and swapping places like they're not sure where they belong either.

You sit down at your desk. Your computer is already on, and there are 1,000 unread emails, all with the same subject line: **URGENT — START NOW.**

You click the first one. It opens a second computer inside your computer. On that screen, there's another tiny version of you, sitting at another tiny desk, looking up at you, terrified.

"I don't know how to do this," tiny-you says.

"I know," you say. "Me neither."

Your boss is still standing behind you. You can feel him waiting. Not angry, exactly — just *waiting*, in that heavy, endless way people wait in dreams. The office is completely silent except for the sound of keyboards typing by themselves at all the empty desks around you.

You put your hands on your own keyboard and realize the keys have changed. There are no letters anymore. Just buttons that say:

**PANIC**
**PRETEND**
**COFFEE**
**YESTERDAY**
**PLEASE**

You press PLEASE.

Instantly, your desk fills with paper. Stacks and stacks of it, growing taller than you. Reports, charts, sticky notes, blueprints for a building that keeps changing shape every time you look at it. One page just says *MAKE IT PERFECT* over and over again until the words run off the edge.

The clock chimes. It's 5:01 PM forever now.

That's when you notice something strange — your feet aren't touching the floor. You're floating, just an inch or two above your office chair. The stacks of paper have turned soft, like clouds. Your boss is floating too, still with his arms crossed, slowly drifting toward the ceiling along with the filing cabinets and the water cooler.

And suddenly, in the perfect logic of a dream, you understand.

The deadline is impossible because you're not supposed to meet it here.

You push off your desk and swim through the air to the big office window. Outside, the city isn't a city anymore — it's the hallway of your childhood home, and beyond that, a beach at sunset, and beyond that, your classroom from third grade.

You open the window. A warm wind rushes in and scatters all the paper into white birds.

Your boss finally speaks, but his voice is your alarm clock.

"Time to start," he says. "Time to start. Time to start."

The office fades. The birds turn back into sunlight through your bedroom blinds.

You wake up at 7:00 AM, heart pounding, with your real laptop closed beside you — and for one strange, relieved second, you're incredibly grateful that time is moving forward again, one normal minute at a time.
