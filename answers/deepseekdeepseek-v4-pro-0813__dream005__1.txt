As you try to find them, you encounter various groups of people and none of them are quite right. A cluster of guests in evening wear turn to you in unison, their mouths moving a half-second before their words, saying something you almost understand. By the buffet table, old friends are laughing at a joke you told years ago, except they are all wearing your face. You try to ask if they’ve seen the one person you know, but your voice comes out as a low hum, and they nod sympathetically as if that answers everything.

The room folds. A doorway that should lead to the kitchen opens onto the same party, slightly smaller, with the same groups rearranged. There is a circle of children playing a game with no rules. They hand you a ribbon and tell you to keep walking. The ribbon grows longer behind you, tangling around chair legs and strangers’ ankles, but no one seems to mind.

Then you spot the person you know across the room. They are standing very still, holding two glasses, looking at you with the precise expression you remember from some ordinary afternoon. You push through the crowd, but each step makes the room stretch. The person doesn’t get farther away; they just stay exactly as far as they were, like a moon following a car.

Finally you reach them. They smile and open their mouth, but the sound that comes out is the sound of a door closing. You understand: they have been trying to find you too, all along, in a building made of afterthoughts and borrowed music. You sit together on a couch that wasn’t there before. Neither of you speaks. The party goes on around you, softly, like weather.

Then the lights flicker once, and you are awake, but their hand is still warm on yours for a second longer than the room.
