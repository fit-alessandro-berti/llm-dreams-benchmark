But when you step back, the floor becomes water.

Your friend stands on the opposite shore, still speaking, though their words arrive as small paper boats. You catch one and unfold it. Inside, instead of the accusation you expected, is a memory: the two of you laughing in a kitchen years ago, before either of you knew how much you could hurt each other.

You try to answer, but your own words turn into stones. You hold them for a moment, feeling their weight, then set them down.

“I don’t want to lose you over this,” you say.

This time, your voice stays a voice.

Your friend looks across the water. “Neither do I.”

A narrow bridge appears between you. It doesn’t reach all the way across.

You both begin walking anyway.
