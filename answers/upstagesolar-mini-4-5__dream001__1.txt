...start to walk, though my feet move without consulting my will, as if the city itself were guiding me. The streets stretch on in impossible ways—narrow alleys that widen into broad boulevards, lampposts that hum with a faint blue light, shop windows displaying mannequins who all seem to be wearing the same coat. I check my pocket for my phone, but instead find a folded paper map, warm to the touch, its lines shifting like something breathing.

I glance at it and notice the meeting is circled in red ink—but the address changes each time I look away. A child selling roasted chestnuts asks if I'm "late for the important thing," and when I nod, she nods back gravely, then tosses me a chestnut as if it were a key. I crack it open, and inside is a tiny brass key with a number stamped on it: 11:00.

At the corner, a bus stop where the timetable reads only one route: a bus that leaves every hour, exactly on the hour. The digital display flickers—*Now: 10:47*—and I realize I must decide whether to run for the next stop or trust the route's own sense of timing.

Behind me, footsteps—but when I turn, only a shadow stretches across the pavement, shaped like a door.

I begin to run.
