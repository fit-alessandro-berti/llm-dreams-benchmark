…encounter various groups of people, each one muttering a different name that sounds almost right but isn’t quite. At first I mistake a woman in a red coat for my friend because her laugh reminds me of hers, but when I turn around she’s already been absorbed into a circle arguing about a lost umbrella. A man in a suit insists I’m early, but my phone shows no signal and no clock—only a soft hum like a refrigerator in another room.

I try to move through a crowd that keeps forming bridges between tables, but every bridge is made of chairs that won’t hold. The host’s voice arrives from everywhere at once, announcing that “the person you’re looking for is near, but they’ve changed their name to avoid the cat.” I look up and see a tabby sitting calmly on the dessert table, wearing a tiny name tag that reads “Kevin.”

In the corner, a group of strangers is playing a board game with no rules except that everyone must tell one true story per turn. A boy with a birthday-cake candle for a hairclip says, “I’ve never been invited to a party before,” and for a second I almost sit down with them. But then the music changes, and I hear my own name spoken from the kitchen—not by someone I know, but by the way I’ve been saying it to myself when I’m nervous.

I push through a beaded curtain that smells like rain and finds a hallway lined with doors. Each door has a small placard: “Ask me about my family,” “I work nights,” “I’m fine, thanks,” “Please don’t.” One door has my friend’s name on it, but the handle is warm and the room beyond is bright and full of people I can’t quite see. I knock anyway, and someone inside says, “You took your time.”

And then I wake up—just as I’m about to say their name.
