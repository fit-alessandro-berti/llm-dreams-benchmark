The groups are arranged in concentric circles, though no one seems to have decided that. That's the first strange thing. The second is that everyone is speaking at once, and every voice carries with the same clarity, so the sound doesn't become noise but a kind of pressure — a low warm roar pressing against your eardrums like water.

You move through them. A cluster of people in blue jackets are discussing a door they all remember seeing, but the memory changes each time one of them mentions it, and they keep agreeing. A group near a windowless window is laughing at something you can't hear, and when you look at their faces, their laughter keeps going but their eyes stay perfectly serious. They notice you noticing, and one of them — a woman with a face like a photograph of a face — points politely at the far side of the room and mouths *you'll find them over there*.

You go over there.

Over there, a man in a rumpled suit is telling three people about your friend. He says your friend's name is *David*, and that David is late, and that David is probably at the other side of the room now, looking for you. The three people nod. One of them says, "That's how it goes at these things," and you realize with a lurch that the man has your friend's voice, exactly — the cadence, the little upward lilt at the end of a sentence — but his face is a stranger's, and when you say *David?* he tilts his head and asks, in your friend's voice, *David who?*

You keep moving. The room is larger now. It has been larger the whole time, of course, and you hadn't noticed, and the ceiling is high enough to lose in the dark. Somewhere behind you a glass breaks and the sound travels a long way before it lands. No one reacts. Everyone keeps talking, because that's what they were put here to do.

You find a staircase. You go up. On the landing there is a coat on a hook, and it is your coat, the one you left at home, and it is warm, and the pocket holds a small folded note in your handwriting: *Don't look for me in the crowd. Ask them what they're afraid of.*

So you go back down. You approach the woman with the photograph-face and you ask her, quietly, what she's afraid of, and she looks at you with enormous relief, and says, "That you'll turn out to be someone else entirely."

And then she takes your hand, and her hand is warm and real and slightly damp, and she leads you not to the far side of the room, but out a side door into a hallway that smells like rain and pavement and someone's cigarette, and there, leaning against the wall with his hands in his pockets, is your friend.

He looks up.

"You took forever," he says.

And you understand, with the strange clarity dreams sometimes hand you, that he has been looking for you the entire time too — that both of you spent the whole night searching separate crowds, each of you certain the other was just on the other side of the room, and neither of you thought to walk toward the door.

"Nobody told us where to go," you say.

"They never do," he says, and pushes off the wall. "Come on. There's someone I want you to meet."

He walks into the hallway, and you follow, and behind you the roar of the gathering swells again, and you don't turn around, but you can feel it — the whole warm weight of it — closing over the doorway like water.
