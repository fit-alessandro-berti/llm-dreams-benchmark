...and each group you approach seems to know you, though you have no idea who they are. A woman in a red scarf clasps your hand and says, "You made it! We saved you a seat," but when you glance over, there's only a chair floating slightly above the floor. You politely excuse yourself.

The room seems to shift. The ceiling is suddenly much higher, and the party has spread across multiple floors that connect in ways that make no sense — a staircase that loops back to the same level, a balcony that overlooks a garden that's clearly *outside* but somehow *inside*. You call your friend's name but the sound comes out too quiet, like the air is swallowing it.

You find yourself at a table of people playing a card game, but the cards have faces on them — not portraits, but *moving* faces that whisper the names of people you used to know. One card turns toward you and says, "I'm sorry about what happened at the pier," and you feel a wave of sadness so intense it almost knocks you off your feet. But you don't remember any pier.

Then you spot them — your friend — across a wide room, laughing with a group of strangers. Relief floods through you. You wave. They look right at you... and don't recognize you. They tilt their head like a confused dog. A cold wind blows through the room even though all the windows are closed.

You step closer and your friend's face begins to blur at the edges, like a photograph left in the rain. The crowd thickens between you. Everyone is wearing the same expression — pleasant but unknowing. The music swells. The lights dim to a deep amber.

And then you hear your friend's voice — close, right behind your ear — whispering: *"You were never the one I was looking for."*

You wake with your heart pounding and a stranger's phone number written on your hand.
