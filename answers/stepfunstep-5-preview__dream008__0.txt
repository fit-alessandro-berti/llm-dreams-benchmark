…sitting right beside your desk, arms crossed, tapping one foot with a metronome’s precision. The project is already there, waiting in a binder that appears to be breathing. You open it. The pages are blank, but the words hover just above the paper, written in ink that smells like your own handwriting.

*“We need the proposal finalized by tomorrow morning,”* your boss says, glancing at a wall clock whose hands spin backward. *“You’ve had six months.”*

“But I just got this,” you say. Your voice sounds like it’s coming from the break room.

You sit down. The chair wraps around you, not unpleasantly, like a hug from someone who is trying to keep you still. You reach for your keyboard, but the keys have rearranged themselves into soft, warm dough. You type anyway. Words bloom on the screen—dozens of them, thousands, all in languages you almost understand. The deadline on the monitor doesn’t move. It simply stares at you: **DUE YESTERDAY.**

Your boss sighs. “Great start. Now do it again, but underwater.”

And then, suddenly, the office floods. Not with panic, but with water—warm and clear, like a swimming pool. Your coworkers are swimming past, delivering staplers to whales, completely unfazed. You try to stand, but gravity has changed its mind; your feet stick to the ceiling. Your boss floats upside down, perfectly composed, holding out a red pen.

*“Almost there,”* they say. *“Seven minutes.”*

You look at the clock. It has no numbers, just the word **NOW**, ticking louder with every second. You grab the binder. The pages are flooding with ideas, each one a small, glowing fish. You try to catch them, but they slip through your fingers. The deadline approaches, breathing down your neck, and you realize with the strange, calm acceptance of a dream that you have been working on this project your entire life, and you have never once known what it was.

Just as the clock begins to strike, you speak the only line that makes sense.

*“I need to check with my team,”* you say.

The office exhales. Your boss nods, proud.

“Excellent,” they say. “They’re all waiting for you in the lobby.”

You turn. There is no lobby. There is only a single door, slightly ajar, and beyond it, the exact, hollow feeling of your own bedroom at 3:17 A.M. You step through—

—and your alarm goes off.
