Before you can speak, the kitchen floor begins to tilt. A lemon rolls off the counter. Then a spoon. Your friend catches the blue-striped cup just before it hits the floor, and for some reason this is what makes you start to cry. You bought that cup together. It cost a quarter. Neither of you even likes it.

“I don’t want you to sell the place,” you say.

“I know.” Your friend sets the cup in the sink, where it can’t fall. “But I can’t keep paying for a house just so you have somewhere to come back to.”

All this time you’ve been arguing about the roof, the taxes, the crack above the stove. You look at the crack now. There’s daylight in it, and a little green leaf.

“I thought if you left,” you say, “you’d stop being the person I could come back to.”

Your friend sits down on the sloping floor. You sit too, and slide the last few inches until your shoulders touch.

“I’m moving six blocks,” your friend says.

In the dream, six blocks is a terrible distance. It contains a railway crossing, an ocean, the whole winter you didn’t call. But your friend takes a grocery receipt from a pocket and draws you a map. At the end of the sixth block, in the window of a crooked little house, there is just enough room to draw a blue-striped cup.
