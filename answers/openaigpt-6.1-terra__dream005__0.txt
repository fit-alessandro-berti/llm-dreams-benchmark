You are at a large social gathering where you only know one person. As you try to find them, you encounter various groups of people, each speaking a language you almost understand.

In the kitchen, someone hands you a plate with a tiny staircase on it. They nod toward the ceiling, so you climb. Each step is warm beneath your feet.

Upstairs, the gathering continues, though the rooms are now train carriages rocking gently through the dark. Through the windows, you see places you’ve lived, passing in the wrong order. You ask a woman in a paper crown if she has seen your friend.

“Of course,” she says. “They’ve been looking for you too.”

She opens a door, and you step into a garden at dawn. Your friend is there, sitting at a table set for two. You’re relieved to see them, though you can no longer remember their name.

“Sorry I’m late,” you say.

They smile. “You’re early. Look.”

The sun rises, and every window in the house behind you lights up at once.
