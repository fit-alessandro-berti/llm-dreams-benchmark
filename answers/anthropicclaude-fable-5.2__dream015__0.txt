...plant a garden.

It makes no sense, and you know it. The soil is gray and the sky is the color of old dishwater. But you found a handful of seeds in the pocket of a coat that isn't yours, and in the logic of dreams, that feels like an instruction.

You dig with a bent spoon in the shell of what was once a library. The roof is gone, and rain falls on the swollen books, which have begun to sprout pale mushrooms along their spines. You plant the seeds between the shelves, in a row where the poetry used to be.

They grow overnight, or maybe time just skips, the way it does here. They are not vegetables. They are small lanterns on green stalks, each glowing a soft amber, and you understand that this is what the world is short on. Food and water matter less than light people can gather around.

Others come. A woman leads a three-legged dog, and a boy carries a radio that only plays the ocean. An old man claims to remember your name, though he pronounces it differently each time. No one asks what the lanterns are. They sit down among them and begin to talk in low voices about what they used to love.

You realize the danger hasn't gone anywhere. Shapes still move beyond the broken walls. But they stop at the edge of the glow, uncertain, as if they've forgotten what they came for.

The boy turns up his radio and the waves roll in. One of the lanterns opens like a flower. Inside it, small and impossibly bright, is the front door of your childhood home.

You reach for the handle...

...and that's where the dream thins out, as dreams do when you get too close to something.

Would you like to step through the door, or shall I take the dream in a different direction?
