The box is warm, the way a sleeping animal is warm. You press your ear to the lid and hear something inside, not rattling but *humming*: a song your grandmother used to sing, though you're sure you've never heard it before. The melody is missing one note. Every time it reaches that place, the song stops, waits, and starts over.

You look around for a key and find that the room has changed. The walls are made of bookshelves now, and every book has the same title: *The Thing You Forgot.* You pull one down, but its pages are blank except for a single pressed flower, a violet, flattened and translucent as tissue paper. It smells like rain on a porch you once sat on as a child, waiting for someone who was late.

Without deciding to, you hum the missing note.

The box answers. The carvings glow faintly gold, the vines uncurl, and the lid lifts just a little, the way a person opens their eyes when they're not sure they want to wake up.

Inside, there is no treasure. There is a small, perfect replica of the room you're standing in, with the bookshelves, the violet, and a tiny version of you leaning over a tiny box. You watch the miniature you lift its lid, and you feel, impossibly, a vast shadow fall across your own shoulders, as if someone enormous and kind is peering down at you too.

You aren't afraid. The feeling is familiar, like being tucked in.

A voice, maybe yours, maybe the box's, maybe the song's, says softly: *"You were never locked out. You were only waiting to remember the tune."*

The violet in your hand blooms back to life, purple and dewy. The walls of books begin to flutter like wings. Somewhere far away, an alarm clock starts to ring, but in the dream it sounds like church bells, like a door opening, like someone who was late finally coming up the porch steps.

You close the box gently, not to lock it but to keep it safe. Then you wake with the melody still in your mouth, whole at last, and for a few seconds you remember exactly what you forgot.

Then, like all dreams, it slips away, leaving only the faint scent of rain and violets.
