You start to pat your pockets for your phone, but your hands find only unfamiliar things - a smooth river stone, a folded paper ticket with no writing on it, and a hotel keycard for a hotel you've never stayed in.

When you finally pull out your phone, the screen is lit but the map is impossible. The blue dot that should be you is pulsing in the middle of a tangled knot of streets that keep renaming themselves as you watch. *Rue de L'Heure. Av. Forgotten. Meeting Place Blvd.* The battery reads 1%.

You start to walk anyway, choosing a direction that *feels* right.

The city is vast and strangely quiet. The buildings are too tall - their tops disappear into a low, amber haze, and their windows are all dark despite it being midday. The few people you pass don't seem to notice you. A woman walking a dog that is somehow also a bicycle. A man in a business suit waiting at a bus stop that has no schedule, only a clock with no hands.

You try to ask someone, "Excuse me, do you know where the conference center is?" 

Your voice comes out muffled, as if you're speaking underwater. The man at the bus stop smiles politely and points down three different streets at once. When you turn to look where he's pointing, the streets have shifted. A grand boulevard that wasn't there a second ago has appeared, lined with fluttering flags that have your name on them.

You start to run.

Your feet are suddenly very heavy, like you're running in soft sand, even though the pavement looks solid. The hour you had is melting. You glance at a clock tower in the distance - it says 11:47. You look away and look back - it says 11:12. Time is pooling and stretching.

Then you notice - everyone else in the city is also running, but slowly, calmly, all in the same direction you are now heading. You fall in with the flow of the crowd, no longer needing to know the address because the city itself seems to be carrying you.

The crowd turns a final corner and opens into a massive, sunlit plaza. In the center is a single, small table with two chairs. One chair is empty. In the other sits a version of you - more relaxed, wearing clothes you don't remember owning - sipping coffee and waving you over.

They smile and say, "I saved you a seat. You're right on time."

And just as you go to sit down, you feel that familiar, gentle tug - the feeling of waking up.
