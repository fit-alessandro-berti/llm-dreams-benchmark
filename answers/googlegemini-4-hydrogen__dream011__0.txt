You blink hard, trying to force the words into focus. For a split second, Question 1 snaps into English: *Calculate the exact weight of a forgotten promise, showing all your work.* Before you can even read Question 2, the ink melts into a complicated blueprint of your childhood house, except every hallway leads to an elevator.

Panic begins to pool in your stomach. You look up to raise your hand for a new test packet, only to realize the lecture hall has silently stretched to the size of an airplane hangar. At the front of the room, half a mile away, the proctor is somehow your third-grade gym teacher, wearing a tuxedo and silently blowing into a whistle. 

Around you, hundreds of other students are writing furiously. The scratching of their pencils sounds like static, growing louder and louder. You glance over at the person sitting next to you, hoping for a clue, only to see they aren't answering questions at all—they are carefully shading in the margins of their paper with a stick of butter.

You look back down at your own desk. You reach for your pen, but your fingers close around a warm, half-melted crayon. The moment you touch it to the paper, the test sheet goes limp and damp, folding itself smoothly into a small paper boat. 

Suddenly, you feel cold water lapping at your ankles. The tiled floor has vanished, replaced by a calm, shallow ocean stretching all the way to the distant chalkboards. Your desk begins to gently bob and drift away from the rows of frantically scribbling students.

A massive grandfather clock on the far wall chimes thirteen times, and its hands unhook themselves, fluttering out a high window like moths. Strangely, the moment they disappear, all your anxiety evaporates. You realize you never actually registered for this class anyway. You set the melted crayon down inside the paper boat, watch it float away across the flooded classroom, and lean back in your drifting chair to watch the ceiling slowly open up into a bright, purple sky.
