You find yourself in a post-apocalyptic world, with resources scarce and dangers everywhere. Despite the hardships, you decide to plant a single sunflower seed in the cracked concrete of a collapsed subway station.

You water it with the last of your canteen, and by morning, it has grown into a towering stalk that pierces the steel beams above, its petals glowing like tiny, defiant suns. You climb it, leaving the dark tunnels and the taste of dust behind, and emerge into a sky that is no longer gray but swirling with slow, ribboning auroras. 

Below, the ruins have begun to change: cracks in the asphalt fill with emerald moss, rusted car husks sprout wildflowers, and the skeletal frames of skyscrapers are laced with flowering vines. A voice—your own, but softer, younger—calls up from the ground: *"It was never the end. It was just the world taking a breath."*

You reach down, and the earth softens into a warm, mossy bed. As you lie back, the auroras dip lower, and the dangerous noises of the wasteland fade into a gentle hum. You realize you aren't just surviving the dream—you're slowly waking up inside it, and the world is remembering how to be a garden again.
