Yes—here’s a possible completion of the dream:

You are at a large social gathering where you only know one person. As you try to find them, you encounter various groups of people and each group seems to know something about you that you never said aloud. A circle by the fountain asks, “Have you seen the door with your name on it?” Three people beneath the chandelier lean in and repeat the exact same question, only softly, as if they’ve been rehearsing it for years. Somewhere near the edge of the room, someone calls your name—but when you turn, the name arrives late, and the person who said it has already forgotten you were there.

The lights are warm, too warm, the way sunlight feels on closed eyelids. The music keeps looping a melody you almost recognize, and every time you try to move toward it, the floor changes shape beneath your feet: first carpet, then tile, then grass, then polished wood. The crowd parts just enough for you to glimpse familiar clothing—a jacket you once liked, a scarf you remember wearing in another life—but the face attached to it is always obscured, turned toward someone else, laughing.

At last, you find the person you came for. They are standing in a hallway that should not exist, holding two small glasses filled with what looks like liquid starlight. They do not seem surprised to see you.

“You always take the scenic route through a crowd,” they say.

Then they hand you one of the glasses. It tastes like déjà vu. Behind you, the party folds quietly into a single room, then a corridor, then a hallway, then the small brass key you just found in your pocket.

You are dreaming, but you decide to stay a little longer—because somewhere beyond the noise there is a door with your name on it, and for the first time, you can spell it.
