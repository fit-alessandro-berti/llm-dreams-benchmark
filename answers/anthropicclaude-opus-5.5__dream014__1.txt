...follow them without quite deciding to, the way you do in dreams.

There are four of them. One wears a coat stitched entirely from old maps, and when she turns, you catch glimpses of streets that don't exist anymore. Another is an elderly man carrying a birdcage with no bird inside, only a small, steady light. The other two are children who seem to know every alley by heart, even though the alleys keep rearranging themselves when you aren't looking.

"Where are you trying to go?" the woman in the map-coat asks.

You open your mouth to answer and realize you don't know. You only know that you've been walking for a very long time, and that somewhere in this city is a place that feels like the end of a long exhale.

"That's all right," says the old man. "Most people don't know. That's usually how we find them."

The city roars around you: taxis honking in chords, pigeons rising in the shape of words you almost understand, crowds flowing like water around stones. But inside the little circle of strangers, it's quiet. The noise can't quite reach you.

The children lead you down a staircase that shouldn't fit between two buildings, into a street where it's evening instead of noon. Paper lanterns hang overhead, and each one holds a memory: a kitchen that smelled like cinnamon, a friend's laugh on a summer night, the weight of a blanket on a cold morning. You realize, with a jolt, that they're *your* memories.

"You weren't lost," the woman says gently, touching one of the lanterns. "You just forgot you were carrying all of this."

The old man opens the birdcage. The light inside drifts out, hovers in front of you, and settles softly against your chest, warm as a hand.

When you look up, the strangers are smiling, and their faces seem strangely familiar: the curve of a grandmother's eyes, the grin of a childhood friend, the patience of a teacher you'd forgotten you loved. Before you can ask, the street begins to glow brighter and brighter, the lanterns blurring into a single golden haze.

"Where are you going?" you ask.

"Nowhere," the youngest child says. "We're always here. You just have to stop long enough to notice."

And then you're standing alone again at the busy intersection where it all began, but it doesn't feel the same. The crowd moves past, and for the first time, you see kindness flickering in the faces around you: a man holding a door, a woman laughing into her phone, someone's dog wagging at nothing in particular.

The light in your chest is still warm.

You don't know exactly where you're going.

But you're not lost anymore.

...and somewhere far away, an alarm begins to ring, softly, like a bell calling you home.
