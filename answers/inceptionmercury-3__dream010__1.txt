…and each challenge seems to be pulled straight from the deepest corners of your imagination — the ones you’ve never told anyone about.

The competition is held in a vast, impossible venue: a spiral of floating rooms that rotate gently in a twilight sky. Each room is a test, but not of trivia or speed. It’s a test of *you*.

The first room is silent, except for a single ticking clock. On the wall, a sentence appears: *“Write the story you were always afraid to tell.”* You sit at an old oak desk, and a pen appears in your hand. The words come easily — easier than they ever have in waking life — and as you write, the room fills with warm light. The page glows, and you realize the story is being read by an audience you can’t see. You feel their laughter, their tears, their recognition. When you finish, the door opens.

The second room is a maze made of mirrors. Every reflection shows a different version of you: the one who took the safe job, the one who moved across the world, the one who gave up, the one who never tried. A voice whispers: *“Find the version that is most truly you.”* You wander, touching cold glass, until you stop in front of a mirror that shows you — not younger, not older, but exactly as you are, tired and hopeful and unpolished. You press your hand to the glass, and it shatters into golden dust. Beyond it is a staircase.

The third challenge is the hardest. You’re placed in a room that contains a single chair. Across from you sits a figure you don’t recognize — but slowly, you realize it’s the person you could have become if you’d never chased this dream. They’re calm, content, safe. They smile and say, *“You can still choose this. No one would blame you.”* Your throat tightens. You almost reach for their hand. But then you remember why you started. You shake your head. The figure nods, fades, and leaves behind a small key.

At the top of the staircase, there’s a door made of starlight. You unlock it with the key and step into the final room. It’s not a stage. It’s a quiet office — your office. There’s a desk with your name on it, a window overlooking a city you love, and a chair that feels like it was made for you. On the desk sits a single card:

*“You’ve already won. The job was never the prize — it was the courage to keep going. Welcome home.”*

You sit down in the chair. And for the first time in the dream, you feel completely awake.

Then you hear a distant alarm — not your morning alarm, but a soft chime. A voice says: *“The competition will resume in 8 hours. Would you like to save your progress?”*

You smile, close your eyes, and press **YES**.
