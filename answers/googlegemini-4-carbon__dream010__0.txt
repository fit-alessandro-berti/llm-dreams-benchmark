The arena is a vast, impossibly tall atrium built from polished obsidian and floating staircases, suspended somewhere in the clouds. Above you, the judges sit on a balcony too high to see clearly; they appear only as towering silhouettes woven from constellations and static, their voices humming like distant cello strings whenever they announce the next round.

In the first challenge, the floor vanishes into a shallow ocean of warm, glowing ink. You are given a silver net and told to catch "the heaviest unspoken words." Around you, hundreds of other contestants—some wearing sharp business suits made of folded paper, others looking like sleek, ticking clockwork mannequins—frantically swipe at the air. You stop running. Closing your eyes, you dip your net beneath the surface of the ink instead, pulling up glowing, pulsing pearls of forgotten apologies and half-remembered lullabies. When you open your eyes, half the contestants have dissolved into flocks of origami birds, flying out through the ceiling. You advance.

Suddenly, the scenery shifts—as it always does in dreams—and you are standing in an endless corridor of doors. Your dream job waits behind just one of them, though you still can’t read the shifting gold lettering on the plaque in your mind; you only *feel* what the job is. It feels like weightlessness, like finally knowing the melody to a song you’ve hummed your whole life. 

To find the right door, you have to outpace your final remaining rival: a mirror-image of yourself, dressed in blinding white light, moving with effortless, intimidating perfection. Your double starts unlocking doors rapidly, swinging them open to reveal roaring applause, towering trophies, and dazzling cityscapes. Panic tightens your chest. Your feet feel heavy, like they’re sinking into wet sand. 

You look down at your hands and realize you don't even have a key. 

Instead of chasing your rival, you turn toward a small, unpainted wooden door hidden behind a velvet curtain. It has no keyhole. Remembering the strange logic of this place, you press your palm flat against the wood and simply knock, offering the quietest, truest piece of who you are. 

The door swings inward. There is no applause, only the sudden rush of a cool, pine-scented breeze and a desk floating at the edge of an infinite aurora. On the desk sits a single brass nameplate. You step forward, squinting as the shifting letters finally settle into place to reveal your new title. 

Just as your fingers brush the warm brass, the constellations above begin to chime—louder and louder, morphing steadily into the familiar, rhythmic ringing of your morning alarm, pulling you gently back toward the surface of the waking world, with the faint warmth of the brass still lingering on your fingertips.
