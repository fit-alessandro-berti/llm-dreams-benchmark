One stranger carries a paper lantern, another a map that keeps folding itself into birds. The smallest of them takes your hand as if you’re an old friend.

“We know the way,” she says.

They lead you away from the traffic and through a narrow passage between two buildings. With every step, the city grows quieter. Car horns become birdsong. Neon signs soften into stars. At the end of the passage stands a little café, its windows glowing amber.

Inside, there’s a chair waiting for you and a cup of something warm. The strangers sit around the table, passing you bread, asking no questions you don’t want to answer.

You reach into your pocket for the address you’ve been trying to find. The paper is blank.

For a moment, the old panic returns. Then the woman beside you turns it over and draws a small square, a door, two windows.

“Was it somewhere like this?”

You look around the café. Rain has begun to tap against the glass. Someone puts another log on the fire.

“Yes,” you say, surprised. “I think it was.”

You stay until morning.
