Yes — …and then you encounter a circle of people wearing identical teal ribbons, a table of guests who keep politely turning their heads away from you, and a string of singers whose voices sound like the same note slightly in different languages. Each group blocks a little of the path, not by force, but by making the gathering feel larger and more impossible. The doors shift. The walls breathe. Sometimes the room looks like a crowded café, sometimes like a garden under a glass ceiling.

You keep circling the edge of the crowd, promising yourself, “If I can just find them, the noise will thin.” Then — faintly, in the exact corner where the lights seem to leak into the ceiling — you see the one person you know. They’re not dancing, not talking, not looking back. They’re standing in front of a small mirror-like doorway, holding a cup of hot cocoa with both hands. As you get closer, the huge gathering loosens its grip; the groups of people you passed begin to blur into shadows, and the noise softens into something like rain.

The person smiles. “I was waiting for you to stop looking?” they say, and then the whole party gives one soft shimmer, like a dream turning over in its sleep.

When you wake, you feel grounded — not at a party, but at the threshold of one, holding the cup and remembering something small and strange: that the gathering wasn’t about crowds or distance at all, but about learning how to find someone who has been standing in the same place the whole time.
