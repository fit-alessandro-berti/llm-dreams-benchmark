reach for your keyboard, but the keys scatter like beetles.

Your boss checks a watch with thirteen hands. “We needed this yesterday,” they say.

You glance at the project brief. It is one sentence long:

**Build a door where there has never been a wall.**

Everyone else keeps typing.

You open your desk drawer, hoping for a pencil, and find a small ocean. A paper boat bumps against the stapler. Written on its sail, in your own handwriting, are the words: *Take your lunch break.*

“I don’t have time,” you whisper.

The ocean rises an inch.

Your boss pulls up a chair beside you. For the first time, you notice they are barefoot, their trouser cuffs soaked.

“Neither did I,” they say.

Somewhere beneath the carpet, a school bell rings. You remember being eight years old and knowing how to do this. You take the ruler from your desk and draw a rectangle in the air.

A brass handle appears.

Behind you, the office falls silent.

“Where does it go?” your boss asks.

You turn the handle. Through the opening you see a sunlit kitchen. Someone has left a peach on the table, split neatly in half. The curtains lift in a breeze you can feel against your face.

Your boss leans forward.

“Could you leave it open?” they ask.
