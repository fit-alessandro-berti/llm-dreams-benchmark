...shifting constellations etched into the grain, pulsing with a faint, internal bioluminescence. When you tilt your head, the lines resolve into a language you don’t speak yet somehow understand: maps of cities you’ve never visited, the veins of leaves you’ve never touched, the personal handwriting of your childhood self.

There is no keyhole, only seams that seem to breathe. You press your thumb against the largest symbol, and the wood is warm—thrumming, you realize, in time with your own heartbeat. The box isn’t locked; it’s *waiting*. You lean close, close enough to smell ozone and old libraries, and whisper the only word that seems to fit the silence between your thoughts.

The patterns bloom outward like unfolding wings. The lid becomes a horizon, the sides become walls that retreat into gravity. Inside, there is no darkness, but a soft, luminous space filled with the smell of rain on hot pavement. Resting on velvet absent any floor: a photograph of a place that doesn’t exist, a single silver key that fits every lock except this one, and at the center, a small mirror that shows not your face, but the person you are about to become, smiling back with the pity of soft recognition.

You reach in, your fingers catching beams of light like threads. As you pull your hand back, the patterns remain faintly traced on your skin—not ink, but a map. And though you do not yet know where it leads, your feet begin, gently and without permission, to walk toward it.
