You decide to stop talking mid-sentence and actually look at your friend’s face.

In the dream, their expression flickers—one moment they’re your best friend, the next they’re a stranger with the same eyes. The room around you softens, walls breathing like lungs. You realize you’re not fighting about the argument anymore; you’re fighting about a moment neither of you ever said out loud. Your friend places something small and cold in your palm—a key, or a stone, or maybe just their hand.

“You were always going to forgive me,” they say, but their voice comes from somewhere behind your own head. When you open your mouth to answer, no sound comes out—only the sound of rain starting inside the room. The floor turns into a river, and the argument drifts away on the current. You reach out, but they’re already fading into the dream’s edge, still holding your hand, still letting you go.
