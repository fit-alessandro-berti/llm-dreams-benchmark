“Turn her toward the light!” you shout.

Only after the words leave your mouth do you notice there is no light. The horizon has vanished on every side, swallowed by walls of black water. Still, the helmsman nods as if you have named a familiar harbor. He wrestles the wheel around, and the rest of the crew hurry to the lines. Their trust frightens you more than the storm. You do not know their names. Worse, you do not know the ship’s name, though you feel it moving beneath your boots like an animal that knows yours.

A wave rises higher than the mast. For a moment it hangs over the ship, perfectly still, and within its green depths you see a city. Lamps burn behind submerged windows. People sit at dinner tables, calmly passing bowls from hand to hand. A little girl looks up through the water and points directly at you.

Then the wave falls.

The deck disappears beneath white foam. You grip the rail, expecting to be torn away, but the sea pours through the crew without touching them. Their bodies flicker like reflections. One sailor loses his hat, and beneath it there is only a smooth hollow full of stars.

“Captain,” he says, as though nothing is wrong. “It’s coming aboard.”

At first you think he means the water. Then three knocks sound from below deck.

Knock. Knock. Knock.

Everyone freezes. Even the storm seems to draw back and listen.

You descend the companionway with a lantern in one hand and a brass key in the other. You do not remember taking either. The passage below is far longer than the ship could contain. Doors line both sides, each marked with a year instead of a cabin number. From behind one comes the murmur of your childhood kitchen. Behind another, someone is singing a song you almost recognize. Water runs along the ceiling while the floor remains dry.

The knocking comes from the last door, the one marked TOMORROW.

The key fits.

Inside is a small, moonlit room. At its center stands a wooden cradle, rocking with the motion of the sea. You approach it reluctantly. Curled beneath a gray blanket is a tiny version of the ship itself, every sail and rope exact, its miniature deck crowded with a miniature crew. A minute figure at the bow raises a lantern. It is you.

When you lean closer, the tiny captain leans toward an even smaller cradle at their feet.

“Don’t look any further,” says a voice behind you.

The little girl from the underwater city is standing in the doorway. Water streams from her hair, but her clothes are dry. She holds out her hand. Resting on her palm is a compass with no needle.

“You’ve been sailing in circles,” she says.

“Where is the land?” you ask.

“There isn’t any yet.”

Another blow strikes the hull. The lantern goes out. In the darkness the ship groans, not like timber, but like someone trying to speak in their sleep. The girl closes your fingers around the compass.

“You have to choose what it points to.”

You return to the deck. The storm has changed. Rain is rising from the sea into the clouds. Lightning crawls silently across the waves, illuminating hundreds of other ships surrounding yours. On each deck stands another you: younger, older, wounded, crowned, alone. Every captain holds the same blank compass. Every crew waits for an order.

You open your hand.

The compass face is warm. You think of home, but no single place appears. Instead you remember the feeling of a door opening before you knock, of dry clothes after rain, of someone keeping a lamp lit because they believe you will return. A needle grows from the center of the compass, thin and bright as a sliver of dawn. It points straight into the largest wave.

“Forward,” you say.

The crew stare at the wall of water. For the first time, doubt crosses their faces, and this makes you love them.

“Forward!” you shout again, and they answer as one.

The sails fill though the wind is against you. The ship gathers speed. Around you, the other vessels turn away one by one, vanishing into rain, but yours drives on. The wave rises until it becomes the whole sky. At its crest, the little girl stands barefoot upon the water, holding the door to your childhood kitchen open.

The bow strikes.

There is no crash. The sea divides like a curtain, and the ship sails into a vast blue silence. Fish drift overhead among clouds. The stars lie below you, trembling in the deep. Your crew laugh, their faces suddenly familiar, and the smooth hollow in the sailor’s head fills with morning light.

Ahead, an island is assembling itself from the mist: first a line of sand, then trees, then a white house with smoke rising from its chimney. On the shore someone has left a lamp burning.

You order the anchor lowered. The chain runs out, down and down, but never finds the bottom. When you look at the compass again, its needle is gone. In its place is a tiny door.

You touch it with one finger, and it opens.

Warm air brushes your face. Somewhere beyond it, someone says your real name.

Behind you, the crew begin to fade with the stars. The ship exhales beneath your feet. You step through just as the first sunlight reaches the deck, and wake with your hand still closed around something that is no longer there.
