Not the truth sharpened into a weapon, but the softer one you’ve been protecting all afternoon.

“I’m not afraid of being wrong,” you say. Your voice cracks, and strangely, so does the mug in your hand. A dark line of coffee runs across the table, dividing you both. “I’m afraid that once we’ve said everything, there won’t be anything left to hold us together.”

Your friend stares at you. For a moment, their anger seems to flicker out, leaving something more fragile underneath.

“You’re the only person I trust with this,” you continue. “And I’ve been treating you like someone I have to defeat before I can tell you what I actually want.”

The room shifts. The walls fade into the dim hallway outside your childhood home, every door open onto a different night. Your friend is still there, though—not a memory, not a reflection, but warm and solid and very much present.

“Maybe we don’t know how to do this yet,” they say.

You look at them, exhausted, uncertain, and still hurt by the disagreement. The argument hasn’t ended. It may not end tonight.

But you reach across the table anyway, and they take your hand.

And for the first time, neither of you is trying to win.
