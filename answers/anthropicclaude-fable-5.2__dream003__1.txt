You're almost certain one of them is your childhood street, seen from above. Another is a word you once knew but forgot upon waking, years ago.

The box is warm, like something that has been held for a long time by someone else. You turn it over, looking for a keyhole, but there isn't one, only a small oval indentation shaped exactly like your thumbprint.

You press your thumb to it. Nothing happens.

Then you notice the room has changed. You're standing in your grandmother's kitchen, except the ceiling is a night sky and the stars are quietly humming. The tune is familiar. It's the one the patterns have been tracing this whole time, a melody written in lines and spirals instead of notes.

So you hum along.

The box doesn't open so much as unfold, each panel peeling back like a petal, each one revealing another, smaller panel beneath. Inside, where you expected treasure or a secret or an answer, there is a tiny window. Through it, you see a figure sitting in a dim room, turning a small box over in their hands, examining its intricate patterns.

They look up, and they have your face.

"Oh, good," they say, relieved. "I was hoping you'd be the one to find it."

Before you can ask what they mean, the humming stars grow brighter, the kitchen floor softens like sand, and you understand, with the certainty that exists only in dreams, that the box was never locked. It was only waiting to be recognized.

The last thing you feel is its warmth in your hands, slowly fading, like a held breath finally let go.
