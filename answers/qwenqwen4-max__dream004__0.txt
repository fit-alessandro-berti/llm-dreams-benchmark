Everything else slides. The hills go from ochre to a green that tastes like pennies. The trees keep deciding how many sides they have. But the canvas holds its white the way a held note holds, and standing in front of it feels like being listened to.

There's a brush in your hand. You don't remember picking it up, and you're a little embarrassed, as though you've arrived at a party carrying something you were meant to leave in the car. The bristles are warm. When you look closely they aren't bristles at all but the fine legs of something sleeping.

You decide to paint the landscape. You turn to look at it and it has already arranged itself into the composition you wanted, obligingly, like a dog that has learned what the leash means. When you turn back, the canvas has a painting on it. It's the landscape. It's older than the landscape. There's weather in it that hasn't happened yet, and a small figure at the bottom edge with its back to you, holding a brush.

You try to paint over the figure. The white accepts the color and stays white. Everything you lay down runs off the surface and pools around your feet, and the puddle is the exact orange of the sky from when you first arrived, and you understand — with the flat, unarguable certainty dreams hand out for free — that the sky is only that color because it's the runoff from this.

So you stop painting and put your palm flat against it.

It's a window. Of course it's a window. On the other side is a room with a low ceiling and a lamp and someone asleep in a bed, and the sleeping face is doing the thing faces do just before they become someone else's. You want to warn them. You lean in and fog the glass with your breath and draw a door in the fog with one finger, and the door opens inward, and it has been open for years, and the draft coming through it is the thing you've been feeling this whole time and calling wind.

You sign the canvas in the bottom corner, because it seems polite. Then you try to read your own name.

The letters won't hold still. They shuffle. They become the shapes of letters, then the idea of letters, then a row of small warm sleeping things with fine legs.

Behind you, the hills stop changing color. Everything stops at once, the way a room stops when the person who was talking leaves it.

The white inhales.

And now you are the only thing in the world that isn't moving, and someone has come up behind you carrying a brush, a little embarrassed, and you hold your white the way a held note holds, and you wait to be listened to.
