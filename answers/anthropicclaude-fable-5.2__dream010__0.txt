...and each one seems to rearrange itself the moment you understand it.

The first challenge takes place in a vast auditorium where the seats are filled with everyone you've ever tried to impress. You're handed a sealed envelope and told to present its contents. When you open it, the page is blank, but as you start speaking, words appear on the paper a half-second after you say them, as though the script is following you.

The second challenge is a hallway of doors, each labeled with a job you once thought you wanted: marine biologist, architect, the person who names paint colors. Behind each door, another version of you looks up from their desk and gives you a piece of a key. One of them whispers, "It's not what you think it is," then goes back to work.

The other competitors keep changing. Sometimes they're strangers in sharp suits, sometimes your childhood classmates, and once, briefly, your grandmother, who winks and lets you pass her on the staircase. The staircase spirals upward through clouds, and you notice you're not tired. You're carrying something warm in your hands, though you don't remember picking it up.

In the final round, there's one judge sitting at a long table. You can't quite see their face. They slide a contract toward you and ask one question:

"What would you do here, if no one were keeping score?"

You open your mouth to answer, and you know exactly what to say. The warmth in your hands turns out to be the assembled key, glowing softly. Behind the judge, a door you hadn't noticed swings open, spilling morning light across the floor.

You step toward it, and just as you cross the threshold, you hear applause, or maybe rain, or maybe your alarm, and the dream folds itself up, leaving only the feeling that you already know the answer.
