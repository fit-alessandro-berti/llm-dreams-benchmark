One holds an umbrella over you. It isn’t raining, but beneath it the city goes quiet enough that you can hear your own footsteps. Another stranger buys a paper bag of warm rolls and gives you the first one. You eat it as you walk, dropping crumbs for a pigeon that seems to know all the shortcuts.

At last you reach a little square. There’s a lamp burning in an upstairs window, and you recognize the crooked shade. You’ve had it since you were nine.

“Is this where you live?” someone asks.

“I don’t think so,” you say, just as a key turns in your pocket.

You invite them up. There won’t be enough chairs, but when you open the door, someone inside is already dragging the piano bench to the table.
