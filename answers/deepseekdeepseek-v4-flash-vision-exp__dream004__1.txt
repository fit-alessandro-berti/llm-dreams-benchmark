…a paintbrush that hums with a soft, electric hum. The moment your fingers close around its handle, the blank canvas begins to breathe—slowly expanding and contracting like a sleeping lung. You lift the brush, and instead of paint, it dips into the air, stirring ripples of color like heat off pavement. You step closer and press the tip to the canvas.

A single line emerges, not of paint but of memory—pale blue, the color of early morning rain. It wriggles, curls, and blossoms into a tiny door. From the door steps a fox made of stained glass, its tail scattering shards of light across the ground. Each shard grows into a small tree, and each tree bends toward you, whispering your name in a language you somehow understand.

You realize now that you are not the one painting. The canvas is painting you—your reflection is being pulled into its surface, becoming a new shape, a new color, a new landscape. You feel yourself stretching into the horizon, your fingertips becoming roots, your laugh becoming wind chimes. The world around you is no longer changing. It is *becoming.*

And in the center of the painting, a tiny dreamer is still standing, holding a brush that hums.
