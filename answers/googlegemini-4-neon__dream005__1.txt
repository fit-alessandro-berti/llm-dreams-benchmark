...each cluster seems to exist in its own tiny pocket of reality.

Near a buffet table that stretches out into a dim horizon, a group in crisp, monochrome evening wear is laughing in perfect unison. As you step closer to ask if they’ve seen your friend, you realize their laughter isn’t made of voices at all—just the delicate, rhythmic clinking of crystal glasses. One of them turns, smiling with far too much warmth, and presses a porcelain teacup filled with warm rainwater into your hand. You take it because it feels impossibly rude to refuse.

You keep moving, weaving between shoulders. Beneath your feet, the polished ballroom floor quietly gives way to thick, plush carpet, and then to damp moss. Overhead, the chandeliers have drifted upward, turning into glowing paper lanterns that bob lazily against a ceiling of dark violet clouds. Just ahead, through an arched doorway, you catch a flash of a familiar coat—the exact color your friend always wears.

You hurry through the arch and stumble into a sunken conservatory. A circle of people in oversized velvet armchairs sits ankle-deep in clear, rippling water, though none of their shoes appear to be wet. They are leaning forward, whispering intently over a board game played with smooth river stones and tiny brass keys. Someone who has the face of an old childhood neighbor—but a complete stranger’s voice—looks up and nods toward a spiraling staircase made of stacked books. 

"They just went up," they tell you, as if you had already asked.

Your legs feel suddenly heavy, like you’re wading through waist-deep water, as you begin to climb. Halfway up, you glance down at your hands and notice the teacup is gone; in its place is a neatly folded map made of tissue paper, completely blank. The murmur of the party fades behind you, replaced by the quiet, distant hum of a refrigerator.

At the top, the stairs open onto a wide stone balcony overlooking an endless expanse of softly lit living rooms floating in the dark. Leaning against the railing is the person you’ve been looking for. A wave of sheer, weightless relief washes over you. You hurry over and tap them on the shoulder.

They turn around, their face slightly blurred at the edges, yet unmistakably theirs. They smile brightly and glance down at your hands.

"Oh, thank goodness," they say. "You brought it."

A quiet flicker of panic rises in your chest as you stare down at the blank tissue paper, trying desperately to remember what you promised to carry. When you look back up to explain, the balcony tilts gently like the deck of a ship, and the drifting lanterns float down around you both, dissolving the party into warm, golden static.
