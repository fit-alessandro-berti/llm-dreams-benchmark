I stopped talking mid-sentence. The argument we'd been circling for what felt like hours just... loosened. I looked at my friend across the table — they were still sharp-eyed, still angry, but I noticed their hands were trembling a little, and that told me more than any of our words had.

"Hey," I said, and my voice came out quiet. "I don't think I'm listening right now. I think I've been just talking to win."

They blinked, caught off guard. The silence stretched, strange and new.

"I'm sorry," I said. "I don't actually know what I want from this. But I know I don't want to hurt you, and I think I was about to."

And then the dream did that thing dreams do — it softened at the edges. The kitchen counter blurred. The clock on the wall showed a time I couldn't read. Somewhere in the apartment a kettle was boiling, or maybe it had been boiling for years. I could smell rain on hot pavement, and then flowers, and then nothing.

"What do you need from me?" I asked them, and my friend finally smiled — tired, uncertain, but real. And I felt something click into place, like a door opening that I'd been pressing on the wrong side of for so long.

Then I woke up.
