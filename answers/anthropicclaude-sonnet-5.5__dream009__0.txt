...**find something to eat before the sun sets.** Your stomach reminds you that you haven't eaten since, well, since *before*, and you're not sure how long ago that was.

You push through a curtain of hanging ivy and step onto a packed-dirt road. Ahead, a village hums with life. Smoke curls from clay chimneys, and a blacksmith's hammer rings out in a steady rhythm. Everyone wears rough-spun wool and linen, and you look down at your jeans and sneakers. *That's going to raise questions.*

A woman at a market stall squints at you, then presses a warm loaf of bread into your hands. "You look like you've traveled through more than just distance, stranger," she says. You open your mouth to answer, but she's already turned away, as though she's seen your kind before.

Then you notice the thing that makes your heart skip. Above the well at the center of the square, carved into the stone, is a symbol you recognize. It's the same one from the pendant you were wearing when you fell asleep. You reach for your neck, and the pendant is there, faintly warm and pulsing like a heartbeat.

An old man sitting by the well catches your eye and smiles. "Ah," he says. "It's about time. We've been waiting for you to wake up."

The bread is still warm in your hands. The sun is sinking. And somewhere behind you, a bell begins to toll.
