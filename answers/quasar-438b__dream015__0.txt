Despite the hardships, you decide to keep moving forward. There's no rhyme or reason to it—you just *can't stop*. Your feet carry you over crumbling asphalt, past hollowed-out buildings with their windows blown inward, their frames twisted like skeletons picked clean by scavengers more desperate than yourself.

You spot a flicker of orange light deep inside one structure. Someone's alive in there. Or something is burning. You can't tell the difference anymore.

You scavenge through rubble, pocketing what you can—a rusted tin, half a pencil, three dried beans stuck to a cracked tile. Enough calcories to bridge another distance.

The wind tastes black now. It leaves a metallic powder on your insides. The scy grows and swirls, but there are still people. You smell them. Hear them. Someone's muffled voice—*—ow much longer?* And then silence.

A survivor—woman, older, perhaps unimportant to the rest of the dead world—strides past you. She's lost to the wind, somewhere after that, gone to keep moving.

You keep moving.

The city dies behind you, kilometer by kilometer. You track how far you've come only by how much more of yourself is left. First the spare energy. Then the optimism. Then the ability to fear things.

And then, above, streaming through the crushing dark sky like a razor's line drawn late and too high—you catch what looks like the last utility cable still dancing, somewhere past where the hills deadend. You don't know whether to approach it or just let it pass like the rest.

You approach it.

The cable thickens, and thickens—until you rouse yourself, and realize you were never walking toward a cable at all.
