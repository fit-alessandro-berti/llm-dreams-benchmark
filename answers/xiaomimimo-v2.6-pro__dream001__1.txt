…start to walk, and the streets seem to rearrange themselves every time you blink. You check your pocket and find a crumpled piece of paper with an address on it — but the numbers keep changing: 742, 319, ∞ Maple Street.

You flag down a taxi. The driver has no face, just a soft, blurred impression where features should be, and they speak in your own voice. "You're already late," they say cheerfully. You get in anyway.

The city outside the window is a collage of places you half-remember — your childhood library merges into a coworker's house into an airport terminal from a trip you never took. Buildings breathe. A crosswalk paints itself onto the road just in time for you to step onto it.

The taxi stops. "Here," the driver says. You're standing in front of an enormous revolving door that leads into a room that is clearly too small to contain the space you enter — a vast auditorium, rows of faces, all turned toward you. They all have *your* face.

You take the stage. A microphone stands there, cold and heavy. You open your mouth to speak, but no words come out — only a bright, humming sound, like a tuning fork pressed against your chest. The audience leans in. They nod. They understand perfectly.

Someone in the front row reaches out and hands you a pair of glasses. The moment you put them on, the auditorium dissolves, and you're back on the street — but now it's dawn, and the address on the paper in your pocket reads clearly: **Here.**

Your phone buzzes. The meeting is in five minutes.

You don't move. You're smiling.
