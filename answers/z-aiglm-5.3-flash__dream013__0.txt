Near the entrance they were dull gray stone, but here they're lined with bookshelves, every volume fallen open to pages of equations that rearrange themselves when you blink. Some of the problems you recognize. One is written in your own handwriting, though you've never been here before.

The corridors twist in ways that shouldn't work — left turns that deliver you somewhere completely new, stairs that ascend while visibly pointing down. At some point you stop arguing with the geometry, and the maze seems pleased.

In the third chamber, a narrow bridge crosses a pit of darkness, and the flagstones bear a question in worn script: *What do you carry that grows heavier the farther you travel?* Tools flicker through your mind — rope, lantern, blade. Then something quieter. "Memory," you say aloud, and the stones warm beneath your feet like acknowledgment.

Deeper still, a small girl sits cross-legged in a corridor, chalking a map of the maze onto the floor. Her drawing is most detailed in the sections you haven't reached yet. Without looking up, she hands you a folded note. Your handwriting again. It says: *It's not the maze you're solving.* Behind you, the passages begin closing — gently, one by one, like eyes drifting shut. It doesn't feel like danger. It feels like being herded toward something patient.

The center of the maze is a round room lit by no visible source. On a stone pedestal rests a single lock: heavy, ancient, iron — with no keyhole, and nothing anywhere for it to hold shut. You turn it over. You apply every trick the labyrinth taught you. Nothing. So finally, absurdly, you sit down on the cold floor across from it and simply stop trying.

The lock exhales — you'd swear it sighs, like something relieved of a long duty — and crumbles into fine gray sand.

The walls fold outward like paper. Above you there is no ceiling, only night sky, stars wheeling slowly, and very far away, the sound of your own name — called softly, patiently, the way morning calls.

You wake with one hand loosely closed, fairly sure you'd been holding the answer to something, and fairly sure it will return exactly when you need it. For a few seconds you can still smell old books.
