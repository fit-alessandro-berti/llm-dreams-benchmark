...set out to climb the skeletal remains of the old skybridge that once spanned the canyon. You leave behind the relative safety of the rubble fields not for shelter or stores, but because the wind from above carries a faint, impossible sound: the steady hum of clean water. 

Each handhold is broken glass and rusted rebar. The air tastes of ash and old rain. Twice you pause, listening to the scrape of something heavy moving through the ruins below, but you keep your eyes forward. When your fingers finally grip the lip of the bridge’s crown, the world opens. Mist clings to cracked concrete. Below, a fissure in the earth breathes out cool, damp air. And there, pooled in a sunken terrace you’d only ever seen in faded photographs, is a shallow spring, ringed by stubborn moss and a handful of pale shoots pushing through the grit.

You don’t reach for it all. You take what you can carry, mark the route in chalk and bent wire, and turn back. Not to hoard, but to share. In a world that broke itself trying to keep everything, you decide to become something it forgot how to be: a keeper of seeds, a maker of paths, someone who believes tomorrow is worth the climb. The dream doesn’t promise a cure or a kingdom. It only offers the next step, and the quiet certainty that you’ll take it with others when you find them.
