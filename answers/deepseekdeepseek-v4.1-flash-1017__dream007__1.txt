Your friend stares at the wasp. The room tilts gently, and the walls turn into chalkboards covered in both your handwriting—old grudges, half-finished apologies, inside jokes from years ago. Your friend picks up a piece of chalk and draws a door. You open it together and step into a rainy version of your childhood kitchen, where the argument is just a kettle whistling. You pour it into two cups, and it becomes warm sunlight. Neither of you says sorry, but you sit down at the table, and the dream lets you both be right for a moment. Then you wake with your jaw tight and your hand open.
