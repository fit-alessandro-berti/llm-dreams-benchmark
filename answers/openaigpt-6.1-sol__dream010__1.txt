One is younger, carrying a backpack full of unfinished plans. Another is older, wearing the confident smile you practice before interviews. A third looks exactly like you, except they never hesitate.

The first challenge is to cross a room without touching the floor. Everyone starts building bridges out of their résumés. You unfold yours, but the paper turns into a flock of small white birds and flies toward the ceiling.

You think you’ve lost.

Then one bird returns and settles on your shoulder. The floor rises gently to meet your feet.

A bell rings.

For the second challenge, you must answer a question written inside a locked box. The confident version of you picks the lock. The younger one shakes the box until it breaks. You hold yours close and hear someone breathing inside.

“Are you all right?” you ask.

The box opens.

Beyond it is the office where you’ve always imagined working. Your name is on the door. Through the window, you can see a city you almost recognize, its streets rearranging themselves whenever you blink.

The final judge sits behind the desk. They have no face until you approach; then they look like someone who once believed in you.

“One last task,” they say, handing you a telephone. “It’s ringing.”

You lift the receiver.

At the other end, a child asks, “Did we get it?”

You recognize your own voice.

“Not yet,” you say.

The child is quiet for a moment. “Do we like it here?”

You look around. There are awards on the walls, a beautiful view, a chair shaped exactly to fit your body. But the window won’t open.

“May I see the other rooms?” you ask the judge.

They smile and hand you a key.

It is warm, as if someone has been holding it for a long time.

When you open the office door, the corridor is gone. In its place is a morning street, and somewhere nearby, someone is baking bread. You step outside with the telephone still in your hand.

The child is laughing now.

Behind you, the bell begins to ring again, but this time it sounds like a bicycle coming down the street.
