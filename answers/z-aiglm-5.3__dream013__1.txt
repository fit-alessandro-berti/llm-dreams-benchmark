...begin to suspect that the maze is solving you.

The puzzles grow stranger as you descend. One door presents no riddle at all—just a question written in handwriting you almost recognize: *What are you looking for?* You don't have an answer, but the door swings open anyway, satisfied by your honesty.

In the next chamber, the puzzle pieces float. You arrange them in midair—each fragment a memory, a conversation, a half-forgotten afternoon—and when the final piece clicks into place, the maze itself rearranges. The corridor behind you is simply gone. Ahead, seven identical hallways stretch into darkness, but somehow you know which one is right. In dreams, you simply know things.

Deeper still, the puzzles stop requiring logic. They require letting go. A lock that opens only when you stop trying to open it. A riddle whose answer changes each time you solve it, teaching you that the solving was the point.

Then, around a corner: a door with light spilling beneath it. No lock. No puzzle. Your hand finds the handle before you've decided to reach for it.

Behind it—maybe a garden, maybe the ocean, maybe a room from your childhood. The dream won't say. But as you step through, you feel that settling click of the last piece of something vast.

You wake with the answer still warm in your palm. By the time you reach for a pen, it's already fading—but the feeling lingers: you were almost there.
