The streets are narrow and muddy, lined with timber houses and flickering lanterns. People stare at your strange clothes, whispering as you pass. From their conversations, you learn that it is the year 1473, and the town is preparing for a rare celestial event: a black moon that appears only once every five hundred years.

An elderly clockmaker takes you in, convinced that you are the traveler described in an old prophecy. Hidden beneath his workshop is a machine built from brass gears, glass tubes, and a stone that hums when you touch it. He tells you the machine can open a passage through time—but only during the eclipse, and only if you repair its missing mechanism.

Using your memories of modern science, you improvise the missing part from springs, magnets, and pieces of an astrolabe. As the sky darkens, frightened townspeople gather outside, believing the eclipse signals the end of the world. The clockmaker starts the machine, and the room fills with brilliant blue light.

A doorway appears, showing your bedroom exactly as you left it. You step toward it, but then notice something unsettling: on the other side, another version of you is asleep in your bed.

Before you can decide whether to enter, the sleeping figure opens its eyes, looks directly at you, and whispers, “You were never supposed to come back.”

The doorway collapses—and you wake up clutching a small brass gear that was not there before.
