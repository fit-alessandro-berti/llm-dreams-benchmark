You’re standing in the middle of a bustling ballroom, the kind of place where laughter ricochets off crystal chandeliers and the scent of cake mingles with perfume. To anyone else, it’s a sea of strangers—families strolling past, colleagues clustered near the bar, teenagers huddled around glowing screens, older couples sharing stories over tea. All you know is that **one** person has been your constant companion since childhood: Maya, the girl who taught you how to dance at the old community center. 

As you push forward, you notice a **tower of seniors**, their hands clasped around steaming cups of coffee, eyes scanning the room for the same familiar face you’ve never seen again. Next, a **row of teens** in hoodies, heads bent over tablets, whispering about memes and future plans. Then a **vibrant family unit** with giggling toddlers, a dad juggling a tray of cupcakes, and a mom humming a lullaby. A **suite of business suits** debates the merits of remote work over espresso, while a **corner of street artists** splashes colors onto walls, their brushes dancing to an invisible beat. Even a solitary figure sits under a single lamp, staring out at the crowd as if waiting for someone to approach.

Each group moves in its own rhythm, oblivious to the way you trace the edges of the throng, feeling like a ghost drifting between them. The world feels both overwhelmingly crowded and strangely intimate—every smile, every laugh, every glance is a fleeting echo of the one person you truly know. And then, amidst the shuffle, you catch sight of a small round table where a woman with a warm, knowing smile waves your name. She introduces herself as **Mia**, invites you to share a drink, and asks if you’d like to join her for a nightcap. For a heartbeat, the maze of strangers collapses into a single thread, and you realize that finding one person among so many isn’t about belonging—it’s about carrying that one memory of her like a lantern in the darkest of nights.
