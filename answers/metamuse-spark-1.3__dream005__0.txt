Here's how the dream continues:

...and each group pulls you in for just a moment as you pass.

First it's the kitchen people — crowded shoulder-to-shoulder around an island that keeps getting longer the more you look at it. They're laughing about something you almost understand, chopping vegetables that turn into confetti. One of them hands you a drink you didn't ask for. You take a sip just to be polite, and it tastes like warm summer rain. You smile, nod, say "Have you seen — ?" but your friend's name dissolves on your tongue. They just smile back and turn to each other.

You drift into the living room. The carpet is impossibly soft, like walking on moss. A circle of old armchairs, and in them, people telling stories in overlapping voices. One woman is describing a trip you swear *you* took. A man finishes her sentences. You linger because for a second you feel like you belong here, like if you sat down they'd make space for you. But a tug in your chest reminds you: you're looking for someone.

Down a hallway that wasn't there a second ago, the music gets quieter. Two kids are sitting on the floor playing with glowing marbles that roll uphill. One looks up at you and says, very seriously, "She's upstairs." You don't ask how they know who you're looking for. In dreams, you just know.

Upstairs is quieter, stranger. The party thins out. A couple is whispering on a staircase that curves like a seashell. A lone older man stands on a balcony staring at a sky full of too many stars, holding a paper plate he’s forgotten about. The house feels bigger on the inside than the outside now, rooms opening into rooms.

And then — out on a back porch strung with lights that sway even though there's no wind — you see them.

The one person you know.

They're leaning on the railing, looking relieved, like they've been waiting. They wave and say your name, and suddenly you can say *their* name again too.

"I've been looking everywhere for you," you both say at the same time, and laugh.

You don't go back inside right away. You just stand there together while the muffled party hums behind the glass doors, feeling the night air, and for the first time all night the restless searching feeling melts away.

That's when you start to wake up — with the porch lights still flickering faintly behind your eyes.
