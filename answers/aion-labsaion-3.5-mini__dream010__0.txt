The first challenge is a presentation. You stand before a panel of judges, but when you look down at your notes, the pages are blank—no, not blank, they're written in a language you knew as a child but have since forgotten. Somehow, you understand it perfectly as you speak. The judges nod. One of them is your third-grade teacher. Another is a stranger who feels like family.

The second challenge takes place in the office building where you'll supposedly work. You step into the elevator and press the button for your floor, but the building keeps growing upward while you ride. The elevator doors open onto a beach. Your desk is there, half-buried in sand, and your coworkers are dolphins in business casual. Nobody finds this strange, so neither do you. You answer emails with your flippers. You're doing wonderfully.

The final challenge is the strangest: you must explain, in one sentence, why you want this job. The other finalists line up beside you—and you realize with a jolt that they all have your face. They are you, from other mornings, other lives, other dreams. Each of them gives their answer. Yours comes out as something simple and true, something you didn't know you believed until you said it.

The judge leans forward. The room goes quiet. The ocean sound from the beach seeps back in, growing louder, louder—

And you wake up, alarm ringing, with the sentence still on your lips. You can't remember what the job was. But you can remember exactly how it felt to almost have it, and the feeling stays with you all day, like sand in your shoes.
