…the longer I stare at them, the more they seem to move. Fine filigree of silver lines winds across the dark wood like veins, and I realize with a strange, sinking certainty that they are not carved at all. They are *growing*.

I trace one line with my fingertip. It is warm. Warmer than the box, warmer than the air of the room, which I now notice has no walls — just a soft gray mist pressing in from all sides, the way the beginning of every dream presses in.

The box breathes. I feel it expand gently against my palms, then contract, as if it has been waiting a long time to be held and is now remembering how. The lock — a brass face with no keyhole — turns its features toward me. It has a face. Of course it has a face. Everything in dreams has a face if you look long enough.

"You already have the key," the lock says, though it has no mouth, and the words arrive the way words do in dreams: already inside me, as if I had thought them myself.

I open my hand. Sitting in my palm is a small, ordinary key — the kind that unlocks nothing important, the kind you find in a drawer years after you've forgotten what it once opened. I don't remember picking it up. I don't remember *not* picking it up.

The keyhole appears only when I stop looking for it, unfolding from the grain of the wood like a eye opening. I slide the key in. It turns with a sound like a held breath finally released.

Inside the box: darkness, and then — a room. A small room, lit by a window I recognize from a house I lived in when I was seven. Someone is sitting at that window with their back to me. They do not turn around, but they say my name in a voice I know, and my chest aches in that specific way it only aches in dreams.

I reach toward the room, and the box grows larger, or I grow smaller, or both, and I step over the brass edge into the light of that impossible afternoon — and I wake, or I don't. In dreams, you can never be sure which one it is.
