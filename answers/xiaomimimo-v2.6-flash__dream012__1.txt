**...grab the wheel and steer into the darkness.**

The ship lurches violently, and salt spray explodes across the deck like shattered glass. Waves tower above us — black and angry — their crests glowing with an eerie phosphorescence, as though the ocean itself is alive with electricity.

"CAPTAIN!" the first mate shouts over the roar, his knuckles white on the railing.

You look out at your crew — tired, salt-crusted faces, wide eyes searching yours for an answer — and you realize there is no harbor, no lighthouse, no other ship on the horizon. There is only the storm, the sea, and **the command still lodged in your throat**.

You point. Not left. Not right. **Straight into the eye of it.**

The crew hesitates for just a moment — the kind of hesitation that feels like a century. Then, one by one, hands return to ropes. Sails are adjusted. The helm responds to your steady grip, and the ship begins to cut through the water with surprising resolve.

The lightning strikes — **close now** — illuminating everything for one brilliant, frozen moment: the rain falling sideways, the rope straining, the face of the storm itself somehow almost... *recognizable*.

And somewhere beneath the chaos, you feel something unexpected:

**Calm.**

As if you've always known this was exactly where you were supposed to be — not despite the storm, but **because** of it.

Then the ship crests a final, impossible wave, and ahead of you—

*something begins to appear through the rain...*

What do you see?
