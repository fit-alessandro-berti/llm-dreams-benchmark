…tiny symbols that seem to shift whenever you look away. At first they resemble leaves, then constellations, then the handwriting of someone you have forgotten.

You trace one with your fingertip.

The box hums.

Around you, the room grows quiet—not merely silent, but expectant. The clock stops ticking. Rain hangs motionless beyond the window. On the lid, a sentence slowly appears:

**What did you leave behind to get here?**

You search your pockets. A button. A train ticket with no destination. A photograph of an empty chair.

None of them fit.

Then you remember a song your mother used to sing, though you cannot remember ever hearing it while awake. You hum the first few notes.

Something inside the box hums back.

The patterns loosen and drift above the lid like silver moths. Without a click, without a hinge moving, the box opens.

Inside is a small garden under a blue evening sky. Wind stirs the grass. At the end of a narrow path stands a child wearing your old shoes, holding a brass key.

“I kept it safe,” the child says.

“The key?”

The child shakes their head and steps aside.

Behind them is a door you recognize, down to the chipped paint beside the handle. From the other side comes the clatter of dishes, and someone calling you in before dinner gets cold.

You reach into the box. Your hand passes through cool air, then warm sunlight.

The child places the key in your palm.

This time, when you turn it, you remember what the door opens onto.
