...begin to notice that the walls are not stone at all. They're made of equations—thousands of them, written in glowing ink that shifts when you look away. You reach out and touch one, and your finger comes away smeared with warm light.

The first chamber holds a door with no handle, only a riddle carved into its surface. You read it, and strangely, you already know the answer—not because you reasoned it out, but because it feels like remembering something you never learned. You speak the word aloud, and the door dissolves into moths that scatter into the darkness ahead.

Deeper now. The corridors begin to fold in on themselves, staircases spiraling both up and down simultaneously, yet your feet always find the right step without your mind deciding where to place them. You pass mirrors that don't show your reflection but instead show the puzzle you'll face next, three chambers early. In one mirror, you see yourself holding a small brass key—and minutes later, there it is in your palm, though you can't remember picking it up.

A room of floating numbers. A bridge built entirely of chess moves, each square vanishing behind you once you've stood on it in the correct sequence. Somewhere in the distance, a clock ticks backward. You realize you've been here before—or will be. The distinction doesn't seem to matter anymore.

At last you reach the heart of the maze: a single door, small and ordinary, nothing like the grand archways behind you. On it is written only one question, and somehow you understand that this is the only puzzle that was ever real. The others were just practice. Warm-ups for this moment.

You don't solve it the way you solved the rest. Instead, you close your eyes and let go of trying—the way the maze taught you, piece by piece, that forcing an answer only makes the walls climb higher. When you open your eyes again, the question has rearranged itself into your own name.

The door opens onto a garden you remember from childhood, impossibly bright, the grass wet with morning dew. Behind you, the maze folds itself shut like wings.

And just as you take that first step onto the grass—just as you think *so this is what I was looking for*—

you wake.
