You begin to speak—and the words come out as warm light. Not sound, exactly, but something the crowd can hold. Each sentence unfurls into the air like a paper lantern, drifting over the seats, and wherever one settles, someone smiles as if they’ve just remembered a name they thought they’d lost.

You look down at your notes. They’re blank. That doesn’t matter. The stage under your feet is soft, like packed snow that never melts, and when you take a step the boards answer with a low, kind chord. The audience leans in. A child in the front row cups their hands, catching one of your sentences before it floats away, and holds it up so the rest of the hall can see it glow.

You were supposed to be nervous. Instead you feel the strange certainty dreams sometimes grant: that everyone here already knows the ending, and they came only to hear you say it out loud. So you do. You tell them the simple thing—that they made it, that the long walk was worth it, that the lights were always going to come up. The lanterns rise all at once, the hall fills with gold, and just as the applause begins, you wake with the last word still warm on your tongue.
