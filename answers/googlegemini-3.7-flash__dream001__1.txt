You begin to run. 

The city around you doesn't obey ordinary geometry. The avenue stretches upward at an impossible angle, turning into a spiral of polished obsidian towers that reflect a sky split between twilight and midday. Crowds of people in tailored overcoats drift past you like schools of fish, moving in complete silence. 

Desperate for directions, you tap the shoulder of a commuter waiting at a crosswalk. When they turn, their face is a soft, shifting mosaic of old friends you haven't seen in years. They don't speak, but they hand you a key made of cold blue glass and point toward a tram floating three feet above the asphalt, glowing with the amber light of a distant autumn afternoon.

The doors open before you can step onto it, and suddenly you aren't on a train at all—you are walking through a quiet, echoing museum hallway lined with floor-to-ceiling windows. The panic about being late begins to dissolve, replaced by a strange, buoyant weightlessness.

At the very end of the corridor stands a pair of towering double doors made of carved oak. You push them open. 

Inside is not a boardroom or an office, but a sun-drenched meadow perched high above the clouds, the vast city humming quietly far below. Around a long wooden table sit all the different versions of yourself from ages past—the child, the teenager, the person you were yesterday—all quietly waiting for you to take the empty chair at the head of the table. 

"You're right on time," one of them smiles, and as you finally take your seat, the alarm begins to echo from somewhere far beyond the sky.
