…each group pulls you in like a tide.

The first cluster you approach is standing in a perfect circle, all of them laughing — but the laughter arrives a half-second before their mouths open, like a badly dubbed film. When you ask if they've seen your friend, they all turn in unison, and every one of them has your friend's face, though you know none of them are her. "She was just here," they say in your own voice.

You move on. The room stretches. The gathering seemed to be in a ballroom before, but now the walls are your old high school cafeteria, and the buffet table is serving birthday cake from a party you attended when you were nine. A woman in a gray coat — you're certain you know her, though you can't place her — presses a phone number into your palm. When you look down, it's written on a leaf.

A group near the window is playing some kind of game with dice that keep changing their number of sides. They invite you to play, and you understand the rules perfectly for as long as you're standing with them, and the moment you step away the rules dissolve from your memory like sugar in water. One of them, an old man with kind eyes, says, "You'll find her faster if you stop looking." This makes complete sense in the dream.

You keep looking anyway.

The crowd thickens. People's conversations overlap into a hum, and you realize the hum is forming words: your name, spoken in the cadence of your friend's laugh. You follow the sound through a door that was definitely not there before — and suddenly the noise of the party cuts out entirely.

You're in a small, quiet room. Your friend is there, sitting on the floor, waiting for you, as if no time has passed at all. "I was wondering when you'd get here," she says. "You always take the long way."

You sit down beside her. The party continues somewhere far away, muffled and irrelevant. You have a thousand things to tell her, but in the way of dreams, you find you don't need to say any of them — she already knows.

And then, softly, the floor begins to tilt, the light warms, and the edges of the room dissolve into morning.
