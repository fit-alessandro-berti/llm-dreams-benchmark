"Don't worry," she says, her voice sounding like it's coming from everywhere and nowhere at once. "No one is ever truly lost. Sometimes we just take the long way to where we're meant to be."

The group begins walking, and you follow. Strangely, you notice you know each of them somehow—the tall man with the umbrella reminds you of your grandfather, the laughing teenager carries your childhood dog on her shoulders as if this were the most natural thing in the world.

As you walk together, the city transforms around you. The harsh neon signs soften into lanterns glowing amber and gold. The crowd's hurried footsteps become a gentle rhythm, like rain on a rooftop. Buildings lean in close, their windows flickering with warm light, and you realize each window shows a memory—your first day of school, a summer afternoon by a lake, someone's hand holding yours.

You want to ask them where you're going, but the question dissolves before it reaches your lips. It doesn't matter anymore. The lost feeling has melted away like frost in morning sun.

Eventually, you arrive at a small door painted deep blue, standing alone in the middle of a quiet square. The kind strangers smile at you—not saying goodbye, because somehow you understand there are no goodbyes here—and one by one, they fade into the golden light of the streetlamps.

You reach for the blue door's handle...

And just as your fingers touch the cool metal, you wake up—with a lingering sense of peace, and the strange certainty that somewhere, those strangers are still walking beside you.
