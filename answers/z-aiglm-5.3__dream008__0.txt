...and you sit down, and the chair rolls away from the desk and keeps rolling, gently, across an office floor that has become much larger than any office floor should be. You arrive back at the desk eventually. The deadline was Friday. Now it's today. Now it was this morning. Now it was yesterday, and somehow you missed it, and everyone knows.

You open the file. The file is blank. You type, but the letters come out in the wrong order, then in a language you don't speak, then not at all — your fingers pass through the keys like they're made of water. Your boss appears beside you. He doesn't walk over; he is simply there, the way people in dreams are simply there.

"We need it by end of day," he says.

"Need what?" you ask.

He looks at you with terrible patience. "You know what it is."

You do not know what it is. You suspect you never knew what it was. You suspect you were hired by mistake and everyone has been too polite to mention it for years.

You go to get coffee, because in the dream this feels like a productive step. The break room is your childhood kitchen. The coffee pot is full of sand. You drink it anyway.

When you return, your desk is at the bottom of the ocean. You can see it down there, papers drifting upward, the monitor glowing faintly like a jellyfish. Everyone else in the office can breathe underwater. There was a training. You missed the training. There's an email about it somewhere, 4,000 unread emails deep.

You wake up at 3 a.m. with no deadlines at all. You lie in the dark anyway, composing an apology for a project that doesn't exist.
