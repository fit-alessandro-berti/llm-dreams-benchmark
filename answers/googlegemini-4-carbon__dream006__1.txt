You look up to apologize, bracing for whispers and laughter, but the auditorium has stretched into an impossible, cavernous space. In the velvet seats, the expectant crowd has subtly shifted; half of them are people you haven't seen since childhood, and the other half are tall, impeccably dressed owls, blinking slowly in the dim light. 

None of them seem upset that you aren't talking. In fact, they begin to nod solemnly, swaying in unison to the music spilling from your lungs. A woman in the front row opens a glowing yellow umbrella, and suddenly it is raining upwards—tiny droplets of warm, silver light rising from the floorboards toward the ceiling. 

Except there is no ceiling anymore. The heavy red stage curtains untether themselves and drift away like ribbons of smoke, revealing a vast, violet twilight sky. You look back at the microphone, which has quietly drooped and bloomed into a tall sunflower. 

All at once, the anxiety vanishes, replaced by the undeniable, crystal-clear logic of the dream: *Of course. I wasn't supposed to give a speech. I was supposed to conduct the tide.* 

You take one last, deep breath, push gently off the wooden stage with your bare toes, and float effortlessly upward, drifting out over the swaying crowd and into the warm, starry dark.
