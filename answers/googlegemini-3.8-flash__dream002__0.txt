...and the canopy overhead begins to churn like a dark, restless ocean. 

The air drops sharply in temperature, turning thick with the scent of crushed pine and electric ozone. Sunbeams snap shut like curtains, plunging the path into a bruised twilight of deep violet and slate gray. The wind arrives in a singular, breathless rush—not merely blowing, but humming with the sound of a thousand overlapping whispers, tugging at the hem of your clothes and urging you forward.

Then the rain begins, though it doesn't fall as water. The droplets drift down in slow motion, glowing with a soft, bioluminescent blue light. Where they touch the ferns and the moss, they don't wet the ground; they burst silently into tiny sparks that float upward toward the sky like inverted fireflies. 

Ahead of you, the path gives way to a wide clearing dominated by an impossibly vast oak tree. Its hollow trunk glows from within with the warm, amber light of a hearth fire. Drawn by an inexplicable pull, you step through the threshold of the roots just as the sky cracks open with silent, branching lightning that paints the world in silver.

Inside the tree, the gale immediately cuts out, replaced by a profound, velvety quiet. Through a spiraling opening in the upper branches, you look up to watch the storm rage, suspended between the fury of the sky and the quiet safety of the earth, realizing you are entirely weightless.
