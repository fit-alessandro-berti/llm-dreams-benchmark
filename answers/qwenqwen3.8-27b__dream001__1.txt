You start to ask the nearest person for directions, but when they turn to face you, their face is blank, like a wall waiting for paint. They point without a hand, and suddenly the street you are standing on bends around the corner and becomes a staircase.

The city is made of paper and light. Buildings fold and unfold as you pass. A clock in the sky ticks backward, then forward, then sideways. You run, and your shoes forget how to land, so you leap from rooftop to rooftop, catching the edges of awnings that ripple like water.

Finally, you come to a door with no building around it. Above the door, in letters that change shape every time you look, is written: “Meeting Room.”

You knock.

A voice from inside says, “You’re early.”

You open the door.

Inside, you are already sitting in a chair across from yourself. The other you looks up, smiles, and says, “I’ve been waiting for you to get here so we can begin.”

You look down at the table and see a map of the city. But every street is labeled with something you have forgotten. As you reach for the map, the room grows bright. The city outside begins to fold into itself, page by page, until there is nothing left but white.

Then, softly, a bell rings.

You wake with the address on your tongue, but instead of a street number, it is a song.
