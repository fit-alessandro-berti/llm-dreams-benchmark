...speak, but the words that come out aren't the ones you prepared. You look down at your notes and find the pages covered in handwriting that rearranges itself each time you blink. One line reads *"Tell them about the lighthouse."* You don't know anything about a lighthouse.

So you tell them about the lighthouse.

Your voice carries farther than it should, rolling out over the audience like warm water. As you describe the spiral staircase and the lamp that turns without anyone tending it, you notice the auditorium has no ceiling. Above the crowd there is a deep violet sky, and the stage lights are slow-moving stars.

The audience leans in. In the third row you recognize your second-grade teacher sitting beside someone you haven't met yet but somehow already miss. They nod along as if you're saying exactly what they came to hear.

The podium is now a ship's wheel. This seems perfectly reasonable. You grip it and keep talking, and the whole theater begins to sway gently, the rows of seats rising and falling like waves. No one seems alarmed. A woman in the front row opens an umbrella, and small paper birds drift out of it and circle up toward the stars.

You reach the end of the speech, though you don't remember deciding to. The last sentence hangs in the air, glowing faintly. It's the truest thing you've ever said, and you already can't recall what it was.

The crowd rises, but instead of applause you hear the ocean. Far off, past the last row of seats, a small light is turning, and you realize it has been guiding you this whole time.

You step off the stage. The floor is sand, and you walk toward the light.
