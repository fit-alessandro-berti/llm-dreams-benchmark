You stop a woman walking a dog that is somehow also a briefcase. "Excuse me," you say, "I'm looking for—" but you can't remember the name of the place. You know it starts with a letter. Maybe an M. Maybe a shape. The woman smiles and says, "It's right behind you," and when you turn around, the city has rearranged itself into your grandmother's kitchen, except the cabinets are skyscrapers and the clock on the wall has no hands, only the word SOON.

You check your phone. The screen shows the time as a color instead of numbers—deep amber, which you understand, with dream-certainty, means you have forty minutes left. You try to open the email with the meeting details, but the text keeps rearranging itself, the letters swimming like fish, and every time you almost read the address, your thumb turns the page.

You decide to follow the smell of coffee, because meetings always have coffee. The smell leads you down an alley that opens onto a plaza you've seen before—in a photograph? In a memory that isn't yours? In the center of the plaza there's a fountain, and sitting on its rim is every teacher you've ever had, all of them checking their watches in unison.

"You're early," one of them says, which feels wrong, because you were so certain you were late.

"Where is the meeting?" you ask.

"Where isn't it?" says another, and they all laugh, and the laugh echoes like a hall of doors slamming.

You look down and realize you're wearing pajamas. Except—no—when you look again, it's the suit you bought for an interview years ago, the one that never fit right. You smooth the lapel, and it ripples like water.

A tram arrives that wasn't there before, empty, doors open, warm light inside. There's no driver, but there is a small paper sign taped to the window that says your name—spelled almost correctly. And underneath, in handwriting you'd know anywhere: *You already know the way. You've always known. You just keep forgetting on purpose.*

You step aboard. The doors close like a held breath. And the city, all of it, every impossible street, begins to move around you instead.
