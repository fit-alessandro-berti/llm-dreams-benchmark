You are in a world where colors and shapes are constantly changing. As you explore this surreal landscape, you come across a blank canvas and it is waiting for you in the middle of the road.

It is impossibly large, taller than the melting towers around you, and it stands upright on nothing at all. The surface is not white, it's the color of a held breath. Around it, the world keeps shifting — a river turns into a ribbon of violet light, a mountain folds itself into a paper crane and flies away — but the canvas stays still, perfectly still, as if it is holding the landscape in place by refusing to change.

You walk up to it. When you get close, you can feel the air in front of it go quiet. No hum, no buzz, just a soft pressure like someone listening.

There is no easel, no frame, no ground under it. A hand reaches out from your own chest and rests against the surface, and you realize you are already holding a brush. The brush has no bristles, just a damp tip that smells like rain on hot asphalt and old paper.

You lift it and hesitate. You know, in the way you know things in dreams, that whatever you put on it will become real here, and whatever you leave blank will stay a possibility.

So you don't paint a picture. You press your palm to the canvas and leave a faint, warm print, and the print begins to fill itself in — with the color you were before you started dreaming, and the shape of the doorway you came through, and a small, steady blue square in the corner that won't change no matter how much the world around you does.

Then the canvas begins to ripple, like water, and you understand it was never blank at all. It was just waiting for you to remember what you already knew.
