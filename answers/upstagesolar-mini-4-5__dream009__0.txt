...find a way to teach a village of suspicious farmers that the electric power line in your pocket isn't a relic or a scam—but proof that the world doesn't end at their village's edge.

You wake on straw in a farmhouse with a single wooden beam, a clay pot of sour milk, and no one blinking at your modern clothes. The first hour is almost peaceful: mist over the fields, rooster, an old goat that ignores you. But by noon, the villagers are gathered. The magistrate has a writ. Somewhere in the back of your mind you realize the problem isn't technology—it's **truth**. They've lived under a quiet, grinding system where questioning the harvest yields or the local lord's tax is dangerous. You have one precious thing: evidence that the world is bigger, and that the system might be *wrong*, not just old.

But you can't explain what you don't yet understand about your era—no internet, no books, no map of how to travel safely. So you do the only practical thing in a dream: you **ask the dream**. You wake yourself with a sharp thought and choose a first task.

The task: **find the miller's daughter who disappeared a season ago**, because the magistrate's writ contradicts the village's official story. While digging into the mill, you find a half-buried chest—not gold, but letters. Old letters. They're in a language you half-remember from a history book, and one letter is a warning about a "league of pilgrims" who once passed through and traded knowledge before the village went quiet. That's your first thread.

You decide to follow it. But you can't leave without the villagers trusting you—so you do what you always do: you trade the one thing you can still make in this era. You take out the phone battery, walk to the well, and ask the old goat-herd to watch it. Then you strike a match from your lighter—cream, smokeless, unburned in a hundred ways—and light the candle wax to demonstrate fire you can **control**. You don't say "magic." You say: *"Watch."* And you hold the flame steady while the wind threatens to take it.

The goat-herd goes pale. Then he whispers: *"The miller's daughter used to say the same trick, when she was a girl. She said if you can make a fire that doesn't want to burn you, she'll meet you at the mill."*

You are still dreaming now. The dream asks you one question before it will continue: **Which do you want to trust first—the miller's daughter, or the letters?**
