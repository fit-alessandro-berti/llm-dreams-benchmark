One of them, a woman with silver braids and a coat the color of wet slate, takes your hand without asking. Her palm is cool and smells faintly of oranges.

"This way," she says, though you haven't told her where you're going.

The city shifts as you walk. The streets fold in on themselves like pages of a book, and the buildings lean closer, their windows glowing with lamplight that doesn't come from any lamp. Trams glide past with no drivers, humming a song you almost recognize. A man selling umbrellas winks at you and presses one into your hands, though it isn't raining, and the sky overhead is a deep, starry violet.

The group leads you through a market where the vendors sell things you can't name: bottled afternoons, keys to doors that haven't been built yet, small glass birds that sing when you look at them. A boy with ink-stained fingers hands you a map. You unfold it, and the lines are moving, rearranging themselves into the shape of a route that ends at a door you somehow already know.

"Do you want to know what's behind it?" the boy asks.

You nod, and suddenly you're not lost anymore. The fear has dissolved like sugar in warm tea, leaving only a lightness in your chest. The silver-braided woman squeezes your hand once and lets go.

The door is painted the blue of deep water. When you push it open, you hear laughter, the clink of cups, and a voice calling your name, the way someone does when they've been waiting for you a long time. You step through, and the street behind you quietly rearranges itself, becoming just a street, full of ordinary people going home, while you walk toward the light.
