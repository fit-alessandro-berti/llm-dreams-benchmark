“There you are,” says a woman wearing a necklace of tiny silver keys. “We were just talking about the sea.”

Behind her, a dozen guests hold seashells to their ears. From every shell comes your own voice, laughing.

You apologize and move on.

The gathering fills a house too large to belong to anyone. Rooms open into courtyards; courtyards lead to staircases carpeted in grass. Somewhere a band is playing the song your one friend always hums, but every time you follow it, the musicians are packing up.

You ask a waiter if he has seen your friend.

“Of course,” he says. “What age are they tonight?”

You try to remember. You can picture their coat, the way they tilt their head when listening, but their face keeps slipping behind other faces.

In the next room, people are standing around a table set for dinner. They move aside to reveal an empty chair.

“We saved your place.”

“I’m looking for someone.”

“So are we,” they say, and return to watching the chair.

At last you spot the familiar coat beyond a glass door. Your friend is outside beneath a tree hung with paper lanterns. Relief carries you across the room.

But the door opens onto a swimming pool.

You stop with one foot over the water.

Your friend waves from the far side. They look exactly as you remember, though you still cannot see their face.

“How do I get over there?” you call.

They point down.

Beneath the water is a staircase, lit by warm yellow lamps. Guests descend it in evening clothes, carrying their shoes. One man pauses to roll up his trousers, although he is already submerged to his waist.

You sit at the edge and take off your shoes. The water is warm. As you descend, you discover you can breathe, and the noise of the party softens into the clink of cutlery in another room.

At the bottom, your friend is waiting beside a little kitchen table.

You know it immediately: the chipped blue paint, the uneven leg steadied by a folded envelope. You have eaten here before.

Your friend pulls out a chair.

“I thought you’d never find me.”

“I kept asking people.”

“I know. They kept coming down to tell me.”

From overhead comes a muffled burst of laughter. You glance up at the soles of shoes moving across the ceiling.

“Do we have to go back?”

“After tea.”

Your friend turns toward the stove. You sit down, and for the first time all evening, you know where to put your hands.
