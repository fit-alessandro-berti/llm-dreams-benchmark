...keep walking east.

You don't remember deciding this exactly—it's more that the decision was always there, folded into your pocket like an old map. East is where the stories say the rain still falls clean. East is where your feet point when you stop thinking.

The road is cracked like old porcelain. Somewhere beneath the weeds, lane markers surface like the bones of a whale. You pass rusted cars that have become planters—morning glories spilling through shattered windshields, thriving on no one's attention.

A dog finds you at midday, or maybe you find the dog. It's thin, gray-muzzled, and it falls into step beside you without ceremony, as if you'd arranged this years ago. You share your water. You have no food. The dog seems to know this and stays anyway.

Dreams don't explain things; you simply know them. You know, for instance, that the radio tower ahead is safe. You know the woman there keeps jars of seeds—tomato, squash, something blue she calls *grandmother's morning*. You know she'll ask you one question when you arrive, and you've been rehearsing the answer your whole life without realizing it.

Night comes sideways, the way it does in dreams. The stars are wrong—too many, too close—but they're wrong in a familiar way, like your childhood bedroom rearranged. The dog sighs against your side. Somewhere far off, wind moves through the ribs of the old world, and it sounds almost like a highway, almost like home.

You wake before you reach the tower. You always wake before you reach the tower.

But this morning there's dirt under your fingernails, and somewhere east of wherever you are, you're certain the rain is falling clean.
