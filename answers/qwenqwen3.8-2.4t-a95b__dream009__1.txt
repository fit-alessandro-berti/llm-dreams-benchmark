

…find the old well before sunset, because someone there is waiting to return what you lost.

You step barefoot onto a road of packed earth, lined with thatched roofs and the smell of woodsmoke. People pass you wearing rough-spun clothes, speaking in a dialect you somehow understand, though you have never heard it before. No one seems surprised by you—only by the fact that you are empty-handed.

“The well,” an old woman says gently, pointing toward a hill where crows circle in the amber light. “You must not arrive after the bells stop.”

You begin to walk. The world feels both ancient and familiar, as if you have lived here in another life. Along the way, you pass a blacksmith hammering iron, children chasing a hoop down a lane, and a woman hanging linen to dry in the fading sun. Each of them gives you the same warning: do not look into the well until you have spoken your true name.

When you finally reach the hill, the well stands alone beneath a sky turning violet. A rope hangs over its stone edge, swaying though there is no wind. You lean forward, heart pounding, and whisper the name you have not said aloud since childhood.

The water below begins to glow.

Instead of your reflection, you see your own bed, your own room, your own time—quiet and waiting for you. A hand rises from the water, holding a small object: the dream you thought you had forgotten.

You reach for it.

And then the bells stop.

You wake with your hand open on the pillow, palm damp, and the faint scent of woodsmoke still lingering in the air.
