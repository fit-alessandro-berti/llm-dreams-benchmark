Their hands become little lanterns guiding you through streets that melt like warm wax into gardens of glowing paper flowers. Together you walk until the city’s roar softens into a lullaby, and the towering buildings seem to lean in to listen rather than overwhelm.

You find your way not to a destination, but to a doorway that opens into a room inside yourself—one you’d forgotten existed—filled with the smell of rain and old books and the sound of someone laughing with your own voice. When you turn to thank them, they’ve become part of the skyline, bright windows glowing against the dusk.

You wake with the hum of their kindness still in your chest, and for a moment, the bustling city outside feels like part of the dream too—gentler, shared, and suddenly, unmistakably yours.
