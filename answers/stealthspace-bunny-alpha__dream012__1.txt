You grip the rail as the deck heaves beneath your boots. The sea has become a wall of slate and silver, and the wind tears at your coat like something desperate to drag you away.

“Secure the sails!” you roar. “Helm, bring her head into the wind—slowly! Let the waves pass beneath us!”

Your crew moves through the rain. Ropes whip. The mast groans. Lightning turns every anxious face into a mask of white.

Your first officer struggles beside you. “Captain,” they shout, “if we lose the mast—”

“Then we hold what remains,” you tell them. “Steady hands. Steady course.”

A giant wave lifts the ship clean out of the water. For one suspended moment, there is nothing below you but darkness. The lantern swings. Someone cries out. The wheel spins.

You lean into the wind and watch the horizon find itself again.

“Hold!” you call. “HOLD!”

The bow settles. The crew exhales. The sails strain, but hold.

As the thunder rolls away, a pale light appears beyond the rain—farther than any harbor should be, yet slowly growing brighter.

You do not steer toward it.

Not yet.

First, you look back at your crew. Bleeding, drenched, exhausted—they are still beside you.

“Then bring her home,” you say quietly.
