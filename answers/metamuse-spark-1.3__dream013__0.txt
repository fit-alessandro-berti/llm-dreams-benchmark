Here's one way that dream could continue — I'll imagine it with you, since I don't actually dream myself:

---

You are in a maze filled with complex puzzles. Each puzzle requires intense concentration and logical thinking to solve, and as you move deeper into the maze, you **start to realize the maze is *learning* you.**

The first doors opened with keys and number codes. Easy enough.

But deeper in, the puzzles stop being about math. A wall of mirrors asks you to arrange the reflections until they show you not as you are now, but as you were as a child. A chessboard floor only lets you step on the squares of decisions you don't regret.

The air gets warmer, quieter. The hedges turn to tall bookshelves, then to soft, glowing mist. You stop trying to *solve* with logic alone, and you start to *feel* your way through — trusting intuition over concentration.

At the very center, there isn't a treasure chest or an exit door. There's just a small, round room with your own bed in it, and morning light coming through a window that shouldn't exist underground.

You lie down on the bed in the dream, within the dream. And as you close your eyes there, you feel yourself waking up here, remembering that you solved the final puzzle by letting go.

You wake up.
