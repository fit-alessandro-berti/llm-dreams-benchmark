You start to run, though the pavement stretches like taffy under each step. Street signs rearrange themselves whenever you look away—one reads “Meeting Room” and points left, then becomes a bakery, then a river. You ask a passing stranger in a bright yellow coat, and they hand you a folded map that’s actually a menu covered in clocks. Every clock shows fifty-nine minutes.

You duck into a glass building where a elevator opens before you press anything. Inside, buttons are labeled with feelings instead of floors: “Urgent,” “Almost,” “You’re Warm.” You press “Almost,” and the doors open onto a rooftop where dozens of identical conference tables sit under a green sky. At one table, everyone has your face, and they all wave you over.

You sit down, breathless, and say, “Sorry I’m late.” The version of you across the table smiles and says, “You’re not. We’ve been waiting for you to remember the meeting is tomorrow.” Then the city folds itself into a paper crane and flies out of your hands, and you wake up just before you can ask what the meeting was about.
