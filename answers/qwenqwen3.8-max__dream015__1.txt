Despite the hardships, you decide to keep moving toward the faint green light flickering beyond the ruined skyline.

You don’t know why it calls to you. It might be a trap, or a fire, or nothing at all—but in a world emptied of color, that small emerald glow feels like a promise.

Your boots crunch over broken glass and ash as you pass the skeletons of cars and buildings. The wind carries whispers that sound almost like voices, warning you to turn back. Hunger twists in your stomach. Your canteen is nearly empty. Somewhere in the shadows, something is following you.

Still, you walk.

At dusk, you reach the source of the light: a cracked greenhouse half-swallowed by the earth. Inside, impossibly, plants are growing—vines crawling over rusted shelves, flowers opening like tiny lanterns, and a tree whose leaves shine with soft bioluminescence.

Beneath the tree sits an old radio, humming static. When you touch it, a voice breaks through:

“If you can hear this, you are not alone. Follow the green lights. We are rebuilding.”

For the first time in what feels like years, you cry—not from fear, but from relief.

Then the dream shifts. You are no longer just surviving. You are carrying seeds in your pockets, planting them in the wasteland, and wherever they take root, the green lights spread.

By morning, the ruined world is no longer dead.

It is waiting to begin again.
