written in your own handwriting.

The first asks: **What did you want to become before you learned to be practical?**

You glance around. Everyone else is writing furiously, their pencils making the sound of rain. The clock above the door has no hands, but you know you are running out of time.

The second question is a photograph of a door you recognize from childhood. Beneath it are four possible answers:

A. Open it.  
B. Knock.  
C. Pretend you never saw it.  
D. None of the above.

You raise your hand. The proctor comes over, wearing your face, only older.

“Is this going to be on the exam?” you ask, pointing at the photograph.

“It already is,” they say.

You look down again. The paper has become a small, warm bird. Its feet grip your fingers, and you worry that letting it go will count as leaving the question blank.

Around you, the other students begin folding their exams into neat paper cages.

You carry yours to the window. Outside is the place you used to walk to when you wanted to be alone. It looks much smaller from here.

The bird lifts its wings.

Behind you, the proctor says, “You still have time.”

You open the window.
