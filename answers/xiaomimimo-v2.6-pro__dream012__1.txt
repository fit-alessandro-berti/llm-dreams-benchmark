You are the captain of a ship in the middle of a turbulent sea. Your crew looks to you for guidance as the storm worsens, and you grab the wheel with both hands, feeling the wood groan beneath your grip. Lightning splits the sky — not white, but *purple*, like the heavens themselves are bruising.

"Hold fast!" you shout over the roar of the wind, but your voice feels strangely thin, swallowed by something deeper than the storm. The waves aren't just water anymore. They *whisper*. Every crest that rises before the bow carries murmurs — fragments of words in languages you've never heard but somehow understand.

"Turn the vessel *toward* it," says a voice behind you.

You glance over your shoulder. There's a child standing on the deck — barefoot, dry despite the rain, wearing a white dress that doesn't move in the wind. Their eyes are completely black, like two pools of ink reflecting nothing.

"Toward *what*?" you ask.

The child points. Through the curtain of rain and fury, you see it — a light. Not a lighthouse. Not a star. A *door*, floating upright on the surface of the ocean, glowing from within with a warm golden luminescence. It stands perfectly still while everything around it rages.

Your first mate, a grizzled woman with salt-streaked hair, stares at the door and shakes her head. "Captain, that wasn't there a moment ago. I don't think it *exists* in any chart I've ever—"

"I know," you say.

And somehow, you do know. You remember the door. You've *always* remembered the door. It's why you became a captain. It's why the sea called to you from the first time you ever saw it from shore as a child — the same child now standing behind you, because you recognize the dress.

Your crew begins to whisper among themselves. Some look terrified. Some look *hopeful*, like they've been waiting for this without knowing it.

You turn the wheel. The ship groans, bows fighting the current, but slowly — *impossibly* — the vessel finds its path. The storm howls in protest, the waves rising taller now, like the ocean itself is trying to stop you. But the closer you get to the door, the quieter everything becomes. The rain falls softer. The thunder fades to a heartbeat.

You pull alongside the door. It stands about seven feet tall, frameless, its edges rippling like heat over sand. The golden light within is calm. Beyond it, you can just barely make out a horizon line — one without storms, without walls, without end.

The child takes your hand. Their skin is warm.

"This is where the dream ends," the child whispers.

Your crew gathers behind you, silent now, waiting.

You reach for the door. It hums against your fingertips.

You push it open —

And for one dizzying, breathless moment, you are not the captain. You are not the child. You are *both*. You are the storm and the calm. You are the sea and the ship. You are the one dreaming and the one being dreamed of.

You step through.

The ocean disappears behind you like a memory fading at dawn. And what lies ahead is—

*beep beep beep*

—your alarm clock.

But your hands still smell of salt.
