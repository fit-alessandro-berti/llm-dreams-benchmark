In the first round, you have to return a lost hour to the person who needs it most. You give yours to a man on a train platform who is still thinking of what to say to his daughter. In the second, you must carry a bowl of water up a staircase without spilling a drop. Halfway up, a dog starts following you. You finish with an empty bowl and a very pleased dog.

Somehow, you make the finals.

The last challenge takes place in your childhood kitchen. Behind the judges sits a small girl in pajamas, kicking her heels against the cabinet. You recognize the gap between her front teeth.

“Tell us why you deserve your dream job,” says the head judge.

You have prepared for this. You reach into your pocket for your speech and find a damp dog biscuit. The girl snorts.

“What?” you ask her.

“You don’t even know what the job is.”

You look back at the banner. GRAND PRIZE: YOUR DREAM JOB. It occurs to you that you’ve spent the whole competition imagining the phone call where you tell everyone you won. You haven’t once imagined a Tuesday afternoon at work.

“What did we want to do?” you ask her.

She slides off the cabinet and takes you by the hand. Out in the yard, under the pear tree, is the crooked little house you built together from orange crates. A snail is halfway through its front door. Inside, somebody has made a bed out of moss exactly its size.

“Places for small things,” she says impatiently, as though you were the one who’d thought of it yesterday.

From the kitchen, a judge rings a bell. Your time is up. The girl tips the roof to show you where the rain gets in, and you crouch beside her to see about fixing it.

You wake before they announce the winner. It’s Tuesday. For a moment you lie still, disappointed that you’ll never know. Then your feet find the floor. There’s some scrap wood behind the shed, and you have an hour before work.
