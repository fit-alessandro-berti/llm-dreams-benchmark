...written in a language that almost makes sense. The letters look familiar — like English seen through water — but every time you focus on a word, it rearranges itself. You squint, and "Question 1" seems to read: *Explain the thing you forgot to learn.* The next one: *Define yourself in under three minutes. Show your work.*

You look up. The exam hall stretches impossibly long, rows of desks receding into a haze, and every other student is already writing, pens scratching in unison like rain on a roof. Their faces are smooth and indistinct, but you know somehow that none of them are struggling. They all studied. You're the only one who didn't — you can't even remember enrolling in this course, but the certainty that your entire future hinges on this single paper sits in your chest like a stone.

You reach for your pencil and it crumbles softly in your fingers, like dry bread. The spare in your pocket is a crayon. The crayon is melting.

The clock at the front of the room has no hands, but you can feel time draining anyway, fast, like a bathtub. The professor drifts between the rows. As he passes your desk, his face comes into focus and it's your third-grade teacher, then your father, then no one at all — just a smear of disapproval.

Frantic, you flip to the back of the exam booklet, hoping for easier questions. Instead you find pages of your own handwriting from years ago, notes you don't remember taking, and in the margin of one page, in your younger self's careful print: *"The answers were never the point. Look up."*

You look up. The ceiling has opened onto a night sky, and the exam hall is dissolving — desks, students, the smell of pencil shavings — everything unraveling into dark. You feel the strange, weightless lurch, half terror and half relief, of falling upward into sleep's exit...

And then you wake, heart pounding, and for one disorienting second you can't remember what subject the exam was even in. It doesn't matter. In a few hours, you have a real exam. And you can't quite shake the feeling that you never studied for it either.
