You start to run, but the pavement keeps changing beneath your feet—cobblestones turning to polished marble, then to tram tracks, then to soft moss. The buildings lean inward as you move, their windows opening and closing like eyelids. You check your pockets for a phone, a wallet, anything with an address, but find only a handful of silver coins and a map of a city that isn't this one.

A woman at a corner newsstand tells you the meeting is "next to the yesterday café," but when you turn to ask where that is, she has become a payphone ringing with your own voice. You follow the sound down an avenue that spirals gently upward. The sky is late afternoon, but the streetlamps are flickering on, and each one holds a tiny projector showing memories you don't recognize—your birthday, though the cake has too many candles; a train platform where everyone is waving goodbye to no one.

The clock in the square has no numbers, only colors. You tell yourself you have thirty minutes, though you're not sure if time is moving forward or sideways. A child offers you directions written in condensation on a window, but the words evaporate before you can read them. You keep running because the meeting feels important the way dreams are important, as if the outcome will ripple into waking life.

The buildings thin out, and you find yourself at a harbor where the water is made of folded paper. A boat awaits, its sail stitched from appointment calendars. You step aboard, and the boat doesn't sail so much as remember sailing, gliding over the creased waves toward a tower that shouldn't be there. Its doors open onto a room that is simultaneously an office, a cathedral, and the back seat of your first car. The conference table is enormous, stretching into fog.

Everyone at the table is waiting for you. They don't have faces, but you know they are smiling. The chair at the head of the table pulls itself out. As you sit, you realize with the calm certainty of a dreamer that you have been here before, that this meeting is between the version of you who is lost and the version of you who knows the way. You open your mouth to speak, and the agenda writes itself in gold ink across the tabletop: *Everything you were afraid to become. Everything you already are.*

You look down at your hands. They are holding a key, a compass, and a clock that has finally stopped ticking. The room holds its breath. You clear your throat to begin.
