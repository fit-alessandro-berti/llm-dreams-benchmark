You glance up. Your boss is leaning over your cubicle wall—which has somehow stretched to twelve feet tall—tapping a massive brass wristwatch. Their face keeps flickering between your actual boss and your fourth-grade math teacher. "We really needed the first draft twenty minutes ago," they say, though their voice sounds muffled, like it’s coming through a drive-thru speaker.

Desperate, you grab the stack of reference files they handed you, only to find you are holding a thick bundle of dry autumn leaves. When you try to explain that the data is missing, your voice comes out as a faint whisper. 

You push your chair back to stand up and look for help, and your feet splash into ankle-deep water. The office carpet has quietly flooded, and the fluorescent lights overhead are buzzing like a swarm of bees. Around you, your coworkers are completely unfazed, paddling past on rolling office chairs and using staplers as oars. 

"Just finish the summary," your boss insists, pointing upward. 

You look up. The ceiling tiles have drifted away, revealing a bruised purple twilight sky filled with giant, glowing spreadsheets floating like clouds. Your deadline is written across the horizon in blinking red numbers, counting down faster than seconds should. You take a deep breath, climb onto your desk, and leap toward the nearest glowing cell, stretching your arm impossibly far to grab it—

And just as your fingertips brush the light, gravity gives out, dropping you backward into nothingness until you jolt upright in bed, heart hammering, staring at your alarm clock and slowly realizing it’s Sunday.
