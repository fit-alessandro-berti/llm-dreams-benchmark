You grip the wheel, though it turns beneath your hands as if something far below the ship is steering.

“Reef the sails!” you shout. Your crew rushes into the rain, their lanterns bobbing like fireflies against the black waves. Then lightning opens the sky, and for one impossible moment you see the sea floor beneath you: streets, rooftops, a bell tower. A whole city lies under the water, its windows glowing gold.

The ship’s bell rings by itself.

From the flooded tower, another bell answers.

Your first mate appears at your side, soaked and pale. “Captain,” she says, “the tide is going out.”

She is right. The water is falling all around the ship, faster than any tide should. You order the crew to hold fast. The hull settles gently onto the roof of a grand old station, and the waves retreat down its steps, leaving fish flickering across the cobbles.

The storm vanishes. Above you, stars shine in a perfectly clear sky.

Your crew waits for a command. You look toward the station clock. Its hands are turning backward, and behind its glass you can see a light moving from room to room.

“Bring a lantern,” you say.

Together, you climb down to find out who has been waiting there.
