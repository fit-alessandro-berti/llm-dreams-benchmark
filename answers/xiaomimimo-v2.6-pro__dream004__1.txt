You are in a world where colors and shapes are constantly changing. As you explore this surreal landscape, you come across a blank canvas and…

...pause. The canvas hums, or maybe it breathes. A soft warmth radiates from its surface, as though it remembers every painting that ever touched it. You reach out and press your palm against it — and suddenly the ground beneath you dissolves.

You fall *through* the canvas.

On the other side, the world is **still**. The colors have stopped shifting. Everything is a soft, diffused gray — like a sketch that hasn't decided what to become yet. Trees stand like pencil outlines. The sky is textured paper, crinkled at the edges where it meets the horizon. A river of liquid charcoal flows silently past your feet.

You notice your hands are made of brushstrokes — loose, impressionistic, barely holding together. When you wave them, tiny flecks of paint scatter and vanish into the gray.

A voice speaks, but it comes from *inside* the landscape itself — the ground, the air, the outline of a distant mountain:

*"You were the one painting the colors earlier. Every shift you saw — the churning violets, the bleeding golds, the collapsing geometries — those were your dreams becoming the world. But you stopped. You went blank."*

You look down at the white canvas at your feet. It's the same one — or its twin — staring up at you like an open mouth waiting to sing.

Slowly, uncertainly, you kneel and dip your fingers into the charcoal river. The liquid is cool and alive. You drag your hand across the canvas and —

**BLOOM.**

Cobalt explodes outward like a shockwave. The pencil trees gain bark and chlorophyll. The paper sky tears open to reveal aurora ribbons spiraling in impossible patterns. Your brushstroke hands solidify into solid, warm skin — and you feel the dream recognize you.

Shapes tumble back into motion. Hexagons orbit like planets. Dodecahedrons bloom from the soil like flowers. The world shivers with every color you never knew you could see — colors that have no names, colors that exist only in the space between sleeping and waking.

A door materializes. It's made of all the colors you just painted, swirling together. Through it, you can see another blank canvas — waiting.

The voice returns, softer now, almost amused:

*"Go on. You're not done dreaming yet."*

And you step through, fingers already reaching for the charcoal river beyond.
