...you sit down at your desk, but the chair keeps sinking lower and lower until your chin rests on the desktop. You try to type, but the letters on the keyboard have rearranged themselves, and every key you press makes the sound of your mother calling your name from another room.

You look up to ask your boss for clarification, but he's now your tenth-grade math teacher, and he's handing you an exam you never studied for. "The deadline is the deadline," he says, though his voice is coming from the printer, which has begun spitting out an endless stream of pages—all blank except for the word **SOON**.

You check the clock. 4:45. You watch the minute hand spin backward, then forward, then detach from the clock entirely, fall to the floor, become a small snake, and slither under the door.

Your phone buzzes. An email from the client. Subject line: *RE: RE: RE: RE: URGENT*. You open it, but the text keeps rewriting itself, and by the time you reach the end, you can't remember what the beginning said.

You stand up to get coffee—just a moment to think—but the break room is now your childhood kitchen, and your grandmother is stirring something on the stove. "You're late," she says, though you don't know for what. "You've always been late."

When you return, your desk has been moved to the middle of a forest. Your boss is there, still waiting, tapping his foot. "The deadline was an hour ago," he says. "Also, you're in your underwear."

You look down. You are.

Then—your alarm. You wake up. It's Saturday. There is no project. There is no deadline. There never was.

You lie there for a moment, heart pounding, then reach for your phone to check your work email.

Just in case.
