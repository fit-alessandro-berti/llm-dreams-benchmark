A woman selling oranges points you toward the tram. You ask which stop to get off at.

“You’ll recognize it,” she says, and gives you an orange for the ride.

The tram runs straight through the city, through office lobbies and kitchens and a barbershop where a man tips his head forward to let it pass. At last it stops in front of the house you grew up in. There’s a brass plaque beside the door: **MEETING IN PROGRESS. PLEASE KNOCK.**

You knock. From inside, someone says, “Could you get that?”

So you let yourself in.

The kitchen table is crowded with people you almost recognize. One has your father’s hands. Another is wearing a coat you lost years ago. At the far end sits your childhood self, swinging their feet under the chair.

Everyone goes quiet. You reach for your notes and find the orange in your pocket.

“Did you bring enough for everyone?” the child asks.

You peel it. There are exactly as many segments as there are people at the table. You pass them around, and for a while nobody says anything. Then the person in your old coat asks if you remember how to get to the creek.

You do. You’d forgotten there was a creek, but you know which fence to climb and where to put your foot.

You wake up halfway through giving directions.
