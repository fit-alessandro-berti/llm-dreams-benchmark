You put your ear to the lid. Someone inside is washing dishes.

“Just a minute,” a voice says.

You wait. The kitchen light comes on around you. It’s the kitchen you grew up in, except the window is at floor level, and on the other side of the glass, clouds drift slowly past.

Your mother is at the sink. She dries her hands on her jeans and says, “Oh, good. Can you hold this?”

She gives you a wet saucer. There’s a silver fish on it, no longer than an eyelash. You cup your free hand over it so it won’t jump.

When you look up, the kitchen is gone. The box is open on your lap, full of warm water. You lower the saucer to the surface and wait until the fish decides to swim off.
