...the patterns begin to move. Silver lines crawl across the lid like ink spreading through water, and they form a spiral that seems to pull your gaze inward. The longer you look, the more the spiral resembles a staircase, and the box grows warm in your hands, as if something inside has a pulse.

You try the latch. It doesn't give, but a faint humming answers you, low and familiar, the sound of a song you once knew as a child and can't quite remember the words to. You hum it back without meaning to, and the lid clicks.

Inside, there is no key. There is a folded map drawn on something softer than paper, with a city you recognize but have never seen: its streets are the veins on the back of your hand, its rivers run uphill, and in the center is a small door marked with your name. Beneath the map lies a single brass bell. When you lift it, the whole room tilts gently, as if the floor has decided to become a sea, and you realize the bell is not meant to be rung. It is meant to be listened to, and from somewhere far off, something is listening back.
