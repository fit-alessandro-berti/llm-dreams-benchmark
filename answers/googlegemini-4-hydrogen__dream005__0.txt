...realize that every time you step near a cluster of guests, their animated chatter drops to a synchronized hum, like a radio losing its signal. 

The room itself doesn’t seem to have walls anymore. What started as a crowded living room has stretched into a cavernous hall with ceilings so high they disappear into a velvety dark mist. You weave past a circle of people dressed in damp overcoats; they are leaning over a glowing glass coffee table, earnestly trading tiny, brightly colored marbles as if they were high-stakes currency. When you ask one of them if they’ve seen your friend, they simply press a finger to their lips and hand you a warm, unpeeled tangerine, gesturing toward a doorway that wasn't there a moment ago.

You step through it and find yourself in a narrow corridor lined with bookshelves, crowded with another group—these ones all wearing identical formal attire, though several of them appear to be your old high school teachers. They are laughing hysterically at a blank painting on the wall. 

Just beyond their shoulders, you catch a sudden, sharp glimpse of your friend’s familiar jacket slipping behind a heavy velvet curtain. Relief floods through you, and you try to hurry forward, only to find that the plush carpet beneath your feet has turned into ankle-deep, slow-moving water. Every step feels heavy, as if you’re wading against a quiet tide.

You finally reach the curtain and pull it aside, stepping into what should be another room—but instead, the party vanishes entirely. You are standing on the edge of a vast, moonlit train platform. The air smells faintly of ozone and rain. Your friend is standing a few yards away under a flickering streetlamp, holding two paper cups of coffee. 

"I wondered when you'd get here," they say, turning around. 

For a split second, you feel completely grounded, until you look closer at their face and realize they have the eyes of a complete stranger—and behind them, the train pulling up to the platform isn't made of steel, it's made of drifting, illuminated ballroom windows, packed full of the same guests still looking for you.
