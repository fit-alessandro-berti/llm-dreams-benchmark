…tiny symbols etched into its surface. They seem to shift when you look away, arranging themselves into constellations you almost recognize.

You trace one with your fingertip. The box gives a soft, startled sigh.

“Finally,” it whispers.

There is no keyhole, only a small hollow shaped like a teardrop. You hold the box to your ear and hear rain falling somewhere inside it, along with the creak of a swing.

You remember that sound. It belonged to the garden of a house you lived in long ago.

“I don’t know how to open you,” you say.

“Neither do I,” says the box. “But you used to.”

You close your eyes and try to remember the garden. The crooked fence. A red bucket beneath the gutter. Someone calling you indoors before the storm.

When you open your eyes, a drop has fallen into the hollow.

The lid lifts.

Inside is a little brass key lying on wet grass. You reach for it, and your hand passes farther into the box than it should. Then your arm, then your shoulder. You climb through and find yourself standing beside the old swing.

The house is dark except for one upstairs window. A figure leans out.

“Did you find it?” they call.

You look down at the key in your palm. Behind you, the box has folded itself shut, no larger now than a walnut.

You don’t recognize the figure, but you recognize their voice.

“Yes,” you answer.

“Good. Leave it under the bucket this time.”

Rain begins to fall.
