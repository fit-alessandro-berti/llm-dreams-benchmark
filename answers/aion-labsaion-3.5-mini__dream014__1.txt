...and you feel a warmth bloom in your chest as they gather around you, their faces kind but strangely blurred, as faces are in dreams.

"We know the way," one of them says, a woman with a voice like wind chimes. "Everyone gets lost here. That's the secret of the city."

They walk with you through streets that seem to rearrange themselves behind you, buildings leaning in like curious old friends. One of the strangers hands you something — a small brass key, warm from their pocket. You don't know what it opens, but holding it makes the fear loosen its grip on you.

"Where are we going?" you ask.

"Home," they answer, though none of them looks at you when they say it.

The city begins to soften at the edges. The harsh neon signs melt into lanterns, the crowds blur into watercolor, and the noise becomes something almost like music. You realize you're no longer walking — you're floating, lifted gently by the group, carried the way you once were as a child.

And then, without warning, you recognize the street. It's the one from your childhood, the one from a house you haven't thought about in years. The porch light is on. It was always on.

The strangers set you down at the door. "You were never really lost," the woman says. "You just forgot you could ask for help."

You turn to thank them, but they're already dissolving into the lamplight, smiling, becoming part of the glow.

You reach for the door handle, the brass key trembling in your hand—

—and you wake, your pillow warm, your heart full, the feeling of being found still lingering like the last note of a song.
