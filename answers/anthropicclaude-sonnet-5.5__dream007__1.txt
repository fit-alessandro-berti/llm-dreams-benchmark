The kitchen, which was your kitchen a moment ago, is now a long wooden pier over still, silver water. The argument is still there, heavy as a coat you can't take off. But you can see its shape now. It isn't a wall between you. It's a rope, and you've both been pulling on opposite ends, each certain the other let go first.

You say, "I think I'm afraid that if I lose this, it means I don't matter to you."

The boats go quiet. Your friend's face changes the way faces do in dreams, older and younger at once, the face from every year you've known each other. "I was afraid of the same thing," they say. "That's why I kept getting louder."

The water begins to glow faintly under the pier. One of the paper boats unfolds itself into a map, though neither of you can read it. The disagreement is still unsolved. The issue hasn't vanished, and you both know it will be waiting when you wake up. But now it sits between you like a table you can put things on, instead of a fire.

Your friend holds out their end of the rope. You look at it, then at them, and you **set your end down, not as surrender but as an offering, and ask them to sit with you while you figure out the knot together.**

Somewhere far off, an alarm clock starts to ring like a distant bell buoy. The pier begins to fold itself up, plank by plank, into paper. Your friend is still smiling as you wake.
