As you climb, the glass doesn't break under your weight—it *sings*, resonating with the frequency of your hesitation. You realize you must climb not by believing you're qualified, but by accepting that you are becoming qualified with every step, the glass growing stronger beneath your feet like ice thickening under a skater.

Then the competition shifts. You're in a library where the books are all the versions of yourself you could have been, and you must find the one volume that contains your future. But every time you pull a book from the shelf, it opens to a blank page except for one sentence: "Turn around." When you finally do, you see that the other competitors aren't rivals—they're echoes of your own potential, and they're holding up mirrors. In the reflection, you don't see yourself winning. You see yourself already there, working, laughing, being exactly who you hoped to become. The prize isn't the job; it's the recognition that you've been carrying the winner inside you all along.

The final challenge materializes as a door floating in an endless white void. On it is a sign that reads "Grand Prize: Your Dream Job," but the letters keep shifting, rearranging themselves into different job titles, then into your name, then into the words *Whatever you need right now*. As you reach for the handle, the door opens from the other side.

A hand reaches out—not to shake yours, but to high-five it, as if to say *welcome to the team*. And behind that hand, you glimpse not an office or a corner suite, but a vast, sunlit room filled with tools and materials and people who look like friends, all working on projects that make your heart ache with joy.

You step through, and the door closes gently behind you. For a moment, you're afraid you've lost the competition. But then you look down, and you're wearing the badge. The lanyard is warm against your chest. The work begins.

And somewhere, an alarm clock is ringing, but you're already awake.
