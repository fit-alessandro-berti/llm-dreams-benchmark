...and you follow them, though none of them asks where you're going. They seem to know already.

The streets rearrange themselves as you walk. Skyscrapers lean aside like tall grass, and the roar of traffic softens into something close to the sound of the ocean. One of the strangers, an old woman in a coat the color of streetlights, presses a warm paper cup into your hands. It's a drink you haven't tasted since childhood, though you can't remember its name.

"You were never really lost," says a man carrying a folded map. When he opens it, it shows no streets at all, only memories: your grandmother's kitchen, a tree you once climbed, a window you used to look out of when it rained. "You just forgot which way you were facing."

The group leads you around a corner that wasn't there a moment ago. The crowds thin. The city grows quieter and softer at the edges, like a watercolor left out in the mist. You notice the strangers' faces keep shifting, and each one is briefly someone you've loved, or someone who was kind to you once.

At the end of the street stands a door you recognize, though you've never seen it before. A light is on inside.

"This is where we leave you," the old woman says, smiling. "But you know the way from here."

You turn to thank them, but there's only the gentle hum of the city behind you, and it sounds like a lullaby now. You reach for the door handle. It's warm, as if someone had been holding it just before you.

You step through, and for the first time in the whole dream, you know exactly where you are.
