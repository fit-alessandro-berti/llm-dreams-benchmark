

…find the river before the bell tower rings three times.

The air smells of woodsmoke, wet stone, and something faintly sweet, like apples left too long in the sun. Your bare feet are cold on cobblestones that have been polished smooth by centuries of footsteps. There are no wires, no screens, no hum of electricity—only the distant clatter of a cart, the cry of a gull, and the sound of water moving somewhere beyond the edge of the market square.

You do not know what year this is. You do not know what country. The signs above the shops are written in a language that almost makes sense, the way a word can look familiar even when you have never seen it before. People glance at you once, then quickly look away, as if they have seen someone who has wandered out of the wrong time.

You need water. You need rest. You need to understand how long “a night” lasts in this place, because the sun seems to move strangely across the sky, as though it is remembering how to set rather than doing it on purpose.

As you pass a fountain carved with a face that looks almost human, you realize the dream is changing. The era around you is not fixed. The market stalls are becoming medieval, then colonial, then ancient, then strange enough that they no longer belong to any history you remember. The people are not afraid of you anymore. They are waiting for you.

One of them steps forward—an old woman with a lantern in her hand, though it is still daylight. She says, in a voice like dry leaves, “You cannot leave until you give something away.”

You reach into your pocket and find a phone. It is dead, its screen dark. You hand it to her.

She smiles, and the phone turns into a seed.

The woman says, “Then plant the year you came from.”

You look down. The cobblestones have cracked, and beneath them is soil.
