A small door opens in its trunk. Inside, you find a round room lit by jars of lightning. A fox in a green coat is hurriedly closing the windows.

“Quick,” it says. “The storm has lost its way, and now it’s frightened.”

Before you can answer, thunder shakes the walls. Rain lashes the oak, and through the window you see the clouds twisting into a vast, dark shape with no eyes and too many wings. Somehow, you understand that it is searching for something—but the harder it searches, the more lost it becomes.

You open the door.

The fox cries out, but you step into the rain and lift your hands. The wind tears at your clothes, yet you feel no fear. You begin to hum a tune you have never heard before, though some part of you has always known it. One by one, the trees join in. Their leaves whisper the melody, and the whole forest shimmers with sound.

The storm grows still.

For a moment, it hangs above you like an enormous listening animal. Then the clouds unfold, revealing a sky crowded with stars—even though it is midday. A single drop of rain lands in your palm, warm and golden.

When you look up, the fox is standing beside you.

“There,” it says softly. “It remembers.”

The forest fills with sunlight, and you wake to rain tapping gently against your window. On the sill lies a single silver leaf.
