First, you must build a bridge from things you’ve forgotten. You reach into your pockets and find the name of your first teacher, the smell of a long-closed bakery, and a song you once knew by heart. Somehow, they hold your weight.

Next, the judges ask you to sell them an umbrella during a rainstorm. You open it above the smallest judge instead. They say nothing, but mark something on their clipboard.

By the final round, only you remain. You enter an office at the top of a tower, where a brass plaque reads: **YOUR DREAM JOB**.

Behind the desk sits your younger self, wearing a suit several sizes too large.

“Well?” they ask. “What is it?”

You open your mouth, but all the titles you’ve rehearsed vanish. Outside the window, the city rearranges itself: a library becomes a garden, a hospital becomes a theater, and people drift between them carrying armfuls of light.

“I think,” you say, “I want to make something useful. And I want to enjoy making it.”

Your younger self considers this, then slides an employment contract across the desk. It’s blank except for one line:

**Start whenever you’re ready.**

You pick up the pen. It turns into a key. The office door unlocks behind you, and beyond it you hear the ordinary sounds of morning.
