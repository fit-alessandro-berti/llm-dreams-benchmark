follow them down a narrow alley where the roar of traffic fades to the sound of rain, though the sky is clear.

At the end of the alley, a woman unfolds a map across the hood of a parked car. The streets wriggle like threads. “Where were you trying to go?” she asks.

You can’t remember. You’re embarrassed to say so, but she just folds the map and says, “Well, we were going to get dumplings.”

There’s a place around the corner with fogged-up windows. Someone pulls up an extra chair, and you spend the rest of the evening trying to learn how to pick up a slippery dumpling without breaking it. Every time yours falls, the man across from you drops his on purpose.

When you wake, you can’t recall a single face. But for a moment, your room feels like a place someone has just stepped out of, and will be back to soon.
