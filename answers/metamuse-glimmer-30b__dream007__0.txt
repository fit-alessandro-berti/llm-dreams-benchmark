You are in the middle of a heated argument with a close friend. The disagreement is about something important to both of you, and you decide to stop talking.

You just close your mouth mid-sentence. Your friend keeps going, louder, and you can see the words coming out of them as actual things — small, sharp stones that clatter to the floor between you. You don’t answer. You stand up.

The room you’re arguing in is suddenly too small. The ceiling is low and the walls are breathing in time with your friend’s voice. You decide to leave.

You walk toward the door, but the hallway you expected isn’t there. Instead there’s a kitchen from your childhood, and the table is covered in the argument stones. Your friend is still standing in the doorway behind you, now also made of the same light as the kitchen, flickering.

You decide to pick one of the stones up. It’s warm. It’s the word you were about to say. You put it in your pocket.

And then the floor tilts. The kitchen spins and becomes an elevator. Your friend is still talking, but you can’t hear the words anymore, only the hum. You press the button for the floor you’ve never been to before, and the doors close on the argument still happening on the other side.

You wake up before you get there, with the stone still in your pocket.
