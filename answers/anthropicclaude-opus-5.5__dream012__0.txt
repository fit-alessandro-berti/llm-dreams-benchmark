...and you open your mouth to give the order, but what comes out is a single note, low and long, like a whale singing through fog.

The crew doesn't seem surprised. They nod as if this were standard procedure. The first mate, who has your grandmother's face and wears a coat made of old maps, turns the wheel hard to port. "Aye, Captain," she says. "Toward the lighthouse."

You look where she's pointing. There was no lighthouse a moment ago, but now there is one, standing impossibly in the open sea, its beam sweeping slowly across the waves. Each time the light touches the water, the waves freeze for an instant into glass sculptures: horses, staircases, hands reaching upward. Then they collapse and roar again.

The rain is falling upward now. You notice this calmly, the way you notice everything here. Droplets rise from the deck and drift toward the clouds, and the storm seems to be drinking itself back into the sky.

"Captain," calls a young sailor from the rigging. You know him, though you can't remember from where. Maybe a classroom, or a train platform years ago. "The compass is pointing at you."

You pull the brass compass from your pocket. The needle spins, then settles, pointing directly at your chest. You understand, with the total certainty that only exists in dreams, that the ship has always been steering toward you, that every voyage was just a long way of coming home.

A wave rises ahead, taller than any mountain, dark green and threaded with light. The crew goes silent. Everyone is watching you.

You don't give an order this time. You walk to the bow, place your palm against the rail, and the ship begins to climb, not over the wave but *into* it, sliding up its face as though the sea had tilted into a hillside. Inside the wave it's quiet, and bright, and full of drifting fish that glow like paper lanterns. Your crew laughs in disbelief. Someone starts to sing the same low note you sang earlier, and the others join in.

The ship breaks through the top of the wave, and there is no storm on the other side. There's only a still, silver sea under a sky full of more stars than you've ever seen, and the lighthouse, very close now, its door standing open, a warm yellow light spilling out across the water.

Your grandmother-first-mate touches your shoulder. "You can go in," she says. "We'll wait."

You step off the ship. The water holds you like a floor. You walk toward the open door, and just as you reach the threshold, just as you catch the smell of bread baking and hear someone inside saying your name...

...you wake up, the sound of the sea still fading in your ears.
