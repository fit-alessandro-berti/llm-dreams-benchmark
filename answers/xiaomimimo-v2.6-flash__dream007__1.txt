Your friend's voice follows you, but the words have become muffled, like hearing someone speak underwater. You look down at your hands and notice you're holding a glass full of confetti — scraps of old messages, ticket stubs, and Polaroids — all swirling as if caught in a tiny storm.

You turn a corner and find yourself in your childhood bedroom, but everything is outsized. The desk is as tall as a building, and a familiar stuffed animal sits on the bed with glowing eyes, watching you patiently. You feel an overwhelming urge to climb, and somehow you do — pulling yourself up effortlessly, defying gravity.

At the top, you find a window overlooking a vast ocean that is, impossibly, dry. The water has turned to glass, and people are walking across it in every direction, all holding the other end of strings tied to things you can't quite see above the clouds.

You hear your friend again — not arguing now, but *laughing* — and when you look back down the hallway, the photographs have sharpened. One shows the two of you, smiling, on a day you can almost remember.

Then the hallway folds in on itself like paper, and you're back where you started — mid-argument — except now you understand *why* you're upset. Not for the reason you thought. The realization feels enormous, almost too big to hold.

You open your mouth to say something, and the dream ends before you get the words out.
