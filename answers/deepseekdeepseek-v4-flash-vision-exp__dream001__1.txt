You start to run. The streets are slick and silver, like the city was poured from moonlight. Every corner you turn looks the same—towering glass, twisting alleys, signs in a language you almost recognize. The clock on the nearest tower is melting, its hands dripping like honey.

You check your phone. No signal. Just a single notification, pulsing: *You’re already late.*

A woman with a birdcage for a head glides past. Through the bars, a small finch taps its beak against a rolled-up map. You ask her for directions. She tilts her cage. "The meeting is where the city forgets," she says, and points to the ground.

You look down. The pavement is covered in faint chalk outlines of footsteps, leading into a tunnel that shouldn’t be there. You follow them, heart pounding. The tunnel smells like rain and old paper. At the end, a door shaped like an eye opens, and inside, a round table sits under a thousand drifting lanterns.

Everyone is already seated. Your chair is empty. The meeting has just begun—and you know, somehow, that you were the one who called it.
