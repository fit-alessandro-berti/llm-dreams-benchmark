You try to explain that the deadline is impossible, but the words come out as paperclips, clinking softly from your mouth onto the desk. Your boss doesn’t seem annoyed. He or she—or maybe a large, friendly office chair wearing a tie—only nods and points to a clock on the wall that is melting into a puddle of numbers.

You sit down to begin, but the chair unfolds into a hallway. The hallway stretches through calendars, each page showing tomorrow instead of yesterday. At the far end, a printer is printing your face, page after page after page, and each copy is already working.

You open your mouth to ask for help, but your voice is a folder. You click it. Inside, instead of documents, there is a tiny version of your workplace, and in that workplace, a smaller you is being given the same impossible deadline.

The dream tightens around your ears.

You pick up the phone to call for backup, but the receiver blooms into a flower. A line of fluorescent lights begins to rain upward into the ceiling. Somewhere, a printer finishes your face and hands it to a stranger who looks exactly like your future self.

You try to stand up, but the office is already folding into an envelope.

And just before it seals itself around you, you remember: the project was never the deadline. The project was the waiting.
