"Hold her steady!" you shout over the howl of the wind. "We don't fight the sea—we ride her!"

You turn the wheel hard to starboard, catching the next wave at an angle. The hull groans, but rises. Water crashes over the deck, and for a moment the world is white and cold and roaring. Then the ship breaks free, tilting up the slope of a towering swell.

"All hands, brace!" you bellow. The crew moves as one, lines tight, sails trimmed. The ship climbs, shudders, and then—like a beast taking flight—surges over the crest. The storm rages on, but you feel it: the rhythm, the give, the secret language of the sea.

You smile, salt crusting your beard. "There, my friends. That's how we dance."

The crew responds with a ragged cheer. The lightning flashes, and in that brief blue light, you see their faces—fear turning to trust. You turn back to the horizon, where the storm is breaking, and a thin line of gold is beginning to show.

"Steady," you say quietly. "We're almost through."

And the ship sails on, carrying you all toward the dawn.
