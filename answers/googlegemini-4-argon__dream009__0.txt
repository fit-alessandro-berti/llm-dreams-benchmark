Looking down, my digital watch is melting anyway, its liquid crystal numbers dripping off my wrist like black ink and turning into beetles as they hit the cobblestone. I am standing in a sprawling marketplace made entirely of petrified wood and giant, hollowed-out gourds. Around me, people dressed in woven moss and hammered copper are floating slightly above the ground, tethered to the earth by heavy iron boots.

There is no electricity, no engines, no screens. Instead, gigantic glass windmills spin slowly in the breeze, not grinding grain, but playing a low, resonant cello music that seems to keep the buildings from floating away. 

Suddenly, a tall merchant with eyes like polished amber grabs my shoulder. "Your lungs are still calibrated to the twenty-first century," she whispers urgently, though her voice comes from a wooden bird perched on her shoulder, not her lips. "The Great Pendulum is about to swing back. If you don't wind the Clockwork Banyan tree at the center of the bazaar before the twin suns eclipse, you'll wake up before you remember where you hid the key."

I reach into my pocket, where my smartphone used to be, and pull out a heavy key made of warm ice. It’s already melting in my palm. I start to run toward the center of the city, where a massive tree made of spinning bronze gears reaches into the clouds, its leaves ticking in a deafening, rhythmic countdown—though in standard dream fashion, my legs feel as though I am wading through chest-deep honey...
