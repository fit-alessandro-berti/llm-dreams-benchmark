...find the woman with eyes like cracked amber who left you a key in a previous dream, before you wake up and forget her name again.

The cobblestones beneath your bare feet are warm—impossibly, feverishly warm, like living skin. You're wearing clothes that don't belong to you: rough linen trousers, a woolen shirt that smells of woodsmoke and something else, something floral that shouldn't exist in this season. The buildings lean together overhead, their thatched roofs whispering secrets in a language that tickles the back of your throat. You can almost understand it. Almost.

A market square opens before you, but the goods are wrong. Glass orbs containing captured sunsets. Books with pages made of pressed autumn leaves. Birds wearing tiny brass bells instead of singing, their wings chiming like wind chimes. A vendor notices you staring and smiles with too many teeth, offering you a fruit that steams in the cold air.

You check your pockets. The key is there—cold, heavy, shaped like a question mark.

You know, with the absolute certainty of dreams, that if you find her before the church bells ring (though you cannot see a church, and you are not sure bells still exist in this place), you will remember why you were sent here. Or perhaps you were never sent. Perhaps you have always been here, and the modern world was the fleeting dream you woke up from just moments ago.

The square begins to rotate, slowly, like a music box winding down. You start running toward the horizon, but your feet don't quite touch the ground. You are flying, or falling, or both at once, toward the edge of the world where the sky bruises purple and gold and the woman with amber eyes is waiting, holding a door that only opens inward...
