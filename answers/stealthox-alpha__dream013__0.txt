...you notice something impossible: the walls are moving. Slowly, almost imperceptibly, the corridors rearrange themselves behind you, sealing off the path you came from. The maze isn't just testing you — it's watching.

The next chamber holds a door with no handle, only a mosaic of colored tiles. Each one hums softly when touched, and you realize — somehow you just *know* — that they must be pressed in the order of a melody you've never heard, yet somehow remember. Your fingers move almost on their own. The door dissolves into mist.

Deeper still, the puzzles grow stranger. In one room, a chessboard plays itself against a shadow version of you, and the only way forward is to teach your darker self how to lose gracefully. In another, a staircase spirals upward and downward at once, and the way through is to stop insisting on which direction is "up" and simply walk.

Time behaves oddly here. A grandfather clock in one corridor ticks backward, and with each reverse tick, the earlier puzzles make more sense — as if the maze is letting you solve them again in reverse, peeling back your own logic layer by layer.

At last, you reach the center: a small round chamber with a single mirror. In the reflection you see yourself — but your reflection is awake, watching you with quiet amusement. Etched into the glass are the words:

*You have solved every puzzle except the one you are inside of.*

You reach out. The glass ripples like water. And just as your fingertips break the surface — just as you begin to understand what the maze was made of, and why it was built from your own thoughts —

you wake up.

The dream fades fast, the way dreams do. But one detail lingers through the morning: the faint, cool feeling of stone beneath your fingertips, and the odd certainty that somewhere, the maze is still there — patient, shifting — waiting for you to fall asleep and find your way back.
