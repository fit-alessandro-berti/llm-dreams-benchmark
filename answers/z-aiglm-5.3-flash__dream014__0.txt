…and you follow them through crowds that part like water. One of them, an older woman with silver hair, takes your hand as if she's known you for years—though you're certain you've never seen her face before. And yet, somewhere underneath, you know you have.

They lead you through an alley that shouldn't exist, between two buildings that weren't standing a moment ago. On the other side, the city is different—quieter, washed in golden light, even though noon had only just passed. In the way of dreams, nobody questions this. Not even you.

"What are you looking for?" the woman asks.

You open your mouth to name a street, a destination, anything—but instead you hear yourself say, "I don't remember anymore." Strangely, saying it feels like setting down a heavy bag you'd forgotten you were carrying.

The others laugh softly, like wind chimes, and someone presses warm bread into your hands from out of nowhere. You eat as you walk, and the city keeps shifting around you—streets becoming rivers becoming the hallway of your childhood home, then streets again—but they remain beside you through all of it.

Eventually you reach a small square with a fountain at its center, and there's a bench with a space exactly your size already waiting. You sit. They gather around—not hovering, just *near*, the way a fire is near a cold room.

"I think I know my way now," you say.

And you do. But no one leaves quite yet. The fountain runs backward. A pigeon wearing a tiny hat regards you solemnly. Somewhere, very far away, an alarm clock begins to hum—

—and you wake with the lingering, unshakable certainty that kindness was always going to find you.
