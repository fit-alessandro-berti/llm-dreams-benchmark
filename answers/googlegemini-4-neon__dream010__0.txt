At first, the competition looks like a normal, high-stakes interview inside a towering glass skyscraper. You are wearing your sharpest suit, though when you glance down, you realize you are barefoot on the plush carpet of your childhood hallway. Nobody seems to notice, so you keep walking. You don't even know the exact title of the dream job anymore—its name slips off your tongue like water every time you try to think of it—but you know in your bones that it is the only thing you were ever meant to do.

The first challenge is a presentation in an auditorium that stretches endlessly upward into a starry sky. When your name is called, you step up to the podium and open your portfolio, only to find that your notes have turned into crisp autumn leaves. A sudden gust of wind rips through the room, scattering them into the rafters. Panic spikes in your chest, hot and sharp, until you realize you can read the veins of the leaves as they drift past. You start speaking, catching them out of the air one by one, and with every sentence you speak, the heavy anxiety in your stomach turns into a strange, buoyant melody. The audience—hundreds of blurred faces, including your fourth-grade teacher and someone you’ve never met but deeply trust—nods in quiet approval.

By the semifinal round, only you and one other contestant remain. The rival is impossibly composed, wearing a coat stitched from polished mirrors that reflects the shifting hallways around you. The skyscraper has now opened into a sprawling, sunlit train station flooded with ankle-deep, crystal-clear water. 

"Round four," announces a voice over the intercom that sounds like wind chimes. "Assemble the morning."

In front of you sits an empty brass suitcase. Beside you, your rival is already scooping shimmering handfuls of light off the water's surface and locking them away with practiced ease. You try to copy them, but the light slips through your fingers. Your heart hammers. You check your watch, and the hands are spinning backward, melting into the glass. 

Then you stop trying to grab the light. Instead, you sit down in the warm water, open the suitcase, and let the sound of a distant train whistle drift inside. You fold in the smell of rain on hot pavement, the quiet hum of a refrigerator at midnight, and a stray memory of laughing until your ribs hurt. When you snap the brass latches shut, the suitcase feels miraculously heavy, glowing softly along its seams.

The train station dissolves into a quiet, glass-walled greenhouse suspended high above a swirling sea of clouds. Three tall judges sit behind a desk carved from a single slab of glacier ice. Your rival’s suitcase is opened first; it releases a blinding, perfect white glare, sharp and cold. The judges shield their eyes and murmur politely.

When you place yours on the ice, you don't even have to unlock it. The lid lifts on its own, and a warm breeze spills out, smelling of pine needles and fresh coffee. The glacier desk softens, tiny green shoots blooming along its edges. 

The judges stand. Their applause sounds like heavy summer rain hitting a tin roof. 

"The position is yours," says the center judge, stepping forward. As they hand you a heavy golden key with no teeth—just a smooth, warm rod of light—you realize their voice sounds exactly like your own.

They gesture to a tall mahogany door standing completely freestanding in the middle of the clouds. Heart swelling with an overwhelming rush of relief and purpose, you fit the key into the lock and push it open. Beyond the threshold isn't an office at all, just an endless horizon of twilight ocean, glowing lanterns bobbing gently on the waves, and a single wooden desk floating peacefully on the water, waiting just for you. 

You take a deep breath and step across the threshold to begin your first day—just as a distant, rhythmic beeping begins to pull the twilight sky apart into pale morning light.
