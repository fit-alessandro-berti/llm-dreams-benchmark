Suddenly you realize you aren't wearing shoes, and the wooden stage beneath your feet has turned into cool, damp moss. You check your pockets for your real speech, pulling out a handful of glass marbles and a house key you haven't seen since you were nine. When you look back up, the audience is standing in unison, not to leave, but to fold themselves neatly into paper airplanes. One by one, they launch silently toward the open sky until the entire hall is empty—except for someone from your high school sitting in the very last row, holding up a small cardboard sign that simply reads: *You forgot the second verse.*

Before you can explain that it wasn't supposed to be a song, the floor tilts gently sideways, and you start sliding off the edge of the stage into a warm, shallow ocean, still clutching your notes.
