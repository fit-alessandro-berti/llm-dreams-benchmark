Night after night, beneath a sky bruised with static and ash, you venture into the skeletal remains of old laboratories, collapsed libraries, and forgotten basements. You aren't hunting for weapons or fuel, but for seeds preserved in glass vials, forgotten books on botany, and shards of intact glass. 

In the dream, time bends. What should take years happens in the space of a few quiet breaths. You patch the colossal glass dome with mismatched panes of colored glass—stained blues, ambers, and greens salvaged from old cathedrals. When the morning sun cuts through the toxic haze, it doesn't bring scorching heat; it filters down into the rotunda as soft, cathedral light.

You pipe water from deep subterranean aquifers, filtering the rust and dust until it runs clear over moss-covered stones. You plant night-blooming jasmine, hardy mountain pines, heirloom tomatoes, and wild ferns. 

Then, you do the most dangerous thing of all in a world ruled by fear: you open the heavy iron doors and leave them wide. 

You string solar-powered lanterns along the shattered boulevards leading to the dome, glowing like a trail of fallen stars. Soon, shadows begin to emerge from the ruins—ragged wanderers, wary scavengers, and frightened families drawn by the scent of damp earth and blooming citrus carried on the wind. 

Instead of raising weapons, you hand them fresh water and ripe fruit. As they step inside, the silence of the wasteland is broken by the sound of flowing water and the collective, quiet exhale of people remembering what it feels like to be safe. 

Standing in the center of the blooming canopy, looking up at the patchwork glass reflecting the dawn, you realize that the world didn't end—it was just waiting for someone to plant the first root.
