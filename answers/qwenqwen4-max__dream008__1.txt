…and you reach for the keyboard, but the keys have been replaced with small white teeth, and each one, when pressed, produces a word instead of a letter: *perhaps. almost. again.*

The project is a single sheet of paper. At the top it says COMPLETE THIS. Below that, in your own handwriting, is everything you were going to write, including the part where you explain why you couldn't.

You check the clock. Its hands are soft, drooping like cooked noodles, and the second hand only moves when you aren't looking at it — so you keep looking away, and the office fills with the sound of time passing behind your back.

Your boss hasn't blinked once. He's wearing your father's coat. When you try to say his name, what comes out is the name of a town you drove through at night, years ago, without stopping.

"It's a small task," he says, kindly, and the kindness is the worst part.

So you begin. The work turns out to be alphabetizing the ocean. You're doing well — you're nearly at H — when you notice that the woman at the next desk has been dead for six years and is still, patiently, formatting a spreadsheet. She doesn't look up. She says, "The trick is not to check the time," and you understand that this is the only useful advice anyone will ever give you.

The hallway to the break room is longer than the building. You walk it anyway, because in the break room there's a window, and through the window is your childhood kitchen, and your mother is standing at the sink, and she is also waiting for you to start.

When you get back, the page is finished. It has your name on it. It has a date, and the date is tomorrow, and it has always been tomorrow.

Your boss puts a hand on your shoulder — heavier than a hand should be, warm as a parked car. Somewhere a fire alarm starts up, shrill and insistent and strangely familiar, and everyone in the office rises calmly and puts on their coats, and you understand that the alarm is your bedroom, that it has been ringing for some time now, that the impossible deadline was only ever this: to wake up, to wake up —

and you can't, you can't, you are still typing *perhaps, almost, again.*
