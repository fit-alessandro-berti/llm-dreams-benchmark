...realize the architecture of the room is making no sense. It started as a normal apartment, perhaps a loft, with exposed brick and warm lighting, but as I push past the first cluster of strangers, the floorboards soften into deep, emerald-green moss. 

The first group I pass is standing in a tight circle. They are all wearing immaculate vintage formalwear, except they have no faces—just smooth, porcelain skin where their features should be. They are clinking champagne glasses together, and instead of a chime, the glasses make the sound of distant thunder. I try to ask them if they’ve seen my friend, Alex, but when I open my mouth, only a cloud of small, iridescent moths flutters out. Nobody seems to notice or care; they just keep clinking their glasses.

I press on, feeling that heavy, urgent anxiety unique to dreams—the absolute certainty that if I don't find Alex soon, something vital will be lost. 

The room opens up into a grand ballroom where the ceiling is missing, revealing a sky filled with two moons and swirling, violet nebulae. Here, I encounter a second group. They are sitting on floating velvet couches, speaking in reverse. As I walk beneath them, one of them—a woman wearing a dress made entirely of cascading water—points downward. I look at my feet and realize I’m not wearing shoes, and the moss has turned into shallow, warm ocean water rolling over tiled floors.

Then, through a forest of marble pillars that definitely weren't there a second ago, I see it: Alex’s yellow jacket. 

Relief washes over me. I try to run toward the jacket, capability of movement suddenly turning to lead. The air becomes thick like gelatin. I pull myself forward using the marble pillars, which are now soft and warm like candle wax, melting under my grip. The chatter of the party morphs into the rhythmic ticking of a massive clock. 

I finally reach the figure in the yellow jacket. I grab their shoulder and spin them around. 

It isn’t Alex. It’s me. 

My double looks at me with wide, terrified eyes, presses a brass key into my palm, and whispers, *"You're at the wrong party. Wake up."* 

The floor drops out from beneath me, the violet sky rushes upward, and—
