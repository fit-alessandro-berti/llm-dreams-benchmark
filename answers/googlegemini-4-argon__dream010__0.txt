You are participating in a competition where the grand prize is your dream job. As the competition progresses, you face various challenges and **realize that the laws of physics—and corporate standards—do not apply here.** 

At first, the setting looks like a sleek, glass-walled skyscraper, the kind where important people make important decisions. You are wearing your sharpest outfit, feeling prepared. Your competitors are a blur of intimidatingly polished figures, though when you look closely at the person to your left, you realize it’s actually your third-grade math teacher, and to your right is a golden retriever in a three-piece suit. Nobody finds this strange.

A voice booms from the ceiling—the voice of the Hiring Manager, which sounds suspiciously like thunder mixed with the hum of a fluorescent light. "Welcome to Round One: The Pressure Test."

Instantly, the plush carpet beneath your feet dissolves into rushing water. The cubicles detach from the floor and become tiny, floating rafts. Your laptop is now an oar, and the deadline you are chasing is a literal tidal wave made of glowing calendar invites. While your competitors panic—the golden retriever simply jumps in to go for a swim—you instinctively start paddling with the keyboard. With every keystroke, you surge forward, riding the crest of the wave until it deposits you safely onto the 85th floor.

You don’t even have time to dry off (in fact, you are already dry) before Round Two begins: "The Presentation."

You are pushed onto a velvet-curtained stage. The audience is a sea of shadowy figures holding glowing clipboards. You open your mouth to pitch your brilliant fifty-point plan, a speech you’ve rehearsed a thousand times. Instead of words, a flock of iridescent origami birds flies out of your mouth. 

Panic spikes in your chest. You try to speak again, and this time, music pours out—a complex, soaring symphony. You think you’ve failed, that you’ve forgotten how to speak entirely. You look down at your notes, and the ink is sliding off the page like ants. So, you stop fighting the dream logic. You raise your hands like a conductor and begin directing the music and the paper birds. They swirl together, forming three-dimensional shapes in the air that somehow, impossibly, explain your exact vision for the role. The shadowy audience gasps, their clipboards turning bright green in approval. 

Suddenly, the stage drops away. Everyone else is gone. It is the Final Round.

You are standing in a vast, quiet room made entirely of clouds and polished mahogany. At the far end sits a single desk. Behind it is a figure shrouded in blinding light. 

"To claim the prize," the figure says, "you must sign the contract. Tell us what you want."

You walk toward the desk, though the floor seems to stretch, making the walk feel like miles. You finally reach it. Resting on the wood is a contract written on a shifting, mirror-like surface, and beside it, a pen that feels as heavy as a sword. 

You look into the mirror-contract to see the title of the dream job you’ve been fighting so hard for. The letters swirl, shifting from *CEO* to *Astronaut* to *Architect of the Stars*, before finally settling into a simple, glowing phrase: *The Architect of Your Own Time.* 

You pick up the heavy pen. You realize the competition wasn't against the other people at all; it was just a test to see if you could trust yourself when the world stopped making sense. 

Just as the nib of the pen touches the glass paper, a brilliant flash of golden light erupts from the desk. The walls of the office dissolve into the morning sky, the booming voice of the Hiring Manager morphs into the beeping of your bedside alarm, and you wake up—heart pounding, holding onto the strange, lingering certainty that you got the job.
