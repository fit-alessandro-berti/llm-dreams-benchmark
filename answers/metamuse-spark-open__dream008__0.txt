and you reach down to open the project file, but your keyboard has no letters.

Only smooth, blank keys. Warm to the touch, like little teeth.

Your boss is still standing there, smiling too patiently. Only now you notice your boss isn't exactly your boss — it's your old middle-school math teacher wearing your boss's suit, holding a coffee mug that says WORLD'S BEST BOSS in dripping red letters.

"Just need it by 5," he says. You glance at the clock on the wall. It says 4:59. Then 4:98. Then it melts a little down the wall and goes *tick*.

You try to say, "That's impossible," but your voice comes out as the office printer's startup whine. No one notices. Everyone else in the office is typing furiously, their fingers blurring. You look closer — their desks extend infinitely down a hallway that wasn't there before, getting smaller and smaller until the furthest workers are the size of ants. Their monitors all show the same thing: a single, blinking document that just says YOUR NAME HERE.

You look back at your own screen, and the project has finally loaded. It's not a spreadsheet. It's a miniature version of your office, and inside that tiny office, there's a tiny you, staring at a tiny screen. The tiny you looks up at you and waves, panicked.

"Need any help getting started?" your boss-teacher asks, leaning so close you can smell old chalk dust from his suit.

You look down at your hands to type again, and your fingers pass right through the keys like water. The floor under your desk has quietly turned to soft, white sand. The fluorescent lights above are flickering like a beach sun through palm leaves. You can hear waves.

And then you understand, with perfect dream-logic: You don't have to finish the project. You just have to remember you don't actually work here.

You stand up. Your chair squeaks and turns into a beach chair. You step away from the desk, and the sand is warm.

Behind you, the impossible clock finally stops ticking.
