At the market, you ask a woman selling pears whose face is on the coins. She turns one over in her palm.

“Yours,” she says. “Though they’ve got the nose wrong.”

Before you can answer, bells begin to ring. The woman pulls a clean apron over her head, the butcher takes off his hat, and everyone turns toward the river.

A boat comes into view, moving upstream without oars. At its bow stands an old woman in your pajamas. She has your face, too, though hers is creased around the mouth in a way that makes you think you’ll laugh a great deal, given time.

“You’re early,” she calls.

“For what?”

She holds up a pear with one bite taken out of it. You look down. The pear the woman slipped into your hand has a bite missing from exactly the same place.

“Never mind,” the old woman says. “You’ve already done it.”

The bells stop. For a moment you can hear the river licking the stones. Then the woman at the stall asks if you’re going to pay for that, and you discover, with immense relief, that you have pockets.
