The crew scrambles up the rigging, silhouettes thrown against a sky the color of bruised iron. Rain lashes sideways, stinging your face like needles. The ship groans beneath you, a living thing in agony, timber flexing and complaining with every mountain of water that rises beneath the hull.

"Captain!" the first mate shouts, pointing starboard. "She won't hold much longer!"

You grip the wheel, your knuckles white, and fight the sea for control. The wheel bucks in your hands like a wild animal. Wave after wave slams against the bow, and each time, you feel the ship hesitate—almost stop—before clawing forward again through the black water.

And then, strangely—as happens in dreams—the storm begins to change. The rain doesn't fall anymore; it falls *upward*, droplets rising past your face and vanishing into the clouds. The waves freeze mid-crash, towering walls of glassy water glittering in sudden, impossible sunlight. Your crew stands motionless on the deck, staring up, their expressions calm now, almost peaceful.

"Captain," the first mate says quietly, without turning his head. "The compass is pointing down."

You look at the compass in your pocket. The needle spins lazily, then settles, trembling, pointing straight through the deck, into the deep.

The sea beneath the ship has gone utterly still and transparent, and far below—immeasurably far—you see a light. Not fire, not lightning. Something softer. Something that seems to know your name.

You feel no fear, only the strange certainty of dreams. You turn the wheel, and the ship begins to descend—not sinking, but *sailing* downward, into the silent blue, past frozen waves and drifting fish that glow like lanterns, while the crew hums a shanty in a language you somehow understand.

The light grows brighter.

And just before you reach it—

—you wake, with the taste of salt still on your lips and your heart pounding like surf against a hull.
