"Captain!" your first mate shouts over the wind. She has your grandmother's face, though you're certain she didn't a moment ago. "What heading?"

You open your mouth to answer, and what comes out is not words but a single low note, like a cello. The crew nods as if this makes perfect sense. They scatter to their posts, hauling ropes that seem to stretch down into the water rather than up to the sails.

A wave rises before the bow, impossibly tall, and inside it, as if behind green glass, you see lights. Windows. A whole city is suspended in the swell, its streetlamps glowing, tiny figures moving along its avenues. One of them stops and looks up at you.

You realize, with the calm certainty that only dreams allow, that the storm is not something to escape. It is a door.

"Hold steady," you say, and this time the words come out right. The compass in your palm spins once and then points not north but down. You turn the wheel into the wave.

The ship tilts. The crew begins to sing something you almost recognize, a lullaby from before you could speak. The water closes over the bow without a splash, without cold, and you find you can breathe just fine. The sails fill with current instead of wind.

Below you, the city grows closer. Its bells are ringing. Somehow you know they are ringing for you, that they have been expecting this ship for a very long time.

Your first mate takes your arm and smiles. "You remembered the way," she says.

And the strangest part is, you did.
