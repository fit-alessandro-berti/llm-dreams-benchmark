You start to run.

But the city doesn’t cooperate. Streets unspool behind you, doubling back on themselves. Street signs flicker between languages you almost understand. Your phone says 9:00, then 9:47, then 8:13. The map app shows only a blue dot trembling in a sea of gray, and beneath it: *You are already here.*

You flag a taxi. The driver has no face, only a kind voice. “Where to?” he asks. You say the name of the meeting, though you don’t remember learning it. He nods and drives through a tunnel that smells like rain and old paper. Outside, buildings lean in like eavesdroppers. Every window reflects you, but slightly older, slightly younger, all of them late.

The taxi stops before a door with no building attached. You pay with a handful of teeth and apologize. Inside, the corridor is endless, lined with clocks all pointing to different hours. Somewhere ahead, a voice calls your name. You follow it through rooms that contain your childhood kitchen, your first office, a beach you’ve never visited.

At last, you open a door.

Everyone is there. They turn to look at you—old friends, strangers, versions of yourself you forgot to become. A table waits. On it is a single sheet of paper that reads: *The meeting was never about where. It was about whether you’d arrive.*

You sit down. The room exhales. The city outside folds itself into morning, and just before the alarm rings, someone says, “You’re exactly on time.”
