...speak, but the words come out strange—muffled, as if underwater. The audience leans forward, straining to hear. I clear my throat and try again, and this time my voice echoes back at me from somewhere behind the stage, a half-second late, like the room is repeating what I say.

I glance down at my notes and the pages are blank. Not empty—blank, like the ink has been poured off them. Somewhere in the crowd, a phone rings. It's my ringtone. I pat my pockets but they hold nothing but sand, warm and fine, spilling through my fingers.

When I look up again, the audience has changed. They're all faces I know—my third-grade teacher, my grandmother, a coworker from a job I left years ago, someone I haven't thought about in a decade. They're all smiling patiently, waiting, as if they have all the time in the world. Maybe they do.

"I'm here to talk about..." I begin, and the sentence finishes itself somewhere in the air above the crowd, glowing faintly, and everyone nods as if that's exactly what they came to hear.

I relax. The podium dissolves under my hands and I find I'm floating an inch above the stage, and no one seems to mind. The spotlight warms my shoulders like sunlight through a window. Words keep coming—not ones I planned, but ones that feel true—and the audience begins to sway gently, like reeds in a current.

Then, at the back of the hall, a door opens. White light spills through it, and I feel a pull, a gentle insistence, like a hand on my elbow. The crowd begins to applaud, slowly at first, then all at once, a sound like rain and like wings.

I step down from the stage. I walk toward the light. The applause follows me all the way out.
