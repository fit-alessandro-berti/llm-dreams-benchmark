...and you begin to speak, but the microphone is a conch shell pressed to your ear, and the audience is a vast ocean retreating into a single, pearl-gray eye. Your notes dissolve into moths that flutter up toward the rafters, which are no longer rafters but the ribs of some great, sleeping whale. You try to stop speaking, but the words have already unhooked themselves from your tongue; they drift into the air, becoming weather above the crowd. Down below, the expectant faces tilt upward—not to judge you, you realize, but to drink the rain you have made. Just as the first drop falls from your dry mouth, the stage begins to rise, not from the floor but from the center of your chest, and you understand that the speech has only just started its long climb out of you.
