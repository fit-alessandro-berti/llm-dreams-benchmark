"Captain!" your first mate shouts over the wind. She has your grandmother's voice, though her face keeps changing. "The compass!"

You look down. The needle spins lazily, then stops, pointing straight at your chest.

The waves rise higher than mountains, and you realize they aren't made of water at all, but of pages, millions of them, covered in handwriting you almost recognize. One wave crests above the ship and hangs there, as though waiting for your decision.

You understand, the way one simply understands things in dreams, that the storm is not something to escape. You let go of the wheel.

The crew gasps, but the ship steadies. The wheel turns on its own, gently, and the bow swings toward the heart of the tempest. You raise your hand, and the words come out of you in a language you've never learned: "Take us through."

The great wave of pages folds over the ship. For a moment there is only the rustle of paper and the smell of old books and salt. Then you break through to the other side.

The sea is perfectly still, a mirror stretching to every horizon. Above, there are two moons, and the stars are drifting slowly downward like snow, landing on the deck with soft chimes. Your crew stands silent. One by one, they turn to you and smile, and you notice that each of them has your eyes.

The first mate hands you the compass. The needle now points up.

"We've been waiting for you to remember," she says.

And the ship, without any wind at all, begins to rise.
