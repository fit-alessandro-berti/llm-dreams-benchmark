Rain doesn't fall. It *rises* — from the mossy ground beneath my feet, the droplets climbing upward into the darkening sky like silver pearls defying gravity. I stand still, mesmerized, as the forest floor becomes a mirror, reflecting a sky full of stars even though it should be midday.

Then I hear it — a low, resonant hum, like the earth itself is singing. The storm clouds aren't gray. They're **deep violet**, swirling in slow spirals, and within them I can see shapes moving — enormous, gentle silhouettes of creatures I've never seen, gliding through the thunder like whales through the deep sea.

Lightning strikes a distant tree, but when it hits, the tree doesn't burn — it **blooms**, erupting into thousands of luminous white flowers that scatter across the wind. Petals drift past my face and each one, when I touch it, hums a tiny note of music.

I feel no fear. Only awe.

The path ahead splits into three — one paved with glowing mushrooms, one made of water that flows uphill, and one that is simply *dark*, but warm, as though it leads somewhere that is waiting just for me.

A voice, neither mine nor anyone else's, whispers from the breathing storm above:

*"You already know which way."*

And I do. My feet move before I decide. The path of darkness doesn't consume me — it **wraps around me like a blanket**, and I step into the heart of the storm and find, impossibly, perfect silence.

A room. My childhood bedroom. The window open. Warm rain on the curtains. And on the nightstand, a letter in my own handwriting that says:

*"Don't be afraid of storms. They're just the world turning pages."*

I wake up with the scent of wet earth on my skin and a melody I can almost remember. ☔✨
