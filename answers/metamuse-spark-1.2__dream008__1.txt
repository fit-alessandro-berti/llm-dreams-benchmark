And you realize you can't move.

Not because you're scared - because your chair has suddenly become much, much too deep. Your office chair is like a soft pit, swallowing you up to your elbows. You try to stand but your shoes are stuck to the floor, which is now faintly buzzing like a giant carpet of bees.

Your boss is still there, smiling. But his smile is a little *too* wide, and his tie is slowly swaying even though there's no wind.

He slides a single sheet of paper across your desk. It says:

**PROJECT: Build the entire building. DEADLINE: 5 minutes ago.**

You look up at the clock. It says 2:47. Then it says 47:2. Then the hands start spinning backwards so fast they blur into a propeller.

"I just need a first draft," your boss says, in a voice that sounds like three people speaking at once.

You turn to your computer to start. But the keyboard is wrong. The keys are all soft and wobbly, like alphabet cereal floating in milk. When you press 'A', it makes a *squeak*. When you press 'Enter', it sighs. You try to type your name and it spells something else entirely.

You look around for help, but your coworkers are all frozen. One woman is mid-sip of coffee, the liquid suspended in the air. A man by the printer is photocopying his own face, over and over.

Panic starts to rise in your chest - that classic dream-panic where everything is urgent and nothing makes sense.

And then... you notice something.

Outside the office window, there is no city. There's just a vast, calm ocean under a purple sky. And the waves are breathing in and out, perfectly in sync with you.

You look back at the impossible project on your desk, at the spinning clock, at your boss whose tie is now floating straight up toward the ceiling.

And you suddenly remember: *Oh. This is a dream.*

The moment you remember, the weight lifts.

You stand up - easily now, your feet unstuck - and you say to your boss, "I think I'll do this later."

He nods, as if that was the most reasonable thing he's ever heard, and slowly begins to float toward the ceiling like a balloon.

You walk straight through the office wall, which ripples like water, and step out onto that purple-sky ocean. The floor is cool under your bare feet. The impossible deadline is still sitting on your desk behind you, but it feels smaller now, like a paper boat.

You don't have to finish it. You just have to wake up when you're ready.

You take a deep breath of that salty ocean air, and let yourself float for a moment before the alarm starts to ring, far, far away.
