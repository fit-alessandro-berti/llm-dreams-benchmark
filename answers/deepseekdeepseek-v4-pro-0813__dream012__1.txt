You plant your boots against the slick deck and grab the wheel with both hands. It bucks like a wild animal, but you hold it steady. “Trim the sails!” you shout, and your voice comes out deeper than usual, as if the sea itself is speaking through you. The crew obeys in slow motion, their faces blurred by spray.

A wave rears up taller than the mast, but instead of crashing, it stops—quivering like a held breath. Inside its green-glass wall you see stars, or glowing fish, and the reflection of your own face, unafraid. You understand then that the storm is not your enemy; it is your dream, your pulse, the roar of everything you’ve carried.

So you loosen your grip. You press one hand to your chest. The wave lowers. The rain softens to a glittering mist. The ship glides onto a silver sea, and the crew turn to you, smiling—their faces all faintly like your own.

A bell rings once, soft and near, from somewhere beyond the horizon. You feel the dream thinning, but for one more breath you let the ship sail on toward the light.
