You are in a maze filled with complex puzzles. Each puzzle requires intense concentration and logical thinking to solve, and as you move deeper into the maze, you begin to feel the shape of your own thoughts changing.

At first the puzzles are made of ordinary things: brass rings that must be turned in the correct order, tiles bearing numbers, doors guarded by riddles. You solve them eagerly. A ring clicks, a sum balances, a stone mouth whispers, “Correct,” and another corridor opens. Somewhere beyond the walls, a clock ticks with enormous patience.

Soon the rules become less certain. You enter a circular chamber where hundreds of black birds perch on silver wires. A plaque instructs you to arrange them from smallest to largest, but every time you compare two birds they exchange sizes. You finally close your eyes and listen. Their heartbeats form a sequence—two, three, five, seven—and when you touch the birds in that order, they fold themselves into paper and fly through a keyhole.

The next room contains a chessboard stretching from wall to wall. The pieces are miniature versions of people you recognize, though you cannot remember their names. One of them is you. Each time you move a piece, a memory vanishes: a summer afternoon, the taste of an apple, the sound of someone laughing in another room. The opposite king is already cornered, but delivering checkmate would cost you the face of the person you love most. Instead you tip your own king onto its side.

For a long moment nothing happens.

Then the board splits down the middle, revealing a staircase descending into blue darkness.

You follow it because returning would mean reconstructing every puzzle behind you, and you know with dreamlike certainty that their answers have changed. The ticking grows louder below. It is no longer a clock but a huge mechanical heartbeat buried under the maze.

At the bottom waits a narrow hallway lined with mirrors. Your reflections do not copy you. One is still solving the bird puzzle. Another kneels over the chessboard, weeping without a face. Others are much older and much younger. At the far end, one reflection stands with its back to you.

When you raise your hand, all the reflections raise theirs except that one.

It turns around.

Its face is covered by a smooth white mask marked with a single question: WHY?

The walls slide inward. Letters appear across the floor in glowing red lines:

WHAT ARE YOU TRYING TO REACH?

You search for the clever answer. Freedom. The center. The final door. None feels true. The corridor tightens until the mirrors press cold against your shoulders. Inside them, all your other selves stop what they are doing and look at you.

At last you say, “I don’t know.”

The ticking stops.

The masked reflection lifts both hands and removes its face. Behind it is not another face but a small sunlit room. You can see a wooden table, an open window, and white curtains moving in a summer breeze. On the table lies a half-finished puzzle made of maze-shaped pieces. One piece is missing.

You reach toward the mirror. Your fingers pass through as though through cool water, and you climb into the room.

The air smells of dust and rain. For the first time since entering the maze, you hear no machinery, no hidden mechanisms, no voice waiting to judge you. Outside the window, green fields roll toward a horizon that curves upward impossibly, becoming the walls of a vast bowl. Along the rim stand countless doors.

On the table, the unfinished puzzle shows the maze from above. Every corridor you traveled is there, including the room where you now stand. At its center is a small empty space shaped exactly like your hand.

You press your palm into it.

The tabletop shudders. All at once you can see through every wall of the maze. You see thousands of people in separate passages, each bent over a private problem. Some are counting stones. Some are trying to unlock doors they could simply walk around. Some are calling for help, though the walls swallow their voices. You understand that the maze has no single center. Its purpose is to keep everyone believing they must solve it alone.

You lift your hand from the puzzle and strike the table as hard as you can.

A crack races through the wooden maze. Far away, walls begin to fracture. Light pours through them in bright geometric seams. The people in the corridors look up. One by one they put down their riddles.

The room breaks apart around you. The table, window, and curtains scatter into a flock of white birds. You fall upward through the blue darkness while the entire maze unfolds beneath you like an enormous sheet of paper. Its passages flatten into lines, its locked doors into harmless ink. The sheet folds once, twice, again and again, until it becomes a tiny paper key resting in your palm.

Ahead of you floats one last door with no walls around it. There is no keyhole. On its surface, in your own handwriting, are the words: YOU MAY LEAVE WITHOUT SOLVING EVERYTHING.

You open it.

On the other side is your bedroom, washed in the gray light before dawn. You step toward the sleeping figure in the bed and realize it is you, one hand clenched beneath the pillow. You gently open its fingers and place the paper key inside.

As you do, the sleeper wakes.

For a second you are standing beside the bed and lying in it at the same time. Then the two of you rush together like reflections meeting at the surface of water. You sit up with your heart hammering. The room is silent.

Your hand is empty, except for a thin blue line drawn across your palm. It branches and turns like a map, leading to a place just beyond the edge of your skin.
