…and each group seems to be speaking a different language you almost understand.

The first is a circle of old friends who laugh too loudly. They offer you a glass of something that glows faintly, and when you sip it, you realize the room has tilted two degrees. You thank them and move on.

A second group stands in a corridor that wasn't there a moment ago. They are all wearing the same coat, and they nod at you as if they know exactly why you’re here. One of them says, “You’re early.” You say you’re looking for your friend. They point down a hallway made of wallpaper that ripples like water.

You pass a table of people arguing about a door that doesn’t exist. A woman turns to you and says, “It’s behind you, but not the way you think.” You turn, and there is no door. There is a mirror instead, and in the mirror you see yourself from behind, walking away, toward a figure you recognize. You try to call out, but your voice comes out as a small bell.

A third group is dancing in slow motion. They wave you in. The music is coming from a piano with no one playing it. You dance a little, and time bends like taffy. You ask them if they’ve seen your friend. They all point in different directions at once.

You choose the direction that feels most like a memory. You walk through a curtain made of rain, and you find yourself in a quieter room. There, sitting on a chair that is too small, is your friend. They look older and younger at the same time. They smile and say, “I was wondering when you’d get here.”

You sit beside them. The room is warm. You forget how you arrived. You forget why you were looking. You only know that this is exactly where you were supposed to be. And then, somewhere far above, you hear the faint sound of an alarm clock, and the dream starts to dissolve like sugar in water.
