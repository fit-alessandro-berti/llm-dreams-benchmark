...the air turns thick and electric. The leaves overhead begin to tremble, though there's no wind yet. You notice the trees have grown taller without your noticing when—they stretch impossibly high, their crowns disappearing into a churning violet sky.

The first raindrop lands on your palm, and it's warm. Strange—you were certain rain would be cold. Another falls, then another, and you realize drops are rising from the ground as well as falling from the sky, meeting in midair around you like tiny collisions of glass.

You begin to run, though you're not sure from what. The forest floor softens beneath your feet, moss giving way to something springier, and each footfall rings out a low musical tone—a different note every time. Without deciding to, you understand that you're playing a melody as you run. Somewhere off in the trees, something is humming along.

The storm arrives all at once. Thunder that sounds like your own name, spoken by a voice you almost recognize. Lightning illuminates a clearing ahead, and in the clearing stands a single door—freestanding, no walls around it, just a door with a brass knob, planted in the rain.

You know—dream-know, the way you know things in dreams—that the door leads somewhere dry. Somewhere safe. But when you reach for the knob, you hesitate, because you also know that on the other side, someone has been waiting for you for a very long time. And you aren't sure you're ready for what they have to say.

The thunder speaks your name again. Gentler this time. Almost kind.

Your hand closes around the knob. It's warm, like the rain.
