You are about to take the final exam for a course that determines your future career. As you sit down to start the exam, you notice the questions are written in a language that seems to shift between English and a soft, humming melody. Each line feels like a riddle that knows your name:

**“What do you fear most when the lights dim and the world forgets your name?**  
**If you could erase one moment from your past, which would you choose?**  
**Describe the dream you think you are having right now, and tell me why it feels more real than waking life.”**

The exam hall itself has no walls—only a vast, star‑filled void that swirls like ink. The desk beneath your hands is a slab of polished obsidian that reflects not your face but the silhouettes of people you have yet to meet: mentors, rivals, strangers who whisper advice in colors. A clock on the wall ticks backward, its hands moving in spirals, and the air smells faintly of old books and fresh rain.

You lift your pen, but the nib dissolves into a flock of silver moths that scatter across the page, leaving the paper blank. The ink you try to write becomes a river of light, flowing upward, forming shapes that hint at possibilities rather than answers. The professor—a tall figure draped in a robe that ripples like a night sky—appears at the front, his eyes twin galaxies. He says nothing, yet his presence fills you with a calm certainty: the exam is not about memorizing facts, but about confronting the inner questions that shape your destiny.

Just as you feel the weight of the unknown settle, the clock stops, the lights flicker, and the room dissolves into a corridor of endless doors, each marked with a different symbol. You stand at the threshold, pen still in hand, knowing that the true final exam lies beyond these doors—in the choices you will make after the paper is turned in.
