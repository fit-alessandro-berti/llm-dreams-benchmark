...not written in English, or even in ink. They are made of tiny, scurrying ants that refuse to stay in a straight line. 

Every time I try to read Question 1, the ants scatter across the desk, reforming into a picture of my childhood dog, who barks silently at me from the paper before dissolving into a puddle of maple syrup. 

I look up in a panic to ask the professor for a new test, only to realize the professor is a giant, ticking grandfather clock wearing a tweed jacket. The lecture hall has stretched into an infinite cavernous void, and all the other students are furiously writing with stalks of celery. They seem to know the answers. The girl next to me is already on page forty-seven, her celery snapping loudly with every confident stroke.

I look down at my own hands and realize I didn’t bring a pencil. Instead, I am holding a melting popsicle. Also, I am suddenly, inexplicably, wearing only a bathrobe and a pair of heavy scuba fins. 

"Five minutes remaining," the clock-professor chimes, his voice vibrating in my teeth. 

I try to use the melting popsicle to herd the ants back into the shape of a paragraph, because I suddenly remember that this is the final exam for *Advanced Gravity*, and if I fail, I’ll float away. Sure enough, my chair begins to drift toward the ceiling. The other students don't even look up as I rise past the fluorescent lights. 

In a last-ditch effort to pass, I decide that the answer to the exam isn't written at all—it has to be sung. I open my mouth to belt out a mathematical equation, but the only sound that comes out is the blaring, rhythmic ring of my real-life alarm clock. 

The ceiling dissolves, the ants vanish, and I jolt awake in my bed, my heart racing, still half-convinced I forgot to study for Gravity.
