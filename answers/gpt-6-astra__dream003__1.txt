tiny silver letters that rearrange themselves whenever you blink.

At last, they settle into a sentence:

**RETURN WHAT YOU HAVE NOT YET LOST.**

You check your pockets. A train ticket with no destination. A blue button. A photograph of a house you almost remember.

The box grows warm when you touch the photograph.

In the picture, someone stands at an upstairs window. You bring it closer. The figure raises a hand.

It is you, much older.

Behind you, a door opens where there was only a wall.

Beyond it lies the house from the photograph. Rain taps against the upstairs windows, though outside the house the sky is perfectly clear. You climb the stairs, carrying the box.

Your older self waits beside the window.

“I’ve been looking everywhere for that,” they say.

“The box?”

They shake their head and point to the blue button.

Only then do you notice their coat, fastened crookedly, one button missing. You hand it over. They close their fingers around it with such relief that you look away.

A latch clicks.

Inside the box is a small brass key, resting on a folded note.

You unfold the note.

*For the front door. It sticks when it rains.*

Downstairs, someone knocks. Your older self is already threading a needle.

“Would you let them in?” they ask.

You head down with the key. Halfway to the door, you recognize the knock.
