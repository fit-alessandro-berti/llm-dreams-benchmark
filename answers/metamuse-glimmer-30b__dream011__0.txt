You sit down, the chair is too small, the desk is too high, and the paper in front of you is crisp and white.

You notice the questions are **blank.**

Or rather, they were there for a second. You blink and the ink has evaporated. You lean in closer, hold your breath, and there’s just empty white space where the questions should be, with the faint indent of someone else’s pen pressing through from the other side.

You try to write anyway. You scribble something plausible in the margins. The proctor walks by and taps your desk. You look up and realize you’re not in a lecture hall at all. You’re in your childhood kitchen. Your mother is grading the paper. The exam is just a grocery list.

You wake up before you can read the first question, with that specific, metallic taste of panic in your mouth like you’ve just realized you were never prepared for the thing that actually matters.

That’s the classic version of this dream.

It usually means the exam isn’t about the course. The test you’re actually taking in the dream is about competence, worth, and the future you’re afraid you’re not ready for. The blank / unreadable / changing questions are your mind’s way of saying the stakes feel undefined and unknowable, and you’re being asked to prove yourself on material you were never given a chance to learn.
