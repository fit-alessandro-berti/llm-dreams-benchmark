Near a roaring fireplace—where stacked encyclopedias burn brightly without ever turning to ash—you pass a circle of guests wearing porcelain animal masks. They are laughing hysterically, though the sound coming from their mouths isn't laughter at all, but the gentle clinking of wind chimes. You tap a man in a fox mask on the shoulder to ask if he’s seen your friend. Without looking at you, he places a warm, ticking pocket watch into your palm and points toward a set of heavy velvet curtains.

Just before the curtains fall shut, you catch a glimpse of your friend’s familiar yellow coat disappearing behind them. You call out their name, your voice feeling thick and muffled, as if you are speaking underwater. 

You push through the velvet, expecting a hallway, and step directly into a humid, overgrown conservatory. The party continues here, entirely unfazed by the jungle growing through the floorboards. A cluster of astronomers in crisp evening gowns stand ankle-deep in glowing moss, casually passing a miniature, floating Saturn back and forth like a beach ball. You try to hurry past them, spotted yellow fabric flashing just ahead through the ferns, when that familiar, frustrating dream-heaviness settles into your limbs. The harder you try to run, the more the air turns to syrup.

You pull yourself forward by grabbing onto vine-wrapped pillars until you reach a spiral staircase leading up into nothingness. At the very top, sitting on the final step as if perched on the edge of a dock, is your friend. 

A wave of profound relief washes over you. The muffled noise of the crowd below fades into a soft hum. You climb the stairs, suddenly weightless, and sit down beside them under a ceiling that has completely dissolved into a violet, starry sky. You open your mouth to tell them how hard they were to find, when they turn to smile at you—and speak in the exact, rhythmic beeping of your morning alarm, gently pulling the world apart into the pale light of your bedroom.
