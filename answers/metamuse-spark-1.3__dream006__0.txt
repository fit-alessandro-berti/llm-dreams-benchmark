...and you begin to realize you have no idea what you were supposed to say.

You open your mouth, and for a second no sound comes out at all. The microphone in front of you is suddenly way too tall, then way too short. The stage lights are warm and blindingly bright, so you can see the first few rows perfectly — hundreds of expectant faces all turned up toward you — but everything beyond that dissolves into soft darkness.

You look down at your note cards for help, but the words are swimming and rearranging themselves on the page. One line melts into a doodle, then into a grocery list. You flip through them faster and faster, your heart pounding louder in your ears.

Then you clear your throat to try again, and this time your voice booms out, huge and echoing, but it's saying something completely different from what you meant. The crowd leans in closer. Someone in the front row smiles kindly at you. The stage floor feels soft under your feet, like it's gently tilting, like a boat —

and that's when you realize the podium has turned into something else entirely, and you're not nervous anymore, just curious where this dream is going to take you next.
