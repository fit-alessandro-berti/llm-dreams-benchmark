...and each group seems to be speaking a slightly different language, though you understand every word. In one cluster, people are arguing passionately about whether the moon is getting closer, and they hand you a pamphlet with diagrams that rearrange themselves when you look away. In another, a circle of elegant strangers stands perfectly still, holding their champagne glasses at the same angle, and when you pass, they all turn their heads together and say, "You're early."

You keep moving. The room stretches as you walk, the ceiling lifting like the sky at dusk, and the floor shifts from marble to sand to a soft carpet of fallen leaves. A band plays somewhere, but the music is made of conversation, each voice a different instrument. A tall man in a velvet coat tells you a long, sad story about a lighthouse keeper who fell in love with a ship, and you feel you should cry, but you're distracted by a small dog wearing a tiny tuxedo, which keeps trotting ahead of you as if it knows the way.

You follow the dog past a group of children playing a game with invisible balls. They shout the scores in numbers you've never heard before, and somehow you know you're winning. One of them grabs your hand and says, very seriously, "The person you're looking for keeps changing their face so you won't recognize them. Look for the one who's already looking at you."

You glance around. Everyone seems to be watching someone else. Then, near a set of tall windows where the night outside is full of slowly drifting lanterns, you notice a figure standing alone, half-turned away, a glass of water in hand. Something about the way they hold their shoulders is familiar, like a melody you can't quite name. You start toward them, and the crowd parts without anyone seeming to move, the way water makes room for a stone. The dog sits down at your feet, as if to say this is the part you have to walk alone.

Just as you reach out, the figure turns around, and the music stops, and the whole room holds its breath.
