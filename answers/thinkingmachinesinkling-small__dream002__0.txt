…and the air turns the color of green glass. The trees don’t bend; they lean in, listening, their leaves suddenly too loud. Thunder isn’t a sound anymore—it’s a taste, copper and old memory, and I can feel it in my ribs. The path beneath me softens, turning to something between moss and warm wool, and I realize I’m not walking forward but sideways, through rain that falls upward.

I seek shelter, but the forest offers none of the usual kind—only a hollow oak that opens like a mouth, and inside, the storm is not weather at all, but a conversation. Lightning speaks in the voice of my oldest friend; thunder answers in a language I’ve forgotten I knew. The walls of the trunk grow warm, and I understand I have always been both the one lost and the one who knew the way home.

When I step back outside, the storm hasn’t passed. It has settled, becoming part of the soil, part of the air. The sky is now a bruise-colored silk, and the sun is setting where the dawn should be. In my open palm sits a single silver leaf—heavy as a heart, warm as a secret. I don’t know if I am holding it, or if it is holding me, but the forest is peaceful again, and I am still walking, and the weather will never be ordinary anymore.
