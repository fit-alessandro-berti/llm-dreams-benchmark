

…and you feel the weight of every ticking clock pressing against your chest. You open your laptop, but the keyboard has turned into soft piano keys, and every time you press one, a new task appears in the air like a floating note.

Your boss is still watching, expression unreadable, arms crossed. The deadline glows red on the wall: **one hour left**—even though the project should take weeks.

You take a breath and realize something strange: the office is not quite real. The windows show different seasons every time you look through them. The fluorescent lights hum like distant voices. Your coworkers move in slow motion, carrying papers that turn into birds and fly toward the ceiling.

Instead of panicking, you stand up and ask, “What is the most important part?”

The room goes silent.

Your boss blinks, as if no one has ever asked that before. Then the impossible deadline on the wall begins to melt like wax, reshaping itself into three smaller, manageable tasks.

Suddenly, you understand. You do not need to finish everything perfectly. You only need to begin.

You start working, and the dream shifts with you. Every choice you make becomes a doorway. One version of the project is finished in sunlight, another in rain, another in a quiet library where your ideas line up neatly like books. When the final hour arrives, you hand your boss not a perfect report, but a small glowing object that contains the essence of the solution.

Your boss looks at it, then at you, and says, “You didn’t finish it the way I expected. You finished it the way it needed to be finished.”

The office dissolves into morning light.

You wake up with a calm feeling in your chest, as if the dream taught you something real: when a task feels impossible, shrink it, ask for clarity, and take the first honest step.
