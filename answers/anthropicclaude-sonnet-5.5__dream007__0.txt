...stop mid-sentence, because you notice something strange: the words leaving your mouth aren't sounds anymore. They're small paper birds, folded and fluttering, and they scatter across the room before you can take them back.

Your friend watches one land on the table between you. It unfolds itself, and written inside, in your own handwriting, is the thing you were actually afraid of. It was never about who was right. It was about being left behind.

Your friend's face changes. They open their mouth, and out come birds of their own, darker and slower, and one settles on your shoulder. You don't have to unfold it. You already know what it says.

The room begins to tilt, the way rooms do in dreams, and the walls turn into the porch of the house you grew up in. The argument is still there, but it's small now, like a stone you could hold in your palm. Your friend sits beside you on the steps. Neither of you says anything for a long while. The birds gather in the yard, pecking at the grass as if it were seed.

"I don't want to win this," you finally say, and your voice is just a voice again.

"Me neither," they say. "I just wanted you to hear me."

Somewhere behind you, a door you don't remember opens, and warm light spills across the steps. You both stand. You can't tell whether it leads to morning or to another dream, but you walk through it together, and the birds follow.

And then you wake, with the feeling of someone's shoulder against yours and the faint sense that you should call them.
