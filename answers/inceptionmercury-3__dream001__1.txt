You start to run, though the streets seem to twist away from you, refusing to hold a straight line. The buildings lean in like curious strangers, their windows blinking. You pass a café where everyone is wearing a watch on both wrists, and a park where the trees are whispering the time in a language you almost understand. You ask a passerby for directions, but their mouth moves a second before their voice arrives. You pull out your phone—it has no map, just a single glowing dot moving in circles over a picture of your own face.

Then you notice a small, familiar detail: a coffee stain on your sleeve that you know from yesterday. The city is not unfamiliar after all. It’s the inside of your own memory, rearranged. You close your eyes and walk toward the place where the meeting has always been—not a building, but a feeling. When you open them, you’re sitting in a room with you, and the chair across the table is empty. The clock says you still have an hour. You smile. You’re already there.
