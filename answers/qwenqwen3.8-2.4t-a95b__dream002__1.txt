

You feel a storm approaching and the trees begin to whisper, their leaves trembling not with fear, but with anticipation. The sky darkens into a deep violet, and the first raindrops fall — not cold, but warm, glowing faintly like liquid starlight.

You hurry along the forest path, but the ground softens beneath your feet, turning into moss that glows silver with every step. Thunder rolls overhead, yet it sounds like distant music, like drums beating in a slow, ancient rhythm.

Ahead, the trees part to reveal a clearing where a great oak stands alone. Its trunk is split open like a doorway, and from inside spills a gentle golden light. The storm grows louder, but you no longer feel afraid. Something inside you recognizes this place.

You step into the tree.

Inside, you find yourself in a vast hall made of roots and light. The storm swirls above like a living thing, and as it spins, it begins to sing — a song of change, of release, of things being washed clean. You realize the storm was never coming to destroy the forest. It was coming to awaken it.

When you step back outside, the rain has stopped. The forest is shimmering, every leaf edged with light. Birds emerge from hiding, and the air smells of honey and lightning. You look down and see that your hands are glowing faintly, as if the storm left something behind in you.

Then, softly, the dream begins to fade — the trees turning into mist, the sky dissolving into morning light. But the feeling remains: calm, wonder, and the quiet certainty that something in you has changed.
