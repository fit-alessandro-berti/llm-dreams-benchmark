The first one reads: **What did you want to be before you knew what a job was?**

You write *a horse*. It’s the only answer you’re sure of.

Somewhere behind you, a chair scrapes. You turn around and see your father at a tiny desk, gripping a pencil in his fist. He catches your eye and mouths, *Number four?*

You look down. Number four is a drawing of your childhood kitchen. Someone has left the back door open. You can smell the wet concrete outside.

“Can we go out there?” you ask.

The proctor checks the clock. “You’ll lose whatever time you spend.”

So you go. Your father follows, still holding his pencil. At the bottom of the steps there’s a puddle with a worm stranded in it. He crouches beside you and holds the pencil steady while you coax the worm onto it.

When you wake, you can’t remember what career the exam was for. You remember your father saying, very quietly, “Wait. Let it get a grip.”
