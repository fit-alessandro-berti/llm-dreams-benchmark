As you step closer, the canvas begins to breathe. Its surface ripples like water, and faint shapes rise from within it—half-formed memories, impossible flowers, staircases that fold into clouds, and eyes that blink with the color of sound.

A brush appears in your hand, though you do not remember picking it up. Its handle is warm, as if it has been waiting for you. When you touch the tip to the canvas, the world around you responds: every stroke pulls colors from the sky, shapes from the ground, and whispers from the air.

You paint a door, and a door appears in the distance, standing alone in a field of shifting glass.  
You paint a key, and the key dissolves into your palm, becoming a tiny song.  
You paint yourself, but the figure on the canvas turns and walks away, stepping out of the frame and into the dream.

Suddenly, you understand: the canvas is not asking you to create something new. It is asking you to remember something you have forgotten.

So you close your eyes and paint without seeing. You paint the feeling of falling upward, the taste of starlight, the sound of a name you almost recognize. When you finally open your eyes again, the canvas shows a simple scene: a sleeping figure beneath a sky full of drifting shapes, dreaming exactly this dream.

The canvas whispers, in a voice made of color:

“Wake gently. You are still inside the painting.”

And as the world folds itself into a single glowing point, you realize that you are both the dreamer and the dream.
