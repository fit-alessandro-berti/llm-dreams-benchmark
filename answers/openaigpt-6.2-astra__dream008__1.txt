Your boss glances at the clock. “Well?”

You press it again. Now there’s room between your desks for a narrow bed, and in the bed is your boss, asleep in the blue pajamas you somehow know his daughter gave him. The boss beside you looks down at the sleeping one.

“Lucky bastard,” he says softly.

The clock says 4:59. The project is due at five. You open the folder for the first time. Inside is a photograph of this office, taken from above. In it, everyone is looking up except you.

You look up.

An enormous, anxious face fills the skylight. It’s yours, a few years older. “Have you started?” it asks.

For some reason, seeing the worry on that face makes it easy to answer. “Not yet. What’s the project?”

Nobody knows. Your boss checks his email. His sleeping self rolls over. High above you, your older self takes off a pair of glasses and rubs the dents they’ve left on either side of your nose.

At five, nothing happens.

After a while your boss pulls a chair over, and the two of you play a quiet game of cards on the unopened folder. You let him win the first hand. He’s had a long day, and only half of him has had any sleep.
