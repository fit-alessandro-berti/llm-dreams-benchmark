…and each group seems to be speaking a language made entirely of familiar phrases arranged in the wrong order. One cluster of people is earnestly discussing the weather, but every time someone says “humid,” a small brass bird appears above their head and chirps a phone number. Another group waves you over, offers you a drink that tastes like the smell of a childhood bedroom, and asks if you’ve seen “the person you know.” You say yes, that’s exactly who you’re looking for, and they nod as if this is the deepest thing anyone has ever said.

You move through rooms that keep rearranging behind you. In the kitchen, everyone is wearing name tags that say *Almost You*. A woman hands you a plate of tiny oranges and whispers, “He went toward the hallway that remembers you.” You follow it. The music fades into the sound of a distant vacuum cleaner, and the walls become soft, like the inside of a coat pocket.

Finally, you find your friend standing alone by a window, looking out at a bright dark ocean. You feel relieved and say their name, but when they turn around, they have your face. They smile and say, “There you are. I’ve been looking for you everywhere.” You both laugh, and the party applauds politely, then turns into a field of tall grass under a paper moon. Just before you wake, your friend—who is you, who is not you—leans close and says, “Next time, don’t look so hard. I’m always the one who finds you.”
