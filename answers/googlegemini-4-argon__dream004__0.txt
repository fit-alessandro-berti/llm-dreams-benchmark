...it is the only thing in this entire kaleidoscopic world that is completely still. 

Around me, the sky is melting from a deep velvet violet into a sharp, citrus green, and the ground beneath my feet ripples like liquid geometric tiles—triangles morphing into spheres, then dissolving into mist. Yet, the canvas sits on an easel of woven tree roots, stubbornly, glaringly white. It hums with a quiet, magnetic pull.

There are no brushes, no palettes, and no tubes of paint. Confused, I reach out my hand, and as my fingers brush the air in front of me, I accidentally catch a drifting ribbon of cerulean blue that was floating by. It feels warm and heavy, like spun sugar. 

Understanding washes over me with the sudden, unquestioning logic of a dream. I don't need paint; I have the world.

I reach up and pluck a sharp, golden triangle out of the sky. It chimes like a struck tuning fork in my palm. I press it against the canvas. Instead of sitting flat, the shape sinks *into* the fabric, expanding into a radiant, three-dimensional sun that casts real warmth against my face. 

Intoxicated by the creation, I begin to gather the chaos around me. I scoop up a handful of the bubbling, magenta ground and smear it across the bottom of the canvas, where it immediately solidifies into a range of jagged, crystalline mountains. I catch a passing whisper of silver wind and flick it from my fingertips, watching it splatter into a galaxy of spinning stars. 

With every stroke I add to the canvas, the shifting world around me begins to slow down. The dizzying mutations of the landscape are stabilizing, anchoring themselves to the image I am creating. I am not just painting a picture; I am giving the dream a memory.

Finally, only the very center of the canvas remains blank. I look at my own hands, realizing they are beginning to flicker, turning translucent as the dream nears its end. Wanting to leave a piece of myself behind, I press my palm directly into the wet, swirling center of the painting. 

The canvas ripples like water. The colors I painted—the golden sun, the magenta mountains, the silver stars—rush inward, swirling around my hand and pulling me forward. I don't pull back. I step through the frame, into the world I just made, right as the colors flash into a blinding, brilliant white—and I wake up.
