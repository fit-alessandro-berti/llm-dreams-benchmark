...you sit down at your desk, but the chair is wet. You don't know why it's wet, and somehow you know not to ask.

You open your laptop to begin the project, but the screen shows your high school report card instead. All the classes are named after the project deliverables. Your boss leans over your shoulder—he is now also your dentist, somehow, and you know this without being told.

"The deadline," he says, "was actually last week."

You try to explain, but your mouth is full of teeth that keep multiplying. You spit them into your hands and they're USB drives. You plug one into the laptop. It works! The project file opens—but it's written entirely in a language you're certain you used to speak fluently.

The office lights flicker. The deadline is now written on the wall in the fluorescent tubes themselves: TOMORROW. But the letters keep rearranging—TOMORROW becomes MORROWTO becomes WOOTMORR—and you understand all of these words perfectly and none of them at all.

You go to find your boss to ask for an extension, but the office has become the hallway from your childhood home, except it stretches much farther than it ever did. At the end of it, a printer is printing the project. You run toward it. The hallway stretches. The printer prints faster, pages spilling out and unfolding like an accordion, and you realize with dream-certainty that the project is *you*—each page is a day of your life, and the deadline is your—

You wake up. 3:47 AM. Heart pounding. You lie there for a moment, relieved it was just a dream.

Then you remember: you don't have a boss. You've been self-employed for six years.

You do not go back to sleep.
