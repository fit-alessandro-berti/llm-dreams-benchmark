You are walking through a peaceful forest when suddenly the weather changes dramatically. You feel a storm approaching, and the birds fall silent all at once.

A cold wind moves between the trees, bending their branches in the same direction, as though the entire forest is pointing toward something. The bright green leaves turn silver beneath a sky that darkens much too quickly. You know you should find shelter, but instead you feel compelled to follow the path ahead.

The first drop of rain lands on your hand. It is warm.

Then the storm breaks.

Rain pours through the canopy in shimmering curtains, and thunder shakes the ground beneath your feet. You begin to run, slipping over wet roots as flashes of lightning briefly illuminate strange shapes among the trees. For an instant, you see people standing between the trunks—motionless figures wearing clothes from different periods of your life. When darkness returns, they vanish.

Ahead, a golden light flickers.

You push through a wall of ferns and discover a tiny cottage in a clearing. It looks impossibly familiar, although you are certain you have never seen it before. Smoke rises from its chimney despite the rain, and its front door is already open.

Inside, the storm becomes silent.

The cottage contains only a wooden table and a large mirror. On the table rests a small glass bottle filled with what appears to be lightning. Each time it flashes, the mirror shows a different reflection: you as a child, you many years older, you standing somewhere you have always wanted to be. Finally, the mirror shows the forest—but in the reflection, there is no cottage. You are standing alone in the clearing, holding the bottle.

A voice behind you whispers, “The storm cannot leave until you open it.”

You turn, but no one is there.

The bottle trembles beneath your fingers. You hesitate, afraid that releasing the lightning will destroy the cottage and everything around it. Yet the walls have begun to breathe, slowly expanding and contracting, and the ceiling drips upward into the darkness.

You remove the stopper.

Instead of lightning, hundreds of glowing moths burst from the bottle. They circle you in a warm spiral before flying through the walls and into the storm. Wherever they pass, the rain rises from the ground back into the clouds. Thunder rolls backward. Broken branches lift and reconnect with the trees.

The cottage dissolves around you.

You are once again standing in the clearing, but the storm has disappeared. Morning sunlight shines through the forest, and every leaf is covered with tiny golden moths. One lands on your hand. Its wings carry a pattern resembling a map.

You understand, without knowing how, that it shows the way home.

You follow the moth deeper into the forest until the trees begin to thin. Beyond them, you see a familiar door standing alone in a field. As you reach for its handle, you hear the distant sound of thunder—not behind you, but from somewhere on the other side.

You open the door.

And just before you see what is waiting there, you wake with your hand still reaching into the air.
