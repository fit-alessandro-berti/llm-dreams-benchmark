You start to walk, and the streets shift under your feet.

The buildings are impossibly tall — glass and stone stretching up into a hazy, lilac sky with two suns that don't cast shadows. The street signs are written in a language you almost understand, the letters wiggling when you look directly at them.

You check your phone. The meeting invite just says:

> **LOCATION: The Blue Door**
> **TIME: in 47 minutes**

No address. No map pin.

You ask a passerby — a woman walking a dog that looks suspiciously like a loaf of bread with legs — where the Blue Door is.

"Oh, it's not far," she says cheerfully. "Just take the tram that only stops when you're not waiting for it."

Before you can ask what that means, you hear a chime. A tram made entirely of bicycles welded together glides silently to a stop right beside you, even though there are no tracks.

Inside, everyone is dressed like they're going to your meeting. They all nod at you knowingly, holding identical silver briefcases.

You sit down as the tram lifts gently off the ground and begins to float between the skyscrapers, and you realize the city below is shaped like a giant clock — and all the hands are pointing right at you.
