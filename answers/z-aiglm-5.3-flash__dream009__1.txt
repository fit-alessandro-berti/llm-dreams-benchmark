…reach the stone well on the hilltop before the last of the daylight slips away.

You don't question how you know this. In the way of dreams, the knowledge arrives whole and certain, as if someone whispered it into your ear while you slept.

You're standing at the edge of a village — thatched roofs, mud lanes, the off-rhythm ringing of a smith's hammer. Smoke curls low over the wet grass. Somewhere a dog barks twice and falls silent, as if even the animals are holding their breath. Your hands are smudged with soil. They feel unfamiliar, and yet they move like they've done nothing else for years.

The path up the hill is longer than it looks. Hills always are, in dreams. The seed heads brush your palms, and halfway up you pass a child crouching among the rocks, who says without looking at you, *"She told us you'd remember the way."* When you turn to ask who — the child is gone, swallowed by the slope.

At the top, the well waits, ringed with moss. You grip the cold stones and lean over.

Far below, where the water should be, there is only night sky. Stars swimming in a circle of darkness. And rising toward you, slowly, your own face — smiling when you aren't, patient the way only something that has waited centuries can be patient.

*"There you are,"* it says, in a voice that comes through the stone rather than the air. *"We kept everything safe while you were away."*

The stars below shift, rearrange themselves into shapes you almost recognize. The sun bleeds out at the horizon. The reflection lifts its hand — offering something small and bright cupped in its palm — and you feel yourself tipping forward, the whole sky rushing up to meet you—

You wake.

Ceiling. Familiar walls. Morning traffic humming somewhere beyond the window, your phone blinking patiently on the nightstand. Everything exactly where you left it.

Except your arms still itch with the ghost of coarse wool. There's smoke in your hair — woodsmoke, faint and sweet. And beneath your fingernails, two thin crescents of dark earth.

For a long moment you lie perfectly still, trying to remember which side of the sleep you actually live on. And somewhere far away — or perhaps just under your ribs — a hammer rings, off-beat and unhurried, keeping time for someone still climbing a hill.
