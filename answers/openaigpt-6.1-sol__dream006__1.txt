speak—but instead of words, a small blue bird slips from your mouth.

It circles the microphone once, then settles on the shoulder of someone in the front row. Nobody seems surprised. They lean forward, waiting.

You try again. This time, a ribbon of ocean spills over your lips, flowing down the steps and between the seats. The audience lifts their feet politely. Somewhere near the back, a woman opens an umbrella.

Your notes have turned into a map of a house you lived in as a child. One room is marked *You forgot something here.*

“I’m sorry,” you say, and the words come out normally.

The audience exhales.

You recognize them now: your old teacher, a friend you lost touch with, the person who once returned your wallet. There are strangers too, holding programs folded into boats.

You look down at the map. The marked room is the kitchen.

Before you can decide what to do, someone in the back calls, “Take your time.”

You step away from the microphone and open the door that has appeared behind you. In the kitchen, the kettle is just beginning to sing.
