Above you, the sky darkens in great sweeping bands, and the first thunder rolls low beneath the earth rather than from the clouds. You stop walking. A single drop of rain lands on your forehead—warm, bright, and strangely familiar.

Then the storm breaks.

Wind rushes between the trees, carrying the scent of pine, wet stone, and something ancient. Branches sway like arms trying to guide you, and though you should be afraid, you feel instead that you are being led. You follow a narrow path that appears only in the flashes of lightning, winding deeper into the forest.

At last, the trees open into a clearing. In the center stands a small wooden cabin you somehow know is yours. Its windows glow gold. The door opens before you reach it.

Inside, there is no thunder—only the sound of rain on the roof, a fire burning in the hearth, and a voice gently saying, “You made it.”

And then you wake, with the taste of rain still on your lips.
