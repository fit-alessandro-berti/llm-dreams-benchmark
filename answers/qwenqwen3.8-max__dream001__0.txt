You start to walk faster, your shoes clicking against pavement that seems to rearrange itself beneath your feet. The city rises around you in impossible layers—bridges leading to doorways in midair, staircases spiraling up the sides of glass towers, streets folding gently into the sky like ribbons.

You ask a passing stranger for directions, but when they turn to face you, their eyes are clocks, their hands moving slowly toward the hour. “You’re already late,” they say kindly, “and also exactly on time.”

Panic rises in your chest. You check your pockets and find only a folded piece of paper. When you open it, the words keep changing:

**MEETING LOCATION: THE PLACE YOU FORGOT.**  
**TIME: WHEN YOU ARRIVE.**  
**PURPOSE: REMEMBER.**

You look up and notice a building across the street that wasn’t there before. It has no sign, only a tall black door slightly ajar. Warm light spills from inside, along with the faint sound of voices—some familiar, some not.

You step inside.

The room beyond is enormous, filled with people seated around a long table. Every chair is occupied except one, at the far end. As you approach, you realize each person there is a version of someone you know—or someone you used to be. One of them smiles and slides a folder toward you.

“You’re right on time,” they say.

You open the folder. Inside is a single photograph of yourself, asleep, dreaming.

And then you understand: the meeting was never about a place. It was about returning to the part of you that knew the way all along.

You sit down.

The city outside exhales.

And the dream settles into silence.
