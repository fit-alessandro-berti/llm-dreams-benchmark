...encounter various groups of people, each speaking a language that shifts as soon as you listen too closely—English becomes chorus, chorus becomes birdsong, birdsong becomes a strange arithmetic rhyme, and then a laugh, and then it starts again. You follow the one person you know by a detail only you remember: they were wearing a coat with a single green button. Except now everyone seems to be wearing the same coat, dozens of green buttons fluttering like small fish as you pass beneath string lights, and you can't tell which is the real person until you find the one button that is missing.

You reach the garden wall where the gathering spills out under a pear tree. The person you're looking for isn't here—but on the wall, painted in chalk, is a map of the room you just walked through, except the hall is bigger in the drawing than it ever was, and the door you came in by is the way out. You turn. The door isn't there.

Then you hear your own voice, from inside the crowd, saying something soothing, and you turn toward it, and it's the green button—your sleeve, suddenly, when it wasn't a moment ago—and the whole hall resolves: it's a party you threw, everyone is someone you once knew and forgot, and they're all waiting, politely, for you to remember one face. The pear tree is a table. The table is set for two. You sit down, and the room keeps going without you, and it's the first time that's ever felt like relief rather than loss, and you realize you're still holding the missing button, and you turn it over, and on the back it says a phone number you don't need to dial anymore.
