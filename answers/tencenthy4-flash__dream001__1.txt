Each step sinks you a little deeper, the concrete tugging at your shoes like wet clay. You fumble for your phone, but when you finally unlock it, the screen doesn't show a map—it shows a live feed of a hallway you've never seen, and the blinking blue dot that's supposed to be your location is spinning lazily in the center, refusing to settle.

You spot a figure ahead—a man in a sharp suit, walking with purpose. "Excuse me!" you call out, but your voice comes out as a dry, hollow croak. He turns, and you notice his face is entirely smooth, featureless, like a mannequin fresh from the box. He raises a gloved finger and points toward a skyscraper that definitely wasn't there a moment ago, then dissolves into the crowd, leaving behind nothing but the faint scent of ozone and old paper.

You glance at your watch. The hands are made of something soft—wax, or maybe taffy—and they're drooping toward the ground, then snapping back to twelve. *An hour*, you remind yourself. Or was it a minute? The streetlights flicker, and for a heartbeat it's midnight, then noon, then midnight again. The buildings around you begin to breathe, their windows blinking like slow, heavy eyes.

You sprint toward the building the man pointed to, pushing through revolving doors that spin too fast, spitting you out into a lobby that stretches into infinity. The floor is a checkerboard of black and white tiles that shift underfoot like a living thing. A receptionist sits behind a desk made of ice; she looks up, and her smile is a little too wide. "You're expected," she says, her voice echoing as if from the bottom of a well. She slides a name tag across the frozen surface. It reads: **LATER**.

Confused, you take it and follow the hallway, which now slopes upward at an impossible angle. You arrive at a heavy oak door. You push it open. Inside, there is no conference table, no colleagues, no agenda. Just a single chair facing a massive window. Outside the glass, the city is unraveling—buildings unfolding like origami, streets curling into spirals, the sky bleeding into shades of static.

On the chair sits a small, folded note. You pick it up. It reads: *"The meeting was never about where. It was about realizing you're already late to yourself."*

You look up, and the reflection in the window isn't quite yours. It's you, but older, or younger, or perhaps just a version of you that isn't entirely tethered to this reality. The figure in the glass smiles, tips an invisible hat, and fades into the gray.

A sudden, violent ringing pierces the air—a shrill, mechanical sound that shatters the window, the city, the very fabric of the dream. You jolt awake, tangled in sheets, heart hammering against your ribs. The phantom sensation of soft, waxen hands still seems to tick in your palm. The alarm clock on the nightstand reads 7:00 AM, and for a long, frozen moment, you're afraid to look away, in case the ceiling starts rippling again.
