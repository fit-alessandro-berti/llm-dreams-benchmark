The box hums softly. Not a sound exactly, but a feeling, like a word remembered from a language you spoke long ago. The patterns begin to move, flowing like water in reverse, and you realize they aren't decorations at all. They're instructions. Or maybe memories.

Without deciding to, you press your palm flat against the lid.

The box doesn't open—it *dissolves*, its edges softening into mist that smells faintly of rain and old books. Inside, where there should be treasure or darkness, there is instead a small door, standing upright, impossibly framed by nothing. It's slightly ajar. Warm light spills through the gap.

You hear something on the other side. Footsteps? Breathing? No—someone humming a tune you almost recognize, a lullaby half-remembered from childhood.

Your hand reaches for the door handle just as—

—you wake, your own hand pressed flat against the nightstand, fingers curled as if gripping something small and warm that is no longer there.

For a moment, you can still feel the weight of the box. And somewhere in the back of your mind, faint but persistent, someone is still humming.
