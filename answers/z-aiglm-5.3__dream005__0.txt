...and every one of them seems to know you. "There you are!" says a man with your uncle's voice and a stranger's face. You've never seen him before, but you say "there I am!" back, because it seems rude not to.

You realize you can no longer remember what your friend looks like. You know their name — you can feel the exact shape of it in your mouth — but their face keeps sliding away, like trying to look at something in your peripheral vision.

The room is longer than it was. You're certain of this. The far wall retreats as you walk toward it, and the party sounds — laughter, glasses, music coming from somewhere inside the walls — turn slightly underwater. Through a doorway you spot them. Definitely them. You call out, but your voice arrives late and thin, a badly dubbed whisper. They don't turn.

The doorway opens into your childhood kitchen. Your third-grade teacher stands at the sink, washing dishes, exactly as she looked twenty-five years ago. Nothing about this strikes you as odd. "They're looking for you too, you know," she says without turning around. "You keep missing each other."

You go back through the doorway. The party is outside now — night, string lights, a courtyard. Everyone is dancing to music that has stopped. Your friend stands in the center of the crowd, facing away. You push toward them, and the other guests give way strangely, like heavy curtains, and just as your hand touches their shoulder you feel the dream begin to thin, the way paper holds up to a window.

They turn around.

It's you, but older, wearing the coat you lost years ago. "Where have you been?" they ask. "We've been looking everywhere."

And then you're descending a staircase that wasn't there before, holding two full glasses of something warm, and you can't remember who either of them is for.
