You are about to take the final exam for a course that determines your future career. As you sit down to start the exam, you notice the questions are unlike anything you studied for. Instead of multiple-choice calculus problems or physics equations, the first question simply reads: **"Who are you?"**

You blink, hard. You check the exam header again—it's the right course code. But the questions continue:

**2.** What does your heart want?
**3.** Name one thing in life you've never regretted.
**4.** If your career is a sentence, what's the verb?

No instructions. No point values. The proctor walks between desks with a soft expression and says nothing.

Half the room is furiously writing paragraph after paragraph. The other half seems to realize something quietly, all around the same moment—their pen stops, their shoulders drop, they simply sit.

Without consciously deciding, you find your own pen has stopped too. A calm has settled. There's still time on the clock; the "answers" are already done, not because you wrote them, but because you chose not to override them. The figure walking the aisles glances at you and, without any words, closes the test.

---

You never learn a grade. A single sealed letter arrives months later with your chosen field and a starting date.

Weirdly, so far, it's been right.
