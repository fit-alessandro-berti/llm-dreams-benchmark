...speak. But the words that come out aren't the ones you prepared. Your voice echoes strangely, as if the auditorium has grown ten times larger since you looked down at your notes — notes that are now blank, or worse, covered in doodles you don't remember drawing.

You look up. The audience is still there, but they're all wearing the faces of people from your childhood: your third-grade teacher, a neighbor who moved away decades ago, your grandmother calmly eating popcorn in the front row. They nod as you speak, as though you're saying something profound, even though you're now fairly certain you've been reciting a grocery list.

The podium begins to feel wobbly. You grip its edges and realize it's made of water — cool and rippling under your fingers but somehow holding your weight. You decide not to think about that too hard, because in dreams, thinking too hard is how the floor disappears.

And there it goes.

You're falling, but slowly, the way a feather falls, and the audience above you applauds politely as you drift down past them. Somewhere in the descent, you remember you're not wearing shoes. This doesn't seem to matter. What matters is that you never finished the speech, and everyone is still waiting, patient as stones.

You land softly on a stage that is now a beach. The audience is the ocean. The applause is the waves. You open your mouth to try again, and—

—you wake up, mouth dry, heart tapping out a little drumroll, with the phantom sensation of blank notecards still in your hand and the distinct feeling that you've just left an entire auditorium hanging. Somewhere, dream-you is still standing on that beach, clearing your throat, about to absolutely nail the second attempt.
