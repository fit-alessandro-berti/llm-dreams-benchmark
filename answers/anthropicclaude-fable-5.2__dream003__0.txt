You're standing in your grandmother's kitchen now, except the windows look out onto an ocean, and the tide is coming in backward, waves pulling away from the shore in slow, deliberate folds. A clock on the wall ticks, but its hands are tiny keys, each pointing in a different direction.

You press your thumb into the indentation.

The patterns begin to move, unspooling like thread and rearranging themselves into a map of every place you've ever lived, connected by glowing lines. At the center, where the lines converge, a seam appears, and the lid lifts on its own.

Inside, there is no treasure. Instead there is a staircase, impossibly small, spiraling down into soft amber light. You hear music drifting up from below, a song you almost recognize, and the distant murmur of voices saying your name as if they've been expecting you.

The box is in your hands, and yet you are already standing on the first step. The stairs are warm beneath your bare feet. Behind you, the lid closes gently, like an eyelid, and the patterns on the walls rearrange themselves into a single sentence you can finally read:

*"You were never locked out. You were only waiting to come in."*
