You have no idea if it will grow. The packet it came in was labeled **PEACHES, SLICED**, and you found the pit at the bottom, which suggests that even before the world ended, someone had been having a bad day at work.

You plant it in the median of a six-lane highway. Every morning you give it the last sip from your cup. Every morning a crow watches from a bent sign that says **MERGE**.

On the ninth day, a woman comes out of the old tollbooth carrying a dented kettle.

“You’re watering it wrong,” she says.

“How should I water it?”

“I don’t know,” she says. “I was hoping you did.”

She pours a little from her kettle anyway.

That night, in the way of dreams, thirty years pass. You wake in the shade of a peach tree. The highway is still cracked, the tollbooth is still there, and the woman is asleep beside you with her head on your shoulder. Farther down the median, someone has hung laundry between two more trees.

A peach drops into your lap. You eat it without getting up. At the first bite, you remember standing barefoot in a kitchen, years before the end, complaining that the peaches weren’t ripe yet. You can’t remember who was standing at the sink. You can hear them laughing, though.

When you’re done, you push the pit into the dirt with your thumb.
