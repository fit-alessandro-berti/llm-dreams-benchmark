Of course — let's slip back into that dream:

---

You wake up in a completely different era, with no modern technology around.

You're on a straw mattress in a small room with whitewashed walls and wooden beams. Sunlight cuts in thin gold lines through the shutters. A rooster is crowing somewhere close. The air smells like woodsmoke, fresh bread, and hay.

Your phone is gone. Your clothes are gone. Instead you're wearing a rough linen shirt and heavy wool pants that itch a little. Your sneakers have been replaced by soft leather boots.

As you push open the creaky wooden door and step outside, your new surroundings bloom around you.

It's a bustling market village — maybe the 1400s? Cobblestone lanes, thatched roofs, horses clopping past with carts of apples and wool. No cars, no wires, no screens. Just the clatter of a blacksmith, children chasing chickens, a woman singing as she hangs laundry, and a town crier ringing a bell in the square.

Everyone is speaking a familiar language, but just slightly... off. Old words mixed in. You understand it perfectly in the way you only can in dreams.

As you explore your new surroundings, you realize you need to **find your way home before sundown.**

An old woman at the bread stall leans in and whispers it like she already knows: *"Dream-traveler, eh? You'd best see the Clockkeeper on the hill. He's the only one who remembers the way back."*

So you go — trading the weird coin you found in your pocket for a warm roll, following the smell of pine up the hill, past fields of wheat swaying in a wind you can *feel* on your skin. Your modern memories start to get fuzzy — what was a password again? What was Wi-Fi? — and you realize you need to hold onto one memory, just one, to anchor you.

You choose the sound of someone you love laughing.

The Clockkeeper has no clock. Just a tower full of sundials and hourglasses and candles marked with lines. He smiles when he sees you.

"Ready?" he asks.

And just as the sun dips and the last bell tolls, he turns over the hourglass — the sand falls UP —

and you wake up.
