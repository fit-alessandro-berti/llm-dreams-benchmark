The title of the project reads: *Catalog the Weight of Every Spoken Word.* The deadline is 5:00 PM. 

You look up. Your boss is standing entirely too close, wearing a tight, polite corporate smile. Behind their glasses, their eyes aren't eyes at all, but the spinning, brass hands of a stopwatch. *“We’re counting on you,”* they say, though their mouth never moves. 

You scramble to your desk. You reach for the keyboard, but your fingers feel impossibly heavy, as if you’re trying to type through wet concrete. Every time you strike a key, it doesn’t produce a letter on the monitor; it releases a tiny, chiming bell that floats into the air and pops like a soap bubble. 

Around you, the office begins to stretch. The gray carpet rolls out toward an infinite horizon, and the hum of the fluorescent lights grows into a deafening, rhythmic roar: *tick, tick, tick.* You check the wall clock, but the numbers have melted into a single, glowing smear, and the hands are spinning so fast they create a breeze that shuffles your water-pages.

Panic seizes your chest. It’s 4:59 PM. You haven’t even typed the first word. 

You open your mouth to call out, to say *“I need more time,”* but your voice comes out completely silent, like a muted television. 

Your boss leans over your shoulder, casting a shadow that swallows the entire room, and whispers, *“Time's up.”*

At that exact moment, the floor beneath your swivel chair simply gives way. You fall backward into a rush of cool, dark air, watching your desk and your glowing monitor shrink into tiny stars far above you—

—and your eyes snap open. 

Your bedroom ceiling is still. The morning light is pale on the wall. Your heart is hammering against your ribs, racing to meet a deadline that was never real.
