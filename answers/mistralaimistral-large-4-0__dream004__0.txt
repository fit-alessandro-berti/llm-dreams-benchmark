You reach out, and your fingertips hover just above the surface, feeling the hum of potential energy vibrating through the air like a plucked string. The canvas is impossibly large, stretching upward into a sky that bleeds from violet to amber, its edges dissolving into the swirling landscape around it.

As your shadow falls across the white expanse, the blankness shivers. Not with emptiness, but with recognition. The surface ripples like water, and suddenly you see yourself reflected—not as you are now, but as you were a thousand dreams ago, and as you might become a thousand dreams hence. Your reflection smiles, though you haven't moved your lips.

The brush appears in your hand without weight, its bristles made of spun starlight. When you make the first stroke—a hesitant arc of cerulean—the color doesn't stay on the canvas. Instead, it peels away from the surface and becomes real, solidifying into a bird with wings like stained glass that takes flight into the shifting sky. With each stroke, the dream world rearranges itself: geometric flowers bloom at your feet, gravity loosens its grip so that you float upward, and the distant horizon folds in on itself like origami.

The canvas begins to fill with the things you've forgotten—the name of a childhood friend written in cursive gold, a door you once feared to open painted in deep crimson, your own heartbeat rendered as a jagged line of emerald. It is painting you back into existence, stroke by stroke, memory by memory.

Then the canvas turns. Slowly, deliberately, it rotates on a hinge you cannot see, revealing that it has no back—only another blank space, infinite and waiting. The world behind you has changed; the shifting colors have settled into a single, perfect hue of dawn-pink, and you realize with a start that you are standing inside the painting now, a figure composed of brushstrokes and possibility, looking out at a new blank canvas that waits in the distance, already humming with your name.
