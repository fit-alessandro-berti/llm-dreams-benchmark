…and you take the wheel with both hands, feeling the storm in your bones before it hits the deck. You do not shout. You breathe. “We don’t fight the sea,” you tell the crew. “We listen to it.”

They stare at you, terrified, then begin to move—not in panic, but in rhythm. Ropes sing. Sails snap. The ship climbs a black wave and hangs there, suspended between lightning and deep water. In that moment you see it: the storm has a pattern, and the pattern has a heart. You steer toward it.

The sea opens like a door. Beyond it is a quiet harbor you have never seen but somehow remember. The crew laughs, soaked and shaking. The sky clears. Then the dream shifts, and you understand the ship was never a ship—it was your fear, your hope, your life—and the helm was always in your hands. You wake with salt on your lips and your heart still steering.
