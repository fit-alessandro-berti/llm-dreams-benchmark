...stop talking.

Not because I've run out of words, but because I notice the words are no longer coming out of my mouth. They're coming out as small paper birds, folding themselves mid-air and fluttering to the floor between us. My friend's words are doing the same thing, except theirs are blue and mine are yellow. The floor is ankle-deep in them already. I can't remember when that started.

"Look," I say, and a single yellow bird lifts off my tongue and lands on their shoulder.

They look down. For a moment, neither of us moves. Then they crouch and pick up one of their own blue birds and unfold it. Inside, in handwriting I somehow know is theirs from when we were twelve, it says: *I'm scared you'll leave.*

I pick up one of mine. It says: *I'm scared I'm wrong.*

We're in my childhood kitchen now, though a moment ago we were somewhere else, somewhere with fluorescent lights. The clock on the wall is ticking backward, and it feels completely normal. The argument is still here too. I can feel it sitting in the third chair at the table, patient and heavy, like a guest who hasn't been asked to leave.

"We still disagree," my friend says. This time the words come out as ordinary sound.

"I know."

"I don't think I'm going to change my mind."

"I don't think I am either."

And strangely, that's the moment the room gets lighter. The paper birds begin to rise all at once, blue and yellow together, swirling up through the ceiling, which isn't there anymore. Above us is the sky from the summer we met, the exact shade of orange I'd forgotten I remembered.

My friend laughs, the real laugh, the one that snorts at the end, and says, "This is so stupid," and I say, "It's *extremely* stupid," and we're both crying a little, and the argument in the third chair stands up, nods politely, and walks out the back door into the orange light.

It didn't leave because anyone won. It left because it was never the thing we were actually afraid of.

I reach out to hug them, and just as my arms close around their shoulders, they turn into the sound of my alarm clock. But I wake up with my hand still curled like it's holding a small paper bird, and the feeling stays with me all morning: *call them.*
