You find yourself in a vast, unfamiliar city, and you realize you have an important meeting in an hour but no idea where it is. You start to run.

The streets are too wide, the buildings too tall, their windows reflecting a sky that changes color with every glance. You pass a café where everyone is drinking from empty cups, a bookstore with no titles, a clock whose hands spin backward. You ask a stranger for directions, but when they turn, their face is a smooth, blank mirror — and in it you see yourself, smaller, farther away, running down the same street.

You keep going. The city unfolds like a paper maze, each turn folding shut behind you. Your phone buzzes. The message reads: *You're already here.* You look down. The phone is made of wet sand. The address is written in your own handwriting on your palm, but the ink keeps bleeding into new words: *the place where you forget* / *the place where you remember* / *the place.*

You stop in a plaza where all the fountains are playing music you've never heard but somehow know by heart. A door stands alone in the middle of the pavement, painted the color of your childhood front door. You know it's the meeting room.

You open it. Inside, the room is empty except for a single chair. You sit. The clock on the wall ticks once, loudly, like a heartbeat. You wait. And then you realize: the meeting was never with anyone else. It was with this moment — this quiet, impossible city — and you have arrived exactly on time.

And you wake up, smiling, with the feeling of a door still closing softly behind you.
