…written in a language you have never studied, though somehow every symbol looks faintly ashamed, as if it knows it has no business being there.

You turn the page. The questions rearrange themselves into English, then into your mother’s voice, then into a math problem whose numbers are dates from your life.

**Question 1:** Explain the difference between a calling and a cage.  
**Question 2:** Prove that you are not afraid.  
**Question 3:** Draw the door you would open if you knew it would close behind you.

The proctor tells you to begin. He has your face, but older, and he is wearing the uniform of a job you’ve never applied for. Around you, other students write confidently; their pens make the sound of rain. Your pencil is warm. The answer sheet has only one blank line, labeled: **FUTURE**.

You start to write, but the letters drift off the page and become a staircase. You climb it because the bell is ringing, or maybe because you’re waking up. At the top is a little desk with one final question, written in your own handwriting:

**What would you choose if no one were grading you?**

You answer before you wake:

**“Something that feels like this.”**
