My throat tightened like a fist made of static. The deadline—written in neon on a crumpled sticky note that smelled faintly of burnt toast—read: *“Done by sunrise.”* But sunrise was *three hours ago*. I blinked. The clock on the wall was spinning backward, then forward, then showing my childhood bedroom wall.  

My boss stood just inside the doorway, exactly as always—blazer slightly askew, coffee mug half-full—but when I opened my mouth to say “This isn’t feasible,” his eyes softened, and the edges of his face dissolved not into fog, but into tiny, fluttering paper cranes made of yesterday’s meeting notes. They rose silently, filling the empty space where his shoulders should be, each crane humming a single note of the elevator music from last Tuesday.  

He didn’t speak. He just placed one crane—small, slightly crumpled—into my palm. It felt warm. When I looked down, the crane unfolded into a single, perfect line of code that wrote itself: `// breathe; // the deadline is a suggestion, not a wall.` The sticky note in my pocket dissolved into dandelion seeds. Outside the window, the office hallway stretched into a meadow under a sky that hadn’t yet decided if it was night or dawn.  

I didn’t start the project.  
I sat down in the empty chair by the window, closed my eyes, and watched the first seed float up.  
No one rushed me.  
The boss—still mostly cranes, still smiling gently—nodded once, and the meadow sighed.  

*(Dream logic: the impossible deadline wasn’t meant to be met rigidly; it was a pressure that released itself through a tiny, physical act—the crane, the breath, the seed. No yelling, no panic spiral. Just quiet, soft surrender to the absurdity, which felt strangely right. The dream ends not with resolution, but with the simple, peaceful choice to *pause*—exactly what the body needed.)*
