You start to run.

The streets are too wide and too quiet for a city that should be bustling at this hour. Glass towers reflect a sun that never seems to move, and the sidewalks are clean in that impossible, freshly-painted way dreams have. You pull out your phone — the screen is black, then shows a calendar notification that just reads **MEETING - URGENT** with no location, no time zone, no address. The battery is at 1%. You try to call someone, anyone, but the numbers in your contacts have all been replaced with names you almost recognize: *MOTHER 2*, *BOSS (OLD)*, *THE BOY FROM 3RD GRADE*.

You ask a passerby in a long coat where you’re supposed to be. He doesn’t stop walking. He turns his head and his face is yours, but older, tired. He mouths the words “you’re late” without sound. 

You try to follow the street signs, but they change every block. 5th Avenue becomes 5th Avenue again, then becomes *The Street Where You Left Your Keys in 2007*. The buildings lean in as you pass, windows like eyes. A billboard flickers on with your own face from a yearbook photo, and underneath it says *WILL YOU REMEMBER ME?*

Panic makes you faster. Your shoes make no sound. You duck into an alley that smells like warm laundry and ozone and find a single door propped open with a red brick. A brass plaque reads **MEETING ROOM**. Inside, a long table sits under a single buzzing fluorescent light. Chairs are empty except for one at the far end where a woman in a blazer is already waiting, tapping a pen.

“Sorry I’m late,” you say, out of breath, even though you don’t know her. 

She smiles kindly and slides a paper across the table. It’s blank. “You’re right on time,” she says. “We’ve been waiting for you to find the room.”

The clock on the wall has no hands, only a soft ticking that sounds like your own heartbeat. The door behind you clicks shut. The walls begin to breathe in slowly, and the city outside the window stretches and folds like paper.

You realize, with a sudden and absolute calm, that the meeting was never about the place or the time. It was about being here, lost and looking.

And then your alarm goes off.
