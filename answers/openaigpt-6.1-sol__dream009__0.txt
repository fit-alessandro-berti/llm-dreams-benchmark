…find out what year it is before anyone discovers you don’t belong.

The street outside is paved with uneven stones. Wooden signs creak above doorways, and the air smells of woodsmoke, rain, and freshly baked bread. You reach into your pocket for your phone, but find only a small brass key.

“About time,” someone says.

An old woman sits beneath a striped awning, repairing a coat. Without looking up, she points her needle toward a tower at the end of the street.

“They’ve been waiting for you.”

“Who has?”

She bites through a thread. “Go and see.”

You approach the tower, passing carts and people carrying baskets. Its clock face has thirteen numbers, but no hands. At the door, you try the brass key. It fits.

Inside, a narrow staircase curls upward. You climb until you reach a room filled with ticking clocks—some carved from wood, others fashioned from glass. None shows the same time.

A boy stands beside an empty pedestal.

“You brought it,” he says.

You hold out the key.

“No.” He points to your closed hand.

You look down. Somehow, you’re holding your phone again. Its screen is cracked, and the battery reads one percent.

The boy steps back as you place it on the pedestal. Every clock in the room stops.

Your screen lights up with a photograph of your bedroom. You can see yourself asleep beneath the blankets. Beside the bed stands the old woman from the market, still holding her needle.

A message appears:

**Come back before she finishes the coat.**

You turn toward the stairs, but they’re gone. In their place hangs a striped curtain.

From the other side comes the soft, steady sound of sewing.
