You are in a maze filled with complex puzzles. Each puzzle requires intense concentration and logical thinking to solve, and as you move deeper into the maze, you notice the walls beginning to change. The stone bricks seem to breathe, expanding and contracting like lungs, and the torchlight flickers in colors that shouldn't exist — pale violet, then a green like deep ocean water.

The first puzzles were simple: sliding tiles, locked doors with number sequences. But now the challenges grow stranger. A doorway appears only when you close your eyes and walk forward blindly, trusting the maze not to hurt you. A bridge over a chasm of swirling fog materializes only when you recite a memory aloud — you choose your grandmother's kitchen, the smell of cinnamon, and the stones hum as they assemble beneath your feet.

Deeper still, the puzzles become personal. A mirror blocks the corridor, and written across it in condensation is a riddle whose answer is your own name — but written backwards, it reads like someone else entirely. You whisper it, and the mirror ripples like water, letting you pass through.

Then you arrive at the final chamber. There are no doors, no levers, no cryptic inscriptions. Just a small table with a single puzzle piece resting on it, and an empty space on the floor beside it shaped exactly like the piece. You realize with dream-logic certainty that this is the last puzzle: you must decide whether to place the piece and complete the maze — or keep it, and remain forever unfinished, forever wandering.

Your hand trembles as you reach for it. The piece is warm, like a living thing. Just as your fingers close around it, the walls begin to dissolve into white light, and you hear a faint voice — perhaps your own — saying something about morning, about coffee, about getting up.

You wake with the shape of the puzzle piece still pressed into your palm.
