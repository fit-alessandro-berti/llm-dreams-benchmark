...you reach for the keyboard, but your fingers won't cooperate. Every key you press produces the wrong letter, and the screen fills with words in a language you *almost* recognize. Behind you, your boss's breathing grows louder, though when you glance back, he's somehow farther away than before, and his tie has become a small silver fish, swimming patiently in midair.

The deadline is tomorrow. No—wait—it's in one hour. Someone has written this on the wall, and the wall smells faintly of your grandmother's kitchen. You try to open the project file, but the computer opens a window into your childhood bedroom instead, and you know—the way you simply *know* things in dreams—that if you crawl through it, you will never finish on time.

Your coffee cup has filled itself with sand. The printer churns out page after page of a single repeated word: SOON. SOON. SOON.

A coworker appears beside you. It's your third-grade teacher wearing your coworker's badge. She leans in and says gently, *"Everyone here already knows the deadline isn't real,"* and you're flooded with relief—and then instant panic, because relief in dreams never lasts more than a breath.

The floor tilts. Your chair rolls backward toward the elevator that has appeared where your desk used to be. Its doors stand open on soft darkness, and hanging neatly inside, on little hooks, are all the socks you've ever lost—one of them, unmistakably, is your boss, folded with his arms crossed.

Then a sound: your alarm, or thunder, or both—a flat electronic beep rolling across a distant storm.

You wake at 3:14 a.m., heart hammering, certain for three full seconds that the report is due today. Then you remember it's Saturday. You lie there in the dark, listening to your actual heartbeat, which sounds nothing like an elevator, and slowly the impossible deadline dissolves—but a small anxious grain of it stays lodged somewhere under your ribs until morning.
