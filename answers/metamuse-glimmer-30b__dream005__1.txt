You are at a large social gathering where you only know one person. As you try to find them, you encounter various groups of people and none of them will look you in the eye.

They are all laughing around low tables that are too crowded with plates you don't recognize. Someone is always mid-sentence when you get close, and the sentence never finishes. You ask "Have you seen Maya?" or "Do you know where Alex is?" and they smile politely and point you in a different direction, always a direction you just came from.

The house is too big. It was a ballroom when you walked in, then a hallway, then a kitchen with a long island and then suddenly a stairwell that goes up forever. The chatter follows you but doesn't get louder, like it's coming from inside your head. You keep checking your phone for a message but the screen is just static, and when you swipe it shows photos of people you don't know in places you've never been.

You find a bathroom and try to catch your breath in the mirror. Your face is there, but for a second it isn't, and when you blink you are suddenly on the other side of the glass looking out. The door handle is warm. Someone knocks once, polite, and says "They're waiting." You open the door and the hallway has changed again — now it's a school hallway with lockers, and the one person you know is standing by locker 214, half-turned away.

You run faster, but the floor is sticky and your shoes make no sound. By the time you get there the locker is empty, except for a single name tag pinned to the metal that says your own name. Behind you, all the groups you passed have merged into one long line stretching down the hallway, and they're all holding the same glass you had in your hand when you first arrived.

You turn to ask one of them where everyone is going, and they all turn to you at once and say in unison: "You were supposed to be here."

You wake up with the taste of metal in your mouth and your hands still clutching the sheets like a doorknob.
