The box is warm. Not sun-warm, but body-warm, like something alive is sleeping inside. I press my ear against the lid and hear breathing — or is it the sea? In dreams, the two sound the same.

Then I realize there's no keyhole. There never was. Where a keyhole should be, there's a knot — a knot of shadow tied tight around itself. And I understand, suddenly and completely, the way you understand things in dreams, that the knot can only be untied from the inside.

So I knock. Three times, softly, the way you'd knock on a friend's door.

The knot loosens. The lid drifts open like a door in a house where no one is home. Inside, there is no treasure, no map, no answer. Inside the box is a smaller box, and inside that, a smaller one still — boxes nested like Russian dolls, shrinking down and down until, at the very center, no bigger than a seed, there is a tiny glowing version of the dream itself: the room I'm standing in, the box, me — all of it, folded up small and warm.

I pick it up and it hums against my palm. And I know, without being told, that if I open it, I'll wake up. But if I keep it closed, I'll stay here forever, holding the dream inside the dream inside the dream.

The box grows heavier. Or I grow sleepier. The walls of the room begin to breathe.

I open it.

Light spills out — and I wake with the feeling of something small and warm still cupped in my hand, though my hands are empty, and the morning is gray and quiet, and somewhere, faintly, I can still hear the sea.
