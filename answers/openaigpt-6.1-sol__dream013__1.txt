At first, you think this is another puzzle. You count your steps, mark corners with chalk, and trace the branching corridors on your sleeve. But each time you check the map, the lines have rearranged themselves into unfamiliar constellations.

You reach a room containing a stone table and three locked boxes. Above them, an inscription reads:

**One contains the way out. One contains the reason you entered. One is empty.**

Each box bears the same label: **OPEN THE OTHER ONE.**

You sit down and reason through the possibilities. Hours seem to pass. Your chalk wears to a nub. Somewhere behind the walls, water drips at intervals that almost form a pattern.

Finally, exhausted, you rest your forehead on the table.

A tiny voice comes from beneath it.

“Are you finished?”

“With the puzzle?”

“With being certain it’s a puzzle.”

Under the table sits a fox made of folded paper. One of its ears is covered in your handwriting.

“What should I do?” you ask.

The fox nudges an unlocked latch on the underside of the table. You lift it, and all three boxes spring open.

Inside the first is a warm loaf of bread. Inside the second is a photograph of a doorway you recognize but cannot place. Inside the third is rain, falling upward.

You eat the bread. You put the photograph in your pocket. The rain rises around you, carrying the ceiling away.

Beyond it stretches a field beneath an enormous blue sky. The maze walls are only waist-high now, their elaborate corridors crowded with grass.

You step over one.

Behind you, the paper fox unfolds into your old map and floats down onto the stone table.

At the edge of the field stands the doorway from the photograph. Someone is knocking from the other side.

You approach, suddenly afraid you are late.

Then you recognize the knock.

It is the sound your own hand makes against a door.
