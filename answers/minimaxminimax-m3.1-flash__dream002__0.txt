The first thing you notice is that the birds stop.

Not gradually — not the way they thin out at dusk, one by one, choosing their roosts. They stop all at once, as if someone has laid a hand over the forest's throat. The silence that follows is so complete you can hear the sap moving in the trees, a slow, wet clicking, like knuckles cracking.

Then the light goes strange. It doesn't darken so much as *green* — the sun comes through the canopy looking like something seen through deep water, and the shadows beneath the ferns take on the hard edges of old iron. The air thickens. You taste it: iron, and behind that, the sweet green smell of a wound in the earth. Somewhere far off, but closer than it should be, there is a sound like a mountain remembering it had once been a child.

You look up.

The sky is the wrong color. It is rolling, folding over itself in vast slow sheets, and in the folds you can see — you *swear* you can see — the shapes of things. Not clouds. Wings, maybe. Or the ribs of something enormous, turning over in sleep above the treeline.

And then you understand, with a certainty that arrives fully formed in your chest without passing through your mind: the forest is not afraid of the storm. The forest is *making* it. Every tree is standing up straight and pulling in its breath, and the ground beneath your feet has gone taut as a drumskin, and you realize you are standing in the middle of something that breathes.

You should run. You know you should run.

But your feet have sunk into the moss, and the moss is holding you the way a hand holds a small, warm thing — gently, so as not to let go. The wind arrives. It comes down through the leaves from above and it smells of rain and pine resin and something older, something with a name you almost had, a name you swallowed as a child along with a story about being afraid of thunder.

The storm touches your hair.

And the forest says, in a voice made of the pressure between the trees, in the language of sap and stone and the small bright panic of every bird asleep in the hollow trunk near your shoulder:

*You came back.*
