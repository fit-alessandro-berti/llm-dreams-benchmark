The audience doesn’t seem puzzled. They lean forward, catching the bubbles in their hands, and wherever a bubble touches, they smile. You realize you’re not reciting your speech anymore; you’re remembering it, and every memory is more vivid than the last.

Then the stage floor softens, becoming grass. The spotlight becomes sunlight. The crowd is no longer seated in rows but standing in a meadow, and you are barefoot, talking to them like old friends. You have no idea what you’re saying, but it doesn’t matter — because in the dream, everyone already understands.

The speech ends before you’re ready. The applause sounds like rainfall, and the curtain turns into a warm blanket. You bow, and as you rise, you realize you’re back in your bed, still smiling, the echo of that dream audience fading into morning light.
