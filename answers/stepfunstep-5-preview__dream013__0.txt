The first threshold presents you with a door made entirely of mirrors, but the reflections show you not as you are, but as you were at every age you've ever been. The lock requires you to arrange the reflections in order, but as you reach out, your fingers pass through glass and come back wet with ink. You realize the mirrors are not showing memories; they are showing drafts of a decision you haven't made yet. You arrange them not by age, but by the weight of regret each face carries. The door dissolves.

Beyond, the maze opens into a cathedral of mathematics. The floor is a chessboard stretching into darkness, with equations carved into each square. To cross, you must solve them, but the variables are emotions. Grief equals distance; longing equals friction. You calculate your path carefully, but halfway across, you notice the pieces moving on their own—giant ivory and obsidian shapes that ignore the rules of the board. They are not chess pieces but people you know, frozen mid-gesture, waiting for you to complete their sentences to release them.

At the center, where geometry folds back on itself like a Möbius strip, the final puzzle waits. It is a simple box with no keyhole, no seam, no hinge. Around it, the maze itself pauses, holding its breath. You understand now that the puzzle requires no logic at all, only absolute, focused honesty. You must tell the truth about why you entered the maze in the first place. You open your mouth—

and the walls exhale. The floor drops away, not as a trap, but as an invitation, and you fall upward into a room that is identical to your childhood bedroom, except the window looks out onto every maze you have ever dreamed of, all at once, all waiting.
