As you step onto the stage, the lights are warm and golden, and the crowd is a sea of faces that somehow shimmers like water. You grip the edges of the podium, but your hands don't feel like yours. They're lighter, almost translucent, as if you could slip them through the wood.

You begin to speak, and the first words leave your mouth as bubbles of color that drift up toward the ceiling and pop into soft music. The audience gasps softly, then leans in. You hear yourself say something you didn't plan, something about the house you grew up in, which now has a staircase that goes on forever. The crowd nods as though they've all been there too.

You look down at your notes and find they've rearranged themselves into a map of a city you've never visited. Streets curl like ribbons, and a small silver train is making its way along one of them. You decide to follow it, and your voice begins to rise and fall like a tide, carrying the audience with it.

Somewhere in the middle of a sentence, you notice a child in the third row. She's holding a balloon that is slowly turning into a bird. She winks at you, and you know, without knowing how, that she's the reason you're here. So you tell the story she's been waiting to hear, and as you finish, the walls of the hall melt away, and the whole audience rises together, floating gently up into a sky full of stars.
