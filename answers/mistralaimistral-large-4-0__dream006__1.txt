…speak, but the words solidify in the air before they leave your lips—crystalline, fragile things that catch the stage lights and refract them into rainbows across the faces of the crowd. The audience gasps, not in judgment, but in wonder, as the words float upward like lanterns. One by one, they drift toward the ceiling, where they pop with soft, musical sounds, releasing scents of lilac and ozone.

You realize with a start that you are not wearing shoes, and the stage floor is not wood but cool, wet sand. The ocean is lapping at your ankles. The crowd has become a forest of coral, swaying in a current you cannot see, and you are speaking not to people but to the deep, listening to your voice echo through water that tastes of copper and honey. You try to stop, but your mouth continues on its own, telling stories you don't remember learning—about a door that only opens at twilight, about a name that hasn't been spoken in three hundred years, about the exact weight of a secret.

The lights dim. The water rises to your waist. You keep speaking, because the dream has already decided what you will say, and the coral leans closer, listening.
