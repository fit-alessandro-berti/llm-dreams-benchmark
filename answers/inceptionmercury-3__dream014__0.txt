They’re holding a map made of threads of light, and each thread leads somewhere you thought you’d lost.

One of them smiles and says, “You were never lost. You just forgot the way home.”

As they walk with you, the city begins to soften — the neon signs blur into fireflies, the traffic roar becomes a lullaby, and the pavement turns to warm, familiar grass. You look down and find your feet know the path without being told.

When you turn to thank them, they’re already fading into the morning light — not disappearing, but becoming part of you. You wake up with the feeling of a hand on your shoulder, and a street name on your lips you’ve never heard before.

You go there that afternoon. At the end of the block, there’s a small café with a table saved just for you — and a menu with your name written on it, in handwriting you somehow remember.
