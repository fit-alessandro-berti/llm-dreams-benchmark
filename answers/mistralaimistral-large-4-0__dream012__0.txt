You grip the wheel until your knuckles turn white as bone, though strangely the wood feels warm, almost alive, pulsing with a heartbeat that matches the thunder above. The crew stands frozen at the rail, their mouths open in silent screams, but you realize they aren't looking at the waves—they're looking *through* you, at something flickering behind your eyes.

The lightning doesn't flash; it lingers, painting the sky in shades of violet and gold that don't exist in the waking world. When you shout orders, your voice comes out as the sound of breaking glass, and the ship doesn't rock—it *breathes*. The waves aren't water anymore; they're sheets of silver paper, thousands of them, each one covered in handwriting you almost recognize: names, dates, moments you've forgotten you lived.

You steer toward the eye of the storm, not to escape it, but because you suddenly understand that the tempest isn't trying to sink you. It's trying to wake you up.

And as the first ray of impossible sunlight pierces the clouds, you feel the deck dissolve beneath your boots, and you begin to fall—not down, but *up*, into the light, while the crew below raises their hands not in farewell, but in greeting, as if they've been waiting for you to remember that you were never the captain at all.
