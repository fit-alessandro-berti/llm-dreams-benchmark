You decide to keep walking toward the faint green glow on the horizon, the one the other survivors call a ghost light. Your canteen holds three swallows of water; your boots are held together with wire and luck. But staying still feels like dying slower.

The road cracks open into weeds, then vines, then a hidden greenhouse slumped against a collapsed overpass. Inside, a single tomato plant has threaded through a rusted car seat. You don’t eat the fruit. You kneel, press your palms into the warm soil, and listen. The silence doesn’t feel empty anymore. It feels like something waiting to grow.

You wake with dirt under your fingernails that isn’t there.
