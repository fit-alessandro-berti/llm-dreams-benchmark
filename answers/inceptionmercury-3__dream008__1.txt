Your boss taps their watch—which has no hands. The office lights flicker into a forest of filing cabinets, each drawer breathing softly.

You know the deadline is a door, and you have to walk through it, but every step takes you backward. The project is no longer a project: it’s a tiny bird in your palm, singing the report you were supposed to write. You understand, in that dreamlike way, that deadlines are just another way of saying *let go*. You open your hand, and the bird flies straight into the sun.

Your boss smiles and says, “That’s the best deliverable I’ve ever seen.”

And you wake up—strangely peaceful, as if the impossible had always been possible.
