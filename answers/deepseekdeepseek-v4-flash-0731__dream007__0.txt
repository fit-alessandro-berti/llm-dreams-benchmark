You decide to stop talking mid-sentence. The words hang in the air like fog. Your friend’s face is flushed, eyes bright with frustration—but also something else. You notice the details shifting slightly, the way dreams do. The room grows softer at the edges, the colors bleeding like wet paint.

You take a breath. “Wait,” you say. “I don’t actually want to win. I want to understand why this matters so much to you.”

The silence that follows is deep and wide, like standing at the edge of a lake at night. Your friend’s shoulders drop. The anger in their face melts into something more fragile, and they say, quietly, “Because I thought if you really knew me, you’d already know that.”

The dream tilts. You reach out and hold their hand. It feels warm and real—more real than the room, more real than the argument. And in the way dreams make impossible things true, you both understand each other completely at once. No more words needed. The fight dissolves like morning mist.

Then someone calls your name from far away. The dream begins to shimmer and fade, and you wake with your friend’s voice still echoing softly in your ears, saying, “Thank you for seeing me.”
