You fumble for your phone, but the screen glows with a calendar dated three years from now. You try to type the meeting address, but the letters rearrange themselves into a language you almost—almost—understand. The battery icon is a small, laughing moon.

A stranger in a gray coat offers directions, gesturing down a street that wasn't there a moment ago. You follow it anyway. The pavement ripples like the surface of a pond. You pass a café where everyone at every table is checking their watch, and you realize with growing dread that they're all waiting for you.

Finally, you find a building that might be the right one. The elevator buttons are labeled with floors that don't exist: 14, 23, ⦿, NOW. You press one and the doors open directly into a conference room—except it's your childhood bedroom, and someone is sitting at your old desk, slowly turning around.

It's you. And you look disappointed.

"You're late," your double says, "and you're not even dressed for it."

You look down. You're wearing pajamas made of maps—maps of every city you've never been to. One of them might be this one. You try to read the street names, but you wake up before you can.

Heart pounding. An hour before your alarm. The ceiling of your actual bedroom, familiar and still. Somewhere, faintly, the sound of a city that stays where you left it.
