You start to run, but the motion doesn’t feel like yours—more like the city is sliding past you on a conveyor belt of moving sidewalks. The buildings are too tall to be made of stone; they seem knitted from light and shadow, and every street sign is in a language you understand only in flashes: **NOW**, **SOON**, **ALMOST**, **NEVER**.

Your pocket buzzes. You pull out your phone to check the address, but the screen has become a pool of still water reflecting a sky you’ve never seen. A paper airplane slips from your fingers, unfolds itself into a map, and points north. You follow it, though *north* keeps migrating.

The streets narrow into corridors of doors. Behind each one, you hear your own voice rehearsing apologies, presentations, confessions—none of them sound ready. You knock on one, but it has no knob, only a keyhole shaped like your eye. When you lean close, the door dissolves. Through it, you see the meeting: a long table in a room made of rain, where faceless colleagues sit waiting, their watches all ticking backward.

The map tears itself in half. One piece says **TOO LATE**; the other says **YOU’RE EARLY**. You press them together, and the paper combusts into a flock of starlings that lead you four blocks, then nine, then one hundred, to a building that hums when you touch its frame. The lobby is filled with your own breath. The elevator moves sideways.

On the top floor, there are no walls, only the city suspended in amber, frozen mid-stride. At the center sits a table with a single chair pulled out, waiting. You sit. The clock on the wall has no hands, but you know, somehow, that you are exactly on time.
