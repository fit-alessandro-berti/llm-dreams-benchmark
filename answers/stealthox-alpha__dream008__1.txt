...and you sit down at your desk, but the keyboard has somehow grown larger overnight. Each key is the size of a dinner plate. You press one and it sinks slowly, like pressing into wet sand.

The document on your screen is already open, but the words keep rearranging themselves into sentences you never wrote—performance reviews for people you've never met, all addressed to you.

"Three hours," your boss says. You glance at the office clock: it reads 4:47, then 9:15, then *Tuesday*. Outside the window, the sun crosses the entire sky in a single arc and sets twice.

You try to type anyway. Your fingers move, but the letters come out as tiny insects that scurry off the edge of the desk. Behind you, a printer begins producing pages no one sent—hundreds of them, spilling across the floor like snow.

You turn to ask for an extension, but your boss's face keeps subtly shifting—now a coworker, now a teacher from high school, now someone you can't quite place but who looks disappointed in you in a deeply familiar way.

"I just need more time," you say.

"You had all the time," they reply, not unkindly. "It was in the drawer."

You open the desk drawer. It's full of clocks—all of them ticking slightly out of sync, like rain on a tin roof.

And then—the relief of waking. Ceiling. Alarm clock. The particular weight of a weekday morning. For a moment you can still feel the deadline humming somewhere beneath your ribs, until coffee dissolves it, and the impossible project fades back into whatever quiet place unfinished dreams go to wait.
