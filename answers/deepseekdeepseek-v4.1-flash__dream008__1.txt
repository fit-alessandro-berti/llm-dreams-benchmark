You are at your workplace, and suddenly, you are given a project with an impossible deadline. Your boss is waiting for you to start, and you open your mouth to explain, but the words come out as paper clips. The clock on the wall has no hands—only your name, blinking. The project brief is one page, blank except for the line: “Finish before you begin.” You sit down; the chair is a carousel horse. Your keyboard types by itself, filling the screen with a report about a meeting that hasn’t happened yet. The phone rings. It’s you, calling from next Tuesday: “Don’t start. The deadline is a door.” You look up. Your boss is still waiting, but now their face is a calendar with every day crossed out. You stand, walk toward the door marked DEADLINE, and just as you reach for the handle, the dream folds—and you’re back at your desk, your boss waiting, the project impossible, and you finally begin.
