You feel a warm rush of gratitude as you walk beside them through the winding streets. Each stranger takes turns pointing out landmarks—a cracked mosaic on a building front, a lamppost wrapped in flyer paper, a flickering neon sign—all woven into an impromptu map just for you.

The oldest of the group, a man with worn hands and a gentle smile, keeps pace with you even when the others drift ahead. *"This alley feels closer to home,"* he says, and for a moment you think he means the city, but then you realize he means *your* home—a place he somehow senses you've been missing.

The streets narrow. The noise of the bustling city starts to fade. A turn, a bridge, a quiet slope downward. The group whispers among themselves like a crew steering a small boat through fog.

At last, a plain bench beside a low moonlight glow comes into view. *"Here,"* they say with soft certainty. You sit. They linger a moment, then quietly scatter—some upward into frantic light, some along the railing along the river, one into shadow beside you before he too is gone.

You never learn their names. But in your dream, you remember their backs as they left—each one carrying a little of your lostness away.
