...check your pockets for an invitation, but all you find is a key with a number worn off and a receipt written in a language that rearranges itself when you look away.

The streets tilt gently, like the whole city is a ship at anchor. You ask a woman selling umbrellas for directions. She doesn't look surprised. "Everyone's meeting is at the clock tower," she says, "but the clock tower is wherever you're not looking." She presses an umbrella into your hand, though the sky is perfectly clear.

You walk. The buildings grow taller and begin to whisper, not words exactly, more the rhythm of words, like a conversation in the next room. You realize you're following the sound without deciding to. It leads you through an arcade of shop windows, each one displaying a slightly different version of your own apartment.

Somewhere along the way the urgency softens. You notice you're no longer afraid of being late. You just wonder who is waiting for you.

At the end of an alley, the umbrella opens by itself, and rain falls from nowhere, only on you, in a perfect circle. Through the silver drizzle you see the clock tower at last. Its face has no numbers, just a single word, and the word is your name.

The door beneath it is ajar. Inside, a long table is set with a hundred chairs, and every one is empty but one. Someone sits there with their back to you, and as you step closer, you recognize the shape of their shoulders, the way they tilt their head.

They say, without turning around, "Good, you're right on time."

And the clock begins to chime, though you still can't say what hour it's striking.
