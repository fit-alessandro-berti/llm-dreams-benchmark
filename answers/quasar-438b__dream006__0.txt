You are about to give a speech to a large audience. As you step onto the stage, you see the crowd looking expectantly at you, and you begin to speak.

You open with "Ladies and gentlemen..." — but something strange happens. Every time you say a word, the audience hears a *different* word. You say "Welcome," and they hear "Goodbye." You say "thank you," and they hear "weird." But somehow, the wrong words string together into a speech far more beautiful than anything you planned. Poetic. Coherent. Inspiring. The crowd begins to cry — not from sadness but from the sheer beauty of the words you never actually said.

You glance down at your notecards, but the ink is alive. The letters swim and rearrange, tiny black fish, spelling out sentences you've never thought of: *When we close our eyes, the world doesn't disappear. It just waits.*

A child in the third row raises her hand as if to answer an unasked question. Her hand stays raised, and slowly turns to glass — transparent — while the rest of her remains skin and bone. Beside her, a businessman in a sharp gray suit nods, but instead of his head, a second, smaller head nods beneath his chin, like a Russian nesting doll.

You don't panic. You lean into it. You let the dream teach you the speech it wants you to give. You start speaking in metaphors you've never understood before, about rivers that flow backward and mornings that remember themselves. The audience leans forward like leaning into wind.

An elderly couple in the back row begins to age — but only from the waist up. Their legs stay young while their faces turn ancient, lined, wise. The old gentleman leans over and whispers in your ear from the back of the room, even though you're thirty feet away: "Say *remember* again. Please."

Come morning.
