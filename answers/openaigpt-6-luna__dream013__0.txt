…begin to notice that the puzzles are changing behind you.

A corridor that was empty now holds a chessboard, its pieces arranged in a game you somehow remember dreaming before. A door opens only when you name the thing you fear most. Another asks you to choose between two paths, though both seem to lead nowhere.

You stop trying to solve everything at once. Instead, you listen. Beneath the ticking clocks and shifting walls, you hear a faint rhythm—your own heartbeat. You follow it through the maze, answering each puzzle not with certainty, but with the first honest thought that comes to mind.

At last, you reach a small room lit by dawn. In its center is a mirror. You look into it and see yourself, not as you are now, but as you were before you entered: calm, curious, unafraid.

The mirror cracks—not shattering, but opening like a door. Beyond it is your bedroom, quiet and familiar. You step through, carrying no map and no answers, only the strange sense that the maze was never trying to keep you out.

It was teaching you how to find your way back.
