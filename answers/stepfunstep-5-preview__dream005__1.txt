…everyone welcomes you with too much warmth, as if you had just returned from somewhere dangerous.

The first group stands near a staircase that spirals up into fog. They are dressed in evening clothes, holding champagne glasses filled with something silver and swirling. When you ask if they’ve seen the one person you know, they all answer at once in different languages you somehow understand: *“They were just here. Or they will be. It depends on which hallway you trust.”* One of them points toward a door that isn’t there.

You move on. A cluster of people sits on the floor in a tight circle, laughing at a game played with cards showing scenes from a life you don’t recognize. They invite you to sit, and for a moment you almost do, because one of the card faces has your own eyes. But then the floor begins to tilt gently, like a boat, and you step away.

Another group is gathered around a piano made of ice. Every time someone plays a note, it drifts into the air as a small glowing animal and disappears behind the curtains. They wave you over, smiling, and ask what brings you here. When you open your mouth to explain, you say the wrong name—your own name, then your mother’s, then a name you’ve never heard before. The pianist nods sympathetically and keeps playing.

Near a window at the far end of the room, you see a familiar coat. Your chest loosens. You push through the last crowd, murmuring apologies, and reach out—but the figure turns, and it is a stranger with your friend’s posture and someone else’s face.

“Try the balcony,” the stranger says.

You climb a hidden staircase you somehow missed. The gathering continues below, a hundred overlapping conversations, glasses clinking, laughter arriving in waves. Through tall glass doors, on a small balcony lit by a single green lamp, you find them.

They look exactly the same, as if they have been waiting there for hours.

“You’re late,” they say, not unkindly.

“I got lost,” you say.

They glance back at the party. “Everybody here is a version of somebody you used to know. It’s easier to stay on the balcony.”

You stand together at the railing, looking down at the crowd. Somewhere below, the ice piano plays a note, and a glowing animal leaps into the dark. Your friend hands you a glass that wasn’t empty a moment ago. And just before you wake, you realize the gathering is gradually dissolving, room by room, while the two of you remain standing on the balcony, still talking, still lit by that green lamp.
