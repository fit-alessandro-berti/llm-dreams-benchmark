*Question 1: What did you almost become, and why did you stop?*  
*Question 2: Whose disappointment are you wearing today?*  
*Question 3: If this room could speak, what would it call you?*

The pencil in your hand becomes too heavy. Around you, the other students have no faces, only open notebooks that fill themselves with regrets you don’t remember making. The proctor’s watch ticks backward, and each tick removes a year from the classroom walls until the posters show futures that never happened.

You try to answer, but your handwriting comes out in a stranger’s hand—your hand from childhood, the one that believed everything could still be chosen. The moment you write a truthful answer, the paper tears open like a door.

On the other side is your career, waiting in an empty hallway. There are no trophies, no titles, no applause. Only a desk, a lamp, and a nameplate bearing your name in faded letters.

A voice says, calmly: “You pass if you admit which life you actually want.”

You wake with the pencil pressed into your palm, still warm, still trembling.
