At last you hear your friend’s laugh on the other side of a swinging door. You push through and find yourself in the coatroom. Your friend is sitting on the floor among the coats, eating a piece of cake with a teaspoon.

“There you are,” you say. “I’ve been looking everywhere.”

“So have I.” She lifts the hem of a fur coat. Underneath it is a small white goose with a blue ribbon around its neck. “Would you mind getting the door?”

You do. The goose walks into the party with the unhurried air of someone returning from the bathroom. A moment later the three men by the piano let out a great cheer.

Your friend hands you the teaspoon and you finish her cake. Then the two of you go back in. The woman in green makes room for you on the sofa.

“I’m sorry,” you say, “but how *did* the boat get into the kitchen?”

She sets down her glass, delighted. “Well,” she says, “first you have to understand how narrow the river used to be.”

Under your shoes, the carpet gives a gentle roll.
