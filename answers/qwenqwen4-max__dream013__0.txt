...begin to notice that the puzzles are getting easier.

Not simpler. Easier. The locks tumble open a half-second before your hand reaches them. The tile sequences arrange themselves as you approach, like a crowd politely making room. You solve a cipher of interlocking brass rings and feel, instead of triumph, a small cold doubt — the way you feel when a dog brings you a stick you're certain you never threw.

The walls, you realize, are made of the same substance as the puzzles. Not stone. Something closer to chalk, or to the inside of an equation. When you brush against them your sleeve comes away white, and the white smells faintly of a classroom in winter.

You turn a corner and find handwriting on the wall. Your handwriting. *Not this one. Try the other corridor.* Below it, in the same hand, pressed harder: *You have been here eleven times.*

The corridors narrow. Soon they are the width of a thought, and you have to move through them sideways, and then you're not sure you're moving at all — the maze may be flowing past you, the way a sentence flows past someone reading it. On the other side of the wall there is breathing. It matches yours exactly, except that it stops when you stop, a beat too late.

You begin to recognize the puzzles. That one is your grandmother's phone number, the digits scattered and asking to be put back in order. That one is the floor plan of a kitchen you haven't thought about in twenty years, and the task is to find where the light came in. You solve them all instantly and each solution takes something small away from you, and you keep going, because that is what you are doing here, that is the whole of what you are.

At the center is a room the size of a held breath. A table. A chair. On the table, the first puzzle — the very first one, the one from the entrance, still unsolved, patient as a stone.

You understand two things at once, the way you only can in dreams: that solving it will wake you, and that you have been carrying the answer since before you entered.

You sit down anyway. You take your time. You let your hand hover.

And the maze — all of it, every corridor, every wall of soft white logic — goes very quiet, and leans in to watch.
