...the questions are written in a language you almost recognize, the letters shifting slightly whenever you look away. You understand the first few words anyway. *Describe the last thing you were afraid to say.*

Your pen is already in your hand. You don't remember picking it up. It's heavier than it should be, warm, and the ink inside it moves like something alive.

Around you, the examination hall stretches farther than the building could hold. Rows of desks run into a haze where the ceiling should be. The other students don't look up. They're all writing steadily, and when you glance at one, you see that her paper is covered in your handwriting.

The clock on the wall has numbers but no hands. A voice from the front of the room says, calmly and without any sound, "You have as long as you need. Time is not the thing being tested."

You turn to the second question: *Where did you leave the map you were supposed to bring?*

You reach into your coat pocket and find a folded paper. When you open it, the map shows the building you're sitting in, but the lines keep redrawing themselves as you watch. A small red dot pulses in the corner of the room, marked with a single word: *You.*

You look up. The desk beside you is empty now, and the chair is still warm. Someone was just there. On the chalkboard at the front, written in chalk that drips upward instead of down, is a final question you hadn't seen before:

*If the career you're testing for was never the one you wanted, what will you write instead?*

Your pen begins to move on its own. The first line appears on the page before you've decided what it should say, and it's something you've been waiting a long time to admit. As the words form, the hall around you starts to dissolve like paint in water. The desks soften, the rows blur, and the last thing you see is the ink spelling out your answer, bright and certain, just before you wake.
