You are about to give a speech to a large audience. As you step onto the stage, you see the crowd looking expectantly at you, and you begin to… reach for the microphone. But as your fingers brush against the cool metal, the spotlight above flickers and dissolves into a soft, golden mist. The floor beneath your feet feels less like a stage and more like the deck of a ship gliding over a still, dark sea.

You look out at the audience. The rows of faces blur, shifting and merging like watercolors in the rain, until each face reveals itself not as a stranger, but as someone you’ve loved, lost, or always hoped to meet. You straighten your shoulders, not out of nervousness, but out of quiet recognition, and instead of a rehearsed speech, words rise from somewhere deeper than preparation. You begin to speak in rhythm with the gentle sway of the ship, telling stories that aren’t yours alone yet feel familiar in your bones.

The air grows warm. Pages of an unseen manuscript flutter from your hands, but instead of falling, they turn into moths that spiral upward into the mist. You keep speaking, not to persuade or perform, but because the act of voice itself feels like breathing. The audience leans in, not to judge, but to listen. And as your final sentence leaves your lips, the stage, the crowd, and the sea all soften at the edges, fading into the quiet hum of a dream that knows it’s time to wake, but lingers just a little longer in the space between words.
