You trace one of the patterns with your fingertip, and it's warm. Warmer than it should be. Warm like a hand you once held, though you can't remember whose. The pattern pulses gently under your touch, and you realize it's not decoration at all — it's a language. The box is trying to tell you something.

You turn the box over, and the room turns with it. The walls now have doors you didn't notice before — dozens of them, each one slightly the wrong size or the wrong shape. One is round like a porthole. One is tall and thin as a letterbox. One is just an outline of a door drawn in chalk, and somehow you know that's the one that works.

You reach into your pocket, even though you know the pocket was empty a moment ago. Dreams are generous like that. Your fingers close around something small and cold — and you pull out a key, except it's not a key. It's a tooth. Yours, maybe. You lost it when you were seven, and here it is, patient as ever, waiting all this time in a pocket that didn't exist.

The box hums with recognition.

You press the tooth into the seam of the lid, and the patterns bloom open like a flower made of clockwork — petals of brass and light unfolding in every direction. Inside, there is no treasure. Inside, there is a smaller box, identical to the first, locked the same way.

And another inside that one.

And another.

You laugh, because in the dream this doesn't frustrate you — it delights you. It means the mystery never has to end. You reach into your pocket again and find it full of keys now, hundreds of them: teeth, and feathers, and a subway token, and a snowflake that doesn't melt, and the name of someone you loved spelled out in tiny gold letters.

You sit down on the floor of the impossible room, surrounded by nested boxes, and you begin opening them one by one, unhurried, knowing that somewhere in the very center of all of them — smaller than an atom, lighter than a thought — is the thing you've been carrying all along.

You wake just before you reach it. You always wake just before you reach it.

But your hand still curls around the shape of something small, and for a moment, before the morning dissolves it, you remember exactly what it was.
