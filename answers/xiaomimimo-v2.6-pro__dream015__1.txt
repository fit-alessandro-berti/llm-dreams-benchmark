You find yourself in a post-apocalyptic world, with resources scarce and dangers everywhere. Despite the hardships, you decide to **walk toward the distant skyline of what was once a city.**



The road stretches out before you — cracked asphalt split by weeds and wildflowers that don't seem to care the world ended. The morning air smells like rain and rust. Somewhere behind you, you left the shelter of an abandoned gas station where you'd spent three restless nights listening to the wind rattle broken windows.

You travel light. A worn backpack, half a bottle of water, a tangle of matches in a sealed bag, and a crowbar you found leaning against a dumpster like it had been waiting for you.

By midday, you encounter your first soul in weeks — an old woman sitting cross-legged on the median of an overgrown highway, boiling something suspicious in a kettle over a small fire. She doesn't look up.

"You heading to the Ring?" she asks.

You don't know what the Ring is. But you nod anyway. *Why not? It's a dream, after all.*

"Take the left fork past the collapsed billboard," she says. "They're not all bad there. Some of them remember what community used to mean."

She offers you a cup of whatever she's boiling. It tastes like chamomile and smoke. It's the most warmth you've felt in months — real or imagined.

You press on. The left fork takes you through a neighborhood where houses still stand but windows stare back like empty eyes. In one yard, someone has planted a full garden — tomatoes climbing wooden trellises, sunflowers standing guard like sentinels. No one is tending it. The sunflowers seem to *watch* you as you pass.

By evening, you see it. The Ring is an old sports stadium, its outer walls reinforced with scrap metal and shipping containers. Campfires glow inside like a constellation on the ground. A hand-painted banner above the gate reads: **"YOU ARE STILL HERE. SO ARE WE."**

A young woman with sharp eyes and a shotgun across her back steps forward.

"What do you bring?" she asks.

You open your empty hands. You have nothing but yourself and a dream to contribute.

She studies you for a long moment. Then she smiles — tired, cracked, but *real.*

"That's enough. We've been building something here. Not just survival. Something better. *Something worth waking up for.*"

She steps aside. You walk through the gates into the firelight, and for the first time in this broken world, the cold in your chest begins to thaw.

And just as you hear the murmur of voices, laughter — *actual laughter* — and the sound of someone playing a guitar badly but lovingly…

You feel yourself beginning to wake.

*Not yet,* you think. *Please. Not yet.*

The dream resists, tries to hold on, but the light is already creeping in—

*And somewhere, in the space between sleep and morning, you make yourself a promise:*

**Whatever world I wake up to — the real one, the broken one — I will build that Ring. I will leave the door open. I will remember that community is not a luxury. It is the thing that saves us.**

Your eyes open. Sunlight on your ceiling.

But you *remember*.

And that's how the dream changes the waking world. 🌅
