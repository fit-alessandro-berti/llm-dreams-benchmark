As you examine it closely, you notice intricate patterns and symbols that will not hold still. Vines become rivers; rivers become the grain of a table you have not seen in years. If you look too directly the lines freeze; if you look away they crawl. There is no keyhole—only a place where the pattern thins, like skin over a pulse.

You set your palm against it. The wood takes your fingerprints and gives them back rearranged. A seam remembers itself. The lid does not lift so much as unfold, the way a letter unfolds.

Inside is a smaller box, identical, and inside that one a smaller still, until the last is the size of a heartbeat and holds a key of the same dark wood. The key does not fit the first box. It fits a door behind you that was not there when you began.

You turn it. For a moment you hear your own breathing from the other side of the wood, as if you have been the thing waiting to be let out. Then the box is gone. The patterns remain on your wrists, fading the way tide-marks fade. You wake holding nothing except the sense that something was returned to you.
