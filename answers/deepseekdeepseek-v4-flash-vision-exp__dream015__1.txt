…despite the hardships, you decide to build a garden.

Not a big one. Just a patch of earth behind a collapsed supermarket, where the soil is still dark and soft. You clear the rubble with your bare hands, carefully, like uncovering something sacred. You find a rusted watering can, half-buried in ash. You fill it from a trickle of rainwater that seeps through a cracked pipe.

You plant seeds you’ve been carrying in a cloth pouch for weeks—saved from an old pantry, traded for a knife, guarded like treasure. Corn, beans, squash. You don't know if they'll grow. The air tastes of smoke and metal, and the sun hides behind a gray haze. But you plant them anyway.

Every morning you walk to the garden. You check the leaves for aphids. You whisper to the sprouts. You talk to them like old friends, because in this world you are allowed to be tender. The soil remembers warmth. The seeds remember light. You watch them rise, pale green, stubborn, alive.

One day, a stranger appears at the edge of your garden. They carry a rifle, a thin blanket, and a child wrapped in a torn coat. They stare at your rows of green shoots, and for the first time in months, their eyes soften. You hand them a handful of beans and a place to sit.

In this dream, the world is broken. But you decide it doesn't have to end that way. You decide to plant a future, one seed at a time, in the ashes of the old one.

And in the morning, the sun breaks through the haze, and it falls on your garden—and maybe, just maybe, on the rest of the world, too.
