...open the project folder. Inside is a single sheet of paper that says **"EVERYTHING — DUE: YESTERDAY."**

You look up to ask a question, but your boss is now a very tall heron in a necktie, tapping one thin foot against the carpet. *Tap. Tap. Tap.* Each tap makes the clock on the wall jump forward an hour.

"I'll need the report in forty languages," the heron says. "Including the one we haven't invented yet."

You nod as if this is reasonable, because in the dream it is. You sit down at your desk, but your keyboard has rearranged itself. The letters are now tiny doors, and when you press the "E," it swings open, and a draft breezes out smelling like your elementary school cafeteria. Somewhere inside, a voice whispers, *You forgot to study for the test.*

You decide to ignore that.

Your coworkers are no help. Dave from Accounting is methodically filing clouds into a cabinet labeled *Q3.* Priya is on a conference call with the ocean, nodding gravely and saying, "Yes, but what are the *deliverables*, tide?" The printer in the corner has become a small, anxious horse that only prints the word *"Soon."*

The deadline, which is written on the whiteboard, keeps sliding down the wall like it's melting. You try to push it back up with your hands, but it's warm and sticky, like taffy, and it gets on your sleeves.

So you do the only logical thing: you climb inside the project folder.

It's bigger in here. It's a long hallway lined with filing cabinets that stretch up into fog, and every drawer you open contains a different version of you. One is finishing the project easily, humming. One is asleep. One is on a beach, sipping something with an umbrella in it, and waves at you, unbothered. "Oh, *that* deadline?" beach-you says. "It isn't real. Check the date."

You pull the sheet of paper out of your pocket. The date on it now reads **"Someday, Probably."**

A great weight lifts off your chest, literally. It's a filing cabinet, and it floats up into the fog like a balloon.

You walk back out of the folder. The heron is still waiting, but now it's holding a cup of coffee for you. "Take your time," it says kindly, in your mother's voice. "Nobody finishes everything."

You sit down, crack your knuckles, and open the first tiny door on your keyboard. This time, sunlight comes out.

And just as you type the very first word, the one that will make everything make sense, the office fills with a loud, rhythmic beeping, and the heron says, "That's your alarm. You should probably get that."

You wake up.

It's 6:47 a.m. Your actual project is due Friday, and it is completely manageable. Still, for the rest of the day, every time you hear someone tapping their foot, you glance over, just in case there's a heron.
