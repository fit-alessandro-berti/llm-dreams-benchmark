You pull out your phone, sighing in relief, only to watch the glass screen ripple like dark water. Where the time should be, glowing symbols shift and rearrange themselves into shapes that look almost like numbers, yet mean nothing at all. 

Panic prickles at the base of your neck. You look up to orient yourself, and the city seems to inhale around you. Towering skyscrapers of mismatched architecture—Gothic stone fused seamlessly with blinding chrome—bend slightly inward, blotting out a sky the color of a healing bruise. 

You try to run toward a busy intersection, desperate to ask someone for directions, but the pavement beneath your shoes suddenly feels soft, like deep, wet sand. Every stride takes monumental effort. People brush past you at impossible speeds, blurred streaks of trench coats and umbrellas, even though it isn't raining. 

"Excuse me!" you try to shout to a man standing by a lamppost, reading a newspaper made of transparent plastic. Your voice comes out as a hollow whisper, swallowed instantly by the hum of the city. 

The man lowers his paper. He has the face of your third-grade teacher, though he’s wearing a tailored pinstripe suit. "You're taking the wrong altitude," he says matter-of-factly, pointing straight up with a pen that is leaking silver ink onto the sidewalk. "The meeting started ten minutes ago on the mezzanine."

Suddenly, you remember. Of course—the mezzanine. Why hadn't you thought of that? 

You spot a brass elevator door set directly into the trunk of a massive, leafless tree growing out of the concrete. Your legs work again, and you sprint inside just as the doors slide shut. There are no floor buttons, only a dial marked with seasons and emotions. Knowing instinctively what to do, you turn the dial past *Autumn* and *Nostalgia*, clicking it firmly onto *Urgency*. 

The elevator doesn't go up; it shoots sideways, accelerating until you feel weightless. 

*Ding.*

The brass doors glide open. The vast city is gone. You step out onto a polished mahogany floor, finding yourself in a sleek, glass-walled boardroom floating high above an endless ocean. A dozen faceless figures in sharp suits sit around a massive table, turning in unison to look at you. 

"Ah, good, you have the presentation," the figure at the head of the table says, gesturing toward your hands. 

You look down. For the first time, you realize you are wearing your pajamas, you have no shoes on, and instead of a briefcase, you are cradling a heavy glass fishbowl sloshing with glowing blue water. You open your mouth to explain, inhaling sharply—and wake up to the sound of your own breathing in a dark, quiet room.
