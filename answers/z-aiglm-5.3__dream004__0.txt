...you realize it's the only thing here that holds still.

Everything else refuses to stay itself. The sky was lilac a moment ago and is now the green of deep water. The hills swell and subside like something breathing. A path unspools ahead of you in ribbons of color that rearrange themselves whenever you blink. But the canvas — leaning on an easel that seems grown rather than built — stays blank. White as a held breath.

A brush appears in your hand, the way things do in dreams. You don't remember picking it up. You don't entirely remember having a hand, but there it is.

You touch the bristles to the canvas and draw a single line, and the line hums. In the corner of your eye, a hill settles into place — the exact grey-blue of the stroke. You paint a circle of yellow, and somewhere behind you, a sun agrees to exist. And you understand, the way you understand things in dreams, completely and without evidence: the world isn't changing for no reason. It's changing because it hasn't been decided yet. The canvas is where it gets decided.

So you paint carefully. A tree. A window. A small blue door standing alone in an open field, attached to nothing, which feels somehow important.

Then your hand keeps going without you. It's a strange feeling, watching your own wrist move as if it belongs to someone patient and much older. The brush sweeps out a horizon, a shoreline, a long slow curve that might be a river or a sleeping body — and then, delicately, at the center of everything, a tiny figure standing before a tiny white canvas.

You lean closer. So does the figure.

You can't tell which side of the painting you're on, and it doesn't seem to matter, and the brush is warm in your hand like something alive, and the edges of the world keep rippling, patient, unfinished, waiting to see what you'll paint next—

And then, the way dreams do, it all goes soft and white. Like a canvas. Like a held breath. You wake with the vague certainty of paint under your fingernails, and somewhere behind your ribs, a small blue door standing in a field, attached to nothing, which feels somehow important.
