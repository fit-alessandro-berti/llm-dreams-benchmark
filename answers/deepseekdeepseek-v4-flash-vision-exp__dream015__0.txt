You decide to keep moving.

The road is cracked and swallowed by weeds, but the old asphalt still glows faintly under a pale, dusty sun. You carry a backpack heavier than it looks—mostly water, a rusted knife, and a photograph of a house you can’t remember. Every step feels like a decision, a refusal.

You walk past the skeletons of cars, their windows shattered like frozen mouths. In the distance, smoke curls from a chimney. Someone is alive. Maybe. Or something that used to be.

Your throat is dry. The wind smells like ash and rain that never comes. Ahead, a child stands in the middle of the road—barefoot, silent, holding a tin can.

You hesitate. In this world, kindness is a risk. But the child doesn’t move. Just stares. And you realize the choice is already made.

You kneel, open your pack, and offer the last of your water.

The child takes it. Doesn’t drink. Just holds the can like a treasure. Then one small hand reaches out and takes yours.

And in that quiet moment, under the ash-gray sky, you decide to trust someone again. Not because it’s wise. Because you are still human.

The road stretches on. But now you’re not walking alone.
