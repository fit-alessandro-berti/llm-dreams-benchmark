The first drops of rain begin to fall, but they aren't water. When they strike your skin, they chime like struck glass, leaving behind small, glowing ripples of warm light. Through the deafening roar of the tempest, a melody emerges—a sound as ancient and familiar as your own heartbeat. 

Higher you drift, breaking through the thrashing crowns of the ancient trees, until suddenly, the howling wind ceases entirely. 

You are suspended in the eye of the storm. All around you stands a towering, silent cylinder of rotating ink-black clouds, its interior glowing with constellations that don't exist in any waking sky. You look down at your hands; they are semi-translucent, reflecting the starlight. 

You exhale, and with that single breath, the storm gently folds in on itself like a closing book, leaving you floating in weightless, peaceful dark, right on the edge of waking up.
