At first, the competition feels like a job interview crossed with a video game. A vast hall of glass doors stands before you, each one marked with a role: “Design,” “Research,” “Code,” “Teach,” “Build.” But every door is slightly ajar, and behind each one you hear the sound of your own heartbeat.

The first challenge is a labyrinth made of résumés. Every turn reveals a version of yourself—more confident, more polished, more tired. You realize the walls are lined with people who once wanted your dream job and are now standing in the hall, waiting to see if you’ll step into their shoes. You don’t have to choose yet. You just have to keep walking.

The second challenge is a mirror room. In it, you meet your future self, who is older, calmer, and holds a small wooden box. “This is what you’re really chasing,” your future self says. Not the title, not the salary, not the applause. The box contains one thing: the moment when you first felt genuinely useful to someone. You open it. Inside is a single seed.

The third challenge is a test you didn’t expect. A board of judges asks you to solve a problem no one has solved before—not with a correct answer, but with a story. You realize the dream job isn’t a door you walk through; it’s a story you keep telling. So you tell one: about a person who wanted a dream job so badly they forgot how to rest. About a competition that turned out to be a training ground. About a labyrinth that was always just a hallway with good lighting.

When the final door opens, there is no prize table. There is only a chair, a notebook, and a blank page. The judges—who turn out to be the people from the hall of résumés—lean back. “Welcome to the actual work,” one says. “Now go make the seed grow.”

You wake up with the taste of paper in your mouth and a notebook already open on your desk. The dream job, it turns out, was always waiting in the page after the one you’re writing now.
