You go looking for the bell tower. The streets smell of horse and hot bread. With nothing in your pocket to consult, you have to keep asking people the way, which is how you acquire half a pear, a blister, and the name of a dog who has decided to come along. His name is Turnip.

At the top of the tower you find a boy asleep beside a rope. Through the open shutters you can see the market woman folding up her awning.

“Is it the third bell yet?” you whisper.

The boy opens one eye. “Not till I ring it.”

“Could you wait till I get back down?”

He sits up. Apparently no one has ever asked him this before. “How fast can you go?”

You and Turnip take the stairs at a run. The first stroke sounds as you cross the square, the second as the woman lifts her latch. At the third, you are inside, laughing too hard to explain why.

There is barley soup and a narrow bed under the eaves. Turnip turns round three times on the blanket. Just before sleep, you remember the alarm you set in another century, and the great relief of knowing you won't hear it.
