Despite the hardships, you decide to plant a garden in the cracked asphalt of an abandoned parking lot.

It starts with a handful of heirloom tomato seeds you traded from a nomad—a small, wrinkled packet labeled *“Summer’s Promise”* in faded ink. You clear the debris, lines of weeds pushing through the cracks like green protests, and build raised beds from rusted car doors and scrap wood. Every drop of rainwater is collected in dented barrels; every hour of sunlight is guarded like treasure.

The first shoots emerge, tiny and defiant, and you find yourself talking to them—quietly, at first, then with full conversations about the world beyond the fence. You name the strongest plant "Hope," and when it produces a single, misshapen tomato, red and imperfect, you eat it slowly, savoring the taste of something that didn't have to be scavenged or stolen.

One evening, as the sun bleeds purple over the ruins, you notice a blue butterfly resting on a leaf—impossible, delicate, a creature that shouldn't exist anymore. It stays for three days. On the fourth, it's gone, but in its place are dozens of tiny eggs, and you realize with a jolt that life, stubborn and beautiful, is finding its way back.

So you keep planting. You save seeds from the healthiest plants and trade them at the weekly market, where people barter batteries and canned goods for packets of green possibility. The garden grows. Others join you—a former teacher who remembers how to graft fruit trees, a kid who builds intricate irrigation systems from plastic bottles. The lot becomes a sanctuary, a patch of living color in a gray world.

And in the quiet moments, when the wind howls through the skeletal buildings and the nights are cold enough to freeze your breath, you look at the rows of green and understand: this is how the world begins again. Not with a bang, not with a grand gesture, but with a seed pushed into dirt, with hands willing to nurture it, with the stubborn belief that something beautiful can grow from ruins.

You drift off to sleep beneath a sky full of unfamiliar stars, dreaming of a future where the only things more abundant than weeds are tomatoes, and laughter, and the sound of children chasing butterflies through fields of wildflowers.
