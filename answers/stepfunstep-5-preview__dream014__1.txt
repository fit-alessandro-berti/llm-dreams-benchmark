As you walk, the city begins to shift around you—the skyscrapers soften into something organic, their windows glowing like fireflies, and the noise of traffic fades into a gentle, rhythmic humming, like a lullaby sung by the earth itself.

They bring you to a small, hidden place: a garden suspended somewhere between two alleyways, lit by lanterns that hang from invisible strings. In the center stands an old wooden bench, and on it sits a map—but not of this city. It’s a map of your own heart, its contours drawn in ink that shimmers and rearranges itself as you watch.

“You weren’t lost,” one of the strangers says, and now you recognize their voice—it sounds like your own, or perhaps like someone you once loved. “You were just walking toward the part of yourself you hadn’t met yet.”

They sit with you as the constellations above slowly rotate, aligning into shapes that feel like memories: childhood laughter, quiet triumphs, moments of tenderness you had forgotten. The city around you breathes, and for the first time, it doesn’t feel like a maze. It feels like an embrace.

When you finally look up to thank them, the bench is empty, but the map remains in your hands, now small enough to fold into your pocket. The garden begins to fade into soft morning light, and you realize the destination was never a place at all—it was this: the sudden, profound knowledge that even in a crowd of millions, you are connected, you are guided, and you are not alone.

Then you wake, cheeks damp with tears you don’t remember crying, the hum of the city still echoing softly in your chest.
