Wind rushes through the canopy. You expect thunder, but instead a deep bell rings somewhere beneath the earth. The forest floor trembles, and a narrow staircase appears between the roots of an ancient oak.

You descend just as the first raindrops begin to fall upward.

Below the forest is a small, warmly lit kitchen. A kettle hums on the stove. At the table sits a fox wearing your childhood raincoat.

“You’re early,” it says, pushing a cup toward you.

“Early for what?”

The fox glances at the ceiling, where roots curl around a sky full of stars.

“For the part where you remember.”

You look into the cup. Instead of tea, it holds a tiny version of the forest. A figure walks beneath the trees, pauses as the wind rises, and looks up at you.

Before you can speak, the fox places one soft paw over your hand.

“Let them find their own way down.”

Above you, the bell rings again. This time, you recognize it: the sound of someone tapping a spoon against a cup, calling you home.
