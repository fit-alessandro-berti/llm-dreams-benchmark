You’re standing on the edge of the neon‑lit crossroads, the city’s roar wrapping around you like a restless tide. A sudden hush falls over the throng as a cluster of strangers—an older woman with a weathered satchel, two younger friends wearing matching scarves—steps forward, their faces lit by soft lanterns.  

“Lost?” she asks, her voice warm as sunrise. “Don’t worry, we’ll show you the way.” One of them gestures toward a narrow alley lined with flickering street‑lamps, another points to a mural of a forgotten garden tucked behind a brick wall. Together they lead you past towering skyscrapers, past the scent of coffee and exhaust, and down a cobblestone lane where the air smells of rain and jasmine.  

As you follow, the strangers share little tales—a legend of a hidden fountain that blooms only when the city sleeps—and hand you a small, polished stone etched with a simple symbol. “This is a compass of memories,” one says, “it will remind you where you’ve been and where you might be heading next.” With each step, the chaos recedes; the city feels less like a maze and more like a living tapestry, and you find yourself breathing easier, no longer alone.
