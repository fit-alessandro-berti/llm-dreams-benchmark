You start to walk, because walking feels like the only reasonable response, and because—you're suddenly certain—you've been here before. The city is unfamiliar the way dream-cities always are: every street is new, yet you *know* that if you take the second left, there will be a bakery. There is a bakery. You feel briefly triumphant.

You take out your phone to look up the meeting, but the screen shows only a photograph of a lake. You swipe it away and it returns. The clock reads 3:00, though you can't remember if that's morning or afternoon, or whether it matters. You have an hour. You had an hour. You might still have an hour—in this city, time moves like it's wading through something.

A man at a newsstand—your father's face on a stranger's body—points down an alley without being asked. "Everyone's waiting," he says. You don't ask who. In the dream, you know exactly who, the same way you knew about the bakery. But when you try to look at the knowledge directly, it scatters like birds.

The alley opens onto the same street you started from. This doesn't alarm you. You walk it again, and this time the buildings have rearranged themselves into something more official—glass, brass, a lobby. A receptionist with your kindergarten teacher's voice tells you to take the elevator to the ninth floor. The buttons read 4, 7, 9, 13, and a small picture of a boat. You press 9. The elevator goes sideways.

The doors open on a long hallway carpeted like your grandmother's house, smelling of her perfume and photocopier toner. At the end of the hall is a door with your name on it—misspelled, but yours. Your hand is on the handle when you notice you're wearing pajama pants and one shoe, and that you can't for the life of you remember what the meeting is about. You decide it doesn't matter. It never did. What mattered was getting here.

You open the door. Around a long table sit everyone you've ever known, patient, unhurried. Someone—you can't see who—pulls out the empty chair beside them.

"You're right on time," they say.

And you are. That's the strangest part of the whole dream. For the first time all day, in this city where nothing holds still, you are exactly, impossibly, on time.
