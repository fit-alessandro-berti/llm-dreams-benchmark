They look like vines at first, but when you tilt the box toward the light, the vines become rivers, and the rivers become the lines on the palm of a hand. *Your* hand. You glance down at your own palm and find it smooth and blank, as if the patterns have migrated from your skin into the wood.

The box is warm, and it hums faintly, like a held breath.

You look around for a key, but the room you're in keeps changing whenever you look away. First it's your grandmother's kitchen, smelling of cardamom and burnt sugar. Then it's a train station where every clock shows a different hour. Then it's a library whose shelves stretch upward into fog. The box is the only thing that stays the same.

In the library, a moth lands on the lid. It's made of paper, and printed on its wings, in tiny typewriter letters, are the words: *It doesn't open. It remembers.*

You aren't sure what that means, but somehow you understand. You stop searching for a keyhole and press your blank palm flat against the carved patterns. The rivers begin to flow. You feel them move back into your skin, cold at first, then warm, like sunlight on water.

With a soft click, the box unlocks itself. It isn't a lock turning. It's more like a door recognizing someone who has finally come home.

Inside, there is no treasure, only a small glass jar of fireflies and a folded note in your own handwriting, though you don't remember writing it. The note says:

*You already knew the way. You just needed to stop looking for the key.*

The fireflies drift out of the jar and rise toward the foggy ceiling, and as they climb they become stars, and the library becomes an open field at night. The grass is cool under your bare feet. Somewhere far away, a train whistle sounds, and every clock in the world agrees at last on what time it is.

It's time to wake up.

But just before you do, you look down at the box one more time and notice that new patterns have begun to carve themselves into the lid, slowly, the way frost spreads across a window. Tomorrow's dream, perhaps, already on its way.
