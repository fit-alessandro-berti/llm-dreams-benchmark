…and each challenge feels like it was designed by your own subconscious.

First, you must cross a hallway that stretches every time you compare yourself to someone else. The faster you run, the farther the door gets. You stop, breathe, and walk by remembering one small thing you actually love doing. The hallway shrinks.

Then you face a panel interview. The judges are your childhood self, your burned-out future self, and a cat wearing your old boss’s tie. They ask, “Why do you deserve this job?” You begin listing achievements, but your words turn into paper and scatter. The cat yawns. Your childhood self asks, “But are you having fun?” You answer honestly: “Not always. But I want to be.” The panel nods.

Next is the final test. You stand in a room with three doors: Security, Prestige, and Passion. You reach for Passion, but it opens into a mirror. Your reflection is already doing the dream job—except the job isn’t a title. It’s a life where you get to be curious, useful, and kind without waiting for permission.

The host appears, wearing your face but with starlight in their hair. They hand you a key. “Grand prize,” they say. “Your dream job.”

You unlock the last door and step into a workshop filled with warm light, half-finished projects, and people who laugh at impossible ideas. Your desk has a nameplate that keeps changing: Artist. Engineer. Teacher. Healer. Storyteller. It finally settles on “You.”

You sit down. The work is hard and real and yours.

Then the alarm clock rings. But before you wake, you feel the key still warm in your palm—and you know the competition isn’t over. It’s just beginning. You get to choose the next challenge.
