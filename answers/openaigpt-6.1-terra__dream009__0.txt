The village has no name you recognize. Carts rattle over cobblestones, and the people wear wool cloaks fastened with bronze pins. When you ask what year it is, a woman selling pears laughs.

“Whose year?”

She gives you a pear and points toward a tower on the hill. “The clockmaker might know.”

Inside the tower, hundreds of clocks tick at different speeds. The clockmaker, a boy no older than twelve, studies you for a moment before opening a drawer.

“I’ve been waiting for you,” he says.

He takes out your phone. Its screen is cracked, but it glows in his hand. There is one notification:

**WAKE UP — 1 minute remaining**

You reach for it, but the boy pulls it away. “Not yet. You have to remember what you came here to find.”

Outside, every clock in the village begins to strike. The pear in your pocket is suddenly warm, and when you take it out, you see a tiny door in its side.

You open it.

Your bedroom is on the other side, impossibly small. Someone is asleep in your bed.

Then they open their eyes.
