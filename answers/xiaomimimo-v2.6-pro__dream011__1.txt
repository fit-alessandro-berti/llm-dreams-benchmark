You are about to take the final exam for a course that determines your future career. As you sit down to start the exam, you notice the questions are

not in any language you recognize. The symbols swirl and rearrange themselves on the page like living things, shifting just when you think you've identified a pattern. Your pencil — which now feels like a fish — keeps slipping from your grip.

You look around the exam hall. The other students are writing furiously, their pencils scratching in perfect unison like the ticking of a thousand clocks. The clock on the wall has no numbers — just a smiling face that keeps blinking at you.

"Fourteen minutes remaining," announces the proctor, who is now your mother, though she is also somehow the teacher from the course — a face you've never actually seen.

You try to flip to question one, but the pages are stuck together. When you finally peel one free, the ink is wet and the answers to *every question* are written on the back… but only in reverse. You hold the paper up to the light and read: *"You should have gone to dental school."*

The room begins to flood — not with water, but with more exam papers, rising from the floor like snowdrifts. The clock is laughing now. The smiling face has become your own.

You stand up to shout for help, but your voice comes out as a dial tone. The proctor, now also your first crush from middle school, pats your hand and whispers, *"Just answer question seventeen. It's the only one that matters."*

You look down. There are only sixteen questions.

The exam paper crumbles into sand. The room dissolves into the parking lot of a grocery store where you worked in high school. A shopping cart rolls past on its own, filled with neatly stacked, completed exams.

You wake up with your heart pounding, a crease on your cheek from sleeping on something — maybe a textbook, maybe nothing at all — and the unshakable feeling that somewhere, in some other dream, you still haven't finished the test.
