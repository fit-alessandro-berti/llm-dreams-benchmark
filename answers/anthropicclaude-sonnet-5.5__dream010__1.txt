**...As the competition progresses, you face various challenges and each one bends the rules of reality a little more.**

The first challenge takes place in a hallway of endless doors. A voice that sounds like your childhood teacher announces, "Only one door leads to the next round." Every door has your name on it, in handwriting that shifts from your own to someone else's. You stop trying to choose and ask yourself which door you're *afraid* to open. The answer is a small green one at the end, and behind it is a staircase going up.

The second challenge is a bridge made of unsent emails. Some are half-finished apologies, some are ideas you abandoned, and one is a thank-you note you always meant to write. The bridge gets shakier as you cross, and a rival contestant behind you begins to slip through the gaps. Without thinking, you reach back and pull them up. They stare at you as if you've broken a rule. Somewhere above, a bell rings, and the bridge solidifies beneath both of you.

The third challenge is an interview. Across the table sits a panel of your past selves: the seven-year-old who wanted to be an astronaut, the teenager who quit the piano, and last year's you, exhausted and skeptical. They ask one question in unison: **"What do you actually want?"**

You open your mouth to recite your polished answer, but it comes out differently: "I want to make something that matters to someone, and I want to still recognize myself while I do it."

The seven-year-old grins. The teenager nods. Last year's you exhales, finally.

The room dissolves into a sunlit rooftop where the judges are waiting, and they are all the people you've helped along the way, including the rival from the bridge. One of them holds out an envelope. Inside is no job title, only a key and an address you somehow already know.

"The job was never the prize," someone says. "It was the proof that you'd take it."

You turn the key in the air, and a door appears. As it swings open, the light pours through and your alarm begins to chime, faintly at first, like a bell from far away...

...and you wake with the feeling that you already know what to do next.
