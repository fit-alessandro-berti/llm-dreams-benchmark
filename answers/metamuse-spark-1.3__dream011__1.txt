Then the ink starts to swim.

The first question ripples and rearranges itself into your own handwriting: *“Describe the smell of your childhood kitchen at 6pm.”*

The second question is in crayon: *“Why didn’t you call your dad back?”*

The third is just a math problem that keeps changing numbers every time you blink — 7 + 4 = __, then 7 + 5 = __, then 7 + your age = __.

You look up. Everyone else is writing furiously, calmly, like this is totally normal. The girl next to you is using a carrot as a pencil. The professor at the front now has the face of your high school dentist and is slowly melting into his chair, saying, “No extra time. No extra time. No extra—” on loop.

You try to write your name, but your pen is too heavy to lift. Your hands are sticky with honey. Your desk is getting farther and farther away, sliding across an endless classroom floor. The clock above the whiteboard is spinning backward, then it falls off the wall and shatters into birds that fly around the room, stealing everyone’s papers.

You stand up to shout that this isn’t fair, that you *studied* for this, but when you open your mouth, a single baby tooth falls out onto the exam. Then another. You try to pick them up and the exam paper suddenly folds itself into a paper airplane and flies out the window.

You chase it, and outside the window isn’t campus — it’s your old backyard at dusk, and you’re late for dinner, and somewhere your mom is calling your name, louder and louder until—
