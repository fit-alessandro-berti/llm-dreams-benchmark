You are in the middle of a heated argument with a close friend. The disagreement is about something important to both of you, and you decide to say the one thing you’ve been afraid to say: “I don’t need to win. I need to understand you.” The words come out as fireflies. They drift between you, and the argument’s heat cools into a long hallway lined with doors. Each door is a memory you both share; behind one, you’re laughing so hard you can’t breathe. Your friend opens it, and you step through together. The disagreement is still there, waiting in the hallway, but it is smaller now—small enough to carry, or to leave until morning.
