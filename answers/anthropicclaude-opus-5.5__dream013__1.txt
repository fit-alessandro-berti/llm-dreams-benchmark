...notice that the puzzles are changing to match you.

At first they were ordinary: sliding tiles, riddles carved into stone, locks with too many tumblers. But by the third corridor, the tiles show fragments of your childhood kitchen. The riddles ask questions only you would know the answers to. *What did you almost say, and didn't?* One lock opens only when you hum a song you forgot you knew.

The walls are made of hedges, then bookshelves, then the fogged glass of a train window. Nobody finds this strange, least of all you. Dreams don't argue with themselves.

Deeper in, you meet a woman sitting cross-legged beside a puzzle box the size of a piano. She has your handwriting on her sleeves. "I've been working on this one for years," she says. "Every time I solve a side, another side appears." You kneel beside her and see that the box is covered in small brass dials, each labeled with a word: *Later. Enough. Almost. Should.* When you turn one, the whole maze shifts. Somewhere far off, a door closes and another opens.

"What's inside?" you ask.

She shrugs. "I assumed the exit."

You work together. The concentration feels good, the kind of focus where time dissolves and your thoughts run clean as water. You realize you've been solving puzzles your whole life this way, in the shower, on long drives, at 3 a.m. staring at the ceiling. The maze isn't new. You've just never seen it from the inside.

Finally, the last dial clicks. The box unfolds like a paper flower, petal by petal, and inside is...

nothing. Just a small, warm, open space, and the sound of birds.

The woman laughs, and it's your laugh. "Oh," she says. "It was never locked. We just liked the puzzle."

The hedges begin to lower. The corridors flatten into an open field under a soft gold sky. You can see the whole maze now from above, and it's beautiful: intricate and looping, like a fingerprint. Like a brain. Like something that was built with care by someone who wanted to stay busy so they wouldn't have to sit still in the quiet.

You sit down in the grass. For the first time in the dream, there is nothing to solve.

And just before you wake, you understand that this is the hardest puzzle of all, and the only one you've ever really needed to figure out.
