…and the moment your fingers touch it, every color you have ever known rushes into the frame.

The world around you shivers. Mountains fold into ribbons, rivers climb into the sky, and the sun changes from a gold coin to a blinking eye. The canvas waits, perfectly white, impossibly bright.

A brush lies at your feet, but it has no handle—only a strand of living light. When you pick it up, it pulls gently toward the horizon, where a door is forming in the distance.

You begin to paint.

At first, you paint the room you remember from childhood: yellow walls, a blue window, the faded outline of someone you loved. But the canvas refuses to hold them. The shapes rise off the surface and drift into the air, becoming birds, then music, then a memory you can almost hear.

So you paint the landscape instead—mountains with restless edges, rivers that flow upward, flowers whose petals open into tiny galaxies. With every stroke, the world outside becomes more impossible and more beautiful. Colors you've never seen spill from the brush: colors that smell like rain, sound like bells, and feel like the instant before thunder.

Eventually, the door reaches you.

Beyond it is another blank canvas, and beyond that, another world, and another, and another. Each one is waiting for a different version of you to finish what you started.

Behind you, the first canvas begins to dry. When you turn to look, you discover that the picture you painted has changed. The landscape is gone.

In its place is a figure standing at the center of an unfinished world, holding a brush made of light.

It looks exactly like you.

The figure smiles, raises the brush, and points toward the next door.

Then the dream begins again—but this time, you are the one holding the canvas.
