…plant a garden.

You find the seeds in a rusted tin at the back of an abandoned pharmacy. There are only seven, shriveled and black, and the word **TOMORROW** is scratched into the lid.

You plant them in the atrium of a collapsed library, where the roof has fallen away and rain can reach the floor. You make soil from ash, crumbled brick, and the pages of books whose titles have faded. Water is the hardest part. You carry it in a dented helmet, mouthful by mouthful, from a pump three miles away.

Every morning, you expect to find the garden trampled. Instead, you find offerings: a bent spoon, half a candle, a strip of blue cloth. You never see who leaves them.

On the night the first green shoot appears, something howls beyond the shelves. You grip a length of pipe as shadows gather in the doorways. But the shadows do not attack. One by one, they step into the moonlight—people you thought had vanished, each carrying a cup, a jar, or a cracked bottle of water.

No one speaks. They simply pour.

By dawn, the library is filled with trees. Their leaves shimmer like fragments of old photographs, and their fruit glows faintly, lit windows in a city seen from far away. You bite into one and taste summer rain.

Then someone shakes your shoulder.

“Your turn to keep watch.”

You open your eyes beside the dying fire. The library is gone. The trees are gone. Beyond the barricade, something moves through the dark.

But your fist is closed around something small and round.

A seed.
