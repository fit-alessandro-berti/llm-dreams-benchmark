At first, the challenges are crisp and reassuringly solid. You align brass astrolabes to unlock iron gates, untangle mathematical ciphers glowing faintly on obsidian walls, and navigate tiled floors where only prime numbers hold your weight. Your mind feels razor-sharp, humming with the quiet satisfaction of clicking gears and sliding stone.

Yet the deeper you descend, the less the corridors obey the laws of the waking world. The ceiling stretches up into a starry void, and the air begins to smell faintly of ozone and old paper. You arrive at a chamber where there are no levers or locks—only a massive brass scale. On one pan rests a heavy, polished stone; on the other, a flickering shadow cast by your own hand. You rack your brain, trying to calculate the weight of darkness, until you realize you have to step backward into the light, stretching your shadow until it grows long enough to tip the balance. With a resonant chime, the wall dissolves into mist.

A few turns later, the puzzles stop being made of stone and metal altogether. You find yourself balancing melodies played by dripping water, arranging floating fragments of forgotten conversations to build a bridge across a bottomless chasm. Every time you solve one, the maze exhales, shifting its walls around you like a living thing rearranging its ribs.

At last, you step through a narrow archway and emerge into the heart of the labyrinth: a vast, quiet courtyard bathed in twilight. In the center stands a towering monolith covered in thousands of shifting, luminescent tiles. They slide and rearrange themselves faster than you can track, forming equations in a language you almost recognize, yet can never quite read. 

You step closer, furrowing your brow, summoning every ounce of focus you have left. You try to map the patterns, to predict the next click, feeling the rising pressure of a problem too immense to hold in your mind. The tiles spin faster, blurring into ribbons of silver light.

Then, a cool breeze drifts across the courtyard, carrying the faint, inexplicable sound of distant wind chimes—or perhaps an alarm clock far, far away. 

You freeze. Looking down at your hands, you notice they are faintly translucent. A quiet, startling clarity washes over you: the tiles aren't shifting to keep you out. They are only moving because you expect them to be complicated.

You stop trying to solve it. Letting go of the math, the strategy, and the tension in your shoulders, you simply walk forward and press your palm flat against the warm surface of the monolith. 

Instantly, the frantic clicking stops. A single, harmonious chord vibrates through the soles of your feet, and the towering stone walls of the maze turn to frosted glass, then shatter silently into drifting motes of golden dust. There are no more corridors, no more riddles—just an endless, open expanse of pale morning sky. You take a deep breath, feeling yourself grow weightless, and begin to float upward as the light grows brighter, pulling you gently back toward the surface of sleep.
