First, you step onto a floor of glass panes, each one displaying a different version of your résumé. They fracture and reform under your weight, whispering doubts in languages you almost remember. To cross, you must lay down a stone not labeled with achievements, but with a question you've been avoiding. The moment it touches the glass, the paths realign, steady beneath your feet. You learn that progress doesn't require certainty; it requires honesty.

Next, you enter a theater where the audience is made of folded origami birds. They take flight only when you present work that feels alive rather than polished. You struggle at first, trying to tailor each piece to invisible expectations, until a feather drifts down and dissolves into ink. The message is clear: stop editing for approval. Start creating for resonance.

Then comes the ascent. A staircase spirals upward, each step carved with a compromise you've made: *“Wait until I’m ready,” “Play it safe,” “Hide the weird part.”* As you climb, the steps become translucent, then vanish. You're left balancing on air, carried forward not by ambition alone, but by the quiet refusal to abandon what genuinely moves you.

At the summit, there's no podium. Only a round table set beneath a canopy of slow-turning constellations. Seven chairs sit empty except one, positioned directly across from you. The voice that speaks doesn't come from outside; it vibrates in your ribs: *“The final challenge is to accept that the role will never be fully yours. It will change hands with deadlines, with missteps, with unexpected joy. You don't win it. You inhabit it.”*

You sit. The constellations dip low enough to brush your shoulders like warm water. A simple notebook appears, blank except for the first line: *“Begin where you are trained to wait.”* You pick up a pen that writes in light, and with every mark, the dream room softens into the familiar glow of your desk lamp.

Morning arrives. Steam curls from a mug. Your phone buzzes with a calendar invite, an email draft left unfinished, a project just beginning. The competition is over. Or perhaps it never was. You stretch, smile at the ceiling, and start working. Not because you've proven yourself, but because you finally recognize that the dream job was never a destination. It's the shape you give to your days.
