Of course — let's slip back into the dream:

You wake up in a completely different era, with no modern technology around.

Cold stone under your back. The smell of woodsmoke and fresh hay. Church bells are ringing somewhere far off, not a recording — real, uneven, slightly out of tune.

You sit up. You're in a straw-lined market stall, wearing a heavy linen tunic that isn't yours. Your jeans, your phone, your shoes — gone. Around you, a medieval town square is waking up. Merchants shouting in a language you *shouldn't* understand, but somehow do. Horses clip-clopping on cobblestone. No cars, no wires, no screens. Just sky, huge and pale gold.

As you explore your new surroundings, you realize you need to **deliver a letter you don't remember receiving.**

It's in your pocket — sealed with dark red wax, stamped with a spiral. You don't know who it's for, but your dreaming hands are shaking because you *know* it's urgent. The ink on the front is shifting when you're not looking at it directly, spelling out different names: *The Clockmaker. The Widow At The Hill. You.*

The sun is moving too fast across the sky, like time is fast-forwarding. People in the market glance at you knowingly, as if they're all waiting for you to remember your part.

A little girl tugs your sleeve and points to a stone tower on the hill above the town. "He's waiting before the last bell," she says. "If you miss it, you'll get stuck."

So you run. Barefoot up the hill, past goats and laundry lines and startled chickens, the letter getting warmer and warmer in your hand. By the time you reach the tower door, the bells start to toll — one... two...

You push it open, out of breath —

and wake up with your real blanket tangled around your feet, morning light in your actual room, the phantom weight of that wax seal still warm in your palm.

Want to go back in and find out who was in the tower?
