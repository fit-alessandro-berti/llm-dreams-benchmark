The clock above the station says you have forty minutes. Then, as you watch, it says eleven.

You hurry onto a tram with no driver. Its windows show places you almost recognize: your childhood kitchen, an empty beach, a classroom where rain falls upward. At each stop, a passenger gets off and leaves behind a small brass key.

By the time you reach the end of the line, the tram is full of keys.

You step into a quiet square. In the center stands a single door, freestanding and painted blue. A sign above it reads: **MEETING IN PROGRESS.**

You try one key, then another. None fits. The clock strikes the hour, and your stomach drops.

Then someone knocks from the other side.

You open the door.

Inside is a little room with a table set for two. Your younger self is sitting there, swinging their legs and looking mildly annoyed.

“You’re late,” they say.

“I couldn’t find the place.”

“I know.” They slide a cup of tea toward you. “I’ve been waiting to ask you something.”

You sit down. Outside the window, the city goes still.

“Are we happy?” they ask.

You open your mouth, and wake before you can answer.
