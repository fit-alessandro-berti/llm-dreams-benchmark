…discover that the rules are written in disappearing ink.

First, you must cross a bridge suspended above a city of ringing alarm clocks. The bridge narrows whenever you look down, so you cross by watching the other finalists, who have begun calling out directions to one another.

Next, you are given a pile of broken oars and told to build a boat. “Only one of you can win,” the judges remind everyone. You and your rivals build it anyway.

The final challenge takes place in a white room. A card reads: **WAIT FOR FURTHER INSTRUCTIONS.**

Hours pass. Perhaps years. A clock appears on the wall, checks the time, and politely leaves. At last, you fold the card into a paper key and slide it into the blank wall. A door opens.

Beyond it, the arena is flooding. You drag your boat into the water, and the other finalists pile in. The judges shout that this is not part of the competition. The crowd cheers anyway. With everyone rowing together, you burst through the stadium wall and sail into an apricot-colored dawn.

On the far shore stands a small building with your name above the door. Inside waits your dream job. Strangely, you cannot remember its title, but you recognize everything: the tools, the desk, the window, the feeling that the work has been waiting for you.

On the desk is a note:

**The final challenge was to begin before anyone gave you permission.**

A bell rings. Your first visitor steps inside.

Then you wake. For a moment, the loss is sharp enough to hurt. But the room is quiet, the morning is real, and there is something you have been putting off for a very long time.

So, before the dream can fade completely, you begin.
