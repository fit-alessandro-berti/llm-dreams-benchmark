You feel a storm approaching and the sky bruises from the first hint of gray, the sun slipping behind a veil of low‑hanging clouds. A sudden gust snaps the leaves, and the once‑peaceful chorus of crickets fades into a distant rumble of thunder that vibrates in your chest. The air grows thick with the scent of wet earth and pine, and the ground beneath your feet turns slick, as if the forest itself has taken a breath before the downpour. 

As you dart toward a hollowed oak for cover, the branches sway in a rhythm that feels almost alive, whispering names you don’t recognize. Shadows stretch longer than they have at noon, and tiny fireflies flicker out, leaving only the soft glow of lightning that paints the canopy in fleeting arcs. In that moment, the world outside the trees blurs—rain begins to tap against the leaves, turning the forest floor into a mirror of shimmering puddles, each one reflecting a fragment of the storm’s fury and calm all at once. 

You step inside, feeling both the chill of the coming rain and the strange warmth of the electric charge humming between the trunks. And then, without warning, the wind howls a song that sounds like a lullaby, pulling you deeper into the heart of the woods, where the path loops back onto itself, looping around the same ancient stone arch you passed moments ago.
