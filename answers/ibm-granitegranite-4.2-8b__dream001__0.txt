Your pulse is low thrumming in your throat; the hour feels thick, heavy with *almost*. You pull out your phone (screen dark, no signal), then sigh and put it away—dreams don’t obey apps. Instead, you walk without purpose, not rushing, just noticing.  

Your feet hit pavement that’s slightly warm where the sun lingers, cool where it’s shaded by a low wall covered in tiny, tangled vines that shimmer faintly when you pass. You glance up: above a bakery with no sign, a single paper crane hangs from a string tied between two lampposts, swaying. Below it, written in chalk on the curb near a closed flower stall, are two words you hadn’t seen before: *Turn left at the weeping stone.* You didn’t notice the stone earlier—it’s small, half-buried, gray, and one slow tear trickles down its side, catching the light like a bead.  

You stop. The meeting feels less urgent, more like a thread already in the city’s weave. You don’t *search* for it; you simply turn left at the weeping stone. The street bends gently, and suddenly the architecture softens—a door with a brass knocker shaped like a keyhole appears where there was only wall before. You don’t knock. You just walk toward it, the hour still an hour away, but the city no longer feels unfamiliar. It feels *waiting*, and you feel, for the first time, exactly where you need to be.
