and they are moving.

Not carved into the wood, as you first thought, but crawling slowly beneath it, like veins under skin. Spirals unwind into rivers. Small silver stars blink open into eyes, then close again. Every time you try to follow one pattern to its end, it slips around the corner of the box and becomes something else — a keyhole, a mouth, a tiny door with light leaking from underneath.

The box is warm. It hums against your palms, in time with your heartbeat.

*Tick.*

You turn it over. There is no lock. There is no hinge. Only a seam so fine it looks like a held breath.

*Tick. Tick.*

Somewhere far away, a clock is counting, but when you look up you realize you are not in a room at all. You are standing in a long hallway made entirely of doors you recognize: your childhood bedroom, painted blue. A classroom with rain on the windows. A door you have never opened in waking life, but have dreamed a hundred times.

The box grows heavier. The patterns are faster now, rearranging themselves into letters, then faces, then letters again.

They spell your name. Then they spell: **OPEN FROM THE INSIDE.**

You knock, politely, because in dreams that seems like the right thing to do.

Something inside knocks back.

*Let me out,* it whispers, in your own voice, but younger. *You locked me in when you stopped believing this was real.*

Your hands are shaking. You don't need a key, you understand suddenly. The key was never metal. It was permission.

You close your eyes and say, "You can come out."

The lid doesn't open. It *blooms*. Petals of dark wood fold back, silent as night, and inside is not darkness, but a miniature sky. Clouds drift across its surface. A tiny moon rises. And sitting in the middle of that impossible sky, cross-legged and waiting, is you — at seven years old, holding every dream you ever forgot to finish. Flying over oceans. Talking to animals. A house in the trees that could walk. A friend made of light.

The child-you holds one out to you. It's still warm.

"Take one," they say. "You have to carry it back, or you'll forget again when the clock stops."

You reach in. Your hand passes through stars.

*Tick. Tick. Tickticktick—*

The hallway begins to flood with morning light. The doors dissolve. The box is closing, gently, not to trap anything this time, but to keep it safe.

You wake with sunlight on your face and your hands curled tight around empty air —

except for the small, silver pattern still drawn on your palm, fading slowly, like a key remembering its shape.
