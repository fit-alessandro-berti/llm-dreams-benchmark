

…open the project file, and the screen is not a screen at all. It is a narrow hallway, lit by the blue glow of fluorescent lamps that hum in the key of your heartbeat.

Your boss stands before you, but their face is a clock with too many hands, all moving backward. They do not speak. They only nod, and the nod becomes a stamp pressed into the air in front of you: **START NOW**. The date on the deadline is written in ink that keeps changing—today, yesterday, a day that does not exist.

You reach for your keyboard, but the keys have melted into small silver puddles. As you watch, the puddles rise into the air and form a calendar. Every page rips away at once, leaving only one square: **NOW**.

You try to stand, but your legs are filing cabinets, and your spine is a long corridor of closed doors. Somewhere down the hall, a printer begins printing your name, over and over, but each copy is slightly different. One says you are already finished. Another says you never began.

Your boss leans closer, their clock-face ticking loudly. “The deadline,” they say, with the sound of a filing cabinet sliding shut, “is not a time. It is a place.”

The fluorescent lights flicker. The hallway stretches. The door at the end opens, and beyond it is not the office anymore, but a vast empty room filled with desks, each one smaller than the last, all facing away from you. At the farthest desk, so tiny it is almost a speck, you see yourself.

You are already there, working.

You are already late.
