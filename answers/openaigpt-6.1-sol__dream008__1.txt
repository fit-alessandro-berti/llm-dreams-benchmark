Your boss checks a watch with thirteen hands. “We needed it yesterday,” they say.

You open the project folder. Inside is an ocean.

Somehow, you understand the assignment: count every wave before five o’clock.

You try. One, two, three—but each wave divides into smaller waves, and the clock begins ticking faster. Water spills across your desk. Your coworkers lift their feet without looking up from their screens.

Then a paper boat bumps against your wrist.

Inside is a note in your own handwriting:

**Ask what actually needs to be finished.**

You look at your boss. “What is this project for?”

Their watch stops.

For the first time, they seem uncertain. “I don’t remember.”

The ocean becomes quiet. Beneath your feet, the office carpet turns to warm sand. Your boss sits beside you, takes off their shoes, and watches the tide.

Far away, a phone rings.

Neither of you answers.
