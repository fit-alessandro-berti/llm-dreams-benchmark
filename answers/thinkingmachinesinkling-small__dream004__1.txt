...the canvas isn’t just blank—it’s *absent*, a perfect rectangle of soft, breathing white that hums where the shifting sky should be.

You reach out to touch it, and the moment your fingers brush the surface, the world stops its frantic morphing. The purple triangles and melting orange spheres freeze mid-flight, suspended like dust in a sunbeam.

But the canvas is hungry.

Instead of paint, it pulls colors *from you*. Your dreams begin to bleed onto it—not images, but sensations. The cold taste of a childhood hallway. The sound of a clock that ticks backward. You watch in wonder as these feelings become shapes: a towering arch of corkscrew light, a garden where the flowers are made of whispered secrets, a river that flows upward into your own eye.

You realize the canvas isn’t empty. It’s a mirror made of potential, and you are the brush.

You step closer, and the white surface ripples like water. Your feet sink into it—not into paint, but into the space between waking and sleeping. The canvas expands, becoming a doorway, and through it, the surreal landscape transforms into a version of itself that recognizes you. The shapes don’t just change anymore; they rearrange themselves to form a path, a bridge of liquid cobalt and velvet geometry.

You walk through.

On the other side, you find yourself standing in a garden where the trees are made of folded paper, each leaf a different shade of a memory you haven’t had yet. At the center stands a second canvas—but this one is already covered in your face, smiling, even though you’ve never seen yourself this way.

You try to step back, but the canvas pulls gently, not with force, but with the logic of dreams: every time you turn to leave, the landscape has already shifted behind you, leaving you with only one choice—forward, into the painting, where colors and shapes are not just changing, but remembering you, and you realize with a gentle, sinking clarity that you are not exploring the dream at all.

You *are* the canvas now.

Your hands dissolve into strokes of amber and silver. Your thoughts become the shifting sky, and as the first true light of morning tries to press against the edges of your sleeping mind, you feel yourself spread out—wide, bright, and infinitely changing—into a blank page that is finally, perfectly, full.

Then you wake up, and for a moment, the air in your room holds the faint color of a door you never opened, and the shape of a garden you’re already walking through.
