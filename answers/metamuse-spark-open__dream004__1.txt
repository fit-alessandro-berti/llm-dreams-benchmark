You come across a blank canvas and it is impossibly still.

In a world where everything else ripples, melts, and reassembles itself — where the sky shifts from violet to amber with each breath and the hills fold like origami — this canvas is the only thing that isn't changing. It's floating just above the ground, framed in light, untouched.

As you step closer, you notice there's no easel holding it up. It just *is* there, waiting for you. The surface isn't white, exactly, but the absence of color, like fresh snow or static before a song begins.

Then you realize your hands aren't empty anymore. A brush has appeared in your palm, but it has no paint on it.

When you lift the brush to the canvas, the colors of the world around you begin to drift toward it. A streak of turquoise peels off the sky and follows your movement. A swirl of crimson unfurls from a passing shape and pools at the tip of the brush.

You understand, without anyone telling you: this canvas doesn't reflect the world. It decides it.

With the first stroke, the shifting landscape steadies. With the second, it sings in a new color that you’ve never seen before but have somehow always known.

What do you paint first?
