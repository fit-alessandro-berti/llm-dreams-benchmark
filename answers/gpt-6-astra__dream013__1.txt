A lock clicks open, and you forget the name of your first school. You arrange seven silver tiles into a perfect square, and the sound of an old friend’s laughter vanishes from your memory. Beyond each door, the maze grows more beautiful: walls of blue glass, staircases threaded with flowering vines, fountains whose water hangs motionless in the air.

At the center, you find a small wooden table.

On it rests the last puzzle: a sheet of paper with a single question.

**What have you come here to find?**

You search your pockets for clues. Out tumble a brass key, three black stones, and a photograph of a doorway. You recognize none of them.

You work for what feels like hours. You count the stones, measure the key against the photograph, fold the paper until its edges meet. Eventually you turn it over.

On the back, in your handwriting, someone has written:

*You can leave this one unfinished.*

You dislike this answer. There must be a reason for the stones.

Then you hear a kettle boiling.

The sound comes from behind a vine-covered wall. You push the leaves aside and discover an ordinary door, standing slightly open. Beyond it is a kitchen washed in morning light. Someone has put out two cups.

You hesitate, still holding the key.

“Leave that there,” a familiar voice says. “It’s for the back gate.”

You set it on the table beside the puzzle and step inside.
