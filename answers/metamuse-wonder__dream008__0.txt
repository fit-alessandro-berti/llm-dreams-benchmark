...stare at the blank screen, and the cursor just blinks back at you.

Blinking. Blinking. Blinking.

Your boss is standing right behind your chair. You can hear him breathing. You can feel him waiting. On your desk, the project folder is already a foot thick, and every time you look away, it gets thicker.

"Need it by five," he says.

You glance at the clock on the wall. It's 4:59.

But when you look again, it's 3:00. Then 6:30. Then the hands start spinning backwards so fast they blur. All the clocks in the office — on the walls, on everyone's computers, on the microwave in the break room — start ticking louder and louder until it sounds like rain on a tin roof.

You open the folder.

Inside isn't paperwork. It's your to-do list from *every* day you've ever worked here, all printed on the same page. Emails spill out across your desk like water. Your keyboard keys turn soft, like gum, and stick to your fingers as you try to type your first sentence.

*Tap. Tap. Tap.*

Every word you type appears on the wall behind you in huge letters, so the whole office can read it.

Your boss leans in. "How's it coming?"

"Almost done," you hear yourself say, though you haven't written anything at all.

Then your chair starts sinking. Just an inch at first, then slowly down through the carpet, like the floor is made of sand. Your coworkers keep working like nothing is wrong. Someone walks by holding a coffee cup that's overflowing forever, never emptying, leaving a trail of coffee across the ceiling.

That's when it hits you — the weird, calm little thought in the middle of the panic:

*None of this makes sense. I'm dreaming.*

The ticking stops.

Your boss freezes mid-breath. The papers stop multiplying. Your sunken chair rises back up.

You stand up, suddenly light. You pick up the impossible folder, and now it's weightless. You flip it open and the pages turn into a calendar, but all the deadlines are blank. You take a pen and write, in big calm letters: *ONE THING AT A TIME.*

Your boss looks at it, nods like that's exactly what he wanted, and says, "Perfect. That's what I was waiting for."

The office stretches out, quiet and golden like late afternoon. The clock settles on a normal time. Your screen isn't blank anymore — it's just waiting, patiently, for the very first, small step.

You sit back down, take a breath, and start typing.

And as your fingers hit the first real key, you wake up.
