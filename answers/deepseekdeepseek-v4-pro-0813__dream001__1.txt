You start to run down a boulevard that keeps tilting upward, as if the whole city is being lifted by one corner. Your phone’s map only shows a flickering blue dot labeled *you are elsewhere*. You ask a street vendor for directions, but he hands you a warm croissant and says, “The meeting is already remembering you.” You bite into it, and it tastes like your childhood kitchen. Then every clock you pass shows a different century. You finally reach a glass tower, but the elevator buttons are months, not floors. You press *now*, and the doors open onto a long table where everyone is you, at different ages. The oldest you smiles and says, “You’re just in time to arrive late.” You wake with crumbs on your pillow.
