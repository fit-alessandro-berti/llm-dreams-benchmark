Here’s one possible completion:

You find yourself in a post-apocalyptic world, with resources scarce and dangers everywhere. Despite the hardships, you decide to plant the last of your seeds instead of eating them.

Your hands shake as you bury them in the cracked soil behind a collapsed library, where a thin stream of water still seeps through broken concrete. It makes no sense. You are hungry. Winter is coming. Every survival instinct screams at you to store the calories, to run, to hide. Instead, you kneel in the dust and press life into the ground.

For days you guard the little patch. You scare off crows with a sling and sharpened sticks. You ration your own water so the sprouts can have a little. At night, the wind sounds almost like voices, but you stay.

Then, one gray morning, a single green hook breaks the surface. Then another. The sight is so small and so enormous that your throat tightens. You laugh, and the sound surprises you—it has been years since you laughed.

Others find you eventually: a woman with a limp, a boy carrying a mismatched pair of shoes, an old man who knows how to graft apple branches. They bring scrap metal and stories. Together you build a low wall, then a rain catcher, then a real greenhouse from scavenged glass. The garden grows into a village, and the village grows into a promise.

When the dream finally releases you, you wake with the echo of dirt beneath your fingernails and the taste of green things in your mouth. Outside your window, dawn is breaking, stubborn and gold—and for a moment, you believe, really believe, that the world might still be worth saving.
