You know you should be afraid, but you aren't. Instead, you begin to walk faster, then run, and your feet barely touch the ground. The path rearranges itself beneath you, roots sliding aside like curtains, guiding you somewhere.

Thunder rolls, but it sounds like a voice calling your name from very far away, or from very long ago.

At the heart of the forest stands a door. It has no house and no walls, only a weathered wooden frame between two ancient oaks. Lightning flashes, and for a moment you see that the trees are full of lanterns, hundreds of them, swaying in the wind but never going out.

You reach for the handle. It's warm, as if someone on the other side has just let go.

The door opens onto your childhood kitchen. Rain patters against the windows, and a kettle is just beginning to sing. Someone has set out two cups. Though the room is empty, you understand, in the way one understands things in dreams, that you were expected. The storm was never chasing you. It was walking you home.

You sit down. The forest closes softly behind you. Outside, the thunder settles into something like a purr.

And as you lift the cup to your lips, you notice the tea is full of tiny stars, turning slowly, waiting to see what you'll do next...
