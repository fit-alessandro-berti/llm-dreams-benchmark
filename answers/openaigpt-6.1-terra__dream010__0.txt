You are participating in a competition where the grand prize is your dream job. As the competition progresses, you face various challenges, each stranger than the last.

First, you must build a bridge from books while a crowd of silent judges watches. You place the final book just as the river below begins to rise. When you cross, the judges applaud, but their applause sounds like rain.

Next, you enter an office with a thousand ringing telephones. Every caller is asking a question you once wanted to ask someone else. You answer as many as you can, though some make you laugh and others make you sit quietly for a while.

At last, only you and one other contestant remain. The host leads you both to a door marked **FINAL CHALLENGE**.

“Behind this door,” she says, “is the person you must convince.”

You open it and find a small room with a desk, a lamp, and a younger version of yourself. They are drawing a picture with great concentration.

“What’s the challenge?” you ask.

Your younger self looks up. “Tell me what we get to do every day.”

You begin to describe the job: the work, the people, the things you hope to make. Halfway through, you notice they have stopped drawing and are listening.

When you finish, they smile and push the picture across the desk. It shows a crooked building with a bright yellow door. Above it, in uneven letters, they have written your name.

The host appears beside you. “You’ve won,” she says, and hands you a key.

You step outside, and there it is: the building from the picture. As you put the key in the lock, the morning alarm begins to ring.
