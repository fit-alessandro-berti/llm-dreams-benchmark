…and you open your mouth to explain, reasonably, about staffing and hours, but what comes out is a small brass key, which lands in your palm with a warmth like it's been in someone's pocket all day.

Your boss nods as if this is exactly what he asked for. He has been standing at the edge of your desk for what feels like eleven minutes or four years. His tie is very long and goes down past the carpet and into the carpet, the way a root does.

The project is a folder. It's warm too, warm like bread, and when you open it the pages are blank except for a deadline written in the top corner in your own handwriting, which is strange, because you recognize the handwriting but not the date. The date is yesterday.

You understand, with the flat certainty dreams give you, that you must hurry, and also that hurrying is not a thing that exists here. The clock above the break room has soft hands. They droop like tulips left too long in a vase. Every time you look away and back, someone has moved them kindly, the way you'd tuck a blanket around a sleeping person.

You begin to type. The keys are seeds. Each one you press pushes down into soil that is somehow underneath your keyboard, and you can feel the small resistance of earth, and the letters do not appear on the screen but somewhere below, in the dark, where things grow.

Your coworkers are all the same person, and that person is being very patient with you.

The fluorescent lights hum a song your mother used to hum, and you realize you've been humming along, and the humming is the work, and the work is nearly done.

Then the building tilts, gently, like a ship deciding something, and through the window you see the parking lot is a lake, and your car is a small boat that has been waiting for you all along without complaining once.

Your boss says, *You finished this yesterday.*

And the relief is so enormous it wakes you.
