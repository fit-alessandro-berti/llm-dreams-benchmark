…and the deadline is already written on your skin, tattooed just beneath the wrist in ink that shifts between 4:00 PM and 11:59 PM, depending on how you tilt your hand. Your boss is standing right there—except they’re also your dentist, your first crush, and the concept of Thursday itself. They don’t speak; they just point to a conference room that was, moments ago, a supply closet, and is now slowly filling with water at ankle level. The water is warm and smells like toner ink.

You sit down. Your keyboard is made of marshmallows, but their letters are sharp as broken glass. The project file on your screen is titled **“FINAL_v2_ACTUALFINAL_DO_NOT_OPEN”**, and when you click it, it unfolds into a thousand origami swans that swarm the fluorescent lights, turning them into a swarm of startled moths. “We need five-year projections,” your boss says, from inside the water, though their lips do not move. “Based on the color.”

The calendar on the wall is eating itself, pages fluttering down as moths with dates printed on their wings. Your phone rings. It’s the client. You answer, and the voice on the other end is yours, but younger, whispering that you forgot to wear pants and that the meeting is happening inside your own ribcage. You look down. Your tie is a snake. Your shoes are full of rice.

Every time you type, the letters crawl off the screen and scuttle across the desk. You try to gather them, but your hands keep passing through the desk like it’s smoke, and now the desk is a kayak, and you are paddling down the corridor toward the elevator, which opens not onto a floor but into the sky at 4:00 PM, a vast blue ceiling with the sun nailed to it. You reach the deadline just as it happens—not as a time, but as a physical weight pressing on your chest, and you realize you have not been working; you have been asleep in the meeting, and everyone has been waiting for you to speak.

Your boss leans in, close enough that you can see the office reflected in their glasses, an infinite regress of cubicles shrinking into oblivion. “Well?” they ask.

You open your mouth to defend yourself, and all that comes out is the soft *ding* of an elevator arriving on a floor that doesn’t exist yet. The floor gives way. You fall upward, through the ceiling tiles, through the moth-filled lights, through the sky, and as the office collapses into a single spreadsheet cell, you feel the relief of knowing it was never possible in the first place—

—and then your alarm clock, a screaming factory whistle, drags you back to 6:00 AM, leaving you with the taste of toner and the certainty that you’ve forgotten something important.
