...written in a language that shifts every time you look away. You blink, and the words rearrange themselves into something almost readable.

**Question 1:** *Describe the color of the sound your childhood kitchen made at 4 p.m.*

You look up to ask the proctor if there's been a mistake, but the proctor is your third-grade teacher, Mrs. Halvorsen. She is also somehow a heron, standing very still at the front of the room. She taps her watch, which is a small aquarium with a clock-faced fish in it. The fish says you have eleven minutes, though you haven't started and the exam is already half over.

**Question 2:** *Prove that you were here yesterday.*

Your pencil has turned into a warm, slightly damp breadstick. You decide to use it anyway. To your surprise, you write with confidence, and the answer comes out in a looping cursive you've never had. Something about a staircase, a borrowed umbrella, and a promise you made to a stranger. You don't remember any of it, but it feels true, and the page glows faintly in approval.

**Question 3:** *Choose one door.*

The exam booklet falls open, and there are three doors drawn on the page. One is the door of your dream career, which you can only see from behind. One is a door you've walked through a thousand times, and it's slightly ajar. The third is the door to this very classroom, and it has a small sign that says *Exit (Also Entrance).*

The room has gone quiet. Every other student has turned to watch you. They all have your face, and they are all nodding.

You put down the breadstick and press your palm flat against the paper. The door drawn there is suddenly warm, like a door with sunlight on the other side. It swings open, and the smell of your childhood kitchen at 4 p.m. drifts out. It is gold, it hums in a low key, and it tastes a little like cinnamon.

Mrs. Halvorsen the heron lowers her head and says, gently, "That's the correct answer. It was always the correct answer."

Then the fish chimes four o'clock, the walls fold up like paper, and you realize you're no longer taking the exam. You are walking into the kitchen, and someone has saved you a seat.

*(And just as you reach for the chair, the dream begins to fade, leaving only the warmth and the feeling that you already know what to do next.)*
