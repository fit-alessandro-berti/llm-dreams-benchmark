...their voices folding into the rustle of autumn leaves, even though no trees surround you. A circle of strangers laughs in synchronized silence, their expressions shifting like tide pools. One turns to you, eyes reflecting a city skyline that isn't here, and asks, *“Have you checked the space between seconds?”* Before you can answer, the floor tilts, and you're standing in a grand foyer where everyone wears masks carved from familiar faces—you recognize your third-grade teacher, your favorite bookstore clerk, your own reflection at sixteen—but none of them move when called.

You walk past rooms that hum with half-remembered songs, past balconies overlooking oceans made of stained glass. The air tastes like orange peel and rain. Chandeliers drip wax that solidifies into pocket watches before hitting the ground. And still, you keep moving, drawn by a pull that feels less like memory and more like gravity.

Then, behind a doorway that appears only when you stop looking for it, you find them. Not quite who you expected, but entirely who you needed. They're sitting on a wooden bench under a tree whose branches form slow-moving constellations instead of leaves. When they speak, their voice sounds like your thoughts finally catching up to themselves. *“You knew I’d be here,”* they say. *“Not because I’m lost. But because part of you kept leaving breadcrumbs.”*

You look down. At your feet, scattered like fallen coins, are small origami shapes—each one folded from moments you thought you'd misplaced: a missed train, a word swallowed before it left your mouth, a laugh cut short by duty. You pick one up. It unfolds into a staircase spiraling upward through a sky the color of held breath. You climb it slowly, feeling lighter with each step, until you reach a platform where the dream doesn't end, but simply opens wider.

You realize you aren't searching anymore. You're being carried. And somewhere beneath the surface, far away but unmistakably true, you begin to wake.
