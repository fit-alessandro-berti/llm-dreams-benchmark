The sky above the treetops turns the color of bruised plums, and the air grows thick and heavy, humming with a strange static that makes the hairs on your arms stand up. A wind rushes through the forest, bending the ferns flat and sending a spiral of golden leaves dancing around your ankles. You feel the storm approaching, not just in the sky but deep in your chest, a pressure like the moment before a great bell is struck.

You look for shelter, but the trees around you begin to change. Their trunks stretch upward, silver and smooth, until they look less like oaks and birches and more like the pillars of a cathedral built from moonlight. Their branches knit together overhead into a canopy, and through the gaps you can see lightning, but it moves slowly, like ink spreading through water, tracing silver veins across the clouds.

Then the rain begins, but it doesn't fall. It drifts down in glowing droplets that hang in the air around you, each one holding a tiny reflection of a different place: a seaside village at dusk, a snowy mountain pass, a library with endless shelves. You reach out and one droplet lands in your palm, warm as a heartbeat. As it sinks into your skin, you hear a voice somewhere in the thunder, calm and familiar, saying your name.

The storm breaks open, and for a moment everything is white light.
