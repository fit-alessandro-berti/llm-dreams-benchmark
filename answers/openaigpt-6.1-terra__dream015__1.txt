You choose the roof of an abandoned library, where rain still gathers in a cracked stone basin. Each morning, you climb six flights of stairs with a handful of soil or a scrap of compost. At night, you sleep behind a barricade of encyclopedias.

One day, you find footprints in the dust beside your seedlings. The next, a small jar of water appears.

You leave half a packet of seeds in its place. By morning, it’s gone.

Weeks pass this way. Then, one evening, you climb to the roof and find a girl crouched beside the basin, watching the first green shoots push through the soil. She startles when she sees you, but doesn’t run.

“Are they really tomatoes?” she asks.

“I hope so.”

She considers this, then takes a dented spoon from her pocket and begins loosening the earth around them.

Far below, something howls in the ruined streets. You move closer to the girl, and together you keep working until the light is gone.
