reach for your keyboard, but the keys come loose under your fingers. They’re Scrabble tiles. The deadline is noon. The clock says eleven sixty-three.

“When do you think you’ll have a first draft?” your boss asks.

“What’s the project?”

She looks down at the folder in her hands. There’s a damp ring on the cover from her coffee. Inside is a single sheet of paper: *Make it yesterday.*

“Oh, thank God,” she says quietly. “I thought you knew what that meant.”

You’ve never heard your boss sound scared before. You pull up a chair for her. Between you, you arrange the tiles on your desk until they spell YESTERDAY, though you have to use a blank for the second Y. For a moment nothing happens. Then the damp ring lifts off the folder and fits itself around the bottom of her cup. The sun slips backward across the carpet.

It’s yesterday afternoon. Through the glass wall you can see yourself at your desk, eating a tangerine. Your boss is still beside you. Neither of you has been given the project yet.

“Lunch?” she suggests.

You take the stairs so you won’t run into yourselves.
