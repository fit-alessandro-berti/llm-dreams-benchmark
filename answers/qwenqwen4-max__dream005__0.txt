...and each one closes behind you like water.

The first group is laughing at something that finished before you arrived. They turn to you expectantly, the way people do when a joke needs one more line, and you say a word you didn't choose — *hinges* — and they laugh harder than before, and one of them touches your arm as if you've been friends for years. You excuse yourself. You are already excuse-yourselfing before you finish the sentence.

The house has more rooms than the house has. There's a kitchen with a long table where someone is describing a dream, and you recognize the couch in it, and then the kitchen, and you leave before they get to the part with you.

You keep almost finding them. A green sleeve near the bookshelf. The particular tilt of a head at the top of the stairs. Each time the person turns, it's someone wearing your friend's face the way an understudy wears it — all the marks correct, none of the weight. They ask if you're enjoying yourself. You say you're looking for someone. They say *oh, me too*, with real feeling, and you understand you are supposed to help them, and you can't, and you go.

The stairs go down into the room you just left. Somebody hands you a drink that is warm and tastes like the color of the walls. You check the coats piled on the bed for a name in a pocket, and there is one, and it's yours, spelled slightly wrong.

A hallway. Doors on both sides, all of them open a few inches, all of them containing the same party at a slightly earlier hour. You can hear yourself in three of them, saying *hinges*.

And then, without any transition, the way it happens — they're at the window at the end of the room, mid-sentence with two strangers, holding a plate they don't want. They look up. Their face does the thing your face is doing. The relief is so total it's almost embarrassing, it's the relief of a child in a supermarket, and they cross the room too fast and say:

"I only know one person here."

"I only know one person here."

You both laugh. It's the first real sound in the whole house.

Someone leans over — one of the strangers, friendly, holding the plate now — and asks how you two know each other. You open your mouth. You reach back for it: a car, a bad year, a stairwell, something with rain in it — and it isn't there, it was never loaded into this dream, only the fact of them was.

You say, "I don't remember."

They say, "Yeah," and take your sleeve, and the party goes on being enormous around you, and neither of you looks for anyone else.
