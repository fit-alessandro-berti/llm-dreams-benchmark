As you sit down to start the exam, you notice the questions are already answered.

Not by you. By someone with your handwriting, dated years from now — slower, more certain, a little tired at the edges. Question 1: *What did you actually want before you started wanting what was expected?* Question 2 is four jobs you almost applied for and one that does not exist yet. The bubble next to that last one is already filled in.

You try to erase it. The paper sighs.

The proctor looks like you at forty and will not make eye contact. “You can change them,” they say, tapping a red pen that has not touched the page. “But then you’ll have to live the version you write.”

The clock on the wall has no hands. Your pencil writes a sentence you have not thought of yet. When you look up, the classroom is your childhood kitchen. The other students are versions of you at different ages, all chewing the same eraser. Someone in the back row keeps glancing at your paper. You realize it is also you.

You flip to the last page. There is only one line left:

*Are you sure?*
