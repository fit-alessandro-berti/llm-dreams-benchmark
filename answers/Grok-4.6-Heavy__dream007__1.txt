You are in the middle of a heated argument with a close friend. The disagreement is about something important to both of you, and you decide to stop trying to win.

The last sentence you were about to say sits heavy on your tongue. You take it out and set it on the table. Your friend does the same. Between the two of you the words look smaller than they sounded, like glasses left out overnight.

The room is their kitchen and also the street you both grew up on. Light comes through the window in the wrong season. Neither of you mentions this.

You say the quieter thing. The one the argument had been standing in front of.

They don’t flinch. For a moment their face is yours. Then it is theirs again.

“I know,” they say. “I just didn’t want to be alone until you woke up.”

A kettle sings that was never put on. You are holding nothing. The disagreement is still important, and you are already forgiving them for it.

You wake with the taste of that last sentence still in your mouth.
