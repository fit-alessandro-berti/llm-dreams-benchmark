You start to walk quickly down the nearest street, hoping the building will simply announce itself. But every street sign is written in a language that *almost* looks like English—the letters rearrange themselves whenever you look away and back again.

You pull out your phone to check the meeting details. The screen glows, but your calendar shows only a single entry: **"NOW,"** with no location. You try to text a colleague, but your thumbs sink through the glass like it's water, and the letters float up off the screen and drift away like dandelion seeds.

A woman at a bus stop notices your distress. "You're looking for the Meridian Building," she says confidently. You thank her and hurry in the direction she points—but when you glance back to wave, she's gone. So is the bus stop. There's only a brick wall where it stood.

The streets begin to slope uphill, though the city was flat a moment ago. Your legs move as if through honey. You break into a run, but each stride covers less ground than the last, as if the pavement is stretching beneath you. Somewhere behind you, you hear footsteps—someone else late for the same meeting? You don't dare turn around.

Then, finally: the building. Impossibly tall, its upper floors vanishing into low gray clouds. A clock above the entrance reads 8:59. One minute. You sprint through revolving doors that spin too slowly, as if underwater. Inside, the elevator has no buttons—just a single lever marked with a question mark. You pull it.

The elevator rises smoothly, then begins moving sideways. Then downward. Then somehow *inward*. The doors open onto a conference room you recognize from a job you held years ago. Everyone is seated around the table, and they all turn to look at you. Your old boss smiles warmly and says, "Right on time. We've been waiting since before you were born."

Someone slides a document toward you. It's blank except for a single line at the bottom, where your signature already appears—in handwriting that isn't quite yours.

Just as you lean in to see what everyone else is reading, the room tilts, the faces blur into watercolor smudges, and—

You wake up. Your alarm hasn't gone off yet. For a moment you lie there, heart pounding, half-convinced you really did miss something important. Then you remember: it's Saturday. There was never any meeting at all.
