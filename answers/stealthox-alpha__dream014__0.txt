...and you follow them through streets that seem to breathe and shift with every step.

The strangers walk beside you without speaking, but their presence feels warm, like sunlight through a window you didn't know was there. One of them hands you a paper map, but when you unfold it, the streets are drawn in colors that don't exist—rivers of amber running between buildings made of soft blue light.

"Where are you trying to go?" asks a woman whose face keeps changing gently, like ripples on water. You realize you don't know. You only know you were lost, and now, somehow, you're not.

The city begins to soften around you. The noise of traffic becomes something like ocean waves. The towering buildings lean down, curious rather than imposing, their windows blinking like friendly eyes. You notice your feet are no longer touching the ground—you're drifting, carried along by the kindness of these strangers who ask for nothing in return.

Then, one by one, they begin to fade—not leaving you, but becoming part of everything else. The woman's voice merges with the wind. The man's laughter becomes birdsong overhead. You understand, in the way dreams let you understand things, that they were never really strangers at all. They were pieces of yourself—the parts that remember how to trust, how to accept help, how to belong.

You wake slowly, the feeling lingering like the last note of a song. The room is quiet. But somewhere beneath the loneliness you carried into sleep, something has loosened. You are still here, still finding your way—but you no longer feel quite so alone in it.
