...open your mouth, but no words come out. Instead, a soft chime rings through the auditorium, like a glass bell struck underwater.

The crowd doesn't flinch. They lean forward, thousands of faces glowing in the amber light, and you realize you've never seen any of them before, yet somehow you know each of their names.

You look down at your hands. The speech you wrote is there, but the pages are blank except for a single line in your own handwriting: *Tell them what you were afraid to tell yourself.*

The podium begins to float a few inches off the stage. No one gasps. Somewhere in the back row, a child waves at you as if this were perfectly normal.

You take a breath, and this time the words come, but they aren't in your voice. They sound like many voices layered together: your younger self, your grandmother, someone you haven't met yet. You hear yourself say:

*"I don't have all the answers. I never did. But I've learned that the fear of standing here is just excitement wearing a heavy coat."*

The audience laughs warmly, and the ceiling dissolves into a sky full of slow-turning constellations. Each star flickers in rhythm with your heartbeat. As you continue, the stage beneath your feet becomes a wide, shallow lake, and every word you speak sends ripples outward to the audience's feet.

Then you notice the crowd has changed. Each person now holds a small lantern, and they begin to lift them one by one, as if your words were lighting them from within.

You feel no fear now, only a strange, humming certainty that you're exactly where you're meant to be.

You open your mouth to say the final sentence, and just as the light swells to fill the whole horizon, you hear a faint, distant sound, a ringing that is not quite a bell and not quite an alarm...

...and the dream begins to fold itself gently away, like a page being turned.
