You feel a storm approaching and the air turns electric against your skin.

The peaceful green light of the forest dims to a deep violet. Leaves that were still a moment ago begin to whisper and lift, swirling around you in slow spirals. Thunder rumbles far off — not frightening, but low and resonant, like the forest itself breathing.

You start to run, not out of fear, but pulled forward by instinct, your feet light on the soft moss. Raindrops begin to fall, huge and warm, and each one glows faintly where it lands.

Just ahead, you see an enormous hollow tree, its trunk wide open like a doorway. You slip inside just as the sky opens.

But inside, it's dry and warm. Soft golden light pulses from the bark itself. Through the opening, you watch the storm become beautiful — lightning forking in slow motion across purple clouds, rain turning to ribbons of silver light, wind singing through the branches like a choir.

You realize you're not scared at all. You hold out your hand, and one raindrop of light lands in your palm and blooms into a tiny bird, which flies up and out into the storm.

And then you lift off with it — floating, weightless above the forest, watching the storm wash everything clean and new below you.
