...written in a language you almost recognize. The letters look like English, but they shift when you stare at them too long, rearranging themselves like ants avoiding a fingertip. Question one seems to read, *Explain the migratory patterns of your childhood bedroom.* You blink, and it becomes, *Show all work.*

You look around. The exam hall is enormous, much bigger than it seemed when you walked in. The ceiling is so high that clouds have formed near the light fixtures, and a gentle drizzle is falling on the back rows. Nobody else seems to notice. The student next to you is your third-grade best friend, still eight years old, calmly writing with a crayon and humming the tune of a commercial you haven't heard in twenty years.

You check the clock on the wall. It has no hands, just the word **SOON** printed across its face.

You pick up your pencil, which has somehow become a very long feather, and try to answer the first question. As you write, the words come out in your mother's handwriting. This feels important, but you can't say why.

Question two is just a photograph of a door you've never seen, yet you know exactly what's behind it: the house you'll live in someday, the one with the green kitchen. The instructions say, *Do not open.* Of course, you already have. The door is open on your desk now, lying flat like a trapdoor, and warm air rises from it smelling of cinnamon and rain on hot pavement.

At the front of the room, the professor clears his throat. You realize with a jolt that you've never attended a single class. You didn't even know you were enrolled. The professor turns around, and he's you, older and a little gray at the temples, wearing the sweater you keep meaning to donate. He smiles kindly, the way you'd smile at a nervous stranger.

"There's no wrong answer," he says, "but there is a *late* one."

The clock now reads **SOONER**.

Panic rises in your chest. You flip the exam over, looking for the rest of the questions, and find the back page is a mirror. Your reflection is calmly filling out the test for you, confident and quick, pausing only to wink. You try to copy what it writes, but its answers are backwards, and by the time you decode one word, it has moved three lines ahead.

The drizzle from the back rows has become a real rain now, and the classroom floor is turning into a shallow lake. Desks bob gently like rowboats. Your third-grade friend has folded her exam into a paper boat and is sailing away toward the windows, waving goodbye.

"Pencils down," says the older you at the front of the room.

You look at your paper. The only thing written on it, in your own handwriting this time, is:

*I wasn't ready, but I came anyway.*

The professor walks down the aisle, the water parting politely around his ankles, and picks up your exam. He reads it for a long moment. Then he nods, stamps it with something that looks like a postmark from a country that doesn't exist, and hands it back.

"That's the one we were looking for," he says.

Before you can ask what that means, the bell rings, except it isn't a bell. It's your alarm clock, chirping from somewhere far away, through all that rain.

You wake up with the faint smell of cinnamon still in the room, and for a few seconds you can't remember whether the exam is today, or already over, or whether it ever mattered at all.
