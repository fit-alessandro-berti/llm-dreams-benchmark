…wake up inside the project folder before you ever open your mouth.

The deadline isn’t a date anymore. It’s a sound: a soft gong vibrating through the fan, making the printer hum like a tuning fork. Your boss stands just outside the doorway with a coffee cup balanced on nothing, staring at you as if you’re the equation that’s been missing a variable.

You reach for the laptop and the keys become feathers. The slide deck becomes a staircase. The client’s “final” email slides upward off the screen and lands on your desk as a paper airplane shaped like a question mark. Across the floor, every deadlined item you thought you had is already finished — not because you did it, but because the dream finished it while you were asleep.

Then the boss speaks, not loudly, but with the calm of someone who has already beaten the deadline:  
“The impossible part wasn’t the work. The impossible part is that you’re still awake.”

So you stop trying to finish. You start translating. Deadline into rhythm. Panic into pattern. Boss’s glare into light. Suddenly the project stops being a thing to complete and becomes a shape you can inhabit — and when the gong finally fades, the deadline is gone, replaced by a single demand that feels less like an assignment and more like a question: what should the work be *for*?
