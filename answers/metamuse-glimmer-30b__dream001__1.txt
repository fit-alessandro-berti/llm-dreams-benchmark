You start to run.

The street under your feet is wet even though it hasn't rained, and it reflects a sky that is the wrong color — a bruised peach that never moves. You pull out your phone to check the time and the calendar, but the screen is filled with smudged ink that keeps rewriting itself. 11:47. 9:02. 11:47 again. The notification says *Meeting — Location TBC*.

You try to ask for directions. A woman in a long coat walks past you holding a grocery bag full of live goldfish, and when you call out she turns her head 180 degrees to look at you, smiles politely, and says, "It's the third door on the left," before continuing on. The third door on the left is a brick wall. When you touch it, the bricks are warm and pulse like skin.

The city is rearranging itself as you move. The avenue you were just on is now a narrow stairwell that spirals up into a shopping mall you recognize from childhood, but all the stores are closed and the mannequins are all wearing your face. A soft chime sounds every time you pass one. Somewhere behind you, a crowd is gathering but you can't see them, only hear their shoes squeaking in unison.

You start to panic and start to laugh at the same time, because you realize you know this feeling. The meeting is important, but not the meeting itself — it's the *idea* of being late. You keep sprinting toward a glowing sign that reads MEETING THIS WAY in letters that flicker and change language. Just as you reach it, the street opens up into a vast auditorium filled with empty chairs, and at the front a table is set for one. A nameplate reads YOUR NAME.

You sit down. The clock on the wall ticks backward. A door at the back of the hall opens and you — an older version of you, younger version of you — walk in, take the other seats, and wait with you. The hour is up. No one arrives. The lights dim, and you understand you were never supposed to find the meeting, only to keep looking for it.
