You are about to give a speech to a large audience. As you step onto the stage, you see the crowd looking expectantly at you, and you begin to speak—but your words come out as blue moths, drifting from your mouth into the warm hall air. They glow faintly, landing on the faces of strangers, each one whispering something true only to the person who hears it. The microphone floats a little above its stand, waiting patiently. You realize you are not afraid, and the realization feels lighter than sleep. From the back row rises a child with stars in their hair, holding up a sign that says, “We were listening even before you arrived.” You smile, and the stage beneath you becomes a field, then a lake, then a book, its pages turning themselves toward a light that has no source. The audience is still watching, but now they are also part of the dream, carrying it onward with their eyes closed.
