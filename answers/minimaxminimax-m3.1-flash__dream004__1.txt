…and when you touch it, every color in the world rushes toward the canvas.

Rivers climb sideways. Mountains fold into birds. The sun changes from a golden coin to a silver fish to a bright square that blinks like an eye. Nothing keeps its shape for long, yet the canvas remains perfectly still—a perfect rectangle of white suspended in the air.

As you draw near, the edges begin to breathe.

A brush appears in your hand, warm as living wood. Before you can decide what to paint, a drop of midnight blue falls from the sky and lands on the canvas, blooming into a small, impossible sea. Waves rise from it in miniature, reflecting moons that do not exist.

Then more colors arrive.

They pour from your fingertips, leap from the clouds, and crawl out of the shifting ground. They gather on the canvas, rearranging themselves into a landscape you half recognize: a winding road, a distant city, a window with someone standing behind it.

You paint that window more clearly.

Inside, someone who looks like you lifts their own brush and begins to paint a canvas.

And that canvas starts to breathe too.
