You are in a world where colors and shapes are constantly changing. As you explore this surreal landscape, you come across a blank canvas and it is the only still thing in the whole country of motion.

The ground keeps rearranging itself into dunes, then tessellations, then something like a ribcage of light, but the canvas does not flicker. It hangs in the air as if the world had forgotten to invent a wall for it. Beside it a brush is already wet, though nothing here has held still long enough to keep a single color.

You stop walking, because it is the first thing that does not melt. The white is so complete it makes the rest of the world look unfinished.

You reach out. Your hand leaves no fingerprint. Instead the canvas drinks the colors sliding off your skin — the ones that have been changing you since you arrived — and a first stroke appears without a brush: a thin, trembling line the exact blue your shadow was a moment ago.

The line forks. It becomes a river. A ribbon of violet peels off a passing hill and soaks into the white. A triangle that was a bird a moment ago collapses into gold. The landscape begins to donate itself: rivers flatten into brushstrokes, trees collapse into the idea of green.

You watch your own silhouette appear at the center — not as you are now, but as a geometry of light that has not decided whether it is skin or stained glass. The painted you is already mid-step, already looking back.

You touch the surface. It is warm, like a forehead. Behind you the world freezes mid-shift: a river hanging in the air like a ribbon cut from silk, a tree that is half oak and half staircase.

On the canvas, the frozen world finishes itself. The painted you blinks. The canvas inhales.

You feel the ground become pigment, your breath become varnish. Your foot is already on the frame.

The blankness was never empty. It was the only place the dream could hold still long enough to see itself.
