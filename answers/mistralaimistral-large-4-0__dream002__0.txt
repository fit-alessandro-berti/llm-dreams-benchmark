...the air turns to copper on your tongue, thick with the scent of ozone and wet stone. Your breath clouds in the sudden cold, though summer clung to these leaves only a heartbeat ago.

The trees stop swaying. Instead, they begin to *listen*—their branches stiffening, bark rippling like water disturbed by a dropped pebble. The sky above doesn't darken; it *fractures*, splitting into geometric shards of bruised purple and gold that hang suspended, humming.

Then you notice your shadow. It is stretching in the wrong direction, pooling at your feet like ink, and from its depths something is rising—not a monster, but a door. Wooden, weathered, with a brass knob that gleams as if someone has just polished it.

The wind arrives all at once, but it carries no leaves, no debris. It carries *whispers*—your own voice, from years ago, calling you toward the threshold. The moss beneath your feet grows soft, then spongy, then begins to glow faint blue, revealing roots that pulse with bioluminescent rhythm, mapping a path that leads straight into the heart of the storm.

You step forward. The rain begins to fall upward.

Behind you, the peaceful forest seals itself shut like a book. Ahead, the door in your shadow swings open on its own, revealing not darkness, but a staircase spiraling down into a sky full of stars that shouldn't be there yet.

You reach for the knob. It is warm.
