The metal is warm, almost feverish, and the designs—spirals nested within spirals, dotted with tiny settings of what might be opals or captured moonlight—morph lazily whenever you shift your gaze. You trace a looping motif with your thumb, and the box hums, a low vibration that travels up your arm and settles behind your eyes like the taste of rain on a hot day.

There is no keyhole, no seam, no hinge. Only the patterns, which now begin to rearrange themselves into something almost legible: a question, or perhaps a memory. You lean closer, and the air around you thickens with the scent of old libraries and ozone. The room you're in—a dim, familiar attic you haven't visited in years—starts to dissolve at the edges, the rafters dripping into a twilight sky. You realize you're floating, or perhaps the box is growing, its surface expanding until it fills your vision like a map of a world you've never seen but somehow recognize.

A voice, neither yours nor anyone else's, whispers from the patterns: *“What have you kept locked away that you are afraid to remember?”* The lid suddenly softens, becoming translucent as frosted glass. Inside, there is no treasure, no jewel—only a small, perfectly preserved moment: your seventh birthday, the exact shade of the candles' flames, your mother's laugh before the argument, the way the light hit the kitchen floor. It's all there, suspended in amber-like stillness. You understand with the clarity only dreams allow that the box was never meant to be opened with a key, but with a willingness to look directly at what you've hidden from yourself.

Tears prick your eyes, and as one falls, it lands on the box's surface. The patterns flare brilliantly, and the attic, the twilight, the voice—all of it folds inward like a paper lantern catching fire. You wake with the phantom weight of the box still in your hands, and the taste of rain on your tongue.
