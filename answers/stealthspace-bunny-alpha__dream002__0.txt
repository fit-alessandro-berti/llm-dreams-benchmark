The wind dies first. The birds fall silent, the stream seems to hold its breath, and every blade of grass goes still. Then the trees bend toward you, their leaves flashing pale silver as a cold gust races through the forest.

You look up. The blue has drained from the sky, leaving a deep, bruised violet. Lightning splits the darkness, and thunder follows—not a crack, but a long, rolling rumble, as if the world were turning over in its sleep.

The path beneath your feet begins to move.

You run anyway. Branches whip past your face. Rain needles through the canopy ahead of you, though the clouds are still miles away. With every step, the forest seems to stretch, familiar trees returning in new places, their roots clawing at the earth.

Then you see a light beneath an ancient oak.

You crawl beneath its roots and curl into the shelter of the soil. Outside, the storm tears the night apart. Lightning turns the world into a series of frozen images—silver leaves, black shadows, your trembling hands pressed into the earth. The roar of the rain grows louder—

And stops.

Silence settles, deep and ringing.

When you crawl out, the forest has changed. Every leaf is silver with rain. The air smells of petrichor and warm earth. Above you, the storm clouds have opened to reveal a sky crowded with stars.

And in the clearing ahead, the rain hangs motionless, suspended like a field of tiny stars.

You step toward it.

The water closes around you—not cold, but warm; not heavy, but weightless. And somewhere far away, or very close, a familiar voice whispers:

*You found me.*
