...and you, after a moment of hesitation, decide to trust them.

There are five of them, and none of them look alike — an old man in a bright yellow raincoat even though it isn't raining, a teenage girl carrying a potted plant, a street musician with a violin case, a woman in a business suit holding a paper map upside down, and a little kid wearing light-up sneakers.

"You look like you're looking for home," the old man says, not unkindly, "but not the kind with an address."

You want to tell him he's wrong, that you just need to find the train station, but the words that come out are, "I don't know where I belong anymore."

They don't seem surprised at all. The girl hands you the plant to hold — it's surprisingly warm, like it's been sitting in the sun — and the woman turns her map right-side up. Suddenly all the streets on it have your name on them.

As you walk together, the bustling city stops feeling so loud. The crowds part around your little group like a river around stones. The musician starts humming a song you swear you knew as a child but had forgotten, and the kid's sneakers light up with every step, leaving little glowing footprints on the pavement behind you so you can always find your way back.

You pass buildings you recognize, but they've changed in that dream-logic way — your childhood school is next to the apartment you live in now, a library is growing out of a subway entrance, and above it all the sky is turning that soft, gold-pink color just before evening.

Finally, you stop in front of a door. It's not your door, not exactly, but you know what's behind it: warmth, and people waiting for you, and a place at a table set just for you.

You turn to thank the strangers, but they've already started to fade at the edges, smiling like they were never really strangers at all — just parts of yourself you'd forgotten: the wise part, the hopeful part, the creative part, the capable part, and the small, brave part that still believes in wonder.

"Will I get lost again?" you ask.

"Probably," the old man says with a wink. "But now you know — you never have to find your way alone."

You open the door, step into the light —

—and wake up in your own bed, the morning sun on your face, feeling a little less lost than you did the night before.
