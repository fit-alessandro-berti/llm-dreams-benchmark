"We need it by five o'clock," your boss says, checking their watch. But when you look at the clock on the wall, the numbers are melting, sliding down the face like wax. You try to read the time, but it keeps changing—4:59, then 9:00, then something that isn't a number at all.

You sit down at your computer, but the keyboard has too many keys, more than it should, and they keep rearranging themselves. You type anyway, hoping the right words come out. The document opens, and you start working frantically, but every sentence you write disappears the moment you finish it. You type faster. Your fingers feel enormous, clumsy, like they belong to someone else.

"Don't forget the presentation portion," your boss says. You turn around, and they're suddenly standing much closer than before, though you never heard footsteps. Their face is slightly wrong—not unfamiliar, just... off, like a photocopy of a photocopy.

You look back at the screen. The deadline is now in ten minutes. Or maybe it was ten minutes ago. You realize you're not sure if you've been working for hours or seconds. Your coffee cup is empty, then full, then empty again.

You try to stand up to ask for an extension, but your chair has grown roots into the floor. You pull, and the whole office tilts sideways. Your coworkers are all still typing, faces blank, fingers blurring. Someone is crying quietly, but when you look, everyone is smiling.

"I just need more time," you say, and your voice comes out as someone else's voice.

Your boss leans in. "The deadline was yesterday," they say gently. "It's always been yesterday."

You wake up with your heart pounding, relieved—until you check the time and realize you overslept, and there's a notification on your phone from your actual boss, sent at 6 a.m.: *"Morning! Got a quick project for you..."*
