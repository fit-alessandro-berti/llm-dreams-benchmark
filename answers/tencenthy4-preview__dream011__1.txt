...not multiple choice, but a series of shifting, glowing landscapes trapped within the fibers of the paper. 

The first question is a tiny, incredibly detailed diorama of a forest at night. You somehow understand, with the unshakeable logic of dreams, that to answer it, you must reach your hand into the page and find the specific moonbeam hidden inside the trees. 

You plunge your hand into the paper. It feels like dipping your fingers into cool, rushing water. But as you search the paper-forest, the classroom around you begins to change. The harsh, buzzing fluorescent lights overhead dissolve into a soft, velvet twilight. The desks beside you sprout thick green moss and soft, glowing mushrooms.

You look up at the front of the room. The professor is no longer a professor; he is a tall, elegant owl wearing a tweed jacket with elbow patches. 

"Time is running out," the owl hoots, tapping a claw against a pocket watch that has no hands. 

Panic surges through your chest. You never studied for this! You look back down at your desk, but the forest on the page has grown dark and ominous. A single, luminous question blooms across the paper in glowing blue ink: ***"What is the exact weight of a forgotten dream?"***

You have absolutely no idea. You look down at your hand and realize that somewhere in the paper-forest, you’ve grabbed an object. It’s a single, perfect, iridescent seashell. 

You know, with sudden crushing clarity, that a seashell is the wrong answer. But it’s the only one you have. And just as the owl opens its beak to announce the end of the exam, the seashell in your hand begins to hum, and the sound it makes is your favorite song.
