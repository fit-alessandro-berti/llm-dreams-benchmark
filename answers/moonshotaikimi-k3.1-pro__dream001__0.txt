follow a woman in a green coat who is carrying a clock instead of a handbag. She seems to know you.

“There you are,” she says. “We should hurry.”

“Do you know where the meeting is?”

“Of course. You chose the place yourself.”

She leads you through an arcade where all the shops sell closed doors, then down a flight of stairs that comes out on the roof of a tower. Below, the city rearranges itself whenever you stop looking directly at it. Streets curl into handwriting. Bridges pack themselves away. In the distance, something enormous turns over in its sleep.

The clock under the woman’s arm has no hands, only a small door. At every quarter hour, the door opens and a blackbird flies out.

Eventually she stops before a building with your childhood address written above the entrance. Inside is a long conference room. Seven people sit around the table, and although some are children and one is very old, you recognize all of them as yourself.

An empty chair waits at the far end.

“You’re just in time,” says the youngest.

“For what?”

The oldest slides a sheet of paper toward you. At the top is printed:

**AGENDA: WHERE WE ARE GOING**

Beneath it, the page is blank.

“I thought you were going to tell me,” you say.

No one answers. Outside the windows, the whole city has become still, as if it, too, is listening.

The woman in green checks her clock. “One minute.”

So you pick up the pen. You intend to write an address, but instead you draw a single line. It wanders, doubles back, crosses itself, and finally runs off the edge of the page.

Somewhere far below, a street unfolds with exactly the same shape.

The others rise from their chairs. The youngest takes your hand. The oldest opens the window, though you are many stories above the ground, and beyond it there is no city at all—only a dark, familiar bedroom and the first pale light of morning.

Then the clock’s little door opens.

This time, no bird flies out. Instead, your alarm begins to ring.
