...you follow them, though you never actually told them where you were going.

They seem to know anyway. A woman in a yellow coat takes the lead, and the crowd parts around her like water. An old man beside you hums a song you almost recognize, something from a kitchen long ago. A child tugs your sleeve and hands you a paper map, but when you unfold it, the streets rearrange themselves as you watch, the ink drifting like slow rivers.

"Don't worry," the child says. "It does that until you stop looking so hard."

The city shifts as you walk. The skyscrapers lean in, their windows glowing amber, and the roar of traffic softens into something like ocean waves. You notice that the strangers' faces keep changing. For a moment the woman in yellow has your grandmother's eyes, and the old man laughs like a friend you haven't spoken to in years.

They lead you down a narrow alley that should be dark but isn't. Lanterns float overhead, untethered. At the end of it stands a door, the front door of every home you've ever lived in, somehow all at once.

"Here you are," the woman says.

"But I never told you where I was going," you say.

She smiles. "You weren't lost. You just forgot that you could ask."

You turn to thank them, but the alley is empty. There's only the humming, fading, and the map in your hand, where the streets have finally settled into a single line leading to where you stand.

You reach for the doorknob. It's warm, as though someone has just let go of it.
