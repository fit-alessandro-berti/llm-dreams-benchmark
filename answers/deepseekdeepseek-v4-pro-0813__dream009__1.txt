You wake up in a completely different era, with no modern technology around. As you explore your new surroundings, you realize you need to find food, water, and a way to blend in before anyone decides you don’t belong. The air smells of woodsmoke and damp straw; there are no engines, no screens, only the creak of wooden carts and the murmur of unfamiliar voices.

You trade your modern jacket—too smooth, too strange—for a rough woolen cloak and a heel of dark bread. A farmer’s wife studies your face as if she almost recognizes you from a story. By dusk you find a village and offer your hands: hauling water, splitting wood, feeding the potter’s fire. In exchange, you get a corner of a workshop floor and a blanket that smells of hay.

That night, beneath stars brighter than you’ve ever seen, you realize you need to stop searching for the way back. The dream has its own rules, and it wants you to listen. Just before dawn, a voice calls your name from beyond the gate—not urgent, but patient, like someone holding open a door.

You stand, heart pounding. You can follow the voice back to your old life, or stay and discover who you become without all the noise. The dream tilts around you, waiting.

And then you wake—just for a moment—with woodsmoke on your skin and a strange, impossible peace in your chest.
