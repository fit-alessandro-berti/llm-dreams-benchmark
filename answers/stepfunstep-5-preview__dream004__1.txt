You step closer. Your reflection isn’t in its surface. Instead, the white deepens, becoming not a color but an absence, a pause. When you reach out, your hand doesn’t cast a shadow; it sinks. The texture is cool and rough, like touching the concept of paper.

Then the landscape notices you.

A wave of shifting umber geometry rolls toward the canvas, intending to swallow it whole. Instinctively, you place your palm against the blank surface. A shock of gray blooms outward from your touch—not painted, but remembered. The wave crashes against the gray and dissolves into soft, wet sounds like applause.

You withdraw your hand. The mark remains: a handprint of quiet static.

Encouraged, you try again with your other hand. This time, you think of the smell of rain on hot asphalt, and a scentless shimmer of slate blue spreads from your fingertips, pooling into a small, perfect rectangle of calm. The blue does not move. It does not change. It simply *stays*.

Tears you did not know you were crying in this dream fall onto the canvas. Where they land, flowers with piano-key petals unfurl, each note silent but heavy. You realize the canvas isn’t blank. It is waiting. It is a net catching the flying pieces of this world, letting you choose what to keep.

You begin to draw with your whole arm. You do not use tools; you use the momentum of your heartbeat. A horizon line appears—straight, honest, stubborn. You paint a tree with leaves like forgotten alphabets. You paint a door. You paint a version of yourself that isn’t exploring.

As the shifting world screams and swirls around your island of stillness, you step into the painting. The canvas swallows you from behind, and then—you are standing in the landscape you made. The sky here is a deep, unmoving indigo. The shapes are simple: circles, squares, a single moon that sits perfectly round and does not blink.

Behind you, the canvas has become a mirror. In it, you see the wild, churning world beyond. And in that world, there is another you—colorless and flat—standing before the mirror, reaching out.

The dream tilts. You wake still holding your hand to the glass, your palm warm with the memory of static.
