You decide to **plant a garden**.

The decision feels impossibly small beneath the weight of a ruined world. The wind still carries ash, the sun still burns through a permanent haze, and somewhere beyond the jagged skyline, something metallic occasionally groans in the heat. But you kneel beside a collapsed highway with three packets of seeds in your pocket, and you begin.

You clear stones from the soil with your bare hands. You dig shallow channels for the rain. From a shattered satellite dish, you fashion a funnel to catch what little water falls. When a storm tears the clouds open, you stand beneath it with your face turned upward, laughing until the cold cuts your lungs.

The first seeds disappear without a sign.

You plant them again.

Weeks pass. You ration what you can’t replace, sleep beneath a roof patched with salvaged metal, and listen to every unfamiliar sound outside. Some nights, you are certain footsteps mean someone has found you. You imagine a hard life ended for the sake of a few handfuls of dirt.

But one morning, you see a green shoot—tiny, stubborn, shining with dew.

You kneel beside it.

Soon there are more. Beans climb strings between rusted cars. Squash spreads across the dust. Corn pushes through ground that hasn’t held anything tender in years. You build a scarecrow from old clothes and a coil of wire, though there are no birds left to fear it. It watches anyway, guarding what you have made.

Then the people come.

At first, they watch from a distance. A mother with an empty pack. An old man who knows how to mend irrigation channels. Children who move quietly, as if the ruins might punish them for being happy. You give some of your food away. Later, they return with tools, containers, stories.

You work together.

Nothing is fixed. The seasons remain uncertain, the world remains broken, and tomorrow still carries its teeth. But soil is filled with roots. A water tank stands beside the shelter. Laughter reaches the field, followed by the soft exclamation of another seedling.

Outside, dust still drifts over the horizon.

Beneath your hands, the earth is cool and waiting.

For the first time since the world ended, so are you.
