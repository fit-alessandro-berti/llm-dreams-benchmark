…and as your fingers trace them, the carvings begin to move—not like a trick of the light, but like they’re breathing.

The patterns spiral inward, iron vines and crescent moons and eyes that blink one by one. A voice, soft as dust, whispers from the wood: *“You already have the key.”*

You search your pockets. Nothing. But the voice keeps repeating, closer now, until you realize the key isn’t something you carry—it’s something you remember. A word. A name. A moment you buried long ago.

You lean closer. The lock has no keyhole. Instead, there’s a shallow indentation shaped like a hand. Without thinking, you press your palm to it.

The wood warms. The patterns glow like embers through paper. The lock clicks—not from your hand, but from deep inside your chest.

When the lid opens, light pours out, and you see not treasure, not darkness, but a thousand tiny doors, each one labeled with a memory you’d forgotten. You step inside without deciding to.

And as you fall into the first memory, still dreaming, you hear the box close softly behind you—and the voice says, *“Now open yourself.”*
