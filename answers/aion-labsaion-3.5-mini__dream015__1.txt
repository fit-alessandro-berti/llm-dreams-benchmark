...you decide to keep walking.

The ash falls like grey snow, settling on your shoulders as you pick your way through the skeleton of a city. Most people gave up long ago—most people did—but something in you refuses. Maybe it's dream logic, that strange certainty that there's somewhere you're meant to be.

In your pack you carry the only thing that matters: seeds. Tomato. Carrot. Sunflower. Found in a ruined hardware store three days ago, buried under a collapsed shelf you spent hours moving, cutting your hands in the process. You didn't care. Seeds are the future pretending it still exists.

You hear them before you see them—scavengers moving through the rubble like rats. You press yourself beneath a fallen overpass, hold your breath, and watch their boots pass inches from your face. When they're gone, you emerge, shake the dust from your coat, and keep heading north, toward the valley a dying old man once told you about, where the rain still falls clean.

The days blur together. You drink from puddles you probably shouldn't. You sleep curled in the hollow of a burned-out car. You talk to yourself just to remember what your voice sounds like.

And then, one morning—the ash stops.

You crest a hill and see it: green. Actual green, spilling down the valley floor. A river runs through it, silver in the dawn light. On the hillside, a small cluster of figures tends rows of crops. They see you. One of them raises a hand—not a weapon. Just a hand.

You decide, right there, that you will plant those seeds.

You take your first step down into the valley, and as you do, the dream begins to dissolve at its edges—the colors softening, the world growing warm and weightless—until you wake.

But somewhere in you, the part that never stopped walking is still going. And you carry that with you all day.
