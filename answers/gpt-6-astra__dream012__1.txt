One by one, their small gold flames rise against the rain. The crew watches you, waiting for a heading, but the compass has begun to turn in slow, deliberate circles.

“Hold her steady,” you say.

“Against what?” asks the helmsman.

Before you can answer, something knocks beneath the hull.

Three measured blows.

The storm falls silent. Waves remain standing around the ship, tall and motionless, their white crests hanging overhead. In that impossible hush, you hear footsteps on the other side of the water.

A door appears in the sea beside you. Blue paint, a brass handle, a crack near the bottom: the door to your childhood bedroom.

Your youngest deckhand leans over the rail.

“Captain,” she whispers, “someone’s left the light on.”

You know then why the ship has sailed for so many years without reaching shore. You remember every port you refused to enter, every morning you woke before the gulls began to cry.

The handle turns.

Behind you, the crew draws close. The helmsman takes your coat. Someone presses a lantern into your hand.

“We can keep her steady,” he says.

You climb down the rope ladder. The sea feels firm beneath your boots, though you can feel a distant swell moving through it. At the door, you pause and listen.

From inside comes the soft scrape of a chair being pulled up to a table.

You open it.

Your mother sits beside the bed with a book open in her lap. She looks older than you remember. On the windowsill, a toy ship drips seawater onto the floor.

“There you are,” she says, putting her finger between the pages.

You sit beside her, still holding the lantern.

For a while, she lets you tell her about the storm.
