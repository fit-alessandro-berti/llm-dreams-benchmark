Of course — let's step back into the dream:

You are walking through a peaceful forest when suddenly the weather changes dramatically. You feel a storm approaching and...

... the air grows thick and electric against your skin. The soft sunlight filtering through the leaves flickers and fades, as if someone is dimming the sky.

A cool wind rushes up from behind you, lifting fallen leaves into slow spirals around your feet. The tall trees begin to sway and creak, whispering to each other. Birds go silent all at once.

Then — *crack*. A low roll of thunder in the distance. The smell of rain and pine fills the air.

You start to run, not out of fear, but out of instinct, your feet light on the mossy path. Drops begin to fall, huge and warm. Ahead of you, through the darkening trees, you see a soft golden glow — a small wooden cabin with a lantern lit on the porch.

Just as the sky opens and rain pours down in silver sheets, you reach the porch and step under cover. You're safe, dry, heart pounding. Lightning lights up the whole forest in white for a second, and you see it clearly — every tree, every raindrop frozen in mid-air, beautiful.

And then, as quickly as it came, the storm softens. The thunder fades to a gentle rumble, the rain to a lullaby patter on the roof. A mist rises from the forest floor, and the sun peeks back through.

You wake up feeling calm, refreshed, as if the storm washed something heavy away.
