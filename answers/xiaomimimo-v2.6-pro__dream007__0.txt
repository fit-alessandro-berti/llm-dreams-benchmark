You are in the middle of a heated argument with a close friend. The disagreement is about something important to both of you, and you decide to step outside to cool off.

But when you open the door, you don't step outside — you step into a vast, silver lake that stretches to every horizon. The water is warm and still, and your friend's voice follows you through the door: *"You never finish what you start."*

You wade deeper, but the lake doesn't get deeper. Instead, the sky gets closer. Clouds brush your shoulders. A wooden rowboat drifts past, empty, oars still dripping. You climb in. Beneath the water's surface, you can see all of your past conversations with your friend — hundreds of them, written on paper, sinking slowly like autumn leaves.

You reach down and pull one up. It's dry. It reads: *"This was always going to happen."*

Suddenly the boat isn't a boat — it's the kitchen table where the argument started. Your friend is sitting across from you again, but their face is calm now. Too calm. Like a photograph. They say nothing.

You realize you forgot what you were arguing about. The important thing is gone. There's just a shape where it used to be, like a tooth that fell out and left behind a space your tongue can't stop searching.

Then your friend leans forward and whispers: *"I'm sorry I brought it here."*

You look down. The table has become water again. The lake is beneath you. And somewhere very far away — in a direction that doesn't exist on any map — you hear yourself saying, *"It's okay. Let's start over."*

And the dream begins again.
