...as you move deeper into the maze, the corridors begin to rearrange themselves behind you, as if the maze is learning from each solution. Walls become chalkboards covered in half-finished equations; doors ask riddles in voices you almost recognize. You solve a bridge of interlocking gears, a room where gravity obeys only correct syllogisms, and a staircase that counts backward in prime numbers. But with every puzzle solved, the next grows less like a test and more like a memory.

At the center, there is no final vault—only a small mirror on a pedestal. The inscription reads: *“Solve the one who entered.”* You stare at your reflection, and the maze around you falls silent. The equations fade. The walls stop shifting. The only lock left is the belief that there must be a trick.

When you finally laugh—tired, relieved, a little amazed—the mirror opens like a door. Beyond it lies a moonlit garden with no paths and no puzzles, just the soft certainty that the maze was never keeping you in. It was teaching you how to leave. You step through, and somewhere far away, morning begins to wake you.
