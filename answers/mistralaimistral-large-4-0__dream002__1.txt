You are walking through a peaceful forest when suddenly the weather changes dramatically. You feel a storm approaching and the air grows heavy, charged with the scent of ozone and wet stone—though no stones have been visible on the path for miles. The sky above the canopy bruises from soft green to a bruised purple, and the trees begin to hum, not with wind, but with voices.

The first drops fall. They are not water, but liquid silver that hisses against your skin and leaves behind constellations that fade too quickly. You look down and realize your boots have sunk into the earth, which is no longer soil but a dark, breathing velvet. The forest path spirals upward now, defying gravity, and you follow it without questioning why.

Lightning does not flash; it freezes, hanging in the air like cracked glass sculptures of deer and impossible birds. As you climb, the storm reveals its secret: it is not destroying the forest, but unwrapping it. The bark peels back from the ancient oaks to reveal not wood, but staircases leading into hollow spaces where libraries float in mid-air, their books writing themselves in languages that make your teeth ache with recognition.

You reach a clearing where the rain falls upward, returning to clouds that are actually sheets of parchment covered in your own handwriting from futures you haven't lived yet. A figure stands there—not a person, but the space between two heartbeats—and offers you an umbrella made of your own shadow. When you take it, the storm stops, but you are no longer sure if you are still walking through the forest, or if the forest is walking through you, carrying you toward a dawn that tastes like copper and honey, waking up with the taste of silver still on your tongue and the sound of humming trees in your bones.
