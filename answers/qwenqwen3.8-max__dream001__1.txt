You start to walk faster, your shoes clicking against streets that seem to rearrange themselves when you glance away. Every corner looks familiar for half a second, then dissolves into a different district: a market selling bottled weather, a bridge made of piano keys, a plaza where the fountains flow upward into pale clouds.

You ask a passing stranger for directions, but when they open their mouth, a train schedule flutters out like moths. You check your pockets and find only an old ticket stub, a button shaped like an eye, and the growing certainty that the meeting is not with a person, but with something you forgot you promised.

With twenty minutes left, you see a tower in the distance, black and smooth as wet glass. You know, without knowing how, that the meeting is there. You run, but the city stretches beneath your feet, turning each block into a mile. Doors open along the street—offices, childhood kitchens, hospital waiting rooms, forests lit by green suns—each one whispering, “In here. It was always in here.”

Finally, breathless, you reach the tower. There is no door. Only a mirror.

In it, your reflection is already seated at a long table, calm and dressed differently, as if they have been waiting for you for years. They look up and say, “You’re late. But just in time.”

The mirror opens like water. You step through.

And wake up with the strange, quiet feeling that you arrived exactly where you were supposed to be.
