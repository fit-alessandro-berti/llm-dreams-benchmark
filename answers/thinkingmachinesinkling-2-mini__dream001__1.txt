You start to follow a pale stripe of light that cuts diagonally across the pavement like a chalk mark in a puzzle. The buildings on either side shift in color as you near them—faded ochre becoming lavender, chrome becoming dust, then becoming your own childhood street for a heartbeat before folding away.

Every taxi carries a sign with your name, but none actually stop. A fountain spills orange water onto shoes that are already too empty to match. At the corner, a clerk with a magnifying glass taps a doorplate that simply says “MEETING,” and the shadow of your hand on the glass writes a time: not one hour from now, but the exact minute you stop searching.

You follow the stripe deeper into the city, through tunnels that behave less like streets and more like a held breath. The hour compresses. Announcements crackle in your memory—“all attendees, please wait in the lobby that understands you”—while somewhere above, a clock tower keeps announcing the future while your pocket watch keeps insisting it is already here.

At last you reach a high vestibule with a single chair facing a blank screen. On it, no agenda appears. Instead the room begins to assemble itself around the question you kept circling: not where the meeting is, but why you were summoned. The final sound is soft, like pages turning, and the screen lights up with your own face—not distressed, not lost, but waiting for the sentence you have been trying to finish.
