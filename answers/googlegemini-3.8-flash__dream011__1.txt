...not written in ink, but faintly embossed into the paper like blind Braille that shifts beneath the glare of the fluorescent lights. 

And they aren't about the syllabus at all. 

You lean closer, your heart thumping a frantic rhythm against your ribs. The first question doesn't ask for a formula or a historical date. It reads: 

*1. At what exact moment did you realize you were pretending, and who was the first person to believe you? (Show your work).*

Around you, the lecture hall is dead silent save for the relentless, rhythmic scratching of dozens of pencils against paper. The other students are bent double, writing with a desperate, terrifying speed. You turn to look at the person next to you, but their face is a soft, indistinct blur, like a watercolor left out in the rain.

Your gaze darts back down. The second question is already forming, the fibers of the page darkening as if bruised:

*2. If you carry every expectation placed upon you across a bridge made of your own patience, how much will the river swallow when it collapses?*

You reach for your pen, but your fingers feel thick, numb, and curiously slow—moving through the air as if fighting against deep water. When you finally touch the plastic barrel, it yields like warm candle wax, bending uselessly in your grip. 

"Half the time has elapsed," the proctor announces from the front of the room. Their voice doesn't come from their mouth; it echoes from the air vents, hollow and metallic. 

Panic spikes in your chest. You flip the paper over, desperate for a multiple-choice section, a true-or-false, anything with an objective edge. But the back page is completely blank—until words bleed through from the other side, forming a single, final prompt at the bottom:

*3. You have already chosen. Why are you still sitting here?*

The clock on the wall begins to tick backward, the hands spinning like a silver coin, and the wooden desk beneath your hands dissolves into mist.
