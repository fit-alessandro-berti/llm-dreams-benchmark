You begin to speak, but no sound comes out at first.

Your mouth opens, and for a heart-stopping second, there is only silence. The lights are warm on your face, the microphone is right there, and every eye in the crowd is on you. You glance down at the podium where your notes should be - but the pages are completely blank. Or no, not blank. They're shifting. The words are swimming like little black fish across the white paper, rearranging themselves faster than you can read them.

A wave of panic rises in your chest, familiar and cold.

And then, something strange happens.

You take a breath. You let go of trying to read the pages.

And you begin to speak anyway - not from the notes, but from somewhere deeper. Your voice finally comes, but it doesn't sound quite like your own. It sounds clearer, calmer, echoing slightly as if the whole room is speaking with you.

You talk about the very thing you're feeling right now - about standing on an edge, about being seen, about not having the perfect words prepared. And as you speak, the words become visible. Each sentence leaves your mouth as a ribbon of light, curling up into the air above the audience, hanging there and glowing softly.

The crowd isn't judging you. Their faces soften. They nod. Some of them smile, because they recognize that feeling too. The ribbons of light drift down from the ceiling and settle over them like a warm blanket.

You realize you didn't need a script at all. You only needed to be there, honest and present.

The applause doesn't sound like clapping - it sounds like wind moving through trees, like the whole room exhaling at once. The stage lights get softer and warmer, until they feel exactly like sunlight.

And just as you feel completely at ease, completely at home on that stage, you begin to gently float an inch above the floor, still speaking, still glowing.
