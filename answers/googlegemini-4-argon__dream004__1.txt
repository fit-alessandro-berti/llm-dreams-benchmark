...it is the only still thing in the entire universe. 

As the sky above me melts from a deep, velvety violet into a bright, whistling yellow, and the ground beneath my feet ripples from sharp geometric crystals into soft, rolling spheres, the canvas remains stubbornly, impossibly white. It hums with a quiet gravity, pulling me toward it.

There are no brushes, no palettes, and no tubes of paint. Just the stark rectangle floating at chest height. 

Driven by an instinct that only makes sense in dreams, I reach out toward a passing breeze—which looks like a ribbon of iridescent teal—and pluck a piece of it out of the air. It feels warm and slightly fizzy in my hand. I press it against the canvas. 

The moment the color touches the white surface, it doesn't just leave a mark; it takes root. The teal expands, blooming into a crystal-clear ocean that seems to push *into* the canvas, creating a window of infinite depth. 

Excited, I grab a handful of the shifting ground—currently a vibrant, jagged crimson—and throw it at the canvas. It splatters and instantly freezes into a majestic, towering mountain range rising out of the teal sea. I realize the truth of this place: the world around me is chaotic and unstable because it is unfinished. The canvas isn't for painting a picture; it's an anchor for reality. 

I begin to work frantically, like a conductor leading a symphony of light. I catch a falling star that tastes like cinnamon and smear it across the top of the canvas to create a warm, golden sun. I pull the Indigo shadows from behind my own heels and weave them into a lush, quiet forest at the base of the crimson mountains. 

With every stroke I add to the canvas, the chaotic world around me grows quieter, draining into the frame. The dizzying kaleidoscope of my surroundings is being absorbed, stabilized, and given meaning within the borders of my creation. 

Finally, the world around me is completely empty—a vast, silent void of nothingness. Everything is inside the painting now. It is a breathtaking, breathing landscape of impossible beauty, humming with life. 

The frame of the canvas begins to dissolve, its edges softening into mist. The painted breeze wafts out, carrying the scent of pine and salt water, brushing against my face. I don't just look at the painting anymore. I lift my foot, step over the vanishing threshold, and walk into the world I just made, right as the golden sun begins to rise.
