You press your ear to the lid and hear the ocean—not a recording, but the whole ocean, vast and cold, with a distant bell ringing beneath it. The sound makes you remember a door at the end of a hallway from your childhood, a door you were told never to open. You had forgotten it until now.

Without thinking, you trace the pattern shaped like a wave.

The box clicks.

Inside, there is no treasure. There is only an old silver key resting on a bed of black sand. Beside it lies a note in your own handwriting:

*For the other box.*

You look up. The room has changed. The walls are dark wood, the ceiling is a lid, and far above you, an enormous face—your face—peers down through an opening in the sky.

You lift the key to show yourself, but the giant you only smiles and shakes their head. Behind you, waves begin spilling soundlessly across the floor.

Then you wake.

For a moment, everything is ordinary: morning light, a quiet house, the familiar weight of blankets.

Then you notice the taste of salt on your lips and the small silver key in your closed hand.

From beneath the bed comes a soft, patient click.
