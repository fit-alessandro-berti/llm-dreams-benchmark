The crowd doesn't gasp or recoil; instead, they lean in, their expectant faces softening into looks of quiet wonder as the moth-light dances across their cheeks.

You reach for the podium to steady yourself, but your hand passes straight through the polished wood—it's made entirely of morning mist. A flicker of panic rises, but it's a strange, weightless panic, like trying to run through waist-deep pudding. You try again, forcing air from your lungs, and this time a single, perfectly round glass marble rolls off your tongue, clattering across the stage and coming to rest at the very edge. Inside its curved surface, you see a hundred tiny, distorted reflections of the audience, all still staring up at you.

Suddenly, the vast hall stretches. The ceiling peels away to reveal a twilight sky full of slow-turning constellations, and the theater seats sprout thick, green vines that wind around the audience's ankles. No one seems to notice or care. They are still waiting, still watching you with that same patient, unsettling intensity. You glance down and realize you are barefoot, your toes sinking into warm, damp moss that has crept up from the footlights and swallowed the stage.

"Ladies and gentlemen," you finally manage, your voice returning, though it sounds as though it's echoing down a long, hollow tunnel, "I... I seem to have forgotten my notes."

The crowd does not laugh. Instead, they erupt into a synchronized, low humming—a warm, vibrating sound that you can feel in your teeth. And then, quite suddenly, you are floating. Not leaping or flying, just drifting gently upward, tethered to the mossy stage by a single, silver thread attached to your navel. As you rise higher, looking down at the sea of upturned faces, you see that every single person has their eyes closed, humming your name, and the moths are settling into their hair like illuminated flowers. 

Then the silver thread snaps, and you drift into the constellations, and the humming becomes the sound of rain, and you wake up with the distinct taste of glass marbles in your mouth.
