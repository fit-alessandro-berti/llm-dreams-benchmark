…speak, but instead of words, a small blue bird flies out of your mouth.

It lands on the microphone and tilts its head.

The audience applauds politely, as if this is how speeches usually begin.

You try again. A second bird emerges, then a third, each carrying a tiny scrap of paper. They flutter over the front row, dropping sentences into open hands. People read them and smile. Somewhere near the back, a woman begins to cry—not sadly, but as though someone has finally remembered her name.

You look down at your notes. The pages are blank except for a door drawn in pencil.

Behind you, a latch clicks.

The door is standing in the middle of the stage. Through it, you can see your childhood kitchen, full of late-afternoon sunlight. Someone has set a glass of water on the table.

The audience grows quiet.

You lean toward the microphone. The blue bird shifts aside.

“I didn’t know you were all coming,” you say.

Neither did I, says a sentence someone is holding in the front row.

You step closer to see who it is, but the stage has become very long. The lectern is far behind you now, and the front row remains just out of reach. You recognize a teacher, an old neighbor, someone you once sat beside on a train. They make room between them.

For the first time, you notice there is an empty chair with your coat draped over it.

You turn back toward the kitchen. The glass of water trembles gently on the table, keeping time with the applause.
