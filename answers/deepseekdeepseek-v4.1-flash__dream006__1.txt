You begin to speak, but no sound comes out—only a soft hum, like a tuning fork struck behind your ribs. The audience doesn’t seem to notice. They nod, smile, and take notes on paper that turns to water in their hands. You look down and see the stage is made of glass, and beneath it another audience is watching you from below, mirrored and waiting. You try to say “I’m sorry,” but the words become small paper boats and drift out over their heads. The room fills with the smell of rain. Then the spotlight warms your face, and you realize you’ve been speaking all along—not to them, but to yourself, in a voice so kind you almost don’t recognize it. The dream holds you there, quiet, until morning.
