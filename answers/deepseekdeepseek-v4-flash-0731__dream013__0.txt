...and the walls begin to hum, low and expectant, as if the maze itself is breathing with you.

You step forward. The first puzzle is carved into a stone door: a grid of glowing symbols, some shifting, some still. You kneel, studying the pattern. Time doesn't press you here—only curiosity. Your fingers move over the symbols, rearranging them in your mind until the logic clicks like a lock. The door slides open, revealing a corridor lined with mirrors.

But the mirrors don't show your reflection. They show versions of you from other dreams—one holding a lantern, one turning a key, one standing at the edge of a vast ocean. A voice, soft and familiar, whispers from the glass: *“Only the version who knows why they came may pass.”*

You pause. Why *did* you come? You search your memory, but the reason drifts just out of reach, like a word on the tip of your tongue. Then you notice: each mirror-version is holding something small and bright. The lantern. The key. A flower. A compass. And in the center mirror, a version of you is holding nothing—but smiling.

You step toward that mirror. “I came to remember,” you say.

The mirror ripples. It opens like a curtain of water, and you walk through.

Beyond it, the maze changes. The stone walls soften into root and moss. The puzzles become organic—riddles written on leaves, knots tied in vines, doors made of whispering grass. You realize the maze isn't a prison. It's a garden, grown from all the questions you've ever asked yourself. Each puzzle you solve is just you, untangling a thought you'd buried.

Deeper and deeper you go. The air grows warm and golden. Eventually you reach a clearing. At the center stands a single tree with silver leaves. On its lowest branch hangs a bell, and beside it, a note in your own handwriting:

*“You don't have to solve everything. You just have to arrive.”*

You ring the bell.

The sound is soft, but it echoes through every corridor of the maze, through every puzzle, through every version of you that ever wondered. And then you wake—not with a start, but gently, like surfacing from deep water. For a moment you can still feel the silver leaves on your skin.

And you smile, because you know the maze is still there, waiting for the next time you dream.
