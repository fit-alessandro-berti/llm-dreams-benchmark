You are in a world where colors and shapes are constantly changing. As you explore this surreal landscape, you come across a blank canvas and a brush standing upright in a silver puddle. The brush bows when you approach, bending at its wooden waist, and the canvas trembles as though trying not to breathe.

You lift the brush. The puddle climbs its bristles and turns first blue, then gold, then a color you recognize but cannot name. You draw a door.

The moment the final line meets the first, the door opens inward. Beyond it is the landscape you have just left, only seen from high above: violet forests folding themselves into birds, rivers tying and untying knots, mountains exchanging their peaks like hats. In the middle stands a tiny figure holding a brush before a tiny canvas. When you raise your hand, the figure raises its hand too.

You paint a sun above the door. Warmth spills across your face, and somewhere behind you a crowd applauds. You turn, but the applause is coming from a field of red triangles. Each triangle has two white gloves and claps with perfect politeness. Their shadows, however, point away from them and creep toward the canvas.

You paint eyes in the sky so you can see what the shadows want. At once the clouds blink. The whole world looks down through a thousand blue irises, and you feel the attention of every stone and staircase. The shadows reach the painted door, slip through it, and gather around the tiny figure on the other side. They climb its legs and cover its body until it becomes a single black mark.

Then the mark lifts its brush.

A cold stroke crosses the sky above you. Half the eyes vanish. Another stroke removes the horizon. The distant forests and rivers slide soundlessly off the edge of the world. You understand that the little figure is painting you out.

In panic, you dip your brush into the silver puddle and paint a staircase onto the canvas. You step through the door and begin to climb. Every stair changes beneath your feet—marble, moss, piano keys, sleeping animals—but you keep going. The black figure climbs toward you from the other end. With each step it grows larger, until you meet halfway and find yourself face-to-face with someone whose features will not stay still.

For an instant it has your face. Then it has the face you had as a child. Then it is old, its cheeks full of stars, its eyes two open doors.

“Which one of us is unfinished?” it asks.

You try to answer, but small paper moths fly from your mouth. They circle the two of you, and written across their wings are all the names you have forgotten. One settles on the figure’s forehead. The blackness cracks like thin ice.

Underneath is no person at all, only another blank canvas.

You touch your brush to it. You mean to paint a face, but the brush moves by itself, sketching a bedroom in quick, certain lines: a narrow bed, a curtained window, a chair with clothes draped over it. The room becomes so detailed that you can hear rain ticking against the glass. On the bed lies a sleeping figure. You lean closer and recognize yourself.

The sleeper’s eyes open.

At once the staircase tips. You fall through the painted room, past the window, past the bed, past your own startled gaze. Colors rush upward around you in long ribbons. Circles become bells; bells become fish; the fish open their mouths and sing in the voices of people you love. You reach for them, but your hands have become transparent, crowded with tiny moving roads.

Far below, the original blank canvas waits like a white square at the bottom of a well.

You land without impact. The brush lies beside you, dry now. The shifting world has gone quiet. The triangles have stopped clapping. The mountains hold still. Even the unnamed color has faded from the air.

Only one small detail has appeared on the canvas: a black dot in its center.

You bend toward it. The dot expands into a pupil, and in its glossy depth you see the bedroom again. The sleeper is sitting up, looking directly at you. They reach toward the eye from the other side. You reach back. Between your fingertip and theirs stretches a skin as thin as soap.

When you touch, the canvas bursts into white birds.

They sweep around you in a roaring spiral, stripping color from everything they pass. The forests pale, the rivers clear, the sky becomes blank paper. Last of all, the birds carry away your shadow. You feel yourself growing lighter, lifted by the absence of it.

As the world dissolves, the brush drops from your hand. It falls through the whiteness for a very long time and lands with a faint wooden knock.

You open your eyes in the painted bedroom. Rain taps the window. On the floor beside the bed lies a brush wet with a color you still cannot name, and across the ceiling, where there should be only darkness, a silver door is slowly opening inward.
