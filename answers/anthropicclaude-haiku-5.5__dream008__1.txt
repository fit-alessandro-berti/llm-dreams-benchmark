...look down and realize you're already holding a pen, though your hands feel strangely light, as if they belong to someone else. The project folder is open on your desk, but the pages are blank except for a single line at the top: *Due: Yesterday.*

Your boss leans against the doorframe. Their smile stays the same, but their face keeps shifting, like a reflection in rippling water. First they're your old manager, then your third-grade teacher, then someone you can't quite name who is somehow very familiar. "No need to panic," they say, in a voice that seems to come from inside the walls. "The clock is just a suggestion."

You glance at the wall clock. Its hands are spinning backward, and the numbers have been replaced with tiny, scribbled drawings of doors.

You try to start writing, but the words come out as birds. They flutter off the page, circle the ceiling lights, and settle on the filing cabinets. You chase them, and the hallway stretches longer with every step. Coworkers pass you on both sides, all carrying identical briefcases, all humming the same melody. None of them looks up.

At the end of the hallway is a window, and through it you can see the city, except the skyscrapers are made of stacked paper, and a river of ink runs between them. You realize the project isn't a document at all. It's a bridge, and you're supposed to build it before the tide of ink rises any higher.

You turn back toward your desk. The blank pages have started to fill, one line at a time, in handwriting that looks exactly like yours, though you don't remember writing any of it. The last sentence reads: *You already know how to finish this. You always have.*

Your boss nods approvingly, the clock stops at a number you almost recognize, and the floor softly begins to lift beneath your chair, carrying you upward toward a sky full of unfinished sentences.
