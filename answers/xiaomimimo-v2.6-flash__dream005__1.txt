each group feels more intimate yet stranger than the last. The first group is laughing so hard they're crying, and when you ask if they've seen your one friend, they all point at you with long fingers and whisper, *"You haven't noticed yet?"*

You keep walking. The gathering seems to stretch endlessly, rooms bleeding into rooms. You pass a couple slow-dancing in a hallway to music only they can hear. You pass an old woman in a chair who knits scarves with no ends — they just unravel behind her as she pulls. You pass a room full of people silently stacking cups into impossible towers.

Every time you ask someone about your friend, they smile kindly and say something unsettling: *"They're closer than you think."* Or *"You already left them behind."* Or simply, *"This is the party."*

You start to feel a growing warmth in your chest — not your friend's presence exactly, but something like the *memory* of their company. The gathering grows louder. The lights shift to golden. People begin to move as if pulled by some gentle current toward the center of the space.

And then, cutting through the noise like a bell:

Your friend's voice. Not calling you — just **talking**, mid-sentence, as if they've been beside you the whole time, telling you a story about something ordinary. Their hand lands on your shoulder.

You turn, and they're just… *there*. Relieved. Smiling. Unbothered.

*"Where have you been?"* they ask.

And you realize you have no idea how to answer, because the gathering is still around you, still going, and you're not sure you ever actually left it to look for them in the first place.
