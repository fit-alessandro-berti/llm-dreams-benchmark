You are in a world where colors and shapes are constantly changing. As you explore this surreal landscape—where mountains ripple like liquid amber one moment and dissolve into spirals of indigo the next, where trees grow sideways from floating stones that hum low notes when the wind passes through their hollow trunks—you come across a blank canvas nailed to the side of a smooth, shape-shifting cliff. It isn’t white, exactly; it’s the color *just before* a color decides what it will be—a soft, breathing grey-violet that seems to pulse faintly, like a held breath. The surface feels neither smooth nor textured; it yields slightly under your fingertips, cool and faintly springy, as if it remembers being paint but hasn’t chosen yet.

You reach out. As your skin touches it, the air around the canvas shivers. Not a sound, but a *shift*: the nearby sky, which had been cycling through quick flashes of coral and deep sea-green, slows for a single, clear breath. Where your finger meets the canvas, a single thread of color begins—not poured, not brushed, but *unspooling* from the space between your skin and the surface. It’s not a color you’ve seen here: it’s the quiet warmth of sunlight on old paper, the exact hue of the pause between a sigh and a smile. It spreads outward in a soft, organic bloom, not filling the canvas but *revealing* it: as the new thread appears, the surrounding grey-violet doesn’t vanish but *softens*, revealing glimpses of colors that had been there all along—fleeting hints of burnt umber and pale gold, like shadows beneath the surface of the canvas’s uncertainty—now visible only because the choice has begun.

A small shape appears beside the bloom: not drawn, but *emergent*—a single, simple curve, like the arc of a leaf closing at dusk. It wasn’t there before your touch; it arises from the interaction of your intention (the quiet curiosity in your hand) and the canvas’s readiness. The floating stone nearby pulses once, a soft chime matching the frequency of the thread’s color. The landscape doesn’t react wildly; instead, the nearby rippling mountains ease their motion for a second, the humming trees fall silent just long enough for the sound of your own breath to be heard clearly.

You don’t try to name it, direct it, or fill it. You simply stand there, feeling the slight give of the canvas under your skin, watching the single thread of paper-sunlight expand gently, revealing hidden undertones in the grey-violet, letting the simple curve settle. No words form. There is no need. The world around you continues to change—a distant hill dissolves into a flock of translucent blue birds that re-form as swirling ribbons—but for this small space, bound by the canvas’s edge and your quiet presence, everything slows to the rhythm of *this choice beginning*. The blankness wasn’t empty; it was waiting for the first true, unforced thread. And for now, that thread is enough.

*(The dream softens. You don’t wake. You just keep breathing, watching the light-warmth bloom.)*
