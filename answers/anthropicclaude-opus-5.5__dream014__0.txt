...and you follow them without asking where they're going, because in the dream it feels like you've always known them.

There are five of them. One is an old woman carrying an umbrella that drips even though it isn't raining. One is a boy with a paper map so large it trails behind him like a cape. The others keep shifting. When you look directly at them, they're strangers. When you glance away, they remind you of people you once loved: a teacher from second grade, a friend you lost touch with, someone whose name is right on the tip of your tongue.

"You're not lost," the old woman says, tapping the sidewalk with her umbrella. "You just forgot which way is home."

The city bends around you as you walk. Streets fold like pages. A crosswalk turns into a staircase that goes down into a subway station full of sunflowers, their heads turning to follow you as you pass. The noise of the crowd softens into something like music, a song you used to hum as a child, though you'd forgotten it until now.

The boy unrolls his map and shows it to you. There are no street names on it, only small drawings: a kitchen table, a porch light, a pair of hands holding yours. "This is how we navigate here," he explains. "By what matters."

You ride an elevator that moves sideways. You cross a bridge made of piano keys, and each step plays a note. Somewhere along the way you realize your fear is gone. You aren't sure when you let go of it. Maybe it slipped out of your pocket back on the staircase.

Finally the group stops in front of a door. It's an ordinary door, painted a color you can't quite name, but you recognize it instantly the way you recognize your own handwriting.

"This is as far as we go," the old woman says. The strangers begin to fade, not sadly, but gently, like streetlights at dawn.

"Wait," you say. "Who are you?"

The boy smiles and folds his enormous map into something small enough to press into your palm. "We're the parts of you that remember the way," he says. "You can always find us when you need to."

You turn the handle. Warm light spills out, and the smell of something familiar, maybe bread, maybe rain, maybe a morning from long ago, wraps around you.

You step inside...

...and you wake up with your hand curled closed, as if you're still holding something small and precious. For a moment you're certain that if you opened it, you'd find a tiny folded map.
