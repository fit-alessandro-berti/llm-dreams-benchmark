As you lean closer, the patterns begin to shift beneath your fingertips, spiraling outward like ripples in still water. Silver lines of light trace the carvings, and you realize they are not etched into the wood at all but drifting just above its surface, like slow-moving constellations. Each one pulses faintly, as if waiting for you to recognize it.

You notice a sequence in the chaos: a crescent moon, then an open eye, then a tangle of roots, then a single star. Without thinking, you touch the crescent moon. It warms under your palm and sinks into the lid with a soft click, and a thin seam of light opens along one edge.

The box does not open all the way. Instead, the lid lifts by a finger's width and a sweet, cool air spills out, smelling of rain on old paper and something like the sea. Faint whispers curl from the gap, too quiet to make out, but they sound familiar, like voices you once knew and have half forgotten.

You try the open eye next. The lid lifts further, and the whispers grow clearer. Someone is counting softly, the way a parent counts to help a child fall asleep. The numbers rise, one by one, toward a number you cannot quite hear.

Your hand hovers over the tangle of roots. The patterns around you are slowing now, as if the dream itself is holding its breath. You feel the pull of the last symbol, the single star, and somewhere in your chest you know that whatever waits inside the box is not treasure, but a memory you have been searching for all along.

You reach for the star.
