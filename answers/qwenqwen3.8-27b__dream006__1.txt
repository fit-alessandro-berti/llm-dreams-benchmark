You try again, and this time your voice turns into wind. It sweeps through the seats, rustling pages of open books in their hands, and somewhere in the front row a small girl laughs because your words have become a flock of white birds circling above the stage.

The stage beneath your feet begins to dissolve into mist. You look down and see your shoes are sinking into clouds, but the audience does not notice. They are watching the birds, their expectant eyes bright with wonder. You realize, with the strange calm of a dream, that you are no longer giving the speech—you have become the speech itself, spreading through the room in a hush that tastes of rain and distant music.

Then a spotlight finds you, and the mist hardens into marble. The audience’s faces blur into masks, and you understand you must say the next sentence, or the dream will end. You open your mouth, but instead of sound, a single bell rings through the darkness, and the large audience begins to fade into the quiet white of sleep.
