“Reef the sails!” you shout, but the wind steals your voice and carries it ahead of the ship, where it echoes back from the darkness: *Down. Down. Down.*

The crew waits, soaked and terrified. Beyond them, a wave rises higher than the mast, its crest filled with pale, moving lights. For an instant, you understand that the storm is not attacking the ship. It is searching for it.

So you do the only thing a captain can do in a dream.

“Cut the anchor,” you order. “And follow my light.”

You take the lantern from its hook and hold it above your head. Its flame does not flicker. Instead, it grows—gold at first, then blue, then a color none of you have names for. The crew begins to sing, each voice joining yours until the ship itself seems to hum.

The wave breaks.

For one weightless moment, the vessel falls through silence. Then the sea becomes smooth as glass, and beneath it you see countless stars. The storm remains above you, grumbling like a distant ceiling.

Your youngest sailor touches your sleeve. “Captain,” she whispers, “where are we going?”

You look at the compass. Its needle is pointing at your heart.

“Home,” you answer.

And as the ship sails onward across the upside-down sky, you hear rain tapping gently against a window—and realize, just before waking, that you have been gripping your pillow like a wheel.
