You realize you need to **stop looking for a way back, and instead learn how to be here—because the tools you trusted are gone, and the air itself demands that you build something with your own hands.**

The smoke of a wood fire hangs low over a village that smells of turned earth and animal hide. You are wearing clothes that belong, somehow, to this century—perhaps the 1840s, perhaps the 1400s, time feels thick and pliable here. You try to pull out your phone to check the time, but your pockets hold only a leather pouch of seeds, a rusted key, and a strange, heavy coin that seems to warm in your palm.

As you explore the narrow streets, nobody asks why you’re here. They assume you belong to the harvest, or to the tower, or to the river. A child offers you a cup of sour cider. You realize that to survive this dream, you don’t need technology; you need **patience**. You need to trade the coin for a needle, to mend your clothes. You need to learn that the stars in this sky are not GPS points but stories told in the dark.

But just as you settle into the rhythm of the place—mending, bartering, the taste of oats on your tongue—you feel the familiar pull of waking. The dream doesn’t end with you escaping. It ends with you understanding that you have always been able to live without the static, as long as you are willing to become part of the silence.

When you open your eyes, your modern world is still there, but for a moment, you can still smell the woodsmoke. And in your pocket, you find a single seed, warm and real.

**Did you find the exit, or did you become the story?**
