...and they seem to be turning, slowly, as if the box were a living thing. I put my fingertips to the surface and feel warmth beneath the wood, like a heartbeat that isn’t mine. The patterns arrange themselves into tiny rooms—a hallway I almost recognize, a window fogged with breath, a door I keep almost opening. Inside, instead of objects, I find my own unfinished thoughts: a song I couldn’t find the end of, a conversation I never quite had, a question that keeps returning whenever I sleep.

When I reach toward the keyhole, it doesn’t resist—it whispers. “Open what is already inside,” it says, in a voice like pages turning. And the box doesn’t unlock; it remembers.

I wake to find the patterns still faintly glowing on my own palm, as if I had been carrying the lock with me all along.
