You decide to become a keeper of thresholds.

Not a warrior, not a scavenger — a doorway between worlds. In the hollow hill where the city used to sprawl, you gather the last functioning radios, fragments of maps, a cracked seed vault, and the names of people who didn’t survive. You rebuild the tower not to shelter yourself, but to listen: to storms, to distant sirens, to the low hum of a battery that might wake a community before it wakes a panic. Each hardship becomes a ritual — rationing water with song, repairing a route by candlelight, memorizing the voices of lost friends in case “lost” ever means “not ready.” Danger circles the ruins differently each night: hungry dust, broken medicines, strange lights in the fields. But you keep one promise — to turn scarcity into story, proof, and hope. Around dusk the tower lights blink in patterns you teach the survivors as a code: *we are here, we are fed, we are coming back.*

If you want, I can continue it into the next dream — the moment the code is answered.
