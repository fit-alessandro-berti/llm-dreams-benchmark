find a way home before the sun sets—or so says the woman selling pears in the village square.

“You’ve got tomorrow’s shadow,” she tells you, pointing at the ground. Your shadow is facing the wrong way.

She gives you a pear and tells you to follow the river uphill. At its source, you find a stone door standing by itself in a meadow. You knock. From the other side comes the muffled sound of your morning alarm.

The door has no handle, but there is a small brass keyhole. You press the pear against it, because that seems like the obvious thing to do.

The door swings open. You step through and wake up in bed, relieved, until you notice the pear on your pillow—and your shadow, still facing the wrong way.
