...and you feel a sudden, warm rush of relief as you step closer to them.

Unlike the rest of the crowd rushing past in blurred streaks of gray coats and hurried footsteps, these strangers are completely still, bathed in the soft, amber glow of a streetlamp that hums like a distant lullaby. One of them—a woman wearing a coat stitched together from colorful patchwork quilts—smiles and hands you a folded paper lantern. Even though there is no candle inside, it radiates a gentle, golden warmth against your palms.

"This way," another says, gesturing toward a narrow cobblestone alley you are certain wasn't there a moment ago.

As you walk beside them, the deafening roar of traffic and sirens begins to fade, melting into the quiet sound of wind chimes and rustling leaves. With each step you take, the towering glass skyscrapers around you seem to soften and bend, transforming into ancient trees whose branches arch high overhead to form a canopy of silver leaves. The hard asphalt beneath your shoes gives way to cool, velvety moss.

You glance at your companions and notice their faces shifting gently, like watercolors in the rain, taking on the comforting, half-remembered expressions of old friends and people you have loved. No one speaks, yet the heavy knot of loneliness in your chest unravels completely.

At the end of the path, standing alone in a quiet clearing beneath a sky swirling with violet stars, is a single wooden door. The strangers pause and nod toward it. You reach out, turn the warm brass handle, and swing it open—revealing not a busy street, but a sunlit room smelling faintly of fresh rain and warm bread, where you know, without needing to ask, that you have finally arrived where you belong.
