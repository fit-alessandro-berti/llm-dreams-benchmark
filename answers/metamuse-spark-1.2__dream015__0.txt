Despite the hardships, you decide to **plant something**.

Not just to survive - but to prove that survival is still worth something.

In the dream, it goes like this:

---

You find yourself in a post-apocalyptic world, with resources scarce and dangers everywhere. The sky is a permanent, pale amber from dust that never quite settled. The highway behind you is cracked and swallowed by weeds, lined with the rusted husks of cars. The wind whistles through the skeleton of a city in the distance.

Despite the hardships, you decide to build a garden in the middle of the ruins.

You found the seeds in an old elementary school, in a dusty drawer in the science classroom - a little ziplock bag labeled in a child's handwriting: `SUNFLOWERS - 3rd Period`. Most people would have looked for canned food or batteries. You took the seeds.

You choose the old courtyard. The concrete is broken there, and a little rainwater has pooled in the cracks. You spend hours - days, in dream-time - dragging rubble away, hauling soil from the park in a broken plastic bucket, building a wall from fallen bricks to keep the harsh wind out.

The dangers are everywhere, but they feel distant while your hands are in the dirt. You hear the distant howl of something you can't see, and the creak of unstable buildings, but you keep working.

And then, in the way dreams do, weeks pass in a single blink.

You come back to the courtyard and they're growing. Small, stubborn green shoots pushing through the dark soil. And towering above them, impossibly fast, one sunflower has already bloomed - taller than you, its face bright gold against the grey-amber sky. It shouldn't be alive. Nothing should be.

You hear voices then. At first you think it's the wind again, but it's not.

Other survivors - a few, thin and wary - have seen that impossible yellow from miles away. A color that hasn't existed in this world for a long time. They follow it, and they find you in your little walled garden.

One of them is a little girl holding an empty watering can. She looks at the sunflower, then at you, and smiles.

You realize you didn't just plant a garden. You planted a lighthouse.

And you decide to invite them in.

---

Do you want to continue this dream? Does the garden grow into a village, or do you follow where those strangers came from?
