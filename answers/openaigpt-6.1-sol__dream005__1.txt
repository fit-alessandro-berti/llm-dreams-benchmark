“There you are,” says a woman wearing a necklace of tiny silver keys. She makes room for you in a circle of strangers, but when you ask about your friend, everyone glances toward the ceiling.

Above you, chandeliers sway in a breeze you cannot feel. For a moment, their crystals resemble shoes.

You move on.

In the next room, people are playing cards around a long dining table. The cards show places instead of numbers: your childhood kitchen, a train platform, the corner shop that closed years ago. A man deals you a card showing the room you are standing in. In the picture, your chair is empty.

“Your friend was just here,” he says.

Beyond the table, you spot a familiar coat disappearing through a doorway. You follow it into a corridor lined with bathroom doors. Behind each one, you hear the party carrying on. Someone laughs exactly like your friend, but when you open that door, you find a bathtub full of dark water. A paper cup floats beside the drain.

Your phone vibrates.

**I’m outside.**

You push through a fire exit and find yourself on the roof. The party is spread across the surrounding rooftops, people leaning over the gaps to pass drinks and plates to one another.

Your friend sits beside a chimney, holding two cups.

“I’ve been looking everywhere for you,” you say.

“I know.” They hand you a cup. It is warm, though there is nothing inside.

You sit together and watch people moving from roof to roof. Now that you’ve found your friend, you realize you can’t remember what you wanted to tell them.

Below, someone calls your name. A few people take it up, beckoning you back inside.

Your friend gets to their feet.

“Wait,” you say.

They turn, and you finally remember.

But your mouth is full of seawater.
