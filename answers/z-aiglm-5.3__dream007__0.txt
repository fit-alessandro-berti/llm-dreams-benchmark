You open your mouth, and the room flickers. It's the kitchen from your childhood home now, and your friend is eleven again, the age they were when you first met, holding out their hands like a bowl.

"Look," they say. "It's still alive."

In their palms: a small bird, trembling, its heartbeat visible beneath the feathers.

And you understand — the way you only understand things in dreams, instantly and wordlessly and all at once — that this is what you've been fighting about. Not the topic. This. The small living thing between you, the friendship itself, that you were both afraid of crushing with the wrong words.

You reach out. The bird steps onto your finger, nearly weightless.

Your friend smiles. It's their same smile, even at eleven, even here. You're about to say something true and gentle and long overdue when—

You wake up. Your hand is open on the empty sheet. The morning is ordinary, but there's a specific ache in your chest, the kind that means a dream has left something behind on purpose.

You pick up your phone. Their name is right where it always is.
