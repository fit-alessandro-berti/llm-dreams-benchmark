...take a step back and breathe. But as I exhale, the room begins to warp. The walls stretch like taffy, and my friend's face flickers — one moment furious, the next one impossibly sad.

"We can't keep doing this," they say, but their voice echoes as if coming from somewhere far away, like they're speaking to me through the bottom of a deep well.

I decide to **reach across the table and grab their hand**, but when I look down, my fingers have turned translucent. I can see the wood grain of the table right through my palm. My friend doesn't seem to notice. Their hand is warm and solid in mine.

"Say what you actually mean," they whisper.

And suddenly we're no longer in the living room. We're standing on the roof of our childhood treehouse, even though we've never had a treehouse. But it *feels* like we do. The stars are too close, almost close enough to touch, and they pulse gently like heartbeats.

"I don't want to lose you over this," I hear myself say, and the words dissolve into moths that flutter upward into the sky.

My friend smiles — the kind of smile that makes the stars dim in comparison — and says, "You could never. That's the whole point. That's what we were arguing about, wasn't it? That we both care too much to let this break us?"

The treehouse sways gently in a breeze that carries the scent of rain. We sit down. We don't need to say anything else.

And then my alarm goes off, and the stars fold inward like origami, and I wake up with a lump in my throat and the vague, beautiful ache of having just made up with someone I haven't spoken to in years.
