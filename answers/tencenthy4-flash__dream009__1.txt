...find the clockmaker before the bells strike thirteen.

The air tastes of salt and coal smoke. You are standing on slick cobblestones in a town built entirely of wet stone and secrets. There are no glowing screens, no distant hum of engines—only the rhythmic lap of black water against a seawall and the heavy, uneven swing of a brass bell.

You push open the heavy oak door of a tavern, hoping for a map or a friendly face. The room falls instantly silent. A dozen strangers turn toward you, their features half-hidden by the sputter of whale-oil lamps. The woman behind the bar doesn't ask your name. She just slides a small, wax-sealed envelope across the scarred wood.

"You're late," she murmurs, her voice like grinding stones. "He's been waiting since Wednesday."

You break the seal. Inside is a single line of hurried handwriting: *Do not trust the reflection in the canal.*

You step back out into the fog, clutching the paper. Somewhere above you, an iron weather vane creaks. You glance down at the dark water running alongside the street, and your breath catches in your throat—because the figure staring back up from the ripples is wearing your clothes, but it has a stranger's face. And it is smiling.

You turn to run, but the bell begins to toll again... and you wake up, the phantom smell of sea salt and coal smoke still clinging to your pillow.
