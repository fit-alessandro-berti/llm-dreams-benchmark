Thunder rolls—but instead of fading, it becomes the low note of a distant bell.

You follow the sound to a clearing. There stands a tiny house on tall wooden stilts, its windows glowing amber. Beneath it, a fox waits with a blue umbrella balanced over one shoulder.

“You’re just in time,” the fox says.

“For what?”

The first raindrop lands on your hand. Inside it, you see a summer afternoon from your childhood: warm pavement, bare feet, someone calling you home.

Another drop strikes a fern and bursts into laughter.

“The weather,” says the fox, opening the umbrella, “is remembering.”

Rain falls all around you now, each drop holding a tiny scene. Most are strange to you—a woman asleep on a train, a red boat drifting under a bridge—but every so often you catch a glimpse of something you know.

Then you hear your name from inside the house.

At the top of the steps, the door is open. You cannot see who opened it, only a pair of muddy boots beside the threshold and a kettle steaming on a table.

The fox shakes the rain from its umbrella.

“Go on,” it says. “I’ll wait here.”

You climb the steps. Just before you reach the door, you look back and discover that the forest floor has become a still, dark lake. The fox sits on the last patch of earth, watching the rings spread where the rain falls.

From inside, a familiar voice asks whether you’ve brought the bread.

You look down. A warm loaf is tucked beneath your arm.
