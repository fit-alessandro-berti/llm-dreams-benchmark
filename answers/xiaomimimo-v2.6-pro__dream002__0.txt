...and the sky turns an eerie shade of violet-black. The wind begins to whistle through the trees, bending their branches into shapes that seem almost *recognizable* — like long, grasping fingers reaching for something just out of frame.

Your footsteps quicken. Leaves swirl around your ankles in spirals that defy normal physics, climbing upward instead of scattering. The air smells of ozone and wet earth and something else — something sweet and ancient, like honey poured over old stone.

Then the rain begins. But it falls *upward*.

Each drop lifts from the forest floor and ascends toward the darkened canopy, gathering into shimmering rivers that weave between the trunks like liquid silver. You stop walking and simply stand there, mouth open, watching the impossible inversion.

A crack of thunder shakes the ground beneath your feet, and in the flash of lightning, you see it: at the end of the path ahead, there is a doorway. It stands alone in the middle of the woods — a simple wooden frame with no wall around it. Through it, the colors are wrong. The forest continues, but in soft pastels, golden and warm, the storm completely absent on the other side.

A figure stands in the doorway, silhouetted against the light. They raise one hand and beckon you forward.

The storm intensifies. The trees begin to *sing* — a low, resonant hum that vibrates in your chest. You realize with sudden clarity that the door is shrinking. Slowly. Frame by frame.

You run.

Your feet barely touch the ground as you sprint down the path. The wind howls against you now, pushing you back, as if the storm itself wants to keep you here. Mud splashes up in impossible arcs. Raindrops hang suspended in the air around you like tiny glass beads.

You're close now. The figure is clearer — but their face shifts every time you blink. A child. A stranger. Someone you once loved and lost.

The door is barely the size of a window now.

Without thinking, you dive.

You tumble through the frame and land on soft, warm grass. Behind you, the door closes with a gentle *click*, and the storm is silenced instantly — as though someone had pressed mute on the world. Golden light pours over you. The sky is painted in shades of peach and lavender. Butterflies with wings like stained glass drift lazily overhead.

The figure kneels beside you, smiling with a face you *almost* remember. They whisper:

*"You're safe now. But you have to remember — you only get to go back through the door if you forget something."*

And as the dream deepens around you, you feel a memory beginning to soften at its edges, dissolving like sugar in warm water...

...and you wake up in your bed, heart racing, knowing you've left something important behind — but you cannot, for the life of you, remember what it was.
