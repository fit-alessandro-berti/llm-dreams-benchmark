I need to find water. Your throat is dry, and the sun beats down on the cobblestone streets. You push through a bustling medieval market — the smell of fresh bread, roasting meat, and horse hay fills the air. You stop a woman carrying a clay jug and ask her where the nearest well is.

She squints at your strange clothing and mutters something about "foreigners," but points toward the town square. As you walk, you pass blacksmiths hammering glowing iron, children chasing each other between wooden stalls, and a hunched old man sitting on a stool, sketching designs on parchment.

When you finally reach the well, you lower a rope and hear the satisfying splash below. As you drink the cool water, you notice something strange — a small piece of paper tucked into the rope's loop. You unfold it. In handwriting that looks eerily like your own, it reads:

**"Find the tower before the third bell, or you stay forever. — You"**

You look up from the note. In the distance, beyond the rooftops and smoke, a crumbling stone tower rises above the treeline. Just then, the first bell begins to toll from the church behind you.

Your heart starts to race. The dream sharpens. Time is running out. Do you —run for the tower?
