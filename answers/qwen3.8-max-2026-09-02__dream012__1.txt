The wind tears at your coat, and the waves rise like dark mountains breathing lightning. Yet as your hands find the wheel, you feel something impossible beneath the storm: a rhythm, as if the sea is not trying to destroy the ship, but to teach it how to move.

You call orders that become songs. The crew answers, and their fear turns into purpose. Ropes glow faintly like silver veins. The lantern at the mast burns blue instead of gold. You realize, in the way one realizes things only in dreams, that the storm is not outside you—it is the part of you that has been waiting to be faced.

Lightning splits the sky, and for one breathless moment you see beyond the waves: a horizon made not of water and air, but of memory. There, far ahead, a calm sea shines like polished obsidian, and beside it stands a lighthouse built from every promise you have ever kept.

You turn the ship toward it.

The storm screams, but you no longer hear it as fury. You hear it as a question.

And you answer, with your whole heart:

“Onward.”
