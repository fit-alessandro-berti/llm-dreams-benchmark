Suddenly, the argument vanishes from your mind, replaced by a sudden, overwhelming sense of vertigo. You look at your friend, but their face is shifting rapidly—you see them laughing, then crying, then wearing your own expression, then the face of someone you haven't thought about in years. They speak, but no sound comes out; instead, words float from their mouth like bubbles, each containing a memory you thought was lost.

One bubble drifts toward you and pops on your forehead. You are instantly transported to a vast, white library where the shelves stretch into infinity. Your friend is standing there, no longer angry, holding two keys. They offer them to you without a word. One key is made of iron and feels heavy as guilt; the other is carved from bone and feels weightless as forgiveness.

As you reach for them, the library begins to fill with rising water, calm and warm, and you realize the disagreement wasn't about what happened at all. It's about who gets to hold the anchor while the rest of the world floats away. You wake up gasping, clutching a pillow, with the lingering sensation that you still have to choose, even though you can no longer remember what you were fighting for.
