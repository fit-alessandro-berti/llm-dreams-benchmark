In the first round, they hand you a glass of water and tell you to get it upstairs without spilling a drop. The staircase rolls itself up as soon as you set foot on it. After three tries, you call up to the judges and ask which of them is thirsty.

“All of us,” one says.

“Well, come down, then.”

They do. You pass.

In the second round, you have to find a mistake in a book with no words in it. You turn the book over. There’s your name on the back, spelled correctly, and beneath it a photograph of you at a desk you’ve never seen. You’re eating an orange. Someone just outside the frame must have said something funny, because you’re trying not to laugh with your mouth full.

“I haven’t done this yet,” you tell the judges.

“We know,” they say, and take the book away.

For the final round, they sit you at that very desk. There’s an orange in the drawer. Through the wall you can hear the other finalists being interviewed.

“Where do you see yourself in five years?” a judge asks.

“Could you come get me?” a small voice says.

You know the voice. You follow it out into the hall and find yourself at eight years old, sitting on a bench in shoes that don’t reach the floor.

“How did you get here?” you ask.

“I’m waiting for you to get off work.”

Through the open door behind you, a judge taps her watch. You sit down on the bench anyway. You peel the orange and give your younger self half. For a little while, neither of you says anything.

Then the judge comes out and turns the sign on the door from INTERVIEWS to CLOSED.

“Did I get the job?” you ask.

She looks at the orange peel in your lap. “You got off at five,” she says. “See you tomorrow.”

You wake up with your feet warm where the kid had been leaning against them.
