…each cluster seems to be discussing something just out of earshot—half-heard phrases that dissolve like smoke when you lean in closer. One group is made of people with mirrors for faces, all arguing in a language that sounds like your own heartbeat. Another is standing in a circle around a table that isn’t there, passing around cups of liquid light. You try to ask if they’ve seen your friend, but your mouth fills with the sound of wind chimes, and when you finally point, the space between you and them has stretched like taffy.

You realize the walls of the room are breathing, and the “large gathering” is actually the inside of a library from your childhood, except the books have turned into small, chattering crowds that scatter when you approach. You push through a curtain of warm silk and suddenly you’re in a garden where every flower is a doorway, each one humming a different tune.

And then—there they are. The one person you know. But they’re also the host, and they’ve been waiting in every room at once, wearing a mask that keeps changing to match whoever is closest to them. They don’t seem surprised to see you. They just hand you a key that fits nothing, and say, “You don’t need to find me. I’m the reason you’re still looking.”

At that moment, the entire gathering folds like a napkin, and you realize you’re not lost at all—you’ve just been circling the center of a room you built yourself. The party wasn’t real; it was a shape your loneliness made so you’d have somewhere to wander. You wake up with the taste of copper and the faint sound of laughter that might have been yours, or might have been the room’s, dissolving into the dark just as the alarm tries to pull you back to the one real gathering: breakfast, and the quiet work of the day.
