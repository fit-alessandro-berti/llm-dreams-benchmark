...plant a garden.

It makes no sense, and you know it. The soil is gray and the sky is the color of old dishwater. But in your coat pocket you find a handful of seeds you don't remember collecting, each one warm, as if it had been held in a closed fist for years.

You kneel in the shell of a parking garage, where a crack in the concrete lets through a single column of light. You press the first seed into the dust. With dream-logic certainty, you know its name without knowing what it will become.

Days pass in the space of a breath. You carry water in a dented helmet from a river that runs backward, uphill, toward the ruins of the city. Feral dogs watch you from the shadows, but they never come closer. They seem to be waiting too.

Then one morning, or maybe the same morning, the seed has become a tree, impossibly tall, pushing through every floor of the garage. Its leaves are pages from books you read as a child. Its fruit glows faintly, like lanterns.

Other survivors begin to arrive, drawn by the light. They don't speak. One by one they reach into their own pockets and find them full of seeds.

An old woman with your grandmother's hands, though not her face, kneels beside you and begins to dig. Then another person kneels, and another. The gray earth turns dark and rich beneath your fingers, and somewhere above you a bird that should be extinct starts to sing.

You realize the world didn't end. It was only waiting for someone to begin again.

The tree's leaves rustle, and for a moment you can almost read what they say...

*And then you feel the dream begin to thin, the way dreams do, the light growing brighter at the edges.*
