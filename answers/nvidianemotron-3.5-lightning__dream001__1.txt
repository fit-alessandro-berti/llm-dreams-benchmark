You start to sprint toward the city’s neon-lit horizon, but the streets stretch out endlessly ahead, each block folding into the next like a page in a half-remembered story. The sky shifts colors without reason—from cobalt to amber to a bruised purple—and the architecture morphs between sleek glass towers and crumbling stone arches with every few steps you take. Passerbyes flicker in and out of focus; some mouth directions you can’t quite hear, others carry briefcases that leak golden dust that dissolves before it hits the ground. An hour slips by in the time it takes for a single carousel to revolve above a square that wasn’t there a moment ago. Just as the panic begins to rise, a faint chime echoes from nowhere and everywhere, and a simple, elegant map materializes in your palm, the ink glowing warm and familiar. It doesn’t point to a address, but to a feeling—a quiet doorway at the end of a silent alley, where the meeting was never about time or place, but about the part of you that finally found the way home.
