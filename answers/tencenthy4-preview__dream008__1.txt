...and you realize your hands have turned into clocks, each finger ticking at a different speed.

The deadline is written on a sticky note the size of a thundercloud: **DUE YESTERDAY, BEFORE SUNRISE.**

Your boss says nothing, but their silence is extremely managerial. You try to type, and the keyboard folds itself into a paper staircase. Your cubicle becomes a sailboat. The coffee machine turns into a tiny train station announcing departures for places that do not exist.

The project brief is just one sentence repeated forever:

**Start now. Start now. Start now.**

But when you finally touch the first page, it’s warm, like fresh bread. The impossible deadline purred. The paperwork flapped its corners like wings. Somehow, you knew the task wasn’t meant to be finished—it was meant to be freed.

So you threw the project out the window.

It unfolded into a flock of paper cranes and carried the office into the sky. Your boss nodded solemnly, as if this had been the correct deliverable all along. Then they dissolved into confetti shaped like approval.

You woke up just before the alarm, already late, already forgiven.
