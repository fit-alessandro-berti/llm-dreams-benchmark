# The Meeting

You find yourself in a vast, unfamiliar city, and you realize you have an important meeting in an hour but no idea where it is. You start to **run.**

The streets twist and loop in ways that don't obey physics — turning a corner only to find yourself on a rooftop, then descending a staircase that spirals downward into a subway station that somehow breathes. The city hums. It *knows* you're late.

You reach into your pocket and find a folded paper map, except the streets keep rearranging themselves in real time. Ink rivers flow across the parchment, redirecting boulevards and erasing landmarks. A clock tower in the distance reads 2:00… then 1:58… then 3:45. Time is as confused as you are.

A stranger — tall, faceless, wearing a suit made of glass — taps you on the shoulder and says in a voice like radio static: *"The meeting is in the room you forgot. You built it yourself."*

Suddenly, the buildings fall silent. The sky peels open like wet paper, and you see it — a single glowing door floating above the skyline. You *remember*. The meeting room. Your meeting room. The one you designed years ago but locked away in a dream you no longer visit.

You don't run anymore. You float.

The door opens before you touch it. Inside, seated around a perfect circular table, are **every version of yourself** — the child who believed anything was possible, the teenager drowning in doubt, the adult constantly late for their own life. They are all waiting.

They speak in unison: *"You made it. Now — what do we do next?"*

You sit down. The door closes. And for the first time in the dream, you are exactly where you need to be.

Then you wake up — still late, but carrying the answer in your hands like a warm stone.
