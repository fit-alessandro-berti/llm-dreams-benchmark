There are three of them, though their faces seem to gently blur whenever you try to look directly at them, like watercolors left out in the rain. One of them—a tall figure in a coat the color of twilight—unfolds a map that isn't made of paper, but of woven silver thread. As they trace a glowing line across it, the roaring traffic and towering concrete around you begin to shift.

"Just match our pace," another says, her voice sounding strangely like distant wind chimes.

You fall into step behind them. With every stride you take together, the chaotic city loses its frantic edge. The harsh neon signs melt into soft, floating lanterns, and the crowded sidewalks widen into quiet, moss-covered paths. You realize you aren't looking for a specific street anymore; instead, they are gently guiding you *out* of the noise.

Finally, they stop before an ornate wooden door set impossibly into the smooth glass wall of a skyscraper. One of them turns the brass handle, pulls it open, and steps aside, gesturing for you to go first.

As you cross the threshold, the city vanishes completely. You find yourself standing on a quiet, sunlit dock stretching over a mirror-still lake, the air smelling faintly of cedar and summer rain. When you turn back to thank them, the doorway is gone, leaving only the open horizon—and a sudden, peaceful certainty that you are right where you belong.
