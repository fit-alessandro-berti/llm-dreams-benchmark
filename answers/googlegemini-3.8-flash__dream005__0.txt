...and with every doorway I pass through, the house seems to stretch and quietly rewrite its own architecture.

In one room, bathed in amber light, a circle of people are laughing over glasses filled with swirling, bioluminescent smoke instead of wine. When I politely excuse myself past them, they nod as if they know me, murmuring greetings in a language that sounds like ocean water over pebbles—utterly unintelligible, yet entirely comforting. 

In the next hall, the floor is carpeted in cool, damp moss. A group dressed in heavy winter coats stands around a grand piano, debating the exact shade of a memory none of them can quite name. One of them turns, catching my eye, and offers an open hand holding a smooth, warm river stone. "For the crossing," she whispers, perfectly matter-of-fact. I take it, and its weight is the only solid thing in the room.

Then, at the far end of a glass-walled corridor overlooking a midnight sea that shouldn't be there, I see them. 

The one familiar silhouette. 

They are leaning against the railing, looking out at the dark water, their posture unmistakable. A wave of profound relief anchors me. I quicken my pace, weaving through the drifting, slow-motion current of strangers who gently part like tall grass in a breeze. 

I reach out and lightly touch their shoulder. 

They turn, and their face is clear, calm, and smiling. They don't ask how I found them; they just breathe a quiet laugh, look down at the stone in my hand, and say, *"You made it. Listen—it's about to begin."*

Before I can ask what they mean, the low murmur of the party falls completely silent. The ceiling softly dissolves into the pale, blue-gray mist of an early morning sky, and the ocean outside sighs—a sound that slowly, rhythmically transforms into the steady rise and fall of my own waking breath.
