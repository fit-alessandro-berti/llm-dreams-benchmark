**...realize the project brief is written in a language that rearranges itself whenever you look away.**

Your boss stands at the edge of your desk, smiling in a way that's slightly too patient. Behind them, the office windows show no city, only a slow tide of clocks drifting past like jellyfish.

"It's due yesterday," your boss says gently. "But we've extended it to *last week*."

You look down. The deadline is stamped in red across the top of the page, and as you watch, the ink crawls toward the margin and rewrites itself: *Sooner.*

Something in you goes calm, the way it only does in dreams. You open your laptop, and the keys are made of smooth river stones. When you press one, it doesn't type a letter. It makes a small, clear *plink*, like a drop of water in a cave. The screen fills with whatever you were thinking.

So you think: *finished.*

The document fills itself out, page after page, in handwriting that looks suspiciously like yours. The clocks outside slow down and drift closer to the glass, as if they're curious. Your boss leans in, reads over your shoulder, and their expression softens into something almost like relief.

"Perfect," they whisper. "That's exactly what we needed."

Then they step back, and the floor tilts gently like the deck of a ship. Your coworkers look up from their desks, all of them holding identical paper boats. One by one, they fold your finished project into a boat of its own and set it on the carpet, which has become a shallow, glittering stream.

"Now," your boss says, handing you a tiny oar, "we just have to get it to the client."

The boat is already drifting toward the elevator, which is open, endless, and full of stars.

You step in after it, still holding the oar, and somewhere far above you, an alarm clock begins to ring...
