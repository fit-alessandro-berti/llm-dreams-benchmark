Despite the hardships, you decide to sit in the middle of the empty highway and begin to tell stories—not to anyone in particular, but to the crows that gather on the sagging power lines, to the wind that carries your words up into the poisoned clouds, to the ghosts of the city that linger in the smog like half-remembered faces.

You speak of oceans that no longer exist, of laughter that echoes in rooms that have collapsed, of a color you can't quite name that lives somewhere between blue and purple, humming like a tuning fork behind your eyes. As you speak, the dust around you begins to glow, swirling upward into constellations that spell out names you've forgotten, and somewhere in the distance, a door that has been rusted shut for decades swings open on silent hinges.

It doesn't reveal another room. It reveals a garden. The flowers are singing back to you, and their voices sound like rain.
