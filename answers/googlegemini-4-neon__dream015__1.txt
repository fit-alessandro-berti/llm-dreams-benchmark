...climb the crooked clock tower in the center of the sunken plaza and wind it back up.

It makes no practical sense—you haven't eaten since yesterday, the sky is the color of a bruised plum, and the tall, faceless scavengers are already creeping out of the subway tunnels as the light fades. Still, you have the unshakable, quiet certainty that the world only broke because it stopped keeping time.

You don’t quite remember crossing the plaza, only that the heavy iron door of the tower is suddenly swinging shut behind you, locking out the ash-thick wind. 

Inside, the ascent is strange. The spiral staircase begins as rusted, groaning grates, then shifts halfway up into the creaking wooden stairs of a house you lived in as a child, before turning back into damp stone. Your backpack feels weightless. Somewhere far below, something heavy drags itself against the foundation, rattling the dust from the rafters, and a cold, sluggish dread pools in your stomach—the kind where your legs feel like they are wading through deep water.

You push through it and haul yourself onto the belfry platform.

The machinery behind the giant glass clockface is immense, a frozen skeleton of brass gears choked with pale, dry vines and silver cobwebs. You realize you brought no tools, no oil, no wrench. Instinctively, you reach into your coat pocket anyway, and your fingers close around three things that shouldn't be there: a brass key, a spool of red thread, and a glass marble pulsing with warm, amber light. 

Somehow, you know these are the exact right parts.

Outside the translucent glass face, a massive shadow blots out the dim horizon. Something vast and papery scrapes against the brickwork, searching for a way in. Your hands turn clumsy and thick, moving as if underwater, as you tie the snapped pendulum back together with the red thread. The thread holds like steel cable. You wedge the warm marble into the hollow heart of the central gear, slide the key into a keyhole that wasn't there a second ago, and turn it.

*Click.*

The tower shudders, not from the monster outside, but from a deep, waking breath. The great gears groan and begin to turn—not forward, but backward. 

Instead of ticking, the clock lets out a low, resonant hum that vibrates in your teeth. The shadows pressed against the glass peel away like smoke in a sudden draft. Through the glowing dial, you watch the ruined skyline un-break itself: fallen bridges rise out of the dark water, green moss floods across the cracked concrete, and the soot lifts off the streets in great swirling flocks, turning into starlings as it hits the sky.

The hands spin faster and faster until they blur into a circle of pale gold. The giant glass face melts away into a rushing, warm breeze that smells like wet pavement and summer rain. You step out onto the ledge, feeling entirely weightless, ready to jump into the bright, swirling sky below—

And just as the clock finally strikes the hour, the chime stretches out, turning sharp, rhythmic, and oddly familiar, pulling the whole world apart into soft, blinding morning light.
