*(The dream stays in the first-person present, quiet and slightly hazy—like remembering a fragment while half-awake. No over-analysis, just sensory immediacy and dream logic.)*

Your boss is waiting. Not yelling, not frantic—just standing by your desk, arms loosely at their sides, the morning light through the window catching dust motes. They slide a single sheet of paper across the surface. No email, no Slack message; it’s handwritten on thin, off-white paper, the ink slightly smudged at the corner. The title reads simply: *Q3 Synergy Pivot – Final Draft*. Below it, in smaller, precise script: *Due: 3:00 PM today. No revisions allowed after 10 AM.*  

Your throat goes dry. You glance at the clock on the wall—it reads 9:12 AM. You haven’t even opened the file they mentioned; the project involves mapping three legacy systems, integrating a prototype that doesn’t exist yet, and writing a one-page executive summary that “captures the emotional resonance.” You know, deep in your bones, it cannot be done in three hours, let alone *today*, not without skipping steps, faking clarity, or pulling an all-nighter that the deadline already rules out.  

You open your mouth. Words catch: *“That’s not possible—”*  

But before you finish, the paper shuffles slightly in your hand. You look down. On the back, in the same small script, is a single line you didn’t notice before: *“P.S. If you can’t finish it, just tell me what the first sentence would say.”*  

The boss smiles—a small, easy, utterly ordinary smile. No pressure in it. They don’t look at you expectantly. They just nod toward your laptop.  

So you sit. No panic now, just quiet focus. You close the draft. You don’t try to outline, don’t check specs. You just type, slowly, the first sentence that comes when you imagine explaining the whole thing to a colleague over coffee—no jargon, no buzzwords, just the true, simple hook.  

> *“We built this to stop people from restarting their week just to fix one small thing.”*  

It’s not perfect. It’s not final. It’s just the first sentence. You hit *save* on a new file called *FirstSentence.docx*. You email it to your boss in one line: *“First sentence for Q3 Synergy Pivot: ‘We built this to stop people from restarting their week just to fix one small thing.’”*  

You don’t wait for a reply. You lean back. The dust motes still dance in the light. Outside the window, a single leaf, already turning gold, trembles on its branch—no wind visible. The clock on the wall still reads 9:12 AM. The deadline hasn’t changed. But for the dream, in this quiet moment, the impossible feels just *slightly* less heavy. You exhale. The dream holds the stillness for a few more seconds before the light softens, the desk fades edge-on, and the sound of distant office chatter begins to hum—like it always does—but you don’t turn toward it. You just sit, with the sentence saved, and the quiet knowing that sometimes, the only impossible thing you have to do first is say the first true thing.  

*(The dream dissolves gently into the soft grey of half-waking, no alarm, no urgency—just that small, clear sentence hanging in the air.)*
