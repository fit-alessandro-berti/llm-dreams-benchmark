You are at a large social gathering where you only know one person. As you try to find them, you encounter various groups of people and —

each group seems to *know* you were coming.

The house is too big on the inside. It's a kind of warm, golden-lit mansion where every doorway leads to another living room, another kitchen, another balcony. Music is playing from somewhere you can never quite reach. Everyone is dressed a little too nicely, like for a wedding that hasn't started yet.

**The first group** is crowded around a kitchen island, laughing hysterically at a joke you didn't hear. As you approach, they all turn to you at once, smiling wide, expecting you to laugh too. One of them puts a drink in your hand that you don't remember asking for. It's cold and fizzing. You laugh anyway, a short, awkward sound, and they all laugh louder, as if you've said the punchline. When you ask, "Have you seen — ?" you can't remember the name of the one person you know. It's right on the tip of your tongue, like a word in a foreign language. They nod like they understand and point down a long hallway. *"That way, I think."*

You walk that way.

**The second group** isn't talking at all. They're standing in a perfect circle in the den, shoulder to shoulder, all facing inward, staring at something on the floor. You squeeze between two of them to see. There's nothing there — just the rug. But no one moves. No one blinks. The air around them is very still and quiet. You feel rude for interrupting, so you back away slowly. As you do, one of them whispers without looking at you, "You're going the wrong way."

You turn around, but the hallway you came from is now a different hallway. The pictures on the walls have changed. Now they're all pictures of you, but you don't remember posing for them.

**The third group** is on a sunken couch, and they all have your face. Not exactly — but when they turn to look at you, you recognize your own eyes, your own mouth on strangers. They are having a calm, intimate conversation about memories you haven't had yet. One of them says, "Do you remember the lake?" and you *do* remember, suddenly — cold water, a dock at night — even though you've never been there. They pat the empty cushion between them, inviting you to sit and stay. It would be so easy to sit down. The couch looks incredibly comfortable. The anxiety of searching would finally stop.

But you keep moving, because you can hear your person's laugh from the next room. You're sure of it.

You follow the sound through a set of double doors and end up outside, even though you were sure you were on the second floor. It's an evening garden party now. String lights overhead. The air smells like summer and cut grass. And there, by a table with a half-finished cake, is the one person you know.

They're facing away from you, talking to someone. You call out — and finally, the name comes back to you — and they turn.

They look relieved to see you. They look exactly as you remember them, which is strange because everyone else in the dream felt slightly *off*, slightly blurred at the edges.

"There you are," they say. "I was looking for you too. I thought you got lost."

You both laugh, and for a second the whole sprawling, impossible house makes sense. The music finally gets louder, a song you love, and the crowd doesn't feel so crowded anymore.

You sit down at the table with them, and they pour you some of that cold, fizzing drink, and you realize you were never really alone in the house. You were just taking the long way back to the one familiar chair.

And then you wake up — slowly — with the feeling of the string lights still warm behind your eyes, and the faint, pleasant ache of having walked a very long distance to get just a few feet away.
