—each of them is already in the middle of a story.

Not the beginning. You arrive on the laugh, the part where everyone leans in. Someone puts a glass in your hand that has already been drunk from, and you hold it like a ticket.

The first group is describing a house they all grew up in, though they've never met before tonight. The same staircase, the same loose banister, the same light in the hall that was always left on. You want to tell them you've been there. You have been there, in the way you've been everywhere anyone has ever described to you.

The hallway is longer than the house. You walk it looking for the one person, and halfway down you realize you can't remember their face — only the feeling of not having to explain yourself first. That's the thing you're actually looking for. The face was just where you kept it.

Someone touches your elbow and says, *you're the one who—* and never finishes, and you nod, grateful to be recognized for something. A woman across the room says your name correctly, which almost nobody does, and by the time you reach her she's talking to someone else about a train.

You find them in the kitchen. Of course. They say, *there you are, I've been telling everyone about you,* and you turn around and the whole party is people who know you. Each one remembers a different conversation. Each one remembers a slightly different you — funnier, sadder, more certain about things. They aren't wrong. You go from one to the next and each time you arrive you are also already leaving, and in the dream this isn't sad. It's just the arrangement, like weather.

At the door someone asks if you'll still be here when they get back.

You say yes, and the yes is true, and the dream ends on the sound of a party continuing in a room you're no longer in.
