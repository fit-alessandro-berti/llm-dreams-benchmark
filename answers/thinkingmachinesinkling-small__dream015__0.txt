You find yourself in a post-apocalyptic world, with resources scarce and dangers everywhere. Despite the hardships, you decide to **build a library**.

Not a fortress, not a bunker—just a library. You start with the roof of a collapsed grocery store, propping it up with rusted pipes. You gather every book you can find: water-damaged cookbooks, half-burned novels, a child’s encyclopedia with pages that smell like smoke. You don’t trade for food or ammunition; you trade for stories. A can of beans gets you a chapter of *Moby Dick*. A flashlight gets you a poem you don’t understand but memorize anyway.

In the dream, the dangers don’t vanish—they just become background noise. The mutated creatures that prowl the perimeter learn to sit at your wooden tables, not because you feed them, but because they want to listen. The sky above your library doesn’t stay gray; it shifts into a twilight that feels like watercolor, and when you open a window, you can smell rain that hasn’t fallen in years.

You realize, halfway through arranging books by color rather than subject, that the apocalypse was never the end. It was just the world finally running out of excuses to be boring. The library becomes a lantern—not with electricity, but with voices. And as the dream begins to fade, you feel the weight of a real-world sun on your face, carrying with you the memory of a place where scarcity didn’t mean silence, but the beginning of a very long, very strange conversation.

When you wake, your hands are still shaping the air, arranging invisible shelves. You decide that tomorrow, you’ll start with a single page of anything you have, and tell someone—anyone—what you found there.
