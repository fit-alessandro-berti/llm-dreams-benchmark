The moment you touch it, the canvas breathes. Its white surface ripples like water, and from your fingertips spill colors that do not have names—shades that hum, glitter, whisper, and remember. Shapes rise from the cloth: staircases made of birdsong, doors that open into oceans, flowers whose petals are tiny mirrors reflecting versions of you that have never existed.

You realize the canvas is not asking you to paint it. It is asking you to choose what becomes real.

You imagine a sky, and above you the dreaming world folds into violet waves. You imagine a path, and a ribbon of silver light unrolls through the shifting landscape. Each step you take turns into music, and each thought you release becomes a creature drifting past—some luminous, some shadowed, all curious.

At the center of the canvas appears a single door, small and glowing. You kneel before it, and it opens.

Inside is not another place, but a feeling: the sensation of beginning again without fear. The dream softens around you, colors melting into warmth, shapes dissolving into silence, until all that remains is the understanding that the blank canvas was never empty.

It was full of everything you had not yet dared to imagine.
