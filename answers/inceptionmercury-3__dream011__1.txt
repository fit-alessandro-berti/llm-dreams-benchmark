…written in a language you’ve never seen, but somehow you can read them perfectly. The first question asks:

“Describe the color of Tuesday.”

You write a paragraph about the sound of your grandmother’s kitchen clock. The professor, who is also your childhood dog, nods approvingly.

The second question is a multiple-choice problem with no options—just a blank line that says “Choose wisely.” You draw a small door on the page, and the door opens into a hallway that smells like rain and old library books. You step inside. The exam room disappears.

You realize you’re now holding a golden ticket that says “This is the answer to everything.” But when you flip it over, it’s just a receipt for a coffee you bought six years ago. You start to laugh, because you understand now: the exam was never about knowing the right answers. It was about remembering that you’ve always been dreaming.

You wake up in the exam room. The questions are still blank. The professor—your childhood dog—looks at you and says, “You’ve passed.”

And you believe it.
