A cold flicker of panic rises in your throat. You glance down at the podium, hoping your script is waiting there, but the wooden stand has turned into a mossy tree stump, and the microphone is now a tall, impossibly large dandelion puff. The stage lights feel strangely warm, like a late August sun beating down on the back of your neck.

You don't remember the topic of your speech—only that it is terribly important, and that everyone here has waited a very long time to hear it. 

You lean forward and open your mouth to apologize, to tell them there has been a mistake. Instead of words, a gentle, rushing wind pours out from your lungs, carrying the unmistakable scent of pine needles and rain on hot pavement. It hits the dandelion, and hundreds of glowing white seeds lift into the air, drifting lazily out over the auditorium.

Nobody laughs. Nobody gets up to leave. Instead, the entire crowd leans forward, utterly captivated, holding out their palms to catch the floating sparks. 

As the first seeds touch the velvet aisles, the floor ripples like dark water. Slowly, without a sound, the theater begins to dissolve. The rows of red plush chairs transform into small wooden rowboats, gently bobbing on a vast, calm sea. Above you, the ornate ceiling peels away like curling wallpaper, revealing a night sky crowded with constellations spinning much too close to the earth.

The panic in your chest vanishes, replaced by a quiet, weightless relief. You realize you were never supposed to speak at all. 

You look down at your hands; they are glowing softly from within, warm as glass lanterns. Out among the drifting boats, someone stands up and waves at you—someone you know deeply, though you can't quite see their face through the starlight. The audience begins to applaud, and the sound swells around you, softening and stretching until it isn't clapping anymore, just the slow, rhythmic crash of ocean waves pulling you gently back toward the surface.
