...and you begin to speak, but for a half-second no sound comes out at all.

In that silence, you realize three things at once:

1. You have no notes. Your hands are empty.
2. You have no idea what the speech is supposed to be about.
3. Everyone in the audience is smiling anyway, as if they already know what you're going to say.

You open your mouth again and your voice comes out — but it's not amplified by the microphone. It fills the whole room on its own, calm and clear, like you've rehearsed this a hundred times.

You aren't reciting words. You're remembering them.

You talk about the feeling of being late to somewhere important, of showing up unprepared and being seen anyway. As you speak, the dream does what dreams do and starts to shift to help you:

The huge, intimidating auditorium gets smaller and warmer. The harsh stage lights soften into sunlight through windows. The rows and rows of strangers become familiar faces — old teachers, friends from childhood, that barista who always remembers your order. They're nodding along.

The podium under your hands, which felt so solid and heavy, is suddenly light as paper. You realize you don't need to hold onto it. You step out from behind it and your shoes make no sound on the stage, because the stage is now soft grass.

You aren't nervous anymore. You're not performing. You're just talking to people who came to listen to *you*, exactly as you are.

And just as you get to the most important part — the one sentence you were actually meant to say — you feel that familiar, gentle tug of waking up.

You want to stay to hear how it ends.

Would you like me to continue the dream and finish the speech, or let you wake up right there?
