...and you follow them through streets that seem to rearrange themselves with every step. The buildings lean in kindly, their windows glowing like paper lanterns. One of the strangers — an old woman with laugh lines deep as riverbeds — takes your hand, and her palm is warm as sun-baked stone.

"The thing about being lost," she says, "is that it means you're somewhere new."

The others nod. A young man juggling oranges tosses one to you, and when you catch it, it's a small sun. You pocket it out of politeness. The city softens around you. The crowd that felt so indifferent before now parts like water, and you notice — really notice — the details: a baker sliding loaves from a glowing oven, two children sharing one pair of headphones, a dog asleep in a doorway dreaming its own dog-dream.

Just when you're about to ask where exactly you're going, you realize you've arrived. Where? You can't say. But the feeling of being lost has dissolved completely, replaced by the strange certainty that you were never really alone — the city was simply waiting for you to look at it differently.

You turn to thank the strangers, but they're already walking away, dissolving into the crowd like sugar into tea. Only the old woman glances back, and she doesn't wave — she just smiles, like she knows something.

In your pocket, the little sun still glows.

You wake with your hand curled around something warm, and for a moment, before the dream fades entirely, you could swear the room smells like fresh bread.
