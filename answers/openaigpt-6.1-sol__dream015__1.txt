build a garden.

It seems absurd at first. The city is a skeleton of rust and concrete, and every green thing you’ve seen has turned out to be plastic. Still, you find a packet of seeds in the pocket of a coat that isn’t yours. On the packet, someone has written: *For when you get there.*

You choose the roof of an abandoned library. You haul soil upstairs in a cracked cooking pot, steal rainwater from the gutters, and stretch wire between the chimney pots to keep the birds away.

Every night, something circles the building.

You hear its footsteps below: slow, heavy, stopping whenever you stop. Once, while carrying water, you glimpse an enormous eye through the stairwell window.

Then, one morning, the first shoot appears.

That night the footsteps come up the stairs.

You wait behind the rooftop door with a shovel raised. When it opens, a deer steps through, so tall its antlers scrape the frame. Old keys hang from them. Its mouth is full of newspapers.

It drops something at your feet: a small sack of soil.

By morning there are other gifts beside the door—a bottle of clean water, three potatoes, a pair of gardening gloves with six fingers on each hand.

You never see who leaves them.

You plant the potatoes anyway.

Weeks later, when you pull the first one from the earth, it is warm and faintly ticking. You hold it against your ear.

Inside, very far away, someone is setting a table.
