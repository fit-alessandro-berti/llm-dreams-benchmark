...realize the rules of the maze are quietly beginning to change.

At first, the challenges were purely mechanical and strict: interlocking brass gears that needed precise alignment, sequences of prime numbers etched into obsidian walls, and balanced scales requiring exact weights. Your mind felt sharp, cutting through each obstacle with a deeply satisfying *click* and the heavy grind of stone doors sliding open.

But the deeper you go, the less the maze cares about mathematics.

The cold stone walls gradually soften, taking on the texture of worn parchment covered in shifting, half-remembered handwriting. In the next chamber, there are no levers or locks—only a pedestal asking you to balance a heavy iron key against the memory of a rainy Tuesday. Somehow, with the strange, effortless certainty of a dream, you know how to solve it. You whisper a forgotten childhood secret into the empty air, the pedestal sinks, and a doorway opens through a curtain of hanging mist.

Soon, the corridors begin to fold into impossible geometries. Staircases lead simultaneously up and down, and the equations floating in the air are made of colors and scents rather than numbers. You solve a riddle by rearranging the shadows cast by your own hands. You unlock a gate by letting go of an old regret, watching it crystallize into a silver coin that fits neatly into the keyhole.

When you finally step into the very center of the maze, there are no towering walls left—only a vast, quiet courtyard beneath a sky of swirling indigo ink. 

In the middle sits a simple wooden table, and on it rests one final puzzle box. Its surface is intricate, shimmering with the patterns of every single riddle you solved to get here. You reach out, expecting another grueling test of focus, only to find that the lid isn't latched at all. 

As you lift it open, a warm, golden light spills out, swallowing the courtyard, the sky, and the maze itself—gently pulling you upward toward the waking world just before you can see what was inside.
