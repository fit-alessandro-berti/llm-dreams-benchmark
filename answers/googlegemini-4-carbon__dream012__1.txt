They aren't panicking anymore. They are completely silent, holding coils of silver rope, waiting for you to rewrite the rules of the storm.

With the strange, unshakeable certainty that only exists in sleep, you suddenly understand that the ship isn't sinking—the sky is simply pulling too hard. 

"Cast the anchors," you hear yourself command, though your lips never move. "Throw them upward."

Without hesitation, the lantern-faced crew heaves the heavy iron hooks into the air. Instead of falling, the anchors sail into the vortex overhead and catch fast onto the heavy, billowing thunderclouds. The silver ropes pull taut, humming in the wind like giant cello strings. 

With a deep groan of ancient timber, your ship lifts entirely out of the churning ink. 

As you ascend, the violent gale instantly softens into a warm, velvet hush. You lean over the railing and look down at the raging sea shrinking beneath the keel. From up here, the monstrous waves slow, flatten, and reveal themselves to be nothing more than the rumpled, dark-blue sheets of a vast, unmade bed. 

The storm clouds part around the bow, opening into a calm, endless ocean of stars. Your hands rest lightly on the rooted wheel, steering the quiet vessel effortlessly through the cosmos, drifting further and further until the wood beneath your fingers dissolves into the warmth of your pillow, and the sound of the wind becomes the steady rhythm of your own breathing.
