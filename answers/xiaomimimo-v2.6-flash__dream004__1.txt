As you touch the canvas, the colors don't just appear — they *move*, flowing like rivers of pigment across the surface.

You paint a doorway, and it swings open into a garden where the trees have leaves made of glass that chime in a wind that smells like old books and freshly cut lemons. Butterflies made of mathematics flutter overhead, their wings forming equations that somehow answer questions you didn't know you had.

A creature made entirely of geometric patterns approaches you — part fox, part mandala — and speaks in a voice like rain on copper. *"You are the painter AND the painting,"* it says, bowing. *"Every dream you dream from here changes the world you'll wake up to."*

You look back at your canvas, and you realize the colors you chose — without thinking, without deciding — spell out a word in a language you somehow understand:

***Remember.***

The colors and shapes around you begin to slowly crystallize into something solid, something *real*, like a memory being formed. You feel a pull — gentle but persistent — like the world is fading around you at the edges, softening into white light...

But the brush is still in your hand. And the dream is not finished yet.

*What do you paint next?*
