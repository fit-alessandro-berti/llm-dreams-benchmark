The first group surrounds a kitchen island, all talking over each other about someone named Diane. I don't know who Diane is, but when a woman catches my elbow and asks, "You know Diane, right?" I say yes, because in dreams you always say yes. She looks at me like I've confessed to something.

The apartment keeps growing. The hallway I came through is longer now, lined with coats that might all be mine. I check my phone to text the one person I know, but the screen shows a message I apparently sent hours ago — a string of letters that almost spells their name. The keyboard rearranges itself when I try to fix it.

Through a doorway: a group playing a board game with no rules. Someone is laughing, someone is actually crying, and they wave me over. When I explain I'm looking for someone, they all nod sympathetically — in perfect unison — and it occurs to me they might be a single organism.

Then I spot them. My person. Across a room that used to be the backyard, laughing at something someone said. I start toward them and the room stretches, guests stepping into my path with questions, drinks, a dog on a leash that I am suddenly holding. When I look down, the leash is gone. So is the dog. The room has shrunk, and my person is right there.

I tap their shoulder. They turn, and it's them, exactly them, and the relief is enormous and physical.

"Hey," they say. "How long have you been standing there?"

I try to answer, but the music swells and swallows my voice, and I can't find any of the words I mean to say—

And then the room goes soft at the edges. The party noise folds into the hum of a fan, or traffic, or morning light. I wake up holding nothing but the feeling — the search, the almost — and a certainty, already fading, that I found them just in time.
