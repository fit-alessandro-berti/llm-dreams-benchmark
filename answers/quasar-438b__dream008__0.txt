You feel your heart rate spike. You sit down at your desk, fingers hovering over the keyboard, but they won't move. Your brain is frozen. The deadline looms like a dark cloud on the horizon, ticking closer with every passing breath.

Your boss paces behind you, arms crossed, watching. You can hear his footsteps even though he's trying to be quiet. The whiteboard behind him still has last quarter's metrics on it. No one has erased them. No one ever does.

You open a blank document. The cursor blinks. You type a title. You delete it. You type another. You delete that one too. The clock reads 9:47 AM. The project is due by end of day.

Somewhere in the building, a vending machine malfunctions and starts dispensing free hot chips. You hear about it through a Slack channel. Everyone leaves their desks for three minutes. You consider going but don't. The cursor still blinks.

A notification pops up. It's from Sarah. It says: "Hey, are you okay?"

You type back: "Yeah. Just thinking."

She responds: "Take a walk. Clear your head. The building has good hallways."

You walk. You pass the vending machine. It's fixed now. Someone is standing next to it with a handful of chips and a confused look. You nod at them. They nod back.

You return to your desk. The cursor blinks. Your boss has sent you an invite for a 10:30 AM check-in. You accept it. You don't know what you'll say when it arrives.

You open a new document. You type a title. You keep it. You write a sentence under it. It isn't good. It isn't terrible. It is something.

The check-in arrives. You say, "Here's where I'm at so far." You show the sentence. Your boss says, "That's a start." You both know that's all he's going to say.

You go back to your desk. You type another sentence. The cursor doesn't blink for long anymore.
