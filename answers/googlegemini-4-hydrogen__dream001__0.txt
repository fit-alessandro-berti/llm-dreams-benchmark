When you try to unlock it, the numbers on the keypad rearrange themselves into unfamiliar subway symbols and tiny, blinking eyes.

Panic begins to hum in your chest. You look up at the skyline, which towers impossibly high—sleek obsidian skyscrapers leaning slightly inward over the streets like ancient trees. Streetlights flicker on, though the sky is a bruised, timeless twilight. Every street sign you pass seems to shift just as you focus on it: *Mercer St.* blurs into *Third Floor*, then into *Don't Forget*.

You break into a jog, though your legs feel heavy, as if you’re wading through waist-deep water. You weave through crowds of commuters who all walk in brisk, synchronized silence, wearing identical gray coats.

"Excuse me!" you call out, grabbing the sleeve of a man carrying a leather briefcase. "Do you know where the main building is? I have a meeting."

He stops, and for a brief second, his face looks oddly like your high school math teacher's, before smoothing back into that of a stranger. "You're already late," he says mildly, pointing toward a massive glass atrium across the avenue. "Take the blue escalator to the mezzanine, then cross the bridge before the tide comes in."

Before you can ask what he means, he steps into a revolving door that spins once and turns into a solid brick wall. 

A clock tower chimes somewhere in the distance—a deep, vibrating sound that makes the pavement ripple. You glance at your wristwatch, which only has one hand, spinning lazily backward. Somehow, you know you only have five minutes left.

You sprint across the empty avenue and burst into the glass atrium. There is no mezzanine, only a winding marble staircase that ascends straight into a low-hanging bank of fog. With no other choice, you climb. The stairs stretch on and on, turning from marble to worn wooden planks, then to the carpeted steps of an old movie theater. 

When you finally step through the mist, you find yourself in a quiet, endless hallway lined with identical mahogany doors. You pick one at random—number 404, written in chalk—and push it open.

It opens onto a sunlit conference room suspended high above the city. A dozen people sit around a long glass table, turning their heads in unison to look at you. At the head of the table is a blank projector screen, waiting. You step forward to apologize for being late, only to look down and realize you're wearing mismatched slippers and holding a half-eaten croissant instead of your notes—and as you open your mouth to speak, you have no idea what your own name is.
