You are given a mysterious locked box with no key in sight. As you examine it closely, you notice intricate patterns and **shifting constellations carved into the lid, glimmering as if someone had trapped a slice of night sky beneath the varnish.** They are not random. You trace one silver line with your fingertip and realize it is a river you have never seen yet somehow remember—a river that flows uphill, that turns into a spine, then a sentence written in a language your blood understands before your mind does.

The box is warm. It hums against your palms in time with your own pulse, and suddenly the room around you has grown quiet, as though the world itself is holding its breath. There is no keyhole, only a small, seamless seam where the patterns converge into the shape of an eye. As you watch, the eye blinks—not opening, but inviting. You understand, with the peculiar certainty of dreams, that the lock was never meant for metal. It was a question. And the answer is not something you hold, but something you are.

You press your thumb to the closed eye and whisper a secret you did not know you were keeping. The box does not click. Instead, it unfolds.

The walls of the container peel back like the petals of an enormous, impossible flower, each pattern detaching from the wood and floating into the air around you like luminous fireflies. There is no treasure inside. There is only a doorframe standing in the center of where the box used to be, and through it, a field of tall grass the color of dusk, moving under a wind that smells like rain and old books. Somewhere in the distance, a bell rings once.

The floating patterns spiral around your wrists, gentle as bracelets, tugging you forward. You step through. Behind you, the door frame closes, dissolving back into the look of a simple wooden box sitting innocently on a table in a room you can no longer quite recall. But ahead, the grass parts, and you walk on.
