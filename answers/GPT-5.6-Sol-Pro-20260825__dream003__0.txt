When you trace the symbols with your fingertip, the box becomes warm. A faint ticking begins inside, too slow and irregular to belong to a clock. You shake it gently. Something shifts within, followed by a whisper so quiet you think it might be your own thought:

**“You already have the key.”**

You search your pockets. They are filled with impossible objects: a train ticket to a city that does not exist, a photograph of yourself standing beside people whose faces have been carefully erased, and a tiny glass bottle containing the sound of rain. But no key.

The room around you has changed. You are no longer in your home but in a long corridor lined with doors. Each door bears one of the symbols from the box. Behind the first, you hear laughter from a childhood afternoon you had forgotten. Behind the second, someone is crying in your voice. Behind the third is complete silence—but a warm golden light shines beneath it.

The box grows heavier in your hands. A new pattern appears on the lid: two hands holding it exactly as you are now. Beneath them are the words:

**“Open only what you are willing to remember.”**

You realize the lock has no keyhole.

Holding your breath, you speak the first memory that comes to you—a small moment, almost meaningless, of someone once telling you that everything would be all right. The lock clicks.

The corridor doors fly open at once. Wind rushes through them, carrying voices, music, grief, and the scent of places you have never visited. The lid rises a fraction.

Inside is not an object, but a tiny version of the room you are standing in. Within that room, another you holds another box. And inside that box is another room, and another you, continuing endlessly.

Then the smallest version of you looks up.

Unlike the others, they are not looking at the box.

They are looking directly at you.

They lift one finger to their lips and whisper, somehow loud enough to fill the entire corridor:

**“Don’t wake up yet. We’re almost outside.”**

The floor tilts. The walls fold inward like paper. You fall toward the open box, past countless versions of yourself, each waking from a different dream.

Just before darkness closes over you, something small and metallic lands in your palm.

A key.

And when you wake, your hand is tightly clenched around nothing—except for the faint impression of a key pressed into your skin.
