One stranger hands you a warm cup of tea. Another unfolds a map, but instead of streets, it shows constellations. A third points toward a narrow passage between two buildings, where a small tree is growing beneath a streetlamp.

“This way,” she says.

As you walk together, the noise of the city softens. Car horns become distant music, and the pavement feels pleasantly warm beneath your feet. You begin to recognize things: a blue door, the scent of bread, a window with a crooked curtain. None of them belong to any place you’ve lived, yet each makes you feel a little less lost.

At the end of the passage is a courtyard with a table set for dinner. There is one empty chair.

You turn to thank the strangers, but they are already sitting down, passing bowls and making room.

Someone puts a plate in front of you.

For the first time all night, you stop trying to remember your destination.
