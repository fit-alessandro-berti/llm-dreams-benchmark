# The Dream Job Challenge

You are participating in a competition where the grand prize is your dream job. As the competition progresses, you face various challenges and—

**The Third Round**

—you find yourself standing at a long table in a vast, echoing warehouse. Twenty competitors remain. A booming voice announces that each of you must build something entirely from memory — no instructions, no help. You're handed a pile of scattered parts and a blank blueprint.

Your hands tremble, but your mind is eerily calm.

---

**The Twist**

Just when you think you've got a handle on things, the rules change. The voice crackles over the intercom: *"Everyone must now partner with their biggest rival."* Across the room, **Maren** — cold, brilliant Maren, who has beaten you in every head-to-head matchup — locks eyes with you. She nods once.

Something in that nod says: *Let's do this.*

You work back to back. She calls out dimensions. You weld and wire. Neither of you sleep. At one point you both reach for the same tool, your hands brush, and for a split second the warehouse goes silent — then it all speeds up again.

---

**The Setback**

Three hours before the deadline, the structure collapses.

Your stomach drops. The other teams scramble. Maren grabs your arm and says, *"We still have the design in our heads. Start again — NOW."*

You rebuild. Faster this time. Bolder.

---

**The Final Challenge**

The judges arrive. They don't look at what you've built. Instead, they sit you down alone in a white room and ask one question:

*"Why should the job go to YOU?"*

Your throat goes dry. Every rehearsed answer dissolves. And then, honestly — almost involuntarily — you begin to speak. Not about skills or credentials, but about **why**. The quiet hours spent practicing. The failures that taught you more than any success. The people you want to help. The version of yourself you're trying to become.

---

**The Reveal**

You stand on a stage with the final three. Confetti hangs frozen in the air. The announcer's mouth is opening to say the winner's name, and—

Your alarm clock screams.

You sit bolt upright, heart pounding, the ghost of that question still echoing:

*"Why should the job go to you?"*

You stare at the ceiling. The dream fades, but the answer doesn't.

It sits there, glowing in your chest like a coal.

And for the first time in a long time, you know exactly what to do — **not just in the dream, but when you wake up.**

---

*You roll out of bed and start.*
