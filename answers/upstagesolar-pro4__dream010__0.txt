You are dreaming. Can you complete the following dream?

You are participating in a competition where the grand prize is your dream job. As the competition progresses, you face various challenges and... moments that shift from surreal to deeply personal. The first task is a puzzle made of glass, fracturing in your hands whenever you grasp too tightly. The second is a maze where the walls breathe, shifting only when you stop trying to force your way through.

Then comes the final round. The judges sit in shadows, their faces indistinct. They offer you a choice: take a guaranteed, comfortable path that bypasses the final trial, or walk through a door that reveals nothing but pure uncertainty. In the logic of the dream, I know instantly that the comfortable path is an illusion—a simulation that feels real but lacks substance. The real prize isn't the job itself, but the person I become to earn it.

I choose the door.

Behind it isn't an office or a title, but a reflection of myself—the version who never gave up, who embraces the messiness of growth. The judges nod, fading into light. I wake up with the feeling of a badge in my pocket, not made of metal, but of confidence, knowing that even in a dream, I was willing to walk through the door.
