...the air goes thick and electric, tasting of copper and wet stone. The birdsong stops mid-note, as if someone has pressed pause on the forest. Above you, the sky folds in on itself, turning the color of a bruise: deep violet at the edges, sickly green at the center.

You start to run, but the path beneath your feet has changed. It's softer now, springy, like walking on moss laid over a mattress. Every step takes longer than it should. The trees lean in, their bark rippling like the skin of something breathing.

The first drop of rain hits your hand, and it isn't water. It's a tiny glass bead, perfectly round, with a faint light glowing inside. Then another, and another, until the sky is pouring them down. They chime as they strike the leaves, thousands of tiny bells ringing all at once. Where they land, small flowers bloom instantly, pale blue and luminous, then close again like eyes.

Thunder rolls, but it sounds strangely like a voice, low and familiar, saying a word you almost understand. You've heard this voice before. Maybe in childhood. Maybe in another dream.

Lightning splits an old oak ahead of you, and instead of fire, a doorway opens in the trunk. Warm yellow light spills out, along with the smell of cinnamon and old books. Inside, you can see a staircase spiraling downward, each step made of a different kind of wood.

You hesitate. The wind is howling now, lifting you slightly off the ground, your feet brushing the tops of the glowing flowers. Behind you, the forest is dissolving into silver static. There's nowhere else to go.

You step through the doorway.

The storm goes instantly silent, as if a window has been shut. You descend the stairs, counting them, but the numbers keep slipping away from you. Seven... twelve... forty... three. At the bottom is a small, cozy room with a fire crackling in the hearth and two armchairs facing each other. One is empty. In the other sits an old woman knitting something enormous: a blanket that spills across the floor and out under the door. Looking closer, you realize she's knitting the forest itself, the trees, the path, the very storm you just escaped, all in tiny, intricate stitches.

She doesn't look up, but she smiles.

"You're early," she says. "The storm was meant to find you tomorrow. But sit down. The tea's still warm."

You sit. The chair fits you perfectly, as if it was made for you long ago. She hands you a cup, and when you look into the tea, you see your own face reflected back, but younger, and laughing at something you can't remember.

"What was the thunder saying?" you ask.

She finally meets your eyes. Hers are the same violet-green as the storm sky.

"Your name," she says softly. "The one you had before you forgot it."

You open your mouth to ask her what it is, and the fire flares bright, and the room tilts, and somewhere far above you hear the chime of glass rain on leaves, fading, fading...

...and you wake up, the word still on the tip of your tongue.
