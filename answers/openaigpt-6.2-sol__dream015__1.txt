repair the town clock.

Everyone has an opinion about this. The woman who runs the well says nobody needs to know what time it is. The twins who sleep in the old pharmacy ask if you can make it always be Saturday. You tell them you’ll see what you can do.

You spend three days carrying pieces down from the tower and laying them out on the pavement. People stop to look. Someone brings you a screwdriver. Someone else brings a chair. On the fourth day you find a note tucked beneath a gear: *It ran five minutes fast. Please leave it that way. My husband was always late.*

At dusk, you get the clock running. The bell rings once, though it’s nowhere near the hour.

From somewhere beyond the ruined houses, another bell answers.

The woman from the well hears it too. She sets down her bucket and goes home for her coat.
