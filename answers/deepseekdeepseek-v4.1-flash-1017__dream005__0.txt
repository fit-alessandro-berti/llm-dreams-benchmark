...and every group seems to be discussing a different version of your life. One circle goes quiet when you approach, then asks you to settle an argument about a memory you don’t have. Another hands you a drink that tastes like rain and says, “You’re almost late.” You keep catching glimpses of the one person you know—a sleeve, a laugh, the back of their head—but each time you cross the room, the party rearranges itself into new hallways and unfamiliar rooms. At last you find them alone in the kitchen, calmly washing a single cup. “I’ve been looking for you everywhere,” you say. They look up, relieved, and answer, “Good. Now help me find the way out.” You take the cup from their hands, and behind you the whole party continues in a muffled roar, like applause heard underwater. You wake just as you turn the kitchen doorknob, still holding a cup that isn’t there.
