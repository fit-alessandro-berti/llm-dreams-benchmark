As I turn the box over in my hands, the room around me blurs into a soft, twilight violet, leaving only the box in sharp focus. It feels impossibly heavy one second, and weightless the next, humming softly against my palms in a rhythm that suddenly matches my own heartbeat. 

I trace one of the moving spirals with my thumb. Where my skin touches the surface, the silver lines flare into warm, golden light. There is no keyhole, because it doesn't want a key. With that strange, absolute certainty that only exists in dreams, I know it is waiting for a memory. 

I close my eyes and think of the sound of rain on a tin roof from when I was a child. 

*Click.* 

When I open my eyes, the box isn't unlocking; it is unfolding, its wooden sides peeling back silent and weightless, like the petals of a paper flower. 

I peer inside, expecting velvet or treasure, but there is no bottom. Instead, I am looking down into a vast, miniature night sky, swirling with nebulae and drifting stars. A cool breeze smells of pine and ozone, blowing upward from the depths of the box and brushing against my face. Floating right in the center of that tiny universe is a single, glowing brass key. 

I reach in to grab it, my hand sinking past the wrist, then the elbow, into the starry void. Just as my fingertips brush the cold metal of the key, gravity flips. I am no longer looking into the box—I am falling gently upward into its sky, the wooden petals closing softly far below me, until there is nothing left at all but starlight and the quiet sound of my own breathing.
