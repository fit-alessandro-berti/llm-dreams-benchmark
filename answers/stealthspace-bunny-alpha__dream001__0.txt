You start to run.

The city does not seem to notice. Its towers rise in endless rows, their windows glowing amber and blue, while the streets bend toward unfamiliar horizons. Your footsteps echo from walls that seem farther away than they should be. Above you, a silent train slides between buildings, carrying passengers whose faces are erased whenever you look at them directly.

Your watch says **12:01**.

The meeting is in **59 minutes**, but your phone shows no address, no name, no message from anyone waiting. There is only a small blue dot on its screen, pulsing like a heartbeat.

You stop at an intersection and ask a woman holding an umbrella in the empty, rainless street.

“Excuse me—do you know where the meeting is?”

She regards you with an expression of polite concern.

“If you’re late,” she says, “keep walking. The city will grow tired before you do.”

She turns a corner. You follow, but when you round the corner yourself, she is gone.

Ten minutes pass. The blue dot on your phone never moves.

Twenty minutes pass. You pass a hotel with no entrance, a park filled with trees made of paper, an entire avenue where every door is locked. Twice you see a familiar street ahead, but each time it changes when you approach.

Then something brushes against your coat.

A folded note has appeared in your pocket. You unfold it. The handwriting is your own.

**When you find the clock that has stopped, turn around.**

You stop running.

The city is suddenly quiet. Even the footsteps have vanished.

Behind you, set into the wall between two blank storefronts, is a small red door. Above it hangs a clock with no hands. Its face reflects the empty street, except for one figure standing before it: you.

You glance over your shoulder.

The door is already open.

Beyond it waits a narrow corridor lined with warm yellow light. At the end is a room containing a wooden table, two chairs, and a window overlooking the city. Your watch reads **12:59**.

Someone is already seated there.

They look up as you enter. Their face is indistinct—not hidden, simply difficult to remember, like a name just beyond your grasp.

“You found it,” they say.

“I didn’t even know where I was looking.”

“That isn’t the same thing.”

They gesture to the empty chair. Outside, the silent city waits beneath its thousands of mismatched clocks.

You sit.

The clock on your watch ticks forward.

**1:00.**

The stranger does not mention the time. They place a blank sheet of paper between you and push a pen across the table.

“We have a great deal to decide,” they say.

“What are we meeting about?”

The faintest smile crosses their face.

“Come,” they say. “Let’s find out.”

And somewhere far beneath the city, a bell begins to ring.
