...every cluster seems to belong to a slightly different party. 

To my left, a circle of people in immaculate velvet coats are clinking teacups filled with tiny, glowing moths. One of them leans in as I squeeze past and whispers urgently that the hors d'oeuvres are delayed because the tide hasn't come in yet. I nod seriously, completely accepting this, while suddenly struck by the quiet, sinking realization that I’m wearing mismatched socks and no shoes.

Across the vast, vaulted room—whose ceiling seems to dissolve into a twilight sky—I finally catch a flash of a familiar green jacket. Relief washes over me. I can't quite recall their name right now, only the warm, unmistakable certainty that *they* are the person I came here to see. I try to call out, but my voice comes out hushed and papery, swallowed by the hum of a hundred overlapping conversations that sound like English until I actually listen to the words.

They slip through a tall, arched doorway. I hurry after them, weaving past a group of strangers laughing wildly over a game of chess played with pieces of toast, and push through the heavy doors.

Instead of a hallway, I step out onto a wide stone terrace overlooking an endless, dimly lit library. The floor is covered in an inch of warm, rippling water, though nobody seems to mind. A jazz quartet is playing softly on a floating grand piano, their instruments made of tarnished brass and driftwood. 

A woman with silver hair grabs my sleeve as I wade past. "There you are!" she beams, handing me a tall glass of sparkling rainwater. "Did you bring the keys?" 

I pat my pockets, suddenly anxious, and pull out three smooth river stones and a folded map of a house I used to live in. She nods, satisfied, and drifts back into her group.

My legs feel heavy now, like I'm moving through honey, as I spot the green jacket again—standing alone at the edge of a balcony that wasn't there a moment ago. The music swells, bright and nostalgic. I push through the last of the crowd, climb three impossibly steep stairs, and finally reach out to tap their shoulder.

They turn around, smiling with a warmth that makes the whole strange night suddenly make sense. Their face shifts ever so slightly—partly my closest friend, partly someone I haven't thought about in years—and they say, "Oh, good, you found me. Come on, I want to introduce you to everyone."

They take my hand, and when I look back over my shoulder, the crowd is gone, replaced by hundreds of paper lanterns drifting lazily upward into the dark, waiting for us to follow.
