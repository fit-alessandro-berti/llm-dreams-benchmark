...written in your own handwriting.

Not just handwriting like yours. It's yours, down to the lopsided loop you put on your g's. The first question reads:

**1. What did you mean to say to her that afternoon by the lake?**

You look up. The exam hall is enormous, with rows of desks stretching toward a horizon where the ceiling seems to dissolve into overcast sky. The other students are writing furiously. None of them have faces you can hold onto. Each time you look away, they rearrange.

You look back down. The question has changed.

**1. Describe, in your own words, the sound of your childhood home at night.**

Your pencil is now a small silver key. You try to write with it anyway, and it works. The key leaves a trail of warm light on the paper, and you begin to describe the hum of the refrigerator, the creak of the third stair, the muffled television behind a closed door. As you write, you can hear them, faintly, rising from under your desk.

The proctor drifts down the aisle. She is your second-grade teacher, but also somehow the clock on the wall. She pauses beside you and taps your paper.

"You have all the time there is," she says, "and it's almost up."

You turn the page. There is only one question left, and it takes up the whole sheet:

**What would you do if this weren't a test?**

The room goes quiet. The other students have stopped writing. You realize that the windows have been open this whole time, and that the desks nearest them have begun to float gently out into the pale sky, their occupants still seated, unbothered.

Your own desk gives a small lurch, like a boat nudged from its dock.

You set down the key. You don't write anything at all. As your desk lifts off the floor and tilts toward the open window, the exam paper folds itself into a bird, and you understand, with the perfect certainty that only exists in dreams, that this was the correct answer.

Below you, the proctor is waving. The clock on her face reads the exact time you will wake up.
