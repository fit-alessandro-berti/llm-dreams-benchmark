You wake up in a completely different era, with no modern technology around. As you explore your new surroundings, you realize you need to find shelter before the sun sets. The light is already fading, casting long, distorted shadows across the cobblestone path you somehow woke up standing on.

Around you, people dress in tunics and cloaks, speaking a language that sounds both familiar and strange, like hearing a song you once knew but forgot the words to. There are no engines hums, no electric buzz, only the crackle of open flames and the distant lowing of cattle.

Instinctively, you reach into your pocket for a phone that isn't there, and the realization truly hits you. Panic tries to rise, but it's dismissible, like mist burning off in the morning sun. You force yourself to breathe. You need water, food, and warmth.

You spot a structure nearby—a large hall with smoke curling from a central chimney. People are moving in and out, carrying baskets. One of them, an older woman with weathered hands and kind eyes, notices you shivering in your inappropriate clothing. She doesn't ask your name or where you came from; in this time, perhaps such questions are unnecessary when survival is the common ground. She holds out a piece of dark bread and points toward the warmth of the hearth.

You realize you don't speak the language, yet you understand her gesture. The dream settles around you like a heavy blanket. You begin to walk toward her, not knowing how you got here or how you'll return, but knowing that for tonight, at least, you are safe. The horizon swallows the last of the light, and the dream begins to dissolve into the rhythm of firewood crackling.
