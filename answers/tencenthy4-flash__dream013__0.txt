Every time you solve a riddle, the corridor stretches and rewrites its own geometry to match the exact shape of your curiosity. The logic here is fluid and treacherous; a door that required a brass key a moment ago now only opens if you can genuinely *forget* the key. Shadows detach themselves from the floor and offer you clues in voices you recognize but can’t quite place, and the equations etched into the stones rearrange themselves into the faces of people from your past.

As you reach the heart of the maze, the puzzles stop being external. You are confronted with a final trial: a pair of scales balancing a feather against a feather, yet one is infinitely heavier than the other. You realize the weight is your own belief in the dream’s rules—the very concentration you used to survive the labyrinth is what keeps you trapped in it. When you finally let go of the need to "solve" anything, the scales dissolve into mist, and you find yourself standing in a circular room made entirely of mirrors. Each mirror reflects a different version of you, all of them dreaming slightly different dreams, all of them turning to look at you at the exact same moment.

You reach out to touch the mirror that shows you lying awake in your bed, and the glass ripples like water. The maze folds up like a paper map, slipping quietly into your pocket. There is a sensation of falling upward—and then you are lying in the dark, staring at the ceiling, wondering why the cracks in the plaster look so much like the corridors you just walked. You close your eyes, half-expecting the puzzles to be waiting, patient and hungry, to begin again... and somewhere in the back of your mind, a key turns in a lock that shouldn't exist.
