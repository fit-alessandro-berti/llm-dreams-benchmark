The sky turns from soft blue to bruised purple in seconds. The trees, which were whispering, now hold their breath.

You feel the storm before you hear it—pressure swelling in your ears, the air thick as syrup. The leaves begin to tremble, not from wind, but from something beneath the earth. A low rumble rolls up through your feet, like the ground is waking from a long sleep.

Lightning flickers between the trunks, not striking, just watching. Then the rain comes—not falling, but arriving. Each drop lands like a fingertip on your skin, warm and deliberate.

You should run. You know you should run. But your legs feel rooted, and the forest feels less like a place and more like a memory trying to remember itself.

A path appears where there was none: overgrown, glowing faintly with soft white mushrooms. They pulse in rhythm with the thunder. And from deep in the trees, a voice—familiar, though you can’t place it—calls your name.

Not to warn you. Not to save you.

To show you what the storm is hiding.

You take a step forward. The lightning forks into a shape like a door. The rain begins to fall upward.

And you realize you were never walking through the forest.

The forest was walking through you.
