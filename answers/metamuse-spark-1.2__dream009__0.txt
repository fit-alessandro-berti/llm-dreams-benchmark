You wake up in a completely different era, with no modern technology around. As you explore your new surroundings, you realize you need to **remember how to get back before you forget who you are.**

---

You wake up to cold.

Not the cold of an air conditioner, but a damp, drafty cold that seeps through a thin wool blanket. Above you is not a ceiling, but dark wooden beams and a thatched roof. Dust motes hang in a single shaft of morning light. Somewhere, a rooster crows.

You sit up too fast. You're on a straw mattress on a wooden floor. You're wearing rough linen clothes that aren't yours. Scratchy, too big, smelling faintly of woodsmoke.

Your hand instinctively pats the floor beside you for your phone. Nothing. Just straw and cold wood.

*Right. Dream,* you think. *This has to be a dream.*

You push open the low wooden door and step outside — and your breath catches.

You're in a village. Cobblestone streets, slick with morning dew. Timber-framed houses leaning against each other. In the distance, a stone church bell tolls. The air is thick with the smell of fresh bread, horse manure, and burning fires. No cars. No power lines. No planes overhead. Just the sound of hooves on stone, merchants shouting, children running barefoot.

People stare as you walk by. A woman in a long apron balances a basket of apples on her head. A blacksmith hammers red-hot iron, sparks flying. None of this looks like a movie set. It feels too real — too detailed, too textured to be imagined.

A boy tugs on your strange linen sleeve and speaks to you in an accent you barely understand. You don't know his language, but somehow you *do*.

As he runs off, you notice something strange. Your memories of *before* are already starting to fray at the edges. What was your last name? What was that glowing rectangle you kept looking at? What year were you born?

You look down at your hands. Around your wrist is the only thing that doesn't belong — a simple, modern wristwatch. But it has no numbers. Just a single, thin hand ticking slowly backwards.

And with a jolt of dream-logic clarity, you understand:

You need to find the clock tower in the center of town before that hand reaches zero. That's the door. That's why you're here. Every person in this village has already forgotten where they came from, settled into this life, content to bake bread and shoe horses forever.

If you don't reach it in time, you won't want to wake up anymore.

The bell tolls again. The watch ticks.

You start to run.
