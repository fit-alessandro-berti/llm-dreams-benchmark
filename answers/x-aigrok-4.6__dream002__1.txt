You dash for cover as the wind howls through the trees, leaves whipping past like startled birds. Thunder cracks overhead and the rain arrives in a sudden silver sheet—yet it doesn’t soak you. Instead it beads and lifts you gently off the ground, carrying you upward through the canopy into a sky that has turned the color of deep twilight. Below, the forest rearranges itself into glowing pathways that pulse with every heartbeat. A fox made of starlight glances back at you, tail flicking like a comet, and you follow—knowing, the way one knows things only in dreams, that the storm was never meant to frighten you. It was only the forest’s way of opening a door.
