…a brush appears in your hand, though you don’t remember picking it up.

The blank canvas isn’t truly blank. As you lean closer, you see faint outlines moving beneath its surface—memories that haven’t happened yet, doors that only open when you stop looking for them, faces made of music and rain.

You touch the brush to the canvas, and instead of paint, a color spills out that has no name. It sounds like laughter. It feels like forgetting. Wherever it spreads, the dreaming world rearranges itself: mountains fold into butterflies, rivers rise into staircases, and the sky blooms with moons made of glass.

You realize the canvas is not waiting for you to create something.

It is waiting for you to remember what you are.

So you paint a doorway.

When you step through it, the dream does not end—it turns inside out, and you wake up still holding a single brushstroke in your palm, glowing softly like a star that has chosen to stay with you.
