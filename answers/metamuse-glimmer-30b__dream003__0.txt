You are given a mysterious locked box with no key in sight. As you examine it closely, you notice intricate patterns and they are moving.

The lines of brass inlay shift under your fingertips like slow insects, rearranging themselves into new shapes every time you blink. You lean in and realize the patterns aren't decoration at all — they're tiny doors, tiny windows, tiny faces pressed against the wood, all staring out at you. One of them opens.

A warm, yellow light spills out, and with it the smell of old paper and rain. The box is breathing. Inhale, exhale, the lid lifts a millimeter.

You don't find a key because you are the key. The lock is shaped like your palm, and when you press your hand against it, the intricate patterns warm up and sync with your pulse. The wood sighs open.

Inside there is no treasure. There is only a smaller box, exactly the same as the first, nested inside it. And inside that one, another. And another. Each one lighter than the last, until the final one is so small it fits on your tongue, and it tastes like the moment you first realized you were dreaming.

You wake up before you can open it.
