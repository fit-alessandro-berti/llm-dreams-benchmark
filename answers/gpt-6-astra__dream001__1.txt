walk faster, as if speed might become a direction.

The streets are crowded with people carrying peculiar things: a silver ladder, a bowl of rain, a sleeping fox. Everyone seems to know where they’re going. You stop a woman holding a door beneath her arm and ask if she knows where your meeting is.

“Which version of you is expected?” she asks.

Before you can answer, a tram arrives with your name glowing above its windshield.

You climb aboard.

Inside, every passenger is someone you almost became. There’s a sailor with your hands, a musician with your nervous habit of rubbing one thumb against the other. Near the back, an older you sits beside an empty seat.

“You’re early,” they say.

“I don’t know where I’m going.”

They move their coat so you can sit.

The tram runs through a department store, past lamps lit beneath the sea, then up a steep street paved with dark green glass. You check your watch. There are still fifty-nine minutes left, but a tiny window has appeared in its face. Behind it, someone is setting a table.

“Is that the meeting?”

Your older self leans closer to look.

“Oh,” they say. “I wondered where that room went.”

At the next stop, they insist you get off. You stand before a narrow house squeezed between two enormous office towers. It is the house you grew up in, except for the brass elevator doors where the front porch should be.

Inside the elevator is a single button labeled **BEFORE**.

You press it.

The doors open onto a kitchen. Evening light lies across the table, and there is a damp patch on the ceiling in the shape of the unfamiliar city. You can make out the tram moving slowly along a crack.

A child sits at the table, wearing your coat. Its sleeves hang well past their hands.

“You nearly missed it,” the child says.

“What’s the meeting about?”

They reach into your coat pocket and take out something wrapped in a handkerchief. You know, before they unfold it, that it is the blue marble you lost when you were seven. You had blamed someone for taking it. You can’t remember who.

“I found it under the stairs,” the child says.

You sit down. There are things you want to ask, but the child rolls the marble toward you, and you have to catch it before it falls.

Inside the glass, a little white cloud turns.

Outside, the towers begin switching off their lights, one floor at a time. Someone upstairs runs a bath. You put the marble in your pocket, where it is unexpectedly warm.

“Have you got time for a game?” the child asks.

You glance at your watch.

The hands have gone, leaving two small holes.

“Yes,” you say, and take off your coat.
