…the questions are in your own handwriting. Not exam questions—just lines you remember thinking but never said out loud.

The first one reads: *“What do you actually want?”*

You try to ignore it, flip to the next page. It says: *“Why are you still sitting here?”*

The room feels warmer. The clock on the wall has no numbers, only a smiley face. You look at the other students—they’re not writing. They’re just watching you. Some of them look like you, from different ages.

The last page is blank. But when you press the pencil to it, words appear slowly, as if the paper is remembering:

*“This was never about the exam. You already know what you’re supposed to fear. Now ask yourself: what would you do if you weren’t afraid?”*

You look up. The exam room is gone. You’re standing at the foot of your childhood bed, holding a spoon, wearing pajamas. The future is still unknown. But you feel, for the first time, like you’re the one writing it.

And then—you wake up.
