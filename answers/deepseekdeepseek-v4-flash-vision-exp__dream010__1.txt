…and the challenges keep shifting like sand under your feet.

One moment you’re solving impossible puzzles in a library where the books whisper clues. The next, you’re balancing on a tightrope stretched between two skyscrapers, carrying a glass of water that must not spill—because each drop is a memory of something you love. You don’t know the rules, but your body remembers them.

Then the final challenge appears: a door with no handle. A voice echoes: *“To win your dream job, you must open this door using only what you already carry.”*

You search your pockets. Nothing. No keys, no tools. But then you notice your own hands—warm, alive. You press your palm against the door. It doesn’t budge. You lean your forehead on the wood and close your eyes. And you remember why you wanted this dream job in the first place: not for the title, not for the prize, but for the feeling of being fully, usefully awake.

So you whisper the truth: *“I don’t need to force the door. I need to become the thing that opens it.”*

The door dissolves into light. On the other side, there’s no grand stage, no judges. Just a quiet room with a desk, a window, and your own reflection. You sit down. You feel at home. And you realize the competition was never about the job—it was about becoming someone who could live it.

Then the alarm goes off. But when you wake, the dream lingers, and you know exactly which letter to write, which first step to take, and you smile.

Your dream job is already waiting for you to wake up.
