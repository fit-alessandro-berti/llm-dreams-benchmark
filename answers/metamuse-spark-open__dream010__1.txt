You close your eyes inside the dream, and the competition is already underway.

You are participating in a competition where the grand prize is your dream job. As the competition progresses, you face various challenges and —

...the challenges don't make sense the way waking-life challenges do.

At first you are in a vast, sunlit office building with no ceiling. Where the ceiling should be is just open blue sky. Hundreds of other contestants are milling around in suits and dresses that keep subtly changing color every time you blink. No one seems to notice. A pleasant, disembodied voice over an intercom announces:

*"Welcome, finalists. Challenge One: Please find where you are supposed to be."*

Everyone else immediately knows where to go. They stride off confidently down hallways that stretch and bend like ribbons. You are left holding a single piece of paper. It’s your resume, but the words are written in wet ink that slides down the page. You try to read it and the letters swim away from you.

You wander. The hallway carpet turns to soft grass under your bare feet — wait, when did you take your shoes off? It doesn't feel strange. A door appears that has your name on it in gold letters, but the letters are spelled slightly wrong in a way that feels more *right* than your actual name.

You open it.

**Challenge Two** is inside.

It’s an interview, but the panel of judges are three towering figures with kind, animal heads — an owl in glasses, a fox in a sharp blazer, a bear with a warm smile. They don’t ask you about your strengths and weaknesses.

The owl asks, "What would you do if you had all the time in the world?"

As you start to answer, your mouth feels clumsy, but the words come out not as sound, but as images. You show them: you, working not for a paycheck but lit up from within, completely absorbed in the work you love. You see yourself building, writing, helping, creating — whatever *your* dream job looks like in your heart, you see it clearly now, projected on the wall behind you like a film.

The fox leans forward and whispers, "Challenge Three: Show us you can do it without trying to be perfect."

Suddenly your hands are full of clay. Cold, wet clay. You have to sculpt something while the floor beneath you slowly turns into a stage and all the other contestants become an audience. Your first instinct is to make it flawless. But the clay keeps wobbling and melting. The audience isn’t judging — they’re humming a low, familiar song you remember from childhood but can’t name.

So you stop trying to control it. You laugh — in the dream your laugh echoes pleasantly — and you let the clay be messy and strange and *yours*. It forms itself into a shape that is imperfect, lopsided, and absolutely alive.

The bear-judge claps, and the sound is like a warm wind.

*"Final Challenge,"* the intercom voice says, but now it sounds like your own voice. *"Walk through the door as if you already have the job."*

At the far end of the stage, a simple wooden door appears. Through its frosted glass you can see the light of that perfect workday — colleagues talking, sunlight on a desk that is already yours, a project waiting for you with your name on it.

You realize you’re scared to open it. What if it locks behind you? What if you don’t deserve it?

Then you understand the dream logic: You don’t win the job by beating everyone else. You win it by realizing the competition was never against them. It was permission you had to give yourself to walk forward.

You place your hand on the warm brass handle.

You turn it.

And just as the light floods out to meet you —

you wake up with the distinct feeling that you’ve already said *yes*.
