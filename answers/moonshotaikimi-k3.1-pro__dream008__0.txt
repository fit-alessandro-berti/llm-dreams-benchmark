open your laptop, but the screen contains only one line:

**PROJECT: EVERYTHING  
DEADLINE: BEFORE YOU STARTED**

Your boss places an hourglass on your desk. The sand is falling upward.

“Whenever you’re ready,” she says.

You begin typing, but every keystroke makes the office larger. Cubicles multiply into the distance. The printer down the hall starts producing pages describing everything you are doing, exactly one second before you do it.

You run to stop it, but the hallway stretches beneath your feet. Your coworkers hurry past carrying armfuls of clocks. None of the clocks have hands. Somewhere, your boss keeps asking, “How are we tracking?”

Finally, you reach the printer. It produces one last page. This one is blank except for a single question:

**What happens if you stop?**

So you stop.

The clocks go quiet. The hallway folds itself back into an ordinary room. Your boss is standing beside you, still waiting, but now she looks less like your boss and more like someone who has forgotten why they knocked on a door.

“The deadline is impossible,” you tell her.

She nods. “Then why were you running?”

You turn the hourglass sideways. The sand hangs motionless.

All the printed pages lift into the air and become white birds. They fly through the ceiling, which has quietly become the sky. Your desk softens into a bed, and your boss pulls a blanket over you.

“Good work,” she whispers. “You finished with time to spare.”

Just as you fall asleep inside the dream, your alarm rings.

For one strange second, you can’t tell whether you’ve woken up late—or yesterday.
