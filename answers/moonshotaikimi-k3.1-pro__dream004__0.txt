When you touch the brush to the canvas, the white surface ripples like water. You paint a small red door, and somewhere behind you, something enormous inhales. You don’t turn around. Instead, you paint a window inside the door, then a sky inside the window, then a flock of glass birds inside the sky.

The birds burst from the canvas in a shower of silent wings. Wherever they fly, the landscape becomes softer: mountains fold into bedsheets, rivers loosen into silver ribbons, and the trees unzip their shadows to reveal crowds of tiny, sleeping stars.

One bird lands on your shoulder and whispers, “You left yourself out.”

So you paint a figure standing before the canvas. But the figure turns its head before you give it a face. It steps out of the painting, takes the brush from your hand, and begins painting you—first your shadow, then your hands, then the expression you make just before waking.

You try to ask what is happening, but your voice comes out yellow.

The figure points to the red door. It is open now. Beyond it is a quiet room filled with morning light, and someone is asleep there, dreaming fiercely.

You step through.

For one weightless instant, every color in the world has your name.

Then you wake—and beneath your fingernail is a single fleck of a color you have never seen before.
