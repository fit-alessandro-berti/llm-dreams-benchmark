At first, you mistake it for wind. But the wind has gone still. Above you, clouds gather in slow, dark folds, and a single silver leaf drifts down into your palm.

“Keep walking,” it says.

You follow a narrow trail you hadn’t noticed before. Thunder rolls somewhere beneath the earth. With each rumble, the roots beside the path glow blue, illuminating ferns and small white flowers.

Then the rain arrives—not in drops, but in tiny, shining keys.

They fall softly among the leaves, ringing like distant bells. One lands at your feet. You pick it up just as a door appears in the trunk of an ancient oak.

Behind you, the storm draws closer. Ahead, warm light slips through the keyhole.

The key turns before you finish turning it.

Inside is your childhood kitchen. A kettle murmurs on the stove. Someone has set out two cups, though you cannot remember who used to sit across from you.

You step inside, still holding the silver leaf.

Outside the window, the forest bends beneath the storm. You reach for a cup and find that it is already warm.

From the other side of the table, a familiar voice asks, “Did you get caught in the rain?”

You look up, and for a moment you remember exactly who it is.
