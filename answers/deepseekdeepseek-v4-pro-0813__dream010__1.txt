…and each challenge seems designed to expose a different fear you didn’t know you had.

In the first round, you’re asked to build a bridge out of your own memories while the floor beneath you dissolves into television static. The boards appear as moments: your first bike, a breakup, a Sunday afternoon with sunlight on the carpet. Some hold your weight; others crack and fall into the void. You learn to choose carefully.

In the second round, you have to give a presentation to a panel of your former selves. They interrupt constantly. The ten-year-old you asks why you stopped drawing dragons. The sixteen-year-old you rolls her eyes. The twenty-two-year-old you keeps saying, “This isn’t what we practiced.” You freeze, then finally say, “I’m still figuring it out.” The panel goes silent, and for once, they nod.

The other competitors keep shapeshifting. Sometimes they look like people you’ve envied. Sometimes they look like versions of you who gave up. One of them leans over and whispers, “The judges don’t want talent. They want someone who won’t flinch.”

In the final challenge, the judges hand you a locked box with no key. They say, “Inside is the thing you most want to say but never have.” You sit with it for what feels like hours. Eventually, you stop trying to open it and just hold it. That’s when the box opens on its own, and inside is a mirror. You see yourself, exhausted and hopeful, and you say, “I’m proud of you for still trying.”

The grand prize job shimmers on a contract in front of you. The title keeps changing every time you blink: *Astronaut-Poet. Deep Sea Librarian. Chief Memory Archivist. Person Who Finally Rests.* You pick up the pen. Then you put it down.

Instead, you walk out of the arena into a bright, ordinary morning. The dream doesn’t end with a trophy. It ends with you waking up slowly, still carrying the strange, steady calm of someone who has just chosen themselves.
