...follow them through streets that rearrange themselves behind you, quietly, like the city is tucking in its edges.

One of the strangers — an old woman in a coat the color of autumn — takes your hand. Her palm is warm as fresh bread.

"You've been walking in circles," she says gently, "because you kept trying to get *somewhere*. But look—"

The crowd parts. Ahead of you: not an exit, not a destination, but a small square you somehow already know. A fountain. A bench. A tree heavy with light. You have never been here before, and you have always known it, the way you know your own name.

The strangers begin to fade the way people do in dreams — first translucent, then golden, then simply the feeling of having been loved. The old woman lingers longest.

"You were never lost," she tells you. "You just forgot that you knew the way."

You sit on the bench. The city hums on around you, vast and busy, but it no longer presses in; it sounds almost like breathing now, like waves. You close your eyes inside the dream, and for once the dark behind your eyelids feels like an embrace instead of an absence.

And when you wake, the feeling stays — that being lost was only ever a rumor, and the kindness of strangers was only ever your own kindness, wearing borrowed faces, coming to find you.
