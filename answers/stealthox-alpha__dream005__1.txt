...each group seems to expect you. A woman holding a glass of something amber tells you the bathroom is through the kitchen, but when you get there, the kitchen has become a library, and everyone in it is reading aloud simultaneously from different books.

Someone hands you a plate of food you didn't ask for. You carry it for what feels like an hour. You never eat it. You can't remember why you're still holding it.

You hear your friend's laugh from another room—distinctive, unmistakable—but by the time you push through the crowd, the room contains only strangers playing cards with old photographs instead of playing cards. One of them looks up and says, "They just left," though you never told anyone who you were looking for.

The house keeps growing. Hallways you've already walked lead somewhere new. A staircase going down somehow ends on the roof. Outside, the party continues on a lawn that wasn't there before, under a sky that's gone dusky even though it was midnight a moment ago.

You check your phone. It's full of messages from people at this very party, sent from across the room, all saying variations of *where are you?*

Finally, near the coat closet—which is enormous inside, more like a train station than a closet—you spot them. Your one familiar face. They wave. Relief floods through you, and you start toward them, and just as you're about to reach them, they turn and melt into the crowd—and you realize with sudden, crushing clarity that you've forgotten their name.

Or maybe you never knew it. Maybe you've been searching this whole time for someone you invented.

Then you wake up, heart thudding, with the lingering sense that somewhere there's a plate of food going cold, and someone is still waiting for you to find them.
