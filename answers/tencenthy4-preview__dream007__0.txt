…you decide to stop defending yourself long enough to ask the question you’ve been avoiding all night.

“Are we fighting about the thing,” you say, “or about losing each other?”

The room goes very still. Your friend’s face flickers—first it’s them, then it’s the version of them from years ago, the one who waited for you after school, the one who knew your secrets before you spoke them aloud. The argument—about the letter, the decision, the future you can’t agree on—loses its hard edges. It becomes water. The floor tilts, and suddenly you are both standing on a shoreline at dusk, the sky the color of a bruise slowly healing.

“I don’t want to be right,” they say, and their voice sounds like the tide. “I want to be known.”

You realize the important thing was never the disagreement. It was the fact that somewhere along the way you both started speaking in code, hiding the softest parts of yourselves behind sarcasm and volume. You open your mouth to apologize, but what comes out is a swarm of tiny lights, like fireflies released from a jar. They drift between you, illuminating the space where the fight used to be.

You step forward. In dreams, distance is only a suggestion. Suddenly you are close enough to see the exhaustion in their eyes, the love buried underneath it like a stone at the bottom of a river. You take their hand. It is warm, solid, real. The shoreline dissolves into the ceiling of your bedroom.

You wake with the echo of their name in your mouth and the strange, fragile certainty that the dream was trying to tell you something the daylight has been too loud to hear: that being right is a small, cold country, and you don’t have to live there alone.
