You start to run, but the cobblestones beneath your feet feel like warm honey, slowing each step into a viscous glide. The buildings around you stretch and yawn like waking cats, their windows sliding open and shut in patterns that seem to spell out directions in a language you almost understand. You reach for your pocket to check a map or phone, but find only a ticket stub printed with shifting ink—the destination rewriting itself with each blink: *Platform Nine,* then *The Clocktower That Wasn't,* then simply *Almost There.*

You flag down a taxi, but the driver has no face, just a smooth expanse of skin where features should be, and they hand you a compass that spins wildly before the needle snaps downward, pointing into the earth. You follow it anyway, stepping through a brick wall that offers no resistance, and emerge on the other side into a garden where time moves backward. Children are unpicking daisies, reassembling them into buds, while a woman in a Victorian dress waters a tree that grows downward into the clouds.

Panic begins to dissolve into something else—a strange, buoyant calm. You realize, with the logic of dreams, that the meeting isn't about arriving somewhere, but about *noticing*. You stop running. The city exhales around you, the streets contracting like an accordion, until you find yourself standing in a small café that smells of cinnamon and old books. A clock on the wall shows exactly one hour remaining, though the hands are melting upward like wax.

Across from you sits a figure in a charcoal suit, smiling with teeth that are too numerous but somehow comforting. "You're just in time," they say, though their voice resonates from inside your own chest. "We've been waiting for you to stop looking." Outside the window, the unfamiliar city begins to dissolve into morning light, and you wake with the distinct impression that you were never late at all—only exactly where you needed to be.
