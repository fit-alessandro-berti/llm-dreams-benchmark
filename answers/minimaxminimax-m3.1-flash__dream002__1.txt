The birds fall silent. The sunlight vanishes between the trees, and the forest seems to draw closer. Branches bend overhead, though there is almost no wind. Then the rain arrives, not gradually but all at once, a silver curtain swallowing the path behind you.

You run.

Lightning cracks through the clouds, and in its flash you see eyes watching from between the trunks. Not threatening—just curious. The ground turns to mud beneath your feet. Thunder rolls through the forest like the sound of a giant shifting in its sleep.

Ahead, between two ancient oaks, you find a narrow doorway standing on its own. Rain blows through it, but beyond lies a quiet room filled with warm lamplight. A table waits beside the door, covered with dry leaves, a bowl of fruit, and a silver key that pulses faintly whenever the lightning flashes.

As you step inside, the storm follows you.

It pours through the doorway, filling the room with wind and rain, but the lamp never flickers. The forest outside writhes, its branches becoming long shadowy fingers. Then, just as you close the door, everything goes still.

Silence.

You open it again.

The storm is gone. Moonlight rests across the forest floor, and every leaf glitters with rain. Far away, a night bird begins to sing. The silver key is warm in your hand, and when you look back, the doorway has disappeared.

You walk home through the calmed forest, carrying the secret of the little room—or perhaps the secret is carrying you. By the time you reach the edge of the trees, you realize the storm has washed away your footprints.

Behind you, the forest resumes its peaceful breathing.
