The words hang in the air like fog, heavy and half-formed. Your friend’s mouth is still open, caught mid-sentence. But instead of firing back the sharp reply you had rehearsed, you reach out and place your hand on their shoulder. The room softens around you—the argument was never about the words. It was about wanting to be understood.

“I’m sorry,” you say, and the sentence feels like a key turning in a lock.

Your friend blinks. Their expression shifts from anger to something fragile. Then they laugh, a small broken laugh, and say, “I thought you were going to walk away.”

“I thought I was too,” you admit.

The dream begins to melt at the edges. The walls fade into a cool blue dusk. You both sit down on the floor, and the argument dissolves into a story you tell each other, one memory leading to another. The important thing is still there, but now it’s something you’re holding together, not throwing back and forth.
