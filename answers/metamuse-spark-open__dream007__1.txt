You decide to stop talking.

Not because you've given up, or because they've won — but because mid-sentence, you notice something strange.

Your words aren't coming out right. Every time you try to make your point louder, your voice gets *softer*, until you're both just whispering-shouting at each other in this too-bright kitchen/living room/classroom that feels like all three places at once.

And your friend notices it too. They open their mouth to fire back, but nothing comes out except a little puff of air that hangs there, visible, like cold breath.

You both just stare at that little cloud of what you were *going to* say.

Then, in that weird dream-logic way, you decide to do something different than you ever would awake. You reach out and gently push their cloud of breath back toward them, and you take yours back in.

The heat in the room drops instantly. The walls, which had been creeping inward with every harsh word, sigh and slide back to where they're supposed to be. You can hear the background hum of the dream again — maybe it's rain, maybe it's a song from another room.

You decide to sit down on the floor, right there in the middle of the argument. After a second, your friend sits down too, across from you.

You don't resolve it with perfect words. You just say, very quietly so it will actually be heard: "This matters to me because you matter to me."

And your friend says the same thing back, in their own way.

You wake up before you figure out if either of you changed your minds — but the feeling of anger is gone, replaced by that heavy, tender closeness you only get after a real fight and a real repair.
