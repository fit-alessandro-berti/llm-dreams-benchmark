…you raise your hand, not toward the wheel, but toward the storm itself.

The wind falls silent for one impossible heartbeat.

Every lantern on the ship ignites blue. The waves, huge as mountains, freeze mid-fall, their foam hanging in the air like shattered stars. Your crew stares, but you understand, the way one understands things only in dreams: the storm is not attacking the ship. It is asking a question.

You step to the bow and speak a word you have never learned.

The sea splits—not downward, but inward—opening a path of glassy black water that glimmers with light beneath. The ship glides forward without wind, and the crew whispers as the storm curls itself into a corridor around you, thunder rolling like distant drums.

At the center of the sea you find a lighthouse floating upside down, its beam shining into the depths instead of the sky. There, beneath the waves, you see another ship sailing above you, its captain standing on the deck with one hand raised.

It is you.

The other captain smiles, and when they turn their wheel, your ship turns too.

The storm exhales.

Rain becomes birds.

The ocean becomes a field of dark feathers drifting in every direction. Your crew is no longer afraid; they stand at the rails, watching the horizon fold and unfold like paper.

Then the dream softens.

You are still the captain, but the ship is small now—only a boat, really—and the sea is calm, lit by dawn. In your hand is a compass whose needle points not north, but toward whatever you are trying to find.

You look at it, and it points home.

And when you look up, the storm is gone, and the sea remembers nothing but your name.
