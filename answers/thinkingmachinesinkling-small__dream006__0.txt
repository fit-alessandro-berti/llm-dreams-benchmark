...and you begin to speak—but your voice doesn't come from your mouth. It comes from the air itself, a low hum that makes the lights in the auditorium sway like lanterns in a breeze.

Your hands are holding a microphone, but it’s made of clear glass full of liquid starlight. When you open your mouth, the words don’t sound like language; they feel like colors, spreading across the crowd in slow, warm waves. The audience isn’t made of people anymore—just faces without bodies, floating in perfect silence, nodding with eyes that are slowly becoming mirrors.

You take one step forward and realize the stage is actually a boat, gently rocking on water that reflects a sky you’ve never seen before. You don’t need to finish the speech; the dream knows you were never going to say anything. It just wanted you to stand there long enough to feel the weight of being seen, and to let that weight turn into wings, or into water, or into the quiet moment just before you wake up.

You turn to leave, but the exit doors are just curtains of mist. You slip through them. Behind you, the crowd dissolves into rain, and you feel yourself rising—not with fear, but with a strange, gentle relief—until you’re floating just above the floor, and then the whole scene fades to a single, glowing blue light.

You wake up with the faint echo of applause that sounds exactly like a heartbeat, and the strange feeling that you already gave the perfect speech—without once saying a word out loud.
