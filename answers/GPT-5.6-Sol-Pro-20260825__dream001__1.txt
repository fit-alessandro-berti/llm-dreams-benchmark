You start to run, weaving through crowds that seem to move according to rules you cannot understand. Everyone carries a silver umbrella, although the sky is perfectly clear. Street signs rearrange their letters whenever you look away, and the map on your phone shows only a blinking blue dot surrounded by empty white space.

You stop a woman wearing a bright red coat and ask if she knows where the meeting is.

“Of course,” she says, as though you have asked something obvious. “It’s where you promised to be.”

Before you can ask what that means, she steps onto a passing bus without opening its doors and disappears inside.

The clock above a nearby tower strikes thirteen times. Somehow, only forty minutes remain.

You hurry down a narrow alley and emerge into an enormous train station. Hundreds of departure boards hang from the ceiling, but instead of destinations, they display moments from your life: **FIRST DAY OF SCHOOL**, **THE CALL YOU NEVER RETURNED**, **THE ROAD YOU ALMOST TOOK**.

At the very bottom, one line flashes:

**IMPORTANT MEETING — PLATFORM 0 — DEPARTING SOON**

You search frantically, but the platforms begin at one. Then you notice a staircase hidden behind a curtain of rain. It descends beneath the station, each step becoming softer until you realize you are walking on layers of old letters, photographs, and unfinished plans.

At the bottom is a quiet platform with no tracks. A single white door stands in the middle of it.

Five minutes remain.

You open the door and enter a vast conference room. Every chair is occupied by a different version of you: yourself as a child, yourself much older, yourself dressed for careers you never pursued, and several versions you do not recognize at all. They have been waiting patiently.

At the far end of the table is one empty chair.

Your chair.

You sit down, breathless, and apologize for being late.

Your older self smiles.

“You’re not late,” they say. “You arrived the moment you stopped asking the city where to go.”

A folder lies in front of you. Inside is a single blank page and a pen. At the top, in your own handwriting, are the words:

**Where do we go from here?**

All the versions of you turn toward you, waiting for an answer.

You place the pen on the paper. The moment its tip touches the page, the walls dissolve. The entire city unfolds around the table like a living map, its streets shifting into new paths according to every word you write.

You realize the meeting was never about finding a destination.

It was about choosing one.

Just as you begin to write, every clock in the city starts ringing at once—and you wake with your hand still moving across the bedsheet, as though finishing a sentence you can no longer remember.
