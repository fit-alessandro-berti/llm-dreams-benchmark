The microphone feels less like a tool and more like a living vine wrapped around my hand, pulsing with a low, electric hum that vibrates up my arm and settles in my chest. I open my mouth to say hello, but instead of my voice, a cascade of golden light spills from my lips, hanging in the air like smoke before coalescing into shapes.

The crowd doesn't applaud; they lean forward, their faces blurring into a single, vast landscape of expectation. I realize then that I'm not speaking words; I'm answering a question they haven't asked yet. Each sentence I struggle to form becomes a brick, laying down a path in front of the stage. The floor beneath my shoes turns to water, rippling with every breath I take. 

I don't feel fear, only a profound weight, as if the dream itself is handing me a narrative I forgot I wrote. I look out at the sea of faces, and they begin to dissolve into children, then stars, then just eyes. I take a breath, the light in my hands brightens, and I whisper the one thing that makes the theater disappear: "I know why you're here." 

And then I wake up.
