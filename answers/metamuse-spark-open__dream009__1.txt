Of course — here's how that dream might continue:

You wake up in a completely different era, with no modern technology around. As you explore your new surroundings, you realize you need to **remember how to get back before you forget you don't belong there.**

You sit up and the bed creaks. It's not a bed at all, but a straw mattress on a wooden frame. The air smells like woodsmoke and fresh-baked bread, and light is coming in through a small, wavy-glass window. No buzz of an AC, no glow of a phone — just the distant clop of horse hooves on cobblestone and someone outside calling out in a language that sounds familiar, but just out of reach.

You step outside in clothes that aren't yours — heavy linen, soft leather shoes. The street is narrow and bustling. A market with hand-painted signs, barrels of apples, ink and paper instead of screens. No cars, no wires overhead, just laundry lines strung between timber-framed houses. Everyone moves like they know exactly what they're supposed to be doing.

And as you explore, you start to notice something strange. People smile and nod at you like they *recognize* you. A baker hands you a warm roll without you asking. A little girl tugs on your sleeve and says, "You were supposed to be here yesterday."

That's when you realize it — you need to find the thing that doesn't fit. In a dream, there's always one anachronism, one anchor to your own time.

You search frantically, but gently, trying not to look lost. Past the blacksmith, past the well in the square, until you see it: on the stone wall of the old clock tower — which has no electricity, just a massive swinging pendulum — someone has carved something fresh.

It's your handwriting.

It says your name, and underneath: *"Wake up when the bell rings."*

Just then, the tower bell begins to toll. One... two... Each chime makes the colors around you a little brighter, a little less solid, like a watercolor in the rain.

And you have a choice that only makes sense in a dream: do you run toward the sound to wake up, or stay a moment longer to see what happens at three?

Then — as always in dreams — you start to wake up, the smell of bread fading, the sound of the bell slowly turning back into your alarm.
