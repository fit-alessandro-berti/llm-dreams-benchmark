...decide to climb the rusted water tower at the edge of what used to be a town. Nobody has told you it's a bad idea, and no one is left to tell you otherwise.

The ladder groans, but it holds. Halfway up, you notice the sky has a color you don't recognize, somewhere between amber and pewter, and the clouds move too slowly, as if they're thinking about something. At the top, you find not water but a small glass terrarium, sealed and humming faintly. Inside, a single green sprout is pushing through a bed of gray soil, and it's the only living thing you've seen in days.

Below, the wind carries the distant sound of a radio. You follow it.

The road winds past a hollowed-out cinema, its marquee still reading *TONIGHT: EVERYTHING* in letters missing half their bulbs. A dog with three legs trots alongside you for a while, then stops at a fence and looks back as if to say *this part is yours*. You take a jar of pickled peaches from a collapsed pantry and feel, absurdly, like a king.

The radio leads you to a bell tower in a field of wildflowers that have no business growing in ash. A woman is sitting on the steps with a crank-powered transmitter, her hair braided with copper wire. She doesn't look up.

"You're late," she says. "Everyone's always late. Sit down. The signal only comes through when someone's listening."

You sit. She keys the transmitter, and a voice, tinny and warm, begins reading names: people you've never met, places that might not exist anymore. Each name lifts a little of the heavy air out of the field, until the wildflowers start to glow faintly, like lanterns being lit one by one.

You realize the world isn't ending. It's just waiting for someone to start counting the living again.

And you begin to listen.
