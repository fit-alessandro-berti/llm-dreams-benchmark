The decision feels so strange that the room goes quiet before either of you does. Your friend is still speaking, but their words turn into small white moths, circling the lamp between you.

“I’m afraid you don’t understand why this matters to me,” you say.

Your friend looks down. “I thought you didn’t care why it mattered to me.”

The floor tilts gently. You both grab the table, and for a moment you are children again, steadying a raft you built together from doors.

You haven’t changed your mind. Neither have they. But you sit down.

Outside the window, the rain begins falling upward. Your friend pushes your untouched cup toward you, and you remember that they made it exactly how you like it.
