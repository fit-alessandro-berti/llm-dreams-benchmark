The city’s noise softens behind you. One stranger carries a paper lantern, though it is still afternoon; another hums a tune you almost remember. They don’t ask where you were going.

“We’ll start with somewhere warm,” says the woman beside you.

You come to a café wedged between two tall buildings. Inside, rain taps against the windows, though outside the sky was clear. Someone brings you a cup of tea prepared exactly as you like it.

You search your pockets for a map and find a photograph instead. In it, you are standing with these same strangers beside the sea. Everyone is laughing. On the back, in your handwriting, are the words: *Thursday, after we got lost.*

“But I haven’t been there,” you say.

The woman leans over to look. “Neither have I. It looks windy.”

She seems pleased by this.

For the first time all day, you laugh. The others make room as you pull your chair closer to the table.

When the café door opens again, you hear waves.
