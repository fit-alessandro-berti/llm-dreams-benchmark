…search for the one green place the survivors whispered about: a garden said to have survived beneath the cracked shell of a buried city.

At first, the road is nothing but ash and wind. The sky has the color of old bruises, and the ruins lean together like conspirators. Every shadow moves when you turn, and every abandoned window feels like an eye. But you keep going, guided only by a strange, humming dream-sense—something tugging behind your ribs toward a secret.

You enter the underground city through a collapsed subway mouth, its stairs half-swallowed by dust. Down there, the air changes. It tastes like wet metal and rain. Broken lights flicker on without reason, and the walls are lined with mirrors that reflect not your face, but moments from your life you thought you’d forgotten: laughter before the fall, a hand letting go, a door closing in a house you used to call home.

Then you see it.

Through a rusted archway glows a patch of impossible green.

The garden is small, sheltered beneath a dome of fractured glass. Vines climb the skeletons of old escalators, and flowers bloom in colors that don’t exist in daylight—silver, violet-black, blue that rings like a bell when the wind touches it. A spring runs from somewhere deep below, clean and singing, and beside it sits a single empty chair.

As you approach, the dream shifts around you. The ruins begin to breathe. The glass dome cracks without breaking. From the far end of the garden, a figure emerges wearing your own face—but older, wearier, holding a seed like a promise.

“You were never looking for a place to hide,” it says, its voice echoing in your teeth. “You were looking for a reason to grow again.”

You reach for the seed. It is warm. It pulses once, like a heart.

And the moment your fingers close around it, the garden begins to rise into the sky, roots dragging the buried city upward, stone and blossom and ruin spiraling together. You understand, suddenly, that the apocalypse was not an ending. It was a door left open.

You wake with the smell of rain on your skin, and in your palm, impossibly, a single green shoot curled like a question.
