...not written in ink at all, but formed by thousands of tiny black ants marching in neat, restless rows. Every time you squint to read a sentence, they scatter and rearrange themselves into a completely different alphabet. 

Suddenly, you realize you can’t actually remember what career this exam is for. Architecture? Deep-sea navigation? Cloud maintenance? It feels desperately important, yet entirely out of reach.

You look around the lecture hall for help, though the room is much larger now—stretching upwards into a dim, vaulted cathedral ceiling. None of the other students seem bothered. Everyone is scribbling furiously. The person to your left isn't even using a pen; they are calmly folding their test booklet into an intricate origami swan, nodding as if checking their math. 

At the front of the room, the proctor clears his throat. It’s your seventh-grade gym teacher, wearing a tuxedo, standing waist-deep in water that somehow isn't flooding the rest of the room. He taps a giant wall clock that has no numbers, only colors, and mouths the words, *Five minutes left.*

Panic surges in your chest. You look back down at the paper. The ants are gone, and Question 1 is finally legible: 

*1. Draw the exact sound of your childhood front door closing. Show your work.*

You raise your Number 2 pencil to try, realizing with creeping dread that the graphite tip has gone completely limp, bending like warm butter against the page. It leaves behind only a faint, glowing streak of yellow. 

Desperate to salvage a passing grade, you flip the page to look at Question 2. Instead of turning, the paper unfolds like an endless white bedsheet, billowing off the edges of your desk, covering your shoes, and swallowing the desks around you. The scratching of pencils turns into the sound of howling wind. 

You stand up, only to find the cathedral walls have vanished. You are standing alone in a vast, snowy field, holding a melted pencil, staring down at miles of blank white terrain waiting for an answer—just as a booming bell rings out from the sky, jolting you awake.
