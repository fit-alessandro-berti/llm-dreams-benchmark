As you explore your new surroundings, you realize you need to find the well at the edge of the village before the church bell rings three times.

You don’t know why you know this—only that the thought is planted deep in your chest, urgent and heavy, like a promise you made before waking.

The air smells of woodsmoke, damp earth, and fresh bread. The road beneath your feet is uneven, made of packed dirt and loose stones. Around you, people move in simple clothing: linen shirts, wool skirts, leather shoes worn thin by years of use. No phones. No engines. No electric lights. Only carts pulled by tired horses, chickens pecking near doorways, and the distant sound of someone hammering metal.

You begin walking.

A woman selling apples watches you pass, her eyes lingering too long on your face. A boy carrying a bundle of sticks points toward a narrow lane between two stone houses and says, “If you’re looking for the well, it’s that way—but don’t speak to the man without a shadow.”

You open your mouth to ask him what he means, but when you look again, he is gone.

The lane twists and bends, and the village seems to shift around you. Doors appear where there were none. Windows blink like sleepy eyes. The sky above is pale and endless, though the light feels uncertain, as if the sun cannot decide whether it is morning or evening.

At last, you reach the well.

It sits alone in a small circle of grass, its stones dark and slick. Beside it stands an old man wrapped in a cloak the color of ash. He has no shadow.

He holds out his hand.

“You brought it,” he says.

You look down. In your palm rests a small brass key, warm as though it has been held close for a very long time. You do not remember picking it up. You do not remember ever seeing it before, and yet you know it belongs to him.

The bell begins to ring.

One.

The old man smiles, but his expression is sad.

Two.

The key grows heavier.

Three.

The well begins to glow with a soft blue light, and the old man whispers, “Now you must choose: wake, or stay and remember why you came.”

And in that moment, you realize the dream is not finished with you yet.
