You grip the heavy wooden helm, only to feel it soften beneath your palms, transforming into the braided roots of an ancient tree. Your crew—whose faces keep shifting between people you knew in childhood and glowing, porcelain masks—wait in breathless silence. The roar of the tempest suddenly drops to a low, rhythmic hum, like the purr of a giant cat.

"Drop the anchors!" you shout, though your voice sounds like the chiming of distant brass bells. "Drop them into the sky!"

It makes complete sense in the logic of the dream. The crew doesn't question it. They heave massive iron anchors not overboard into the churning ink, but *upward* into the bruised-purple clouds. The chains rattle and defy gravity, shooting straight up until they catch on something solid above the storm. 

With a great lurch, the ship pulls taut against the sky. The towering waves freeze in place, instantly hardening into jagged mountains of black glass. 

Silence falls over the deck. The wind dies, replaced by the warm, sudden scent of cinnamon and old library books. You let go of the root-helm and walk to the edge of the ship. Looking over the railing, you see that the ocean has vanished entirely, revealing a vast, sunken ballroom miles below, lit by floating chandeliers. 

A single white door stands upright and frame-less on the glass mountain just beyond the bow. It swings open, spilling warm, golden sunlight onto the dark deck. You step off the ship onto the glass, turn back to salute your crew, and realize they have all turned into seagulls. As they take flight, the sound of their wings becomes the sound of rain against a bedroom window, and you slowly wake up.
