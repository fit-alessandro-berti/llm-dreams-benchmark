As you move deeper into the maze, you notice the puzzles are no longer merely external. They start to use your own memories as pieces: a riddle written in your childhood handwriting, a lock shaped like a decision you once refused to make. The deeper you go, the less logic helps. A door demands a password you do not know, but when you whisper your greatest fear, it swings open. A floor of black and white tiles rearranges itself into the face of someone you miss; the only way across is to say their name aloud.

You stop solving. You begin listening.

At the maze’s center, there is no exit, only a mirror. Your reflection is slightly ahead of you, pressing a hand to the glass. It mouths, *Stop looking for the way out.* You close your eyes. The walls fold like paper.

You wake with the scent of stone still in your breath and the distinct feeling that you have left something important behind.
