

…each group seems to know something about the person you’re looking for, but none of them will say it directly.

One cluster of guests is laughing over a photograph, and when you glance at it, the picture shows the person you know—but in a place you’ve never seen them, wearing clothes they don’t own. As you reach for it, the photograph gently folds itself shut like a book.

Near the window, another group is playing a quiet game where everyone must whisper a name into a glass. When your turn comes, you open your mouth, and instead of saying their name, you hear your own voice echo through the room from somewhere far away.

You move through the crowd, but the party keeps changing shape. Doors open into hallways that weren’t there before. A staircase curves upward like a ribbon. Someone hands you a cup that contains not a drink, but a tiny floating light, and when you look into it, you see the face of the person you’re searching for.

Finally, you spot them across the room. They are standing in a circle of people, smiling as though they’ve been waiting for you all along. You push through the crowd, but just as you reach them, they turn—and for a moment, their face is every face you’ve seen tonight.

Then it becomes their face again.

They lean close and say, “You weren’t looking for me. You were looking for the part of yourself that knows how to belong here.”

As soon as they say it, the noise of the gathering softens into music. The strangers around you no longer feel unfamiliar. The room feels warmer, larger, somehow safer. You take a breath, and when you exhale, the party begins to dissolve—not frighteningly, but like fog lifting at sunrise.

You wake with the strange, calm feeling that you were found while you were searching.
