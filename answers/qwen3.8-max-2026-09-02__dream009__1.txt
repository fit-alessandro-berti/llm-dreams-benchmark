You wake up in a completely different era, with no modern technology around. As you explore your new surroundings, you realize you need to find water before nightfall.

The air smells of woodsmoke and damp earth. Your shoes feel too thin for the rough path beneath your feet, and every sound—birdsong, creaking carts, distant hammering—seems louder without the hum of machines underneath it.

You come upon a small village where people wear rough-spoken clothes and eye you with suspicion. A child points at you and whispers something you almost understand. An old woman by a well studies you for a long moment, then gestures toward a clay cup.

As you drink, you notice something strange: the water reflects not your face, but a version of you dressed for this time, calm and weathered, as if you have always belonged here.

When you look up, the old woman smiles and says, “You’re late. We’ve been waiting for you.”
