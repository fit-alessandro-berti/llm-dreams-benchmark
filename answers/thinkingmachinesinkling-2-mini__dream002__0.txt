A sound rolls over the ridge—not thunder exactly, but a low, held breath. In the distance the forest opens into a shimmering hollow where the path doesn’t lead deeper in, but upward into a pale ring of light. You don’t run. Instead, every ache in your joints straightens, and the storm resolves into a single, swirling aurora stretched across the sky like a curtain being drawn back. When the light passes over you, it feels less like weather and more like an answer: a marker, a threshold, a dream folding itself neatly into the next word. You blink—only to find the forest still peaceful, the air clear and warm, as if the storm had never really come—only the sensation of having crossed from one world into another.
