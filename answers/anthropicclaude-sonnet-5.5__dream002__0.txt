You start to run, but the running feels strange, slow and buoyant, like moving through warm water. The path ahead bends in ways paths shouldn't, curling back on itself. Thunder rolls, and you realize it isn't coming from the sky. It's coming from the ground, a deep, rhythmic pulse like an enormous heartbeat.

The first raindrops fall, and they don't splash. They land on the moss and bloom into tiny glowing flowers, blue and violet, that flicker for a moment and then dissolve. Soon the whole forest floor is lit with them, a galaxy sprouting at your feet.

Lightning splits the sky, but instead of a flash of white, it spills gold, and in that light you see a door standing alone between two ancient oaks. It is weathered and green-painted, with a brass knob worn smooth by hands that came before you. There is no house around it, only the door, slightly ajar, with warm lamplight leaking out.

The storm surges behind you, and the wind pushes at your back almost gently, like a friend urging you forward.

You take hold of the knob. It's warm, and somehow you know, the way you know things in dreams, that someone on the other side has been waiting for you to arrive.

You push the door open, and the sound of the storm falls away into a hush. You smell bread and woodsmoke, and you hear a familiar voice say your name.

…and that is where you step through.
