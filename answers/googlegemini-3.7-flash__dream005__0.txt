...and realize the room has no permanent walls, only heavy velvet curtains that part endlessly into wider, brighter halls. 

You weave through a circle of guests laughing in perfect, uncanny unison. When you lean in to hear what’s so funny, their voices sound like water trickling over smooth stones. One of them turns to you, offering a champagne flute filled not with liquid, but with a swirling, starry night sky. The bubbles pop with faint, chime-like sounds. You politely shake your head and keep moving.

Further in, the floor beneath your feet softens from polished hardwood to a carpet of cool, dewy moss. You pass a banquet table where people are quietly peeling glowing clementines, their hushed conversation drifting up like incense. Every person looks vaguely familiar—like someone you sat next to on a train ten years ago, or a face you glanced at in a photograph—yet none of them are the one you're looking for.

Then, across a low-lit dance floor where couples sway in slow, weightless circles, you spot them. The one person you know. They are standing near a set of tall French doors, looking out into the dark.

You call their name, but the word dissolves in your throat, turning into the toll of a distant bell. You push through the crowd, your legs suddenly feeling heavy, as if you are wading through warm honey. 

Just as the space between you threatens to stretch infinitely wider, you reach out and touch their shoulder. 

They turn around, their face warm with immediate relief. They don’t speak with words, but you understand them perfectly: *"I thought you'd never find the way out."* 

They push the French doors open, and together you step out of the loud, labyrinthine party into a completely silent courtyard, where gravity lets go, and you both begin to slowly float upward toward a sky full of quiet, impossible colors.
