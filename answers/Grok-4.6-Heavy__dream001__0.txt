You start to walk, then run, then walk again, because running never covers the right amount of ground here. The pavement is warm, as if the city has a fever. Street names slide off the corners of buildings when you look at them. In your pocket a folded note keeps rewriting the meeting: an interview, a reunion, an apology, a trial. You are wearing two different shoes.

You ask a woman in a rust-colored coat. She smiles as though she knows you and points down an alley that wasn’t there a second ago. At the far end a glass tower holds a clock that reads both 11:03 and something that isn’t a time at all. Inside, the lobby directory lists departments that do not exist: Lost Hours, Unfinished Conversations, Things You Meant to Bring. The elevator buttons go 1, 2, 4, 4, 4. When the doors open you are back on the street, only now it is dusk and every café on every corner is the same café.

In a shop window your reflection is already seated at a long table, waiting. A tram bell rings in a language you almost remember. Someone calls your name from a high window. You look up. You start to answer—
