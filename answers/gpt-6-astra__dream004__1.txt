a paintbrush hovering just beyond your reach. Each time you stretch toward it, your hand becomes something else: a feather, a glass branch, a small school of silver fish.

At last, you stop reaching.

The brush settles into your palm.

You dip it into the air, which is cool and thick with an unfamiliar blue, and paint a door. Before you can add a handle, someone knocks from the other side.

“Come in,” you say.

“The hinges are on your side,” replies a voice you almost recognize.

You paint two hinges. The door swings open onto a kitchen where rain is falling upward into a crack in the ceiling. At the table sits a child wearing your oldest shoes.

“You’re late,” the child says, pushing a cup toward an empty chair.

Inside the cup, a tiny sea rolls against the porcelain. A boat is approaching its rim. You lean closer and see someone on deck, waving both arms.

The child puts a finger to their lips.

You recognize the boat. You drew it in the corner of a schoolbook and never finished its sail.

The paintbrush is still in your hand.

Outside, the landscape folds itself quietly into an envelope. You have time, you think, to finish this one thing.
