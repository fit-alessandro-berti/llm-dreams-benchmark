The word you were about to say hangs in the air between you — literally. It's small and glassy, like a marble, and it drops to the kitchen floor with a soft *click* and rolls under the table.

Your friend, Alex, stares at it. Then they look back at you, and all the anger drains out of their face, replaced by something tired and sad.

"I don't actually think you don't care," they say quietly. "I'm just scared you're going to leave and forget me."

That's what this has been about the whole time. Not the move. Not the new job in another city, or who said what last month. You're arguing because a hallway is growing between you.

And in the dream, it really is. Every time one of you raises your voice, the kitchen stretches. The fridge slides further away. The table gets longer. By now you're shouting across what feels like a train station, your voices echoing.

So you decide to stop shouting, and start walking.

You walk toward Alex past seasons. First it's summer and the floor is warm linoleum, then autumn and dead leaves are blowing through the kitchen cabinets, then it's winter and your breath fogs. Your legs ache like you've been walking for years, because in dream-time, you have.

Alex is walking toward you too.

When you finally meet in the middle, neither of you can remember the exact words that started the fight. You're both out of breath. You just sit down, right there on the floor, which is suddenly the cracked pavement of the street you grew up on.

Alex pulls two oranges out of their pocket — impossibly, perfectly round — and hands you one. You peel them like you did when you were kids, and the smell is so bright and real it stings your eyes.

"I'm not going anywhere that matters," you tell them.

"You'd better not," they say, and smile for the first time.

The hallway shrinks. The kitchen comes back around you, warm and normal and close.

Then the alarm on your phone pulls you up out of it, and you're awake in your dark room with the taste of orange still on your tongue, already reaching to text Alex before you forget how important that felt.
