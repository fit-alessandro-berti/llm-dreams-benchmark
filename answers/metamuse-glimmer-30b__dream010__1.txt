You are participating in a competition where the grand prize is your dream job. As the competition progresses, you face various challenges and every single one of them is being judged by a version of yourself.

First it's a simple obstacle course in an office lobby that keeps stretching. The cubicles become a maze, the carpet is sticky like warm taffy, and your shoes are suddenly filled with sand. You have to present a 5-minute pitch, but the microphone only works when you whisper, and the judges are asleep in beanbag chairs.

Then the challenges get more personal. You're asked to solve a spreadsheet while someone keeps changing the numbers every time you blink. You're asked to shake hands with the interviewer, but their hand keeps turning into a fish, then a doorknob, then your own hand.

The other contestants start fading out one by one, not because they're eliminated, but because they realize they forgot why they wanted the job in the first place and just walk out the back door into a parking lot that smells like childhood.

The final challenge is just a desk. Your dream desk. It's in a sunlit room with the exact view you always imagined. You're told to sit down and start working. You sit. The computer is on. There's one document open: a blank resume with your name at the top.

You start typing, and as soon as you do, the room starts to hum louder and louder until the walls peel back like paper and you realize the whole competition was being held inside a giant alarm clock.

You wake up just as the alarm rings, and for a second you’re not sure if you won the job or if you finally remembered what you actually wanted to do.
