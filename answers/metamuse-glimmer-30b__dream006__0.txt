You begin to open your mouth and nothing comes out. At first you think it's just nerves, you clear your throat, you smile and say “Good evening” — but the sound comes out thin and metallic, like a recording played too fast.

The crowd is still looking expectantly, but now they're too close. The front row is right at the edge of the stage, and you can see their faces individually: your third grade teacher, your dentist, your ex, people you don't know. They all lean forward at once.

You try again. Your notes are in your hand, but the pages are blank. Or worse, they're covered in your handwriting, but the words keep sliding off the page and pooling at your feet. You look down and realize your shoes are gone, and your pants are gone, and you're standing there in just your boxers under the hot lights.

You laugh to cover it, and the laugh echoes wrong in the hall, bouncing back a half-second late. The audience doesn't laugh with you. They just keep staring. The stage is suddenly much higher than it was, and the floor is tilting. You take a step forward to steady yourself and the edge crumbles.

You wake up before you fall.
