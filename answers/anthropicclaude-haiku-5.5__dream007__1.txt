...you decide to stop talking and simply walk away, but the street you step onto isn't the street you know. The pavement is soft and faintly warm, like sun-baked sand, and the buildings lean in over you, their windows glowing with the same amber light as a kitchen at dusk.

Your friend calls after you, and their voice comes from everywhere at once, from the lampposts and the drains and the sky. You turn around to answer, but they're no longer behind you. They're standing at the far end of a long, shallow pool that has appeared in the middle of the road, its surface perfectly still and reflecting a sky full of slow-moving clocks.

"Come look," they say, and they're holding something out to you. It's a folded piece of paper, and when you open it, the words written there are the exact argument you were having, but rewritten. Every sharp thing either of you said has been replaced with something true and gentle, something you both secretly meant.

You wade into the pool. It's warm, and it doesn't feel like water so much as light. The clocks overhead begin to slow, then stop, and for a moment the whole city holds its breath with you. Your friend smiles, a little sheepishly, the way they did when you were children and got lost in the woods together and found your way back laughing.

"I think we were arguing about the wrong thing," they say.

And you realize, with the strange certainty dreams allow, that they're right, though you can't quite remember what the right thing was. You only know that the paper in your hand is getting warmer, and that you're about to find out.
