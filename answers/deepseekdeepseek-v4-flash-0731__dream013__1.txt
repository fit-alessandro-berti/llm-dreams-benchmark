...you begin to notice that the puzzles are not random. The symbols on the walls, the patterns in the floor, the whispered numbers between the ticking of hidden clocks — they all point inward.

You solve a door of shifting mirrors only to find yourself standing in a room you’ve seen before. The same chalk marks. The same half-finished equation. The same feeling that you’ve been here longer than you remember.

Then you understand: the maze isn’t trying to keep you out. It’s trying to keep you in.

The deeper you go, the more the puzzles feel like memories you’ve locked away. A bridge of glass that only appears when you stop thinking. A stone door that opens when you forgive someone who isn’t there. A chasm you cross by letting go of something you never needed.

At the center, there is no treasure. There is only a small mirror on a pedestal, warm to the touch.

You look into it.

The face looking back is not scared. It is patient. It has been waiting for you to stop running.

You realize the maze was never a prison. It was a question.

And you — finally, calmly — answer it.

Then you wake up.
