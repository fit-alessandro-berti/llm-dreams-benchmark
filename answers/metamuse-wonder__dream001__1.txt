In your left pocket there is a warm coin that wasn't there a second ago. In your right, a crumpled piece of paper. You unfold it with shaking hands.

It says, in your own handwriting:

**MEETING — VERY IMPORTANT — DO NOT BE LATE**

Underneath, where the address should be, there is only a drawing of a door.

You look up and the city has changed while you weren't looking. It is vast and beautiful and completely wrong. The buildings are too tall, leaning toward each other like they're sharing secrets. None of them have doors. The street signs are written in an alphabet that rearranges itself every time you blink.

A clock on a tower chimes. You have an hour. Then another clock chimes somewhere else, with a different time. Then every clock in the city chimes at once, not in unison, but in a long rolling wave.

You start asking people.

"Excuse me, do you know where the meeting is?"

A woman walking a dog made of fog nods seriously. "Of course," she says. "It's where it always is. Just follow everyone else."

You turn. Suddenly the street is full of people, all of them walking purposefully in the same direction, all of them dressed for an important meeting. Some carry briefcases. Some carry bouquets. One man carries a large fish under his arm like a folder. They all look a little like you, if you squint — same walk, same worried expression around the eyes.

You join the crowd.

You run up stairs that take you down into a subway station full of sunlight. You cross a bridge over a river that flows upward. You pass through a library where librarians are shelving bottled voices, and through a market where merchants sell minutes in little glass jars — five minutes, ten minutes, an hour, corked and glowing.

"An hour!" you say, reaching for one.

The merchant shakes his head and points ahead. "You already spent yours getting here."

Up ahead, the crowd is thinning. People are peeling off one by one through doors that appear in walls only for them, disappearing with sighs of relief, as if they've finally remembered.

Your door hasn't appeared yet.

Panic rises, familiar and dream-heavy. Your legs feel like they're moving through water. The tower clock chimes again — you have ten minutes, you have five, you have an hour again, all at the same time.

Then you see it. At the end of a quiet street that feels like a held breath, there is a small café with one table outside. On the table are two cups of tea, still steaming. And in the café window, painted in gold letters, is that same drawing from your paper — a door.

You push it open. A little bell rings.

Someone is already sitting inside, waiting for you.

It's you. Older, maybe. Or younger. It's hard to tell — their face keeps settling into versions of yours that you recognize from photographs and mirrors and days you barely remember. They look up, not surprised at all, and smile like they've been patient for a very long time.

"You're early," they say.

"But the meeting—" you stammer. "I didn't know where it was. I didn't even know what it's about."

"You always say that." They slide the second cup of tea toward you, and unfold a fresh piece of paper on the table between you. "Shall we begin?"

You sit down. Your heart is still racing, but the frantic feeling is draining away, replaced by something stranger: the feeling that this is the part you've been rushing toward your whole life without knowing it.

You look down at the paper to finally read what the meeting is for —

—and wake up to your alarm clock ringing, the words dissolving as you open your eyes, with the unshakable feeling that you have somewhere very important to be in an hour.
