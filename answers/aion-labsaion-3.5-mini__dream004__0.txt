...a brush lying beside it, made of a single feather from a bird I've never seen but somehow remember.

I pick it up. The feather is warm, like it was just held by someone else. When I touch it to the canvas, no color comes out — instead, the canvas drinks from me. A swirl of yesterday appears: my childhood kitchen, but the walls are made of flowing water, and my grandmother's voice hums from the ceiling in a language I don't speak yet somehow understand.

I paint a door. The door appears behind me, in the shifting landscape, standing alone on a hill of soft clocks that bend like taffy. I don't remember painting it there, but the dream insists it was always there.

The colors around me begin hurrying faster, as if jealous of the canvas. The purple sky folds into origami birds that fly backward. Mountains ripple like breath. I realize the whole world is the painting — that the blank canvas was the only real thing here, the eye through which everything else is dreamed.

I try to paint myself, but the brush trembles. Every time I begin, the face comes out different: sometimes old, sometimes a child, sometimes made of starlight and static. The canvas seems to be telling me something. Or asking.

Then the feather dissolves into my hand, and the canvas begins painting me instead — slowly, stroke by stroke — and I feel each brushstroke as a gentle touch on my skin. The border between painter and painted softens.

Just before the final stroke, I hear footsteps behind me. I turn —

and the dream folds shut like a letter, and I wake with the faint smell of paint in the air and the certainty that somewhere, someone is still finishing the picture.
