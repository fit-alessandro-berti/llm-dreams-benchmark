You wake up in a completely different era, with no modern technology around. As you explore your new surroundings, you realize you need to find water—and quickly. Your throat is dry, and the air smells of woodsmoke and hay.

You're standing at the edge of a medieval village. Thatched roofs sag under the weight of morning mist, and chickens scatter as you pass. Your clothes feel strange against your skin—coarse wool, nothing like what you fell asleep in. Somewhere, a blacksmith's hammer rings out in steady rhythm.

An old woman notices you wandering and squints at you with suspicion. You realize you need to come up with a story fast. "I'm... a traveler," you manage, and the words come out in a language you somehow understand, though you've never studied it. Dream-logic, you think. That's how you know this isn't real.

She points down a muddy path toward a well. As you drink from the bucket she lowers, cold water dripping down your chin, you notice the details that dreams get *right*—the grit of sand between your teeth, the ache in your feet, the way the sunlight catches dust motes drifting between the timber-framed houses.

A church bell tolls, and the villagers begin moving with purpose toward the square. You follow, drawn by curiosity. A herald is reading a proclamation, and when you hear the year spoken aloud, your stomach drops. It's not just a different era. It's centuries before your time.

You realize you need to decide: do you try to find a way back, or do you let yourself live fully inside this impossible world? In the dream, you feel strangely calm. You watch a group of children chase a dog through the square, laughing, and you think—*people are people, in any century.*

The old woman tugs your sleeve and offers you bread. You take it.

And just as you bite into it—warm, real, impossibly real—you feel that familiar pull, like a tide going out. The village blurs at the edges. The bell's toll stretches long and low, becoming your alarm clock, dragging you back.

You wake up with the taste of bread still on your tongue.
