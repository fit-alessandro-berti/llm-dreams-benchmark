...a brush appears in your hand. You never picked it up, but that's fine — dreams rarely explain themselves.

You notice the canvas is the only thing in this world that holds still. Everything else churns: the sky rippling violet to green, hills folding themselves into spirals, a river overhead deciding whether to be water or birds. But the canvas stays white. Patient. Waiting.

You dip the brush into the air itself, and it comes away wet with a color you've never seen — not quite gold, not quite the feeling of being seven years old. When you drag it across the canvas in a single horizontal line, the whole landscape obeys. Suddenly there's a horizon where none was. The churning sky settles above it. The spiral hills unspool and lie down flat.

So you paint a door. It comes out crooked, and the color keeps trying to become a sound instead — a low, warm hum, like a refrigerator in a house you used to live in — but when you press your palm against the canvas, it gives. Soft at first, like fabric. Then open. Then somewhere else.

On the other side: the same landscape, except everything has stopped changing. Fixed. Vivid. Still. And in the middle of it stands a figure holding a brush, facing a blank canvas, looking back at the exact spot you just came from. They have your posture. Your hands.

You understand, the way you understand things in dreams — instantly, without evidence — that the canvas was never blank. It was a window. The dream on one side, the painter on the other, and you the brushstroke between them.

Then the sky remembers it's supposed to be moving, the stillness dissolves, and the door paints itself shut behind you—

—and you wake, carrying the faint smell of a color you can't name, and the quiet certainty that you left something unfinished there.
