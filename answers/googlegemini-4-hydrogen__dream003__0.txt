There is still no keyhole, only a tiny, crescent-shaped indentation on the front clasp. Without thinking, you hum a note—a tune you haven't heard since childhood—and the box vibrates in response. With a sound like breaking ice, the seams along the lid split open.

You lift the top, expecting velvet or tarnished brass, but there is no bottom to the box at all. Looking inside is like peering down from a great height into a midnight ocean, lit from below by drifting paper lanterns. A cool breeze smelling of rain and cedar rises from the opening, carrying a single folded note that floats up and settles into your palm. 

Before you can unfold it, the lanterns begin to chime like distant alarm bells, and the heavy box dissolves into warm sand, slipping quietly through your fingers as the courtyard fades to light.
