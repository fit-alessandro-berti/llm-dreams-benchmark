Reach for your keyboard—but your desk has grown three feet wider while you weren't looking. You roll your chair forward, yet the wheels spin uselessly, carrying you backward, faster and faster, past the break room, past the elevator, through a door you've never noticed before.

Inside is another office. Somehow yours—but wrong. Your monitor glows with the finished project. Relief floods through you... until you see that every paragraph is written backwards, or possibly upside down, or possibly in Swedish.

Your boss materializes at your shoulder. He doesn't speak. He just points at the clock, which reads 3:00—no, 3:59—and the numbers are melting off the face like candle wax, dripping slowly down the wall.

"I can still get this done," you begin to say, but your words come out as printouts, sliding from your mouth and piling on the carpet, and now you'd have to pick them up and read them to know what you meant, and the little printer inside your chest is jamming.

You lunge back toward the desk (how did you return to the original office?) and type frantically. The keys rearrange under your fingers—the A sliding to where the S was, the entire layout reshuffling like a card trick. A coworker you've never met hands you coffee. The cup is empty but warm. Somewhere behind you, someone feeds page after page into a shredder, the sound growing louder and louder until—

You wake up.

Ceiling. Sheets. The fan spinning at its normal, honest speed. Your heart pounds for a second before your brain catches up: *just a dream.* There's no impossible deadline. You exhale and almost laugh at yourself.

Then your eyes drift to the nightstand.

A sticky note sits there, written in your handwriting:

*Due Friday.*

You have no memory of writing it. And tomorrow is Friday.
