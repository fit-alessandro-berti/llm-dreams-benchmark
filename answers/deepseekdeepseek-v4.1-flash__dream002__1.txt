…and you realize the storm is not coming from the sky—it is coming from behind your eyes. The wind rises in whispers, then in voices, until every leaf is trembling with secrets. The path beneath you softens into moss, then water, then a river running upward through the trees. Rain falls in silver spirals, and each drop holds a tiny reflection of a door you have seen before.

You walk toward the thunder, though it sounds less like thunder now and more like a heartbeat. At the center of the forest, an ancient oak splits open with warm light. Inside stands someone who looks like you, only younger, holding a lantern made of lightning.

“You’re late,” they say, and place the storm in your hands.

It is small and wild and purring. You cradle it against your chest, and the forest exhales. The sky clears. The trees bow. And just as you understand that the storm was never something to survive—it was something to carry—you wake with the smell of rain, moss, and possibility still clinging to your skin.
