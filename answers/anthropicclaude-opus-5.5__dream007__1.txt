...stop talking.

Not in anger. You just close your mouth mid-sentence, and the words you were about to say drift out anyway, as small paper birds. They flutter between you and your friend, circling like confused moths.

Your friend stops too. Their words have been escaping the same way, only theirs are folded from pages of an old notebook you both used to pass back and forth in school. You recognize the handwriting. Some of the birds are yours, some are theirs, and in the air it's impossible to tell whose are whose.

You realize you can't remember what the argument was about. You know it mattered. You can feel the importance of it like a stone in your pocket, but when you reach in, the stone is warm and smooth, like a river stone, and you don't know why you were holding it so tightly.

The room you're in shifts. The walls were your kitchen a moment ago, but now they're drifting apart like the sides of a boat, and you're both standing on a wooden dock at dusk. The water below is perfectly still and reflects a sky neither of you has ever seen, violet with three pale moons.

"I don't want to win," your friend says, and their voice sounds like it's coming from far away and right next to you at once.

"Me neither," you say, and it's true, and saying it feels like setting down something heavy you didn't know you were carrying.

One of the paper birds lands on the water. It doesn't sink. It unfolds slowly, and you both kneel to read it. It says, in both of your handwriting at once:

*We were scared of the same thing.*

You look at each other. You don't need to explain what the thing is. In the dream, you both simply know. It's the fear of losing something, of being misunderstood by the one person you thought would always understand.

The rest of the birds settle on the water one by one, unfolding into small lanterns that float out toward the horizon. Your friend sits down on the edge of the dock and lets their feet hang over the water. After a moment, you sit beside them.

Neither of you says anything for a long time. The argument is still there somewhere, waiting, and you know that when you wake up, it might still need to be talked through. But it feels different now, less like a wall between you and more like a river you'll cross together.

Your friend bumps your shoulder with theirs, the way they used to when you were younger.

And just before you wake, you hear them laugh, and you realize you're laughing too.
