...you notice intricate patterns etched into its surface, swirling lines that seem to shift whenever you look away. The moment you blink, they rearrange themselves, as if the box is shy.

You trace one spiral with your fingertip. It's warm, like a stone left in the sun, and it hums faintly. The hum has a rhythm, and you realize it matches your own heartbeat, or perhaps your heartbeat has begun to match it.

The room around you dissolves without any fuss. The walls melt into a twilight forest, and the floor becomes soft moss. Fireflies drift past, each one flickering in the same rhythm as the box. Somewhere far away, a clock tolls thirteen times.

Then you notice there is no keyhole. There never was. Instead, near the lid, is a small, smooth hollow in the shape of a thumbprint, your thumbprint, though you haven't touched it yet. You know this the way you know things in dreams: completely, and without evidence.

You press your thumb into the hollow.

The patterns flare silver, then go still. The lid doesn't open so much as *exhale*, and out drifts a fragrance: your grandmother's kitchen, rain on hot pavement, a song you forgot you loved. Inside, resting on velvet the color of midnight, is a single brass key.

You frown. *A key? But the box is already open.*

Turning it over, you find a tiny engraving: **"For the door you've been avoiding."**

Behind you, among the trees, a door now stands alone in the forest. It's ordinary and a little weathered, with light leaking from beneath it. The fireflies gather around its frame, waiting.

You close your fingers around the key. It's warm too, and it hums in time with you.

As you take your first step toward the door, the forest begins to fade at the edges, and you feel the pull of waking, soft as a hand on your shoulder. You have just enough time to reach out and turn the handle...

...and the dream folds itself shut, leaving only the warmth of the key in your palm and the certainty that you'll know which door it was when you meet it in daylight.
