As you try to find them, you encounter various groups of people, each certain they know you.

One group is gathered around a long table, laughing over photographs of a house you’ve never seen. Another is dancing in a room where the music seems to come from beneath the floor. They greet you by name, though none of them can tell you how they know it. You ask if they’ve seen your friend, and each group points in a different direction.

At last, you step through a narrow doorway and find yourself outside. The gathering is happening in the middle of a quiet beach, under a sky full of enormous, slowly drifting lanterns. You recognize your friend at the water’s edge. They turn toward you, but before you can reach them, the tide comes in—not water, but a crowd of silent guests, flowing between you.

You try to call out. No sound comes. Your friend smiles as if they’ve heard you anyway, then lifts a hand in farewell.

A stranger beside you offers you a cup of tea. You accept it, and the room around you becomes a kitchen you remember from childhood. The party is gone. The tea is warm. Somewhere in the house, someone is calling your name.
