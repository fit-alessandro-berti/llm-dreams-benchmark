...the light turns the color of old brass. The birdsong stops all at once, as if someone lifted a needle from a record.

You look up. The clouds aren't rolling in, they're *unfolding*, like dark paper being opened by enormous hands. The first raindrops fall upward, rising from the moss at your feet and drifting past your face toward the sky. Each one carries a tiny reflection of somewhere you've been before: a kitchen window, a schoolyard, a road at night.

The wind arrives next, and it knows your name. It doesn't say it so much as breathe it through the leaves, and the trees begin leaning toward one another, weaving their branches into an archway ahead of you. You understand, the way you only understand things in dreams, that you're meant to walk through it.

Thunder sounds, but it's slow and deep, more like a great door closing somewhere far beneath the earth. You aren't afraid. Your feet are already moving.

Beyond the arch, the forest opens into a clearing you somehow recognize. In the center stands a small wooden house with one lit window, and the storm bends around it, leaving a perfect circle of stillness. The rain here hangs motionless in the air like beaded glass. You brush through it and it chimes.

The door of the house is already open. Inside, a table is set for two, a kettle is just beginning to sing, and someone's coat, still wet, hangs on the back of a chair.

Then you notice it's your coat.

From the other room, a voice you almost know says, "You're early this time."

Lightning flashes, and for one bright second you can see the whole forest through the walls, every tree and every raised drop of rain. As you turn toward the voice...

*Shall I keep the dream going, or would you like to decide who's in the other room?*
