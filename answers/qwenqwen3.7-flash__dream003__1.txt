They drift across the surface in slow, tidal arcs, rearranging themselves into symbols that feel familiar yet untranslatable: a crescent moon folding into a leaf, a spiral becoming a fingerprint, a knot loosening into a doorframe. When you tilt the box toward the dim light, the patterns pulse once, softly, as if taking a breath.

You run your fingertips along the seams. The wood is neither cold nor warm. It feels like attention.

There is no keyhole. There is only a smooth, unbroken curve where the lid meets the base. Frustration pricks at your chest, but dream logic doesn't punish; it redirects. You sigh, and the sound seems to linger in the air too long, warping into a low harmonic hum. The patterns react. They slide backward, unwinding like a film played in reverse, until they settle into a single, clear image: an open palm facing upward.

You hold yours up. Your reflection appears in the polished surface of the box, though you're alone in this room. In the reflection, you're younger. And older. All three versions of you turn their hands over simultaneously. Where your skin meets the imagined seam, a sound like distant wind chimes rings out. The box doesn't click. It exhales.

The lid lifts without rising. It unfolds inward like origami, revealing not compartments or treasure, but depth. An impossible well of twilight stretches down, thick with floating embers that drift upward instead of falling. Among them, shapes coalesce: a half-read letter written in your handwriting, a train ticket stamped with a date that hasn't happened yet, a child's drawing of a house with no walls, and your own name, spelled in a language you suddenly understand without knowing how.

One of the embers detaches from the rest. It sails toward you, cool and weightless, and rests on your tongue. Taste of salt, cedar, and something like first snow. Memory floods back, not as scenes, but as sensations: being lost and found, letting go, waiting without fear. You understand now. The box wasn't hidden. It was folded into you. You were just finally quiet enough to notice.

As the realization settles, the edges of the room begin to soften. The walls melt into watercolor sky. The box grows lighter, then translucent, then simply... absent. You stand in an empty meadow beneath a crescent moon that looks suspiciously like the shape you traced on the lid.

A voice, yours but layered with others, whispers: *It's not about opening. It's about remembering how to hold it.*

You close your eyes. When you open them, you're lying in your bed. Morning light filters through the blinds. Dust floats in the air. On your nightstand, beside your alarm clock, rests a single smooth stone you don't remember picking up. It's warm. And tucked beneath it, written in faint graphite on the back:

*Still dreaming. Keep going.*
