…you open your mouth to say you don’t have enough time, but instead of words, a small paper bird flies out.

It lands on your boss’s shoulder, unfolds itself, and becomes the project proposal — already finished.

Your boss nods as if this is completely normal.

The deadline on the wall begins to melt like a clock made of warm wax, dripping seconds onto the floor. Each drop becomes a tiny version of you, rushing to a different task. You realize you are not stressed. You are watching yourself from above, floating near the ceiling lights, and one of your selves looks up and whispers:

“You don’t have to do it all at once.”

The office turns into water.

Everyone continues working calmly, typing underwater, papers drifting like fish. You swim toward your desk, where the impossible project is waiting. But when you touch it, it becomes a single door with your name on it.

Your boss appears beside you, no longer intimidating, and says gently:

“The deadline was never real. It was just your fear wearing a suit.”

You open the door.

Behind it is morning.

Sunlight. Quiet. A clean desk.

And you wake up — but for a moment, you can still feel the paper bird resting in your chest, calm and ready.
