You decide to stop trying to prove your point and let yourself simply listen. The words hanging between you slow down, becoming strange, visible things—tiny paper birds folded from old letters that flutter toward the floor and turn to ash. Your friend’s face changes in the dream: still them, but softened, frightened, younger. You realize the argument is no longer about the disagreement at all; it is about the fear of being abandoned by someone who knows how to make you feel exposed. You take a step toward them, and the floor beneath your feet becomes water, but it holds you anyway. When you finally speak, your voice is calmer than you expect: “I don’t want to win. I want us to stay friends.”

Your friend looks up. In the dream, their eyes are two windows into the same room where your friendship began. The heated air dissolves into sunlight, and you both stand in that first shared space, the argument behind you like a door closing softly. You reach for each other’s hands. The dream lifts, gently pulling you out, leaving only the faint warmth of understanding.
