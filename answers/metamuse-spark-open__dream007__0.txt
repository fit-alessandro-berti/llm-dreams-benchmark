Instead of trying to win, you do something you would never do awake:

You walk over to the sink and turn on the water. Except it's not water. It's light.

You cup your hands and offer it to your friend.

In the dream-logic way, they understand. They stop yelling. They walk over, confused but curious, and dip their hands in too. The buzzing cloud between you shrinks a little.

You both look out the kitchen window and the backyard isn't a backyard anymore — it's an enormous, calm ocean at sunset. The argument feels suddenly very small compared to that.

Your friend sighs and says, "I just didn't want you to leave me behind."

And you say, "I thought you were trying to leave *me* behind."

You both laugh, because awake, you'd never say it that simply.

You sit on the kitchen floor that is now also somehow sand, and just sit there together while the waves come up to your feet, not quite touching you. You don't solve the disagreement completely — dreams rarely tie things up in a neat bow — but the heat is gone. It feels understood.

Then you wake up.
