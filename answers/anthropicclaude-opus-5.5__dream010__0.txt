...obstacles that seem to rearrange themselves whenever you look away.

The first challenge takes place in a gymnasium that is also, somehow, your childhood kitchen. A judge with a clipboard made of toast asks you to alphabetize a pile of clouds. You don't know how to do this, but your hands do. You sort them by the shapes they make, a rabbit, a teapot, your grandmother's profile, and the judge nods gravely and writes something down. The toast crumbles a little.

The other contestants are hard to see clearly. One of them might be your third-grade teacher. Another is definitely you, but taller, and wearing a suit you've never owned. You try not to make eye contact with the taller you.

The second challenge is an interview. You sit across from a panel of three owls in reading glasses. They ask you questions in a language you've never learned, and you answer fluently. You're not sure what you're saying, but it feels important, something about how every door is secretly a window if you're patient enough. The middle owl weeps quietly and passes you a tissue with the number *7* written on it. You understand this is a very good score, or possibly a phone number.

Then the building floods. Not with water but with the smell of rain on hot pavement, so thick you have to swim through it. You realize with sudden panic that you haven't studied for the final challenge. You didn't even know there *was* a final challenge. You check your pockets and find only a brass key, a single piano note (it hums faintly when you hold it), and the tissue from the owls.

The final challenge takes place on a staircase that goes both up and down at the same time. At the top, or the bottom, is a small wooden desk beside a window overlooking a city made entirely of lit windows. This, you know without being told, is the dream job. You don't know exactly what it involves. Something about keeping the windows lit. Something about being the person people call when they've forgotten the word for the thing they need.

The taller you is already climbing. He's faster and has better shoes.

You start to run, but the stairs turn to sand, then to piano keys, and each step plays a note. You realize the notes are spelling something. You slow down to listen, even though the taller you is pulling ahead. It's a song your mother used to hum while doing dishes. You'd forgotten it completely until now.

You stop climbing. You just stand there, listening.

When the song ends, you look up, and you're at the desk. The taller you is gone, or maybe he was never separate from you at all. The owls are perched on the windowsill. The toast judge is there too, mostly crumbs now, still holding the clipboard.

"You were supposed to hurry," the toast judge says.

"I know," you say.

"You won anyway," says the middle owl. "The job was never about getting here first. It was about noticing the song."

You sit down at the desk. The brass key fits a drawer you hadn't seen. Inside is a stack of blank letters, each addressed to someone you've loved. You pick up a pen, and the first window in the city flickers on.

You start to write. *Dear—*

And then an alarm, the ordinary kind, bleats somewhere far above the city of windows. The desk goes soft at the edges. The owls hoot something that sounds like *see you tomorrow.*

You wake up with the melody still in your head, humming it before you've even opened your eyes, and for a few seconds you're certain you remember exactly who that first letter was for.
