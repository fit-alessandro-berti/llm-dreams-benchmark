...and every room is slightly out of joint: the music is a beat too slow, the candles lean toward you like they’re listening, and the people seem to know your name before you say it.

You pass a trio of dancers whose movements match the rhythm of your own breath. Then a circle of longtime friends deep in private laughter, their jokes blurring into each other like color under water. A line of quiet people holding identical tote bags, not waving, just nodding as if you’re expected. A small child in a silver jacket presses a hand-drawn map into your palm—the only landmark is a door, and the door is subtly open to a different gathering.

Then your person appears, not where you expected, but at the edge of the crowd—partly inside, partly not. There’s no fanfare. They look tired and oddly relieved, as if you were the only one who noticed them all along. “Are you looking for someone?” they ask, and when you nod, they say: “You’re in the part of the party where people are almost themselves. I was afraid you’d stay confused.”

You follow them toward the door, not the way you came. The floor becomes softer, the sounds thin, and the crowd dissolves into shapes that feel less like people than like echoes of conversations you’ve had with people missing from your life. Just before the threshold, your friend asks one last thing: “What do you want to keep?”

In the dream, the answer matters more than finding them—and the door opens not into another party, but into a quiet room where everyone looks familiar, until you realize you only needed the moment to remember that being known is enough.
