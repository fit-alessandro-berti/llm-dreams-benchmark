You start to run, though you don't know which direction.

The city is enormous and completely silent except for clocks. There is one on every corner, every building, every bus stop, and they all say the same thing: **59 minutes left.**

You pat your pockets for your phone. You find it, but the map app just shows a blue dot in the middle of a blank, white grid. The streets have names, but when you look up at the signs, the letters rearrange themselves into other words: *Deadline Avenue, Procrastination Square, You-Should-Have-Left-Earlier Road.*

You stop a woman holding an umbrella, even though it's sunny.

"Excuse me, I'm late for an important meeting, do you know where it is?"

"Of course," she says kindly. "Everyone is going there." She points left.

You turn left. A man in a suit points right. A child on a bicycle points straight up.

You start running again. Your shoes make no sound on the pavement. The buildings lean in, taller the faster you go, and the clocks, which hadn't moved at all, suddenly all jump at once: **12 minutes left.**

You find a folded piece of paper in your jacket that you don't remember putting there. It's an invitation, in your own handwriting:

*MEETING AT ONE HOUR FROM NOW. DON'T BE LATE. YOU KNOW THE ADDRESS.*

Underneath is an address you recognize just long enough to forget it again.

A taxi pulls up without you hailing it. The driver rolls down the window. He has your voice.

"Need a ride?" he asks.

"I'm going to be late," you say.

"You already are," he says, smiling. "That's why you're on time. Get in."

He drops you in front of a building with hundreds of doors and only one window. The clock above the entrance says **1 minute left.** You run inside.

There is an elevator with no numbers on the buttons. Instead, they are labeled: *Age 7, The First Day of School, The Interview, Yesterday, Tomorrow.* You press *Tomorrow* and the elevator goes sideways.

The doors open onto a long boardroom. A huge table stretches farther than the room should allow. And all around it, sitting perfectly still and looking at you with faint impatience, are you. Dozens of you. You at ten, you at twenty, you in pajamas, you in a suit, you looking tired, you looking hopeful.

At the head of the table is one empty chair. In front of it is a nameplate with your name on it, and a single sheet of paper that reads:

**AGENDA: Decide what happens next.**

The version of you closest to the door leans over and whispers, "We were waiting for you to wake up and tell us."

And just then, from very far away, an alarm begins to ring.
