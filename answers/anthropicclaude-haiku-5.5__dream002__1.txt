The wind rises all at once, cold and damp, shaking the tops of the pines until they hiss like a crowd of whispering voices. The light drains from the sky, turning the color of old pewter, and you realize the path you were following has vanished beneath a carpet of fallen leaves that swirl around your ankles like restless animals.

You break into a run, but the trees seem to lean in as you pass, their branches reaching for your hair and sleeves. Thunder rolls somewhere far off, and then it is closer, a deep drumbeat you feel in your chest rather than hear. Rain begins as a few fat drops that sting like cold coins, then becomes a sheet of silver that blurs the forest into smudges of green and gray.

Then, through the downpour, you notice a light, small and amber, flickering between the trunks. It doesn't waver the way lightning does. It stays steady, as if someone is holding it for you. You move toward it, your breath loud in your ears, and the storm seems to hesitate, the wind dropping for a heartbeat, as though the forest itself is waiting to see what you will do next.
