...and the walls begin to exhale.

You step into a circle where people are trading shadows for pocket watches. A woman with hair like spun copper offers you a teacup that holds not tea, but a quiet room you haven't entered yet. You nod, thank her, and keep moving. The floor is parquet that clicks like piano keys beneath your shoes, and every three steps the pitch shifts down a half-tone until the entire hall hums in minor seventh.

You pass a group playing chess with ice cubes that melt into constellations on the tablecloth. Someone catches your eye and mouths your name, but the syllables come out as sea gulls scattering over a balcony you can't see. You climb the stairs anyway. They grow narrower, then wider, then dissolve into a ramp covered in fallen program flyers whose headlines keep rewriting themselves: *Gala Night* → *Welcome Home* → *Don't Forget Your Coat*.

At the top, there's a lounge draped in velvet the color of bruised twilight. An orchestra tunes itself by humming the same chord backward and forward. You ask a flutist wrapped in scarves of faded ticket stubs if she's been here before. He smiles, taps his chest, and a small brass compass springs open. The needle spins wildly, then settles, pointing not north, but toward the stairwell you just came down.

You go back down. The banquet hall has rearranged itself. Tables are now long benches carved from driftwood. Glasses ripple with liquid that reflects not your face, but fragments: a door you locked years ago, a train platform at 4 a.m., a phone number you memorized and never dialed. You take a sip. It tastes like mint and old library books. Somewhere, a gramophone begins to spin, playing a melody you're certain you composed in another life.

Then you see her. Standing near the arched doorway, wearing the exact sweater from that winter you stopped calling. She hasn't aged. She hasn't moved. She's simply waiting at the threshold between two versions of the same evening.

You walk toward her. The crowd parts without looking. Your feet don't touch the floor so much as the air agrees to hold you up. When you're close enough to count the freckles along her collarbone, she reaches out and brushes a smudge of gold leaf from your cheek. It comes away clean, leaving no trace.

"You found me," she says, but the voice belongs to the room, to the draft under the door, to the distant click of a clock striking thirteen.

The gathering doesn't end so much as it loosens its grip. Chandeliers drip light like honey. Conversations fold into silence. The music unspools into a single thread you follow down a corridor that stretches into a tunnel of amber glass. At the end, there's only a window, open to a sky full of floating teacups and half-unfolded umbrellas.

You turn back. She's gone. In her place sits a wooden box on a stool. Inside is a single feather, a pressed forget-me-not, and a mirror fogged with breath. You wipe it clear. Your reflection is already smiling.

And then you notice you're no longer searching.

Somewhere far below, a bed springs creak awake. Morning light slips under the blinds. On your nightstand, a damp coaster waits. You blink, stretch, and realize the dream didn't end when you woke up. It just shifted rooms. You press your palm to the glass and whisper the name you've been carrying since the staircase began its slow descent.

It answers back in the sound of wind against the pane.
