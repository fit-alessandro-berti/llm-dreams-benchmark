Your boots crunch over glass and ash, and the sky above is a bruise of violet and gray, streaked with clouds that never seem to move. Around you, the broken spires of buildings twist upward like dead trees, wrapped in vines that shimmer with something too bright to be ordinary. You can hear the wind moving through the empty streets, but beneath it there is another sound: a low, rhythmic hum, as if the world itself is still trying to remember how to live.

You reach the edge of a collapsed bridge and see, far below, a river of silver water reflecting a second sky. On the far bank, there is a campfire, though no smoke rises from it. A single figure sits there, their face turned away, holding up a lantern filled not with flame but with soft white light. The hum grows louder as you step closer, and you realize it is coming from inside your chest.

Despite the danger, despite the emptiness, despite the way your hands tremble with something you cannot name, you decide to cross. The bridge groans beneath you, but it holds. When you reach the figure, they look up, and their face is your own, though older, calmer, and marked by scars you do not remember receiving.

They speak without moving their lips.

“You are the dream,” they say, “and the dream is finally beginning.”
