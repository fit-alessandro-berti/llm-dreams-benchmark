"Haul in the mainsail! Reef it tight!" Your voice barely carries through the thunder, but your crew moves like they've done this a thousand times—because in this dream, they have.

Lightning splits the sky, and for one frozen instant you see the wave coming: a wall of black water taller than the mast itself. There's no outrunning it. No steering around it. You turn the bow to face it head-on, because that's what captains do.

"Hold on to something!" you bellow. "All hands, brace!"

The ship climbs. And climbs. The world tilts until the stars are beneath you, and then—the drop. Your stomach floats free as the vessel plunges down the other side, spray exploding over the deck like cold confetti.

And then, strangely, the sea goes calm.

Not gradually—the way dreams work—one moment there's chaos, and the next, glassy water reflecting a sky full of unfamiliar constellations. The crew gathers at the rail beside you, breathing hard, staring at the stillness.

One of them, an old sailor with salt-white hair, turns to you and asks quietly: "Captain, where are we sailing to?"

You open your mouth to answer—and realize you don't know. You check the charts: blank. The compass: spinning lazily, pointing everywhere and nowhere.

But looking out at that impossible horizon, you feel something unexpected. Not fear. Relief. For the first time in the dream—or perhaps ever—the destination doesn't matter. The storm is behind you. The sea is yours.

"Anywhere," you hear yourself say. "We're sailing anywhere we choose."

Somewhere far off, a lighthouse begins to glow—and you wake up with the faint smell of salt air still in your nose, and the odd, lingering feeling that you left a very good crew waiting for morning.
