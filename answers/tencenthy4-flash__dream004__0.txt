When you touch it, the canvas drinks color from the sky: red drains from a flowering tree and becomes a ribbon around your wrist; squares soften into ponds; triangles ring like bells.

You paint one small circle. Instantly, every wandering shape in the landscape stops and turns toward it. The circle becomes a window, and behind it you see a quiet room where dreams are kept in glowing jars, each one filled with a different kind of weather.

A figure made of folded shadows hands you another brush and whispers, “Don’t choose a color. Let the world choose you.”

So you close your eyes and paint with the sound of your heartbeat. When you open them, the canvas is gone—and everything around you has begun to take the shape of a feeling you can’t name: soft, bright, and almost familiar.

Then you wake with a smear of impossible blue on your fingers.
