You are participating in a competition where the grand prize is your dream job. As the competition progresses, you face various challenges and…

...and each challenge feels strangely familiar, like a memory you haven’t lived yet.

The first challenge is a dark room filled with locked doors. You’re told to find the key, but the key is a question you’ve been afraid to ask. You stand still, and the question rises in your chest: *What do I actually want?* The moment you say it out loud, one of the doors swings open, and a warm light spills onto your face.

The next challenge is a bridge made of glass between two high cliffs. On the other side, you can see your future self—calm, smiling, wearing the clothes you always imagined wearing. But the bridge is fragile, and every step makes it crack. A voice whispers, “If you hesitate, it will break.” So you stop thinking and walk. Each step is terrifying, but you keep your eyes on your future self. And just before you reach the end, the bridge stops cracking. You cross.

The final challenge is simpler: you are seated at an empty desk, with a blank page in front of you. A clock ticks softly. The judge says, “Your dream job is not about what you can do. It’s about what you will do when no one is watching.” You pick up the pen. Without thinking, you write the first honest sentence that comes to mind. And then another. And another. The page fills with your truth.

When you look up, the room has changed. The judges are gone. The audience is gone. There is only a small golden ring on the desk—a key ring, but with no keys. On it, a tiny plaque reads: *The job is not the prize. The prize is the life you build while doing it.*

You hear laughter from behind. Your future self is standing there, still smiling, and hands you a cup of coffee. “Welcome,” she says. “This is not the job. This is the beginning.”

And then, in the dream, you realize you're not competing anymore. You’re already there. You’re home. The alarm doesn’t sound. Instead, the dream gently turns into the morning light, and for the first time, you wake up knowing exactly where you’re going.
