

You are about to take the final exam for a course that determines your future career. As you sit down to start the exam, you notice the questions are not written in ink, but in tiny moving shadows that rearrange themselves every time you blink.

The first question reads:

**“What have you been pretending not to know?”**

You look around the room, expecting others to be confused too, but everyone else is writing calmly, their pens scratching like insect legs. The proctor stands at the front, wearing your old childhood coat, and when you try to see their face, it keeps changing: your mother, your teacher, yourself, someone you have not met yet.

You look back at the paper. The second question has changed.

**“Which fear belongs to you, and which one did you borrow?”**

Your pen feels heavier than it should. You begin to write, and instead of answering in words, memories spill onto the page: a door you once refused to open, a goodbye you never finished, a dream you buried under practical plans. Each answer glows faintly, then sinks into the desk.

The room grows quieter. The walls become thin, like paper soaked in water. Beyond them, you can hear something enormous breathing.

The third question appears:

**“If you pass, what will you become?”**

You hesitate. You think of the career you wanted, the one you studied for, the one that were supposed to make everything stable. But when you try to write it down, the letters scatter into birds and fly off the page.

Then the final question forms, slow and bright, as if written by candlelight:

**“If you fail, what will you finally be free to try?”**

The proctor approaches. They place a hand on your shoulder and whisper, “There is no wrong answer. There is only the one you are brave enough to speak.”

You take a breath. You write one true thing.

The exam hall dissolves.

You are standing in an open field under a sky full of clocks, all showing different times. Some are too early, some too late, but one, directly above you, begins to move forward.

And you realize the exam was never about your future career.

It was about whether you were ready to stop rehearsing your life and begin living it.
