...and the architecture of the room keeps quietly rearranging itself behind your back.

The first group is gathered around a long table set with silver platters, though the food consists entirely of glowing, polished stones and cold tea in porcelain cups. Their laughter is perfectly synchronized, like wind chimes in a sudden breeze. When you step closer, their faces blur softly, like watercolors left out in a drizzle, yet they greet you warmly—congratulating you on an achievement you can’t quite recall, whispering an inside joke from your childhood that you had forgotten until this exact second. You try to ask if they’ve seen the person you’re looking for, but your voice feels thick, moving through the air like honey. They simply nod with profound sympathy and point toward an open French door.

Stepping through, you don't find a terrace, but a sprawling greenhouse where the floor is made of dark, polished hardwood submerged under an inch of warm, still water. Here, a circle of strangers in dark velvet coats is deeply engrossed in a debate about a book that hasn't been written yet. You catch snippets of their words—sounding half like language, half like cello strings. One of them looks up, catches your eye, and hands you an origami crane folded from a handwritten letter with your name on the envelope. "They're just ahead," the stranger says, though their mouth doesn't move.

You press on, your footsteps sending gentle ripples across the mirrored floor. In the distance, framed by towering ferns that graze a ceiling open to a twilight sky, you finally spot them. They are wearing that familiar jacket, leaning against a wrought-iron railing with a glass in hand, looking back over their shoulder as if they’ve been waiting for you the entire time.

Relief washes over you, warm and sudden. You take three hurried strides toward them, your feet barely touching the ground now, lighter than air. They turn fully, smiling that effortless, grounding smile you recognize instantly. 

You open your mouth to say, *“I’ve been looking everywhere for you,”* but the sound of your own voice dissolves into the distant, rhythmic ticking of a clock, and the edges of the room begin to pale into the soft, gray static of morning light.
