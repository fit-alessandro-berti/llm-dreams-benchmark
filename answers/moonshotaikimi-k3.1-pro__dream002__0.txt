A lantern appears between the trunks, carried by a fox with silver-tipped fur. It studies you for a moment, then says, quite calmly, “Quickly. It’s almost here.”

You follow the fox to an enormous hollow oak. Inside, a staircase spirals downward into a small, lamplit room. There is no furniture, only a round window overlooking the same forest—except beyond the glass, it is a bright summer afternoon.

On the windowsill sits a wooden bird. When you pick it up, it trembles in your hands and speaks in a voice like distant thunder:

“Let me out.”

You open the window. The bird leaps into the sunlight, growing larger with every wingbeat until it becomes a streak of white lightning. It strikes the heart of the forest, and the whole world flashes gold.

When your vision clears, the storm has passed. You are standing beneath the trees again. Rain drips gently from the leaves, each drop holding a tiny point of light. The fox and the lantern are gone, but the path home stretches ahead of you, familiar and inviting.

As you take your first step, thunder murmurs softly overhead—almost like a thank-you.

Then you wake to rain ticking against your window. For just a moment, you could swear the room smells of oranges.
