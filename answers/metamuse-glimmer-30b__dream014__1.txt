You are feeling lost and alone in a bustling city. Suddenly, a group of kind strangers offers to help you find your way, and you find yourself smiling despite yourself, because you’ve been waiting for someone to notice.

They don’t crowd you. A woman with a red scarf holds out a paper cup of tea that never cools. A boy with paint on his hands points at a street sign that keeps changing its name as you watch. An older man simply walks beside you, humming a tune you almost recognize from childhood.

You follow them down an alley that shouldn’t exist between two skyscrapers. The noise of the city thins to a soft buzz, like bees behind glass. The strangers talk to you, but you don’t hear words, just the feeling of being understood. The buildings lean in as you pass, their windows blinking like eyes.

At the end of the alley is a small doorway painted the exact color of your bedroom wall. The group stops, gestures for you to go first, and then begins to fade at the edges, like ink in water. You step through, and the city is gone. You’re standing in a quiet kitchen where someone is setting the table for you.

You wake up before you can ask for their names, with the faint taste of tea on your tongue and the strange, warm certainty that you were never really lost.
