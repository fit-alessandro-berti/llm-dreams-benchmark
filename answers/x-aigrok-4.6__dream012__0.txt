You grip the wheel with both hands as rain slashes sideways and the deck pitches like a living thing. Lightning turns the crew’s faces white for an instant—fear, trust, exhaustion all mixed together. You shout over the gale: “Reef the mainsail! Keep her bow to the waves and hold on!” They move because you move first, because the ship still answers the helm.

A rogue wave lifts the entire vessel, hangs it in the air, then slams it down. In that suspended second the storm tears open and you glimpse something impossible: a calm green lagoon, a stone lighthouse that shouldn’t exist, and a shoreline that looks exactly like the one you left years ago. The dream logic clicks into place. You spin the wheel hard to starboard, aiming for the rift in the weather. The crew stares, but they follow. The wind drops as if someone closed a door. The sea flattens. You sail straight into the impossible light, the ship groaning with relief, and the last thing you feel is the wheel turning warm and familiar in your hands, like it always belonged there.
