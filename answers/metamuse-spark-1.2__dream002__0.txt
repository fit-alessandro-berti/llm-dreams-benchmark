You feel a storm approaching and the forest holds its breath.

The air, which was warm and still a moment ago, suddenly turns cool against your skin. The smell of pine and damp earth grows sharper, heavier — mixed now with ozone, like the air before lightning. A low pressure settles in your ears.

Above you, the canopy that was letting soft sunlight through in patches begins to rustle. Not with wind — not yet — but with anticipation. Leaves shiver and turn over, showing their pale undersides. Birds go silent all at once.

Then the wind comes.

It doesn't howl. It *sighs* through the trunks, long and low, making the tall trees sway like slow dancers. Sunlight is swallowed as clouds roll in impossibly fast, bruised purple and deep gray, churning just above the treetops. The peaceful forest dimms to a kind of twilight.

You start to walk faster, your feet soft on the moss, and you realize you know this path even though you've never been here before. That's how dreams are.

A single, heavy drop of rain hits the back of your hand. Cold. Then another on your forehead. Then the sky opens — but it's not a violent storm.

The rain falls warm. It glows faintly as it falls, like liquid light, and where it hits the leaves and the ground, tiny flowers bloom instantly and close again. Thunder rolls, but it sounds distant and deep, like a drum inside the earth rather than in the sky.

Instead of running for shelter, you stop. You tilt your face up and let it fall on you.

The wind lifts around you in a spiral, lifting fallen leaves into a slow vortex of gold and green. In the center of it, the forest is completely calm. You are completely calm. The anxiety you felt when the storm approached is gone, replaced by a strange, clear understanding: the storm wasn't coming *for* you, it was coming *to* wash something away.

Lightning flashes — silent, without a crack — and for a second the entire forest is illuminated, every raindrop frozen in the air like crystal.

Then, as quickly as it came, the wind softens. The clouds thin and break apart, not drifting away but dissolving into mist that catches the returning sunlight. The forest is brighter than before. The leaves are greener, the air is crystal clear, and every surface glistens.

You take a deep breath. The air after the rain smells like a new beginning.

And somewhere ahead, where the path was dark before, you can now see a warm light shining through the trees — waiting for you to follow.
