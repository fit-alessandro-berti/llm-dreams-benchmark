...not about the syllabus at all. They are written in my own handwriting, but dated tomorrow. 

*Question 1: Calculate the exact weight of the things you left unsaid three autumns ago. Show your work in silence.*

*Question 2: If a train leaves the station at 4:00 AM carrying every version of yourself you abandoned to get into this room, which one is waving from the window?*

My breath catches. The fluorescent lights overhead hum a low, continuous chord that vibrates in the back of my teeth. I look around the lecture hall, desperate for solidarity, but the other students are furiously scribbling. Their pens scratch across the paper like beetles, their faces blurred and indistinct under the sterile glare. At the front of the room, the proctor stands motionless, a silhouette against a chalkboard covered in equations that continuously shift and rearrange like swarms of insects.

I look back down at my desk. The paper feels cold, almost damp, and the ink has begun to ripple.

*Question 3: You were given a life. What did you trade it for?*
*A) Safety*
*B) Approval*
*C) Time*
*D) None of the above*

I pick up my pencil, but my fingers feel thick and numb, as though I’m trying to move them through deep water. When I finally press the tip to the paper to choose, the lead snaps. Instead of a mark, the broken point burns a tiny, glowing ember through the page. 

The hole slowly widens, the paper curling into black ash, revealing not the wood of the desk beneath, but a vast, bottomless night sky full of cold, drifting stars. 

A bell begins to chime—slow, heavy, and resonant, vibrating through my ribs. The proctor’s voice echoes from everywhere and nowhere at once: *"Five minutes remaining."*

I drop the pencil. I don't try to answer the questions anymore. Instead, I reach my hand straight into the burning hole in the paper, letting my fingers sink into the cold air of the sky beneath, realizing the exam was never about the career at all—it was a test to see if I would notice I was asleep.
