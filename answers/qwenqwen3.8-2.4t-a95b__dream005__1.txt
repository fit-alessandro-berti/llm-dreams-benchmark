

As you try to find them, you encounter various groups of people, and each group seems to know the person you’re looking for—but none of them can agree on what that person looks like.

One cluster of guests is laughing around a table covered with empty glasses. They tell you your friend was just here, standing beside the tall lamp, but when you look, the lamp is actually a tree growing out of the floor. Another group is whispering near a staircase, and they say your friend went upstairs years ago, though they say it as if it happened only moments before.

You move through rooms that keep changing: a crowded ballroom becomes a quiet hallway, the hallway becomes a garden, and the garden becomes your childhood kitchen, filled with strangers wearing familiar faces. Everyone offers you something—a key, a flower, a note with your name spelled slightly wrong.

At last, you see them across the room, standing near a window that opens onto a sky full of slow-moving stars. You push through the crowd, but as you reach them, they turn and smile. Their face is not the face you expected; it is kinder, older, and somehow like your own.

“I was wondering when you’d find me,” they say.

The music stops. The guests fade. The room breathes out.

And then you wake up, still holding the feeling that you had been searching for more than just one person.
