...start walking, though the streets keep rearranging themselves like pieces in a slow game. The buildings lean toward one another, whispering. A clock tower ahead shows the hour hand moving backward, and the minute hand has been replaced by a small silver fish that swims in circles across its face.

You dig through your coat pockets and find a folded paper with an address written in handwriting you don't recognize. The ink is still wet. When you unfold it, the words shift, rearranging into a single line: *Follow the sound of the river that isn't there.*

So you listen. Somewhere beneath the traffic and the murmur of strangers, you hear water, soft and rhythmic. You follow it down an alley lined with doors painted in every color, and each one you pass opens briefly on its own. Behind one, a grandmother is teaching a parrot to recite stock prices. Behind another, a room full of people is quietly waiting for a train that stopped running years ago.

At the end of the alley, a bridge arches over nothing at all, a gap of empty air where a river should be. A woman in a long green coat is standing at its center, holding a leather briefcase and watching you with an expression of mild amusement, as if she has been expecting you for a very long time.

"You're late," she says, though the clock on her wrist has no hands.

You look down at your own wrist and realize you have no watch either, only a faint sense that the meeting has already begun, and that you are the only one who still doesn't know what it's about.
