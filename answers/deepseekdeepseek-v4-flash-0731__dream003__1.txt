…the patterns seem to shift under your fingertips, like tiny rivers of ink flowing just beneath the surface. The more you look, the more they resemble a language you almost know—curves and spirals that feel like memories rather than symbols. You trace one with your finger, and the box gives a soft, hollow click, as if waking from a long sleep.

A whisper, not quite a voice, curls through your mind: *“You’ve carried me before.”*

Suddenly you realize the lock isn’t a lock at all. It’s a mouth. A small, patient mouth, sewn shut with silver threads. You pull one thread loose. It comes away like a strand of moonlight. The lid trembles.

Inside, you know, is not gold or jewels, but something you lost before you were born. Maybe a heartbeat. Maybe a name. Maybe the first true sentence you never said aloud.

You open it. The darkness inside looks back at you, kindly, and says,

“Finally. I was dreaming about you too.”
