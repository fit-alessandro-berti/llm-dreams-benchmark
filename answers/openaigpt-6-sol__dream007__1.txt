You are in the middle of a heated argument with a close friend. The disagreement is about something important to both of you, and you decide to walk away before you say the thing you know you cannot take back.

The hallway outside the room is much longer than it should be. With each step, the carpet changes color beneath your feet: red, blue, the pale yellow of the kitchen in the house where you grew up. At the end stands a door with your friend’s handwriting on it.

You open it.

Inside is a small lake. Every sentence you shouted floats on the surface, folded into a paper boat. Some are already sinking. You kneel and pull one out, expecting to see the words exactly as you said them. Instead, it reads: *I thought you would understand.*

Across the water, your friend is gathering boats too. They look up at you, holding one carefully in both hands.

“I didn’t,” they say. “But I want to.”

The lake is too wide to cross, so you sit on its shore. After a while, your friend does the same. You begin again, speaking softly enough that the water stays still.
