The air smells sharply of crushed pine, damp limestone, and woodsmoke. Before me lies a vast, mist-draped valley framed by towering aqueducts and ancient stone pavilions overgrown with pale, silver-veined ivy. There are no humming wires, no distant hum of engines—only the rustle of wind through cedar branches and the low, rhythmic toll of a bronze bell across the water.

Down by a crystalline river, people clad in woven linen tunics gather around a massive sundial carved directly into the bedrock. They don’t measure time in frantic seconds, but in the slow crawl of shadows and the turning of seasons. 

As an elder looks up and meets my gaze, holding out a clay vessel of cool spring water, a strange clarity washes over me. I realize that to survive here, I don't just need food or shelter—I need to unlearn the noise of the future. I have to synchronize with the slower, deeper pulse of the earth before the sun dips below the mountains, trusting that the answers to where—and *when*—I am will reveal themselves in the silence.
