At first they seem random, but slowly you recognize familiar shapes: the street where you grew up, the outline of a face you have almost forgotten, the constellation that used to hang outside your bedroom window.

When you trace the final star, the box grows warm.

A quiet ticking begins inside it—not like a clock, but like a second heartbeat. The room around you fades, leaving only the box suspended in a vast darkness. Then a narrow seam of golden light appears along its lid.

A voice whispers, “There was never a key.”

You press your palm against the center of the pattern. The box opens.

Inside, there is no treasure. Instead, you see a small version of yourself standing in a sunlit field, holding something tightly behind their back. They look up at you and smile with the calm certainty of someone who has been waiting a very long time.

“What are you hiding?” you ask.

Your younger self brings their hands forward. Resting in their palms is a tiny door.

The moment you touch it, the door expands until it stands before you, surrounded by tall grass moving in a wind you cannot feel. Beyond it lies a place you somehow recognize, although you are certain you have never been there.

Behind you, the mysterious box begins to close.

You understand that you may take only one thing through the door: the box, your younger self, or the memory of what you saw inside.

Before you can decide, you hear footsteps approaching from the other side.

The door slowly opens—and the person standing there is you, many years older, holding the key that never existed.
