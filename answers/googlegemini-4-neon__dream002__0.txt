A sudden gust of wind sweeps through the undergrowth, carrying the strange scent of ozone and old parchment, bending the towering pines as effortlessly as blades of grass. 

You pull your coat tighter around yourself—though you don't remember wearing one a moment ago—and hurry down the narrowing path. Thunder rolls overhead, a sound so deep you feel it vibrating through the soles of your shoes rather than hearing it with your ears. 

Then the rain begins. You flinch, bracing for an icy downpour, but the drops that strike your face are warm and almost weightless. Holding out your palm, you see they aren't water at all, but tiny, glowing sparks of silver light that dissolve into cool mist the instant they touch your skin. 

Beneath your feet, the mossy forest floor begins to shimmer and soften, turning into dark, rippling glass that reflects the swirling sky. Searching for shelter, you spot a freestanding red wooden door nestled between the tangled roots of an ancient willow. Faint, amber light leaks from beneath its frame. 

Without questioning how it got there, you turn the tarnished brass handle and step inside.

The howling wind cuts off instantly. You are no longer in the woods, but in a vast, quiet conservatory with a domed glass ceiling stretching impossibly high above you. Outside the glass, the violet storm rages in complete silence, casting drifting shadows across rows of potted ferns and towering, crooked bookshelves. 

At the center of the room sits a single worn armchair beside a small wooden table. On the table rests a steaming mug of tea and an open book. Its pages are completely blank, yet as you step closer, you somehow know it contains the map back to the sunlit part of the forest. You reach out to turn a page, and as your fingers brush the paper, the silver rain begins to tap against the glass ceiling overhead—a slow, rhythmic patter that gradually blurs into a familiar, steady hum, gently pulling the room apart as you begin to wake.
