Your crew looks to you for guidance as the storm worsens, and you raise your voice above the howling wind. It does not shake. You know this calmness does not come from the size of the vessel or the strength of your arms, but from the certainty that every wave has a trough, and every storm has an eye.

"Hold the wheel!" you call out, your hand gripping the spokes until your knuckles turn white. "Steady as she goes. Trust the wood, and trust each other."

A massive crest rises ahead, shadowing us in darkness, hungry and towering. The ship groans, timbers protesting against the ocean's might. For a heartbeat, the horizon vanishes, and you are suspended in a void of grey rain and churning black water. The crew is silent now, pinned by the magnitude of the moment. You meet their eyes—wide, frightened, waiting.

In that silence, you find the rhythm. You match your breath to the roll of the deck. You match their courage to your command. You do not fight the sea; you ask it to pass.

"Now!" you shout, and the crew moves as one. You lean into the turn, the ship responding like a living thing, slicing through the base of the wave rather than meeting its face. The water crashes over the bow, a deluge that blinds and soaks, but the hull holds. The engine hums, a steady heartbeat beneath the chaos.

And then, as suddenly as it gathered, the fury breaks. The ship crests the wall of water and slides into the calm beyond. The rain softens to a mist. The clouds fracture, allowing a single shaft of moonlight to pierce through, illuminating the wet decks and the soot-stained faces of your crew. They are breathing hard, some laughing in disbelief, others gripping the rails but standing tall.

You loosen your grip on the wheel, your hands trembling now that the adrenaline recedes. You are still the captain. The sea is still vast and indifferent. But you have led them through the night, and the dawn is beginning to bleed into the eastern sky.
