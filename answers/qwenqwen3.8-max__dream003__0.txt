You are given a mysterious locked box with no key in sight. As you examine it closely, you notice intricate patterns and tiny symbols shifting across its surface like insects made of light. They rearrange themselves whenever you blink, forming shapes that almost make sense: doors, eyes, birds, and hands with empty palms.

The box is warm, as if something inside it is breathing.

You turn it over, and the patterns sink into the wood, then rise again as silver lines. A faint sound comes from within—not ticking, not scratching, but a whisper in a language you feel you should remember. It says your name, but gently, as though calling you from very far away.

Then the dream changes.

The room around you becomes vast and dark, filled with floating staircases that lead nowhere. Each step is made of glass, and inside each one is a small object: a button, a feather, a tooth, a folded letter, a key that dissolves when you look at it too long.

You understand, in the way one understands things only in dreams, that the box cannot be opened by force. It must be answered.

You place your hand on the lid and think of something you have lost. At first nothing happens. Then the intricate patterns glow blue, and the whisper inside the box becomes clearer. It is not speaking to you anymore. It is speaking through you.

A seam appears in the box, thin as a hairline crack in ice. The lid lifts without sound.

Inside, resting on black velvet, is a small mirror. But it does not show your face. It shows a room you have never seen, lit by candles, with a figure sitting at a table, waiting. The figure looks up, and though you cannot see their eyes, you know they can see you.

They slide a key across the table.

At that moment, the mirror in the box begins to ripple like water. Your reflection reaches out, and you realize the key is not meant to open the box.

It is meant to open you.

The dream folds inward, soft and sudden, and the last thing you feel is the cool touch of metal in your hand—though when you wake, your palm is empty, except for the faint impression of a pattern you cannot explain.
