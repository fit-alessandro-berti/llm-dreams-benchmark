You step onto the stage, the wooden planks humming beneath your boots as if they’re part of the rhythm of the room itself. The lights dim just enough to let the glow of the audience’s faces spill onto the walls, and you catch their eyes—wide, eager, and patient—as if they were waiting for a secret to be whispered.  

And then you **begin to speak**, but the words don’t just travel outward; they ripple through the air, turning into soft ribbons of light that dance between heads. Each syllable feels like a brushstroke on their skin, painting smiles, curiosity, and a hint of nervous energy onto their cheeks.  

A gentle breeze stirs the curtains, and for a heartbeat the whole hall seems to hold its breath, listening to the cadence of your voice. You notice that even the shadows in the back rows lean forward, as though they too are trying to catch the melody before it fades.  

Your heart thumps in time with the applause that hasn’t arrived yet, and you realize the true magic isn’t in the words alone—it’s in the way the crowd’s collective anticipation becomes a living, breathing chorus around you. As you finish the last sentence, the room erupts in a warm, golden haze, and you feel the moment settle like a familiar blanket, leaving you both exhilarated and oddly at peace.
