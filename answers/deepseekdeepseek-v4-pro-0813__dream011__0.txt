As you sit down to start the exam, you notice the questions are not in any language you studied, but you can read them perfectly. They are all about your life—the moments you never told anyone. The first question says: “Describe the person you could have been.” The second: “Show your work for every time you said you were fine when you weren’t.” The rest of the page is blank, but the longer you stare, the more questions appear. The other students are all writing furiously, except they all have your face. The clock isn’t counting down; it’s counting up. And then you realize the exam isn’t testing what you know. It’s testing what you’ve been avoiding.
