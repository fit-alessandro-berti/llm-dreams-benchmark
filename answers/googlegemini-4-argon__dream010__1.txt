...the terrain beneath my feet keeps shifting. One moment, I am standing in a sleek, glass-walled boardroom, and the next, the floor dissolves into a labyrinth of towering bookshelves that stretch into a violet sky. 

The other competitors are there, too, though I can never quite see their faces. They are just blurs of charcoal suits and urgent whispers, moving at impossible speeds. A booming voice—coming from everywhere and nowhere—announces the first challenge: *"Build a bridge using only your past mistakes."* 

Everyone around me immediately begins stacking glowing, heavy bricks, their hands moving with practiced ease. I look down at my own hands and find I’m only holding a fistful of water and a silver pocket watch that ticks backward. Panicked, I pour the water onto the abyss in front of me. To my shock, it freezes instantly in mid-air, forming a crystalline, winding path. I sprint across it as the ice shatters just behind my heels.

Suddenly, I’m no longer running on ice; I’m backstage at a massive theater. This is the semi-final. The stage manager—who looks suspiciously like my third-grade teacher—shoves a heavy velvet box into my arms. "You're up," she says. "Pitch the unpitchable."

I walk out onto the stage. The spotlights are blinding, burning like miniature suns. Sitting in the judge’s booth are three giant, shadowy figures made entirely of constellations and television static. I open the velvet box, hoping to find notes or a presentation, but a flock of origami birds bursts out, flying into the rafters. 

I try to speak, to explain why I deserve this job, why I’ve worked my whole life for it, but my voice comes out as the sound of a cello. Strangely, the static-judges lean forward, captivated. I realize they don’t want words; they want the music. I begin conducting the invisible orchestra with my bare hands, pulling melodies out of the humid air, weaving my passion, my late nights, and my wildest ambitions into a symphony. The origami birds dive-bomb the other competitors, turning them into mannequins that harmlessly topple over. 

Then, silence. 

The theater vanishes. I am standing alone on the roof of the tallest skyscraper in the world, surrounded by swirling golden clouds. Before me is a single, floating mahogany door with a brass plaque that reads: **YOUR FUTURE.** 

This is the final test. There is no keyhole, only a mirror where the doorknob should be. I look into the mirror, expecting to see my own exhausted face, terrified of failing. Instead, the reflection smiles at me, reaches *out* of the glass, and grabs my wrist. 

"You already got the job," my reflection says, its voice echoing like thunder. "Now, wake up and do the work."

The reflection pulls me forward. I tumble through the mirror, falling into a blinding, weightless light, my heart pounding with triumph—just as the sound of my real-world alarm clock begins to bleed through the edges of the sky.
