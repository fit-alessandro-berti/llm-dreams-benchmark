But the hallway outside the room stretches farther than it should. The lights hum overhead, and every door you pass opens onto a place you’ve shared with your friend: the café where you first met, the train platform where you said goodbye, the kitchen where you once stayed up laughing until sunrise.

At the end of the hall, you find a door standing open. Beyond it is a quiet beach beneath a pale, impossible moon. Your friend is there, already seated in the sand.

For a while, neither of you speaks. The tide comes in, carrying tiny scraps of the argument—sharp words, old worries, things you meant and things you didn’t. The waves smooth them away.

Then your friend says, “I don’t want to win if it means losing you.”

You sit beside them. The disagreement is still there, important and unresolved. But so are the two of you. And as the moon sinks toward the horizon, you begin again—not by agreeing, but by listening.
