...the air turns heavy with the scent of ozone and wet pine. Above you, the bright canopy fractures into shades of bruised violet and slate gray. Within seconds, the wind rises—not with a roar, but with a low, resonant hum, like a chorus of distant voices holding a single note.

You break into a run as the first drops begin to fall. Strangely, they aren’t cold water; they are warm, glowing flecks of silver light that hiss softly against the ferns and dissolve into rising mist. Around you, the towering oaks begin to bend and sway in slow motion, leaning away from the trail as if making room for something unseen.

Desperate for shelter, you round a bend and spot an anomaly in a small clearing: a tall, weathered wooden door standing completely by itself, unattached to any wall or building. Brass hinges gleam sharply against the darkening sky. Without questioning how it got there, you seize the cold iron handle, pull it open, and step across the threshold.

You pull the door shut behind you, and the storm vanishes into absolute silence.

You are no longer in the woods. You are standing inside an impossibly vast, quiet conservatory filled with hanging paper lanterns and towering bookshelves that spiral up into mist. Rain taps gently against a glass ceiling miles above, though the air around you is warm and smells faintly of cinnamon and old paper. 

On a small mahogany table beside you sits a steaming porcelain teacup and a pocket watch whose hands are spinning rapidly backward. As you reach out to touch the watch, the floor beneath your feet suddenly softens into cool moss again, and the distant chime of a morning bell begins to pull you upward toward waking.
