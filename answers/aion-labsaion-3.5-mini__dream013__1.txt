as you move deeper into the maze, you notice the walls beginning to shift—corridors that were behind you have rearranged themselves, and the air grows thick with a humming sound, like the maze itself is thinking.

The first puzzle was simple: a sequence of numbers etched into stone, begging to be continued. The second was a door with no handle, opened only by speaking a word that described the room behind it. But now the puzzles grow stranger. You come upon a chessboard where the pieces move on their own, playing against themselves, and you realize you must predict the outcome of a game that neither side wants to win.

You concentrate. The pieces blur. Somewhere in the maze, a clock ticks backward.

Further in, you find a mirror that doesn't show your reflection—it shows the maze from above, and you understand that you've been both the wanderer and the architect all along. Each puzzle you solved, you had built yourself, in some forgotten moment before the dream began.

The final chamber is empty except for a single door, and on the door, a question written in your own handwriting:

*"What happens if you stop trying to solve everything?"*

You reach for the handle, but there isn't one. There never was. The door simply opens when you stop pushing.

Light floods in—and for a moment, you see the ceiling of your own bedroom, the soft gray of early morning, the maze dissolving like sugar in water. The last puzzle's answer follows you up through the layers of sleep, almost graspable, almost meaningful—

and then you wake, and it's gone, the way all dreams go. Only the feeling remains: that somewhere, a door is waiting to open the moment you stop trying to force it.
