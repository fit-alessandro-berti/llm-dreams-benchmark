...and as your fingers drift toward it, the canvas *inhales*.

Color rises up through the weave from below—violet first, warm as thunder heard through a wall—and blooms wherever your attention rests. Spirals you almost recognize. Shapes shaped like forgotten names.

You understand, the way one understands things in dreams, that this canvas is painting you from the inside out. Grief spreads along the bottom edge in slow ferns of gray-blue. A warmth behind your ribs freckles the upper corner with tiny suns. When you laugh—startled by your own laughter—the sky copies it, clouds curling into the exact shape of the sound.

Emboldened, you try to paint deliberately. You reach for a bird, but your intention slips sideways and comes out as *the memory of flying*, loose feathers of text scattering across the linen. You draw a straight line and the whole horizon obediently tilts to match it. The world, you see now, has been waiting for instruction all along—or perhaps it was never separate from the canvas at all.

A figure steps out of the treeline, half-rendered, faceless only because you haven't finished imagining them. They stand patiently at the edge of the frame. Before you can decide who they are, they sigh and fold back into a puddle of ochre.

Somewhere beyond the hills, the light begins to thin. The edges of everything soften like watercolor left out in the rain—trees bleeding upward, rivers unraveling into ribbon—you press both palms flat against the canvas to hold on.

When you wake, the room is gray and ordinary.

But there's color pressed into your fingertips, and on the back of your hand, faintly:

a single brushstroke, shaped exactly like the feeling of almost remembering something important.
