You are feeling lost and alone in a bustling city. Suddenly, a group of kind strangers offers to help you find your way, and you accept their hands before you can decide whether you trust them. Their palms are warm in the cold neon. One of them already knows the name you have not said out loud.

You walk. The crowd parts around you as if the city has been waiting for this small procession. Streetlights lean closer. Neon flickers into words you almost recognize. Rain begins, but it smells like the wet pavement of a street you grew up on. A taxi’s roof sign blinks, for half a second, into your name.

They do not ask where you are going. They already seem to know. A woman with your mother’s laugh points down an alley that should not exist between two glass towers. A boy hums a song you thought only you remembered. An old man presses into your palm a key too light to be metal.

The alley opens onto a quiet courtyard and a window glowing gold. Inside, a kettle is already whistling. Someone has left a place set for you.

You sit. The strangers sit too. For a moment you cannot tell who found whom. You are no longer being led; you are walking with them. Outside, the city keeps rushing, glittering and enormous. Inside, the traffic sounds, for the first time, like company.

You were never lost. You were only late, and they saved you a seat.

When you look down, your hands are no longer empty.
