You are participating in a competition where the grand prize is your dream job. As the competition progresses, you face various challenges and begin to realize that none of them have anything to do with the work itself.

In the first round, you must cross a room filled with people who loudly criticize every step you take. Some tell you that you are too slow. Others say you are unqualified, inexperienced, or simply not the kind of person who succeeds. Their voices sound strangely familiar, as though they have been living somewhere inside your head.

At first, you try to prove them wrong. You hurry, stumble, and nearly fall. Then you notice a narrow path appearing beneath your feet whenever you stop listening to them. You take a breath and walk at your own pace. One by one, the voices fade, and the door at the other end opens.

The next challenge takes place in an enormous library. You are told to find the answer to an impossible question, but every book you open contains blank pages. The clock above you moves faster and faster. Panic rises in your chest until you notice a small pencil lying on the floor.

You begin writing your own answer.

As soon as the final sentence is complete, words appear in every book around you, filling the shelves with ideas you had forgotten you knew. The judges exchange surprised looks and allow you to continue.

In the final round, only you and one other contestant remain. They appear confident, polished, and perfectly prepared. You are led onto a stage where your future boss waits behind a large desk. A contract lies in front of them.

“To win,” the boss says, “you must explain why you deserve this job more than your opponent.”

Your competitor delivers a flawless speech. When your turn comes, however, you find that all the impressive phrases you rehearsed have vanished. The audience waits in silence.

You look at the contract, then at the people watching you, and say, “I don’t know whether I deserve it more than anyone else. But I know I can learn. I know I care about doing meaningful work. And I know I’m tired of believing that fear is proof that I’m incapable.”

For a moment, nothing happens.

Then your opponent smiles and begins applauding.

The walls of the competition hall dissolve. The judges disappear, the stage becomes a quiet office filled with warm morning light, and the contract transforms into a blank sheet of paper. At the top, in golden letters, are the words:

**Write the job you truly want.**

You hesitate, wondering what answer will impress them. But there is no longer anyone to impress. So you write about work that challenges you without destroying you, colleagues who respect you, and a place where curiosity matters more than perfection.

When you finish, a door appears beside the desk.

You open it and discover not another test, but your first day at work. Your name is already on the door. The people inside look up and welcome you as though they have been expecting you all along.

Just before you step through, you glance back and see that the competition was never against the other contestants.

Every obstacle had been a version of your own doubt.

You cross the threshold, and behind you, the door quietly disappears.
