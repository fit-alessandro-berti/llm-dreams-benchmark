As the competition progresses, you face various challenges and discover that each obstacle isn't designed to break you, but to strip away the versions of yourself you've performed for others. The first round demands you solve a riddle in a language you don't speak, using only colors and shapes that shift when you're not looking. The second has you navigating a city made of floating shelves, where every book you touch rewrites a memory of your past career choices. Competitors become mirrors, and alliances form in the quiet moments between rounds, built on shared exhaustion and the strange realization that your "dream job" looks bizarrely different to each of you.

As the field narrows, the challenges grow deeply personal: you're asked to present a project that has no audience, to lead a team composed entirely of the parts of your talent you've abandoned, and finally, to spend an hour in a room filled with every job title you've ever held, asking which one still feels like a fit. There's no trophy, no handshake. The final gate opens not into an office, but into a vast, starlit workspace where the work itself is simply waiting—no application, no interview, just the quiet permission to begin. And as you step forward, the dream doesn't fade; it simply becomes the first hour of your actual morning.
