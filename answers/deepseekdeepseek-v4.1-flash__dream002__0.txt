You feel a storm approaching and the trees lean in like they’ve been waiting for it. The sky bruises purple, the wind smells of rain and old coins, and the leaves tear free—only to spiral upward instead of falling, forming a dark tunnel ahead. You run, but the path turns to water beneath your feet, each step rippling without sound. The storm isn’t above you; it’s inside your chest, a low thunder that grows with every breath. Lightning flashes, and in that white instant you see a house you’ve never lived in, its door wide open. Someone inside calls your name in your own voice. You step forward, and the rain begins to fall upward, stars blinking between the drops. Just before you reach the door, the whole forest folds into one green eye and asks, “Are you ready to wake?” You’re not sure, so you say nothing—and the dream keeps going.
