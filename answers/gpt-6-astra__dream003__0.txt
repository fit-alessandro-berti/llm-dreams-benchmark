symbols that seem to rearrange themselves whenever you blink.

At first they look like vines. Then rivers. Then the streets of a town you almost remember.

One tiny window glows in the pattern. Behind it stands someone holding a box exactly like yours.

You tap the window.

They tap back.

A voice from inside the lock whispers, “It doesn’t open with a key.”

“What does it open with?”

“Something you’ve been keeping.”

You search your pockets. A train ticket with no destination. A blue button. A photograph of an empty chair. None of these seems right.

Then you remember a goodbye you never said.

You whisper it into the keyhole.

The box gives a small, relieved sigh.

Its lid lifts, revealing a staircase descending into warm afternoon light. Somewhere below, dishes clink. Someone is laughing in a kitchen you haven’t seen in years.

You set the box on the floor and step inside, although it is far too small to hold you.

As you descend, the lid begins to close overhead.

“Wait,” you say. “How will I get back?”

The voice answers from farther down the stairs.

“Leave the goodbye here. You won’t need it upstairs.”

At the bottom is a door, slightly ajar.

From the other side, someone calls your name as though you have only been gone a minute.
