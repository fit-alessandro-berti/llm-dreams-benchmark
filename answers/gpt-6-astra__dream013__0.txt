At first, you suspect a hidden mechanism. You count your footsteps, map each turn, and scratch small arrows into the stone. But the arrows become birds when you blink, and the birds fly ahead, carrying your careful directions into the dark.

At the center of the next chamber stands a table with a single locked box. Written across its lid is a question:

*What can you keep only by giving it away?*

You try answers aloud. “A promise. Kindness. A secret.”

With each answer, another lock appears.

Exhausted, you sit beside the table. Somewhere beyond the walls, someone is crying. You have heard the sound for a while, you realize, but you’ve been too busy solving things to listen.

“Are you lost?” you call.

“Yes,” says a small voice.

“So am I.”

A door opens behind you.

On the other side sits a child holding the scraps of your map. You kneel and help arrange them on the floor, though they no longer resemble corridors. Together they form a picture of a house beneath a blue tree.

“I don’t know how to get there,” you tell the child.

The child takes your hand. “That’s all right.”

You step through a doorway you are certain was not there before. It leads into a kitchen warm with afternoon sunlight. On the table is the locked box, now open.

Inside lies a brass key and a note in your own handwriting:

*For the back door. It sticks when it rains.*

Outside, something blue brushes against the window.
