The sky darkens in seconds, and rain falls upward, drifting toward the clouds instead of the ground. You look down and realize your path has become a river of silver light, and where you step, footprints bloom into flowers that glow faintly in the growing thunder.

A deer made of wind appears at the edge of the clearing, its antlers cracking open small bursts of lightning. It looks at you with calm, knowing eyes, then begins to run. You follow, and the forest rearranges itself around you—trunks bending into archways, leaves turning into wings, stones becoming doors. The storm is not coming toward you anymore; it is coming from you, rising from your chest with every breath.

When the thunder finally breaks, it sounds like your own name being called from a very long way away. The rain stops. The forest is peaceful again, but everything is mirrored, and at the far end of the path you can see yourself walking toward you, smiling, as if you are the one who is dreaming.
