...and you sit down at your desk, but the keyboard is wrong somehow. The keys have no letters on them, just smooth blank squares that click softly like teeth. The project brief sits in front of you: *Translate this 400-page document into a language that does not exist yet. Due in fifteen minutes.*

You look up at the clock on the wall, but the numbers are sliding around like mercury, and the second hand is spinning backward. Your boss is standing behind you now, closer than before, though you never heard footsteps. When you turn to ask for an extension, your mouth moves but no sound comes out. You try again. What comes out instead is the dial tone of an old telephone.

You start typing anyway. The blank keys somehow produce words, but when you glance at the screen, the document is full of your childhood report cards, your old utility bills, and a photo of a house you've never seen but somehow miss terribly. The page count keeps growing — 400 pages becomes 4,000, becomes a stack of paper rising from the desk like a tide.

In the corner of your eye, a coworker is watching you. It's someone you know, but their face keeps shifting — now a colleague, now a teacher from third grade, now your mother, now your boss, all wearing the same disappointed expression. The office walls breathe in and out, slowly.

Then the clock strikes a deadline that sounds like a school bell, and suddenly you're not at work anymore. You're in a high school hallway, and the final exam started ten minutes ago, and you never studied, and you can't find the classroom, and every door you open leads back to your desk with the blank keyboard.

You wake up with your heart pounding, relief flooding through you — *it was just a dream* — and then a small dread creeps in as you check the time and realize you're late for something. There's always something.
