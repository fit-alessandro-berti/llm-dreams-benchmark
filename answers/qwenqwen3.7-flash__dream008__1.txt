They don't stop; they multiply, each one reflecting a different version of today's date, all bleeding into one another like wet paint. Your boss smiles, but their features begin to soften and rearrange themselves into the silhouette of a lighthouse. "Just follow the current," they say, though their mouth hasn't moved.

You turn to your terminal, but the monitor has become a shallow well. Your reflection stares back, but it's wearing yesterday's clothes. When you rest your hands on the keyboard, the keys dissolve into soil. Seeds sprout instantly, unfurling into glowing threads that weave themselves into a net spanning the room. Progress bars float through the air, tied to your wrists like ribbons. They lengthen when you look away, shorten when you focus.

A calendar peels itself off the wall and folds into a paper airplane. It circles your head three times, drops onto your lap, and opens to a page marked *Now*. The text isn't typed; it's breathing. You realize you're not supposed to finish anything. You're only supposed to let it happen.

The fluorescent lights hum lower, becoming a distant ocean wave. The partition walls fold inward like an accordion, revealing that the entire office is suspended inside a giant, slow-turning mobile. Every file, every email, every impossible metric hangs by a silver thread, swaying gently. None of them fall.

Your chest loosens. You stand, and the floor becomes warm wood grain. You walk past your desk, past the humming server rack that now smells of rain on dry earth, past the boss whose voice has softened into wind chimes. At the far end, where the exit sign should be, there's only a half-open book resting on a reading stand. You turn the final page. It's blank except for a pressed leaf, crisp and golden.

You reach for it. Your fingers brush the edge.

Light floods in through actual blinds. Dust motes dance in the quiet morning. Your hand is resting on your thigh. The dream leaves no deliverable, only a weightlessness in your shoulders, and the quiet certainty that some deadlines are just stories we've been told to believe.
