...plant an orchard of glass trees. 

In the waking world, this would make no sense, but here in the dream, the logic is undeniable: the ash-choked soil can no longer birth wood and leaf, so it must birth light instead. 

I walk through the ruins of a sunken city, my pockets heavy with scavenged shards—broken windshields, fragments of stained-glass cathedrals, the emerald curves of old bottles. The sky above is a bruised, static purple, and the wind carries the metallic taste of the Old World's end. In the distance, the shadows of the Wastes prowl—great, shifting beasts made of smoke and rusted iron, hungry for warmth.

Ignoring their distant howls, I kneel in the gray dust at the center of an abandoned plaza. I dig a hole with my bare hands and bury a shard of ruby-red glass. Then, from a dented canteen, I pour my most precious resource: a single drop of clear, singing water.

Dream-time accelerates. The ground shivers. A translucent shoot pierces the ash, twisting upward, hardening and branching before my eyes. Within seconds, a crystalline tree stands before me. It doesn't bear fruit; it bears chimes. 

Encouraged, I plant more. Sapphire, amber, frosted white, and obsidian. My hands bleed, the crimson drops absorbed instantly by the thirsty dust, fueling the growth. Within minutes—or perhaps years—a forest of jagged, brilliant crystal towers over the ruined plaza. 

Suddenly, the shadows at the edge of the city coalesce. The Rust-Beasts have found me. They surge forward, a tidal wave of jagged metal and suffocating dark, coming to extinguish the last living thing in the valley. I have no weapons. I cannot outrun them. 

Instead, I stand at the center of my fragile grove and wait for the wind. 

A gale sweeps down from the dead mountains. It hits the orchard. Instead of shattering, the glass branches vibrate. A low, resonant hum begins, building into a deafening, harmonious chord—the music of a thousand singing bowls and cathedral organs. 

At the same moment, the bruised clouds above part for the first time in a century. A single beam of blinding sunlight strikes the highest glass bough. 

The light doesn't just pass through; it fractures, multiplies, and ignites. A kaleidoscope of searing, prismatic rays shoots out from the orchard in every direction. Where the beams of ruby, emerald, and gold strike the encroaching beasts, the monsters do not attack—they dissolve. Their rusted armor turns into flocks of startled origami birds; their smoke-bodies scatter into harmless morning mist.

Where the refracted light touches the dead, gray ash at my feet, real grass—impossibly green and soft—begins to sprout. 

I look up into the blinding, singing canopy of my impossible shelter, feeling the warmth of the sun on my face, as the music of the glass trees swells so loud and bright that I begin to wake up.
