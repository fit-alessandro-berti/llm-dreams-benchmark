You are in a world where colors and shapes are constantly changing. As you explore this surreal landscape, you come across a blank canvas and realize it is the only object that refuses to change.

It stands upright in a field of blue grass, balanced on an easel whose three legs bend like the knees of a patient animal. Around it, pyramids soften into spheres, orange rivers climb into the sky, and flocks of tiny silver squares scatter whenever you look directly at them. But the canvas remains perfectly white. Its stillness feels louder than the shifting world.

There is a brush in your hand. You do not remember picking it up.

The moment its bristles touch the canvas, a black door appears. It is small at first, no larger than a postage stamp, but with every breath it grows until its painted knob is level with your palm. You turn it. The canvas swings inward.

On the other side is the room where you slept as a child, except the ceiling is an evening sky and the floor is shallow water. Your old bed floats in the middle, drifting slowly among paper boats. Each boat carries a candle, and each candle burns a different color. When you lift one of the boats, you hear your own voice whispering from inside it: a sentence you once meant to say but never did. You cannot make out the words. The paper trembles in your hands, then folds itself into a red bird and flies through the painted doorway.

All the other boats follow.

You chase them back into the field, but the landscape has changed again. The blue grass is now a yellow staircase spiraling upward with no supports. The birds perch along its railing, waiting. Far above, a vast eye opens in the clouds—not an eye that watches you, but one that seems to be asking you to watch through it.

You climb.

At every step, your shadow becomes a different creature. First it is a dog, then a tall woman carrying a lantern, then a tree heavy with bells. On the final step it becomes a small child. The child-shadow will not move when you move. It points down.

The blank canvas is far below, a pale rectangle in the turning world. Something has begun to press outward from behind it. The fabric stretches. A handprint appears, then another. You understand, with the unquestioning certainty of dreams, that the whole world is trapped on the wrong side of the painting and is trying to wake up.

The cloud-eye blinks, and you fall through its pupil.

You land without impact in a dark gallery. Endless canvases hang on the walls, each containing a frozen scene: a green sun setting into a teacup; a city built inside a sleeping whale; your own face made of windows, with lights switching on and off behind the eyes. At the far end hangs the canvas from the field. In it, the shifting shapes and colors are motionless. The red birds hover in midair. The yellow staircase stops halfway through becoming a wave.

Only one figure moves inside the picture: the child made from your shadow.

The child walks to the foreground and places a palm against the painted surface. You raise your hand to meet it. The canvas is warm. Color flows from the child's fingers into yours—first blue, then gold, then shades with no names, colors that taste of rain or sound like distant singing. They travel up your arm and fill your chest until your heartbeat flashes through the gallery.

The child mouths a question.

What will you make?

You look down and find the brush in your hand again. The gallery walls are no longer walls but one enormous white surface curving around you. You begin to paint without deciding what to paint. A line becomes a road, the road becomes a river, and the river coils into the outline of a sleeping person. You paint the rise and fall of their breathing. You paint a window above them. Through the window, dawn waits in soft gray layers.

As you add the final stroke, every trapped picture bursts into motion. Bells ring from the tree-shadow. The whale rolls beneath its city. The colored birds pour out of their frames and circle you so quickly that their wings blend into a single bright halo. The child steps through the canvas and takes your free hand.

Together you walk into the painted window.

For a moment you are standing inside the color gray, and you discover it is not empty at all. It is made from millions of tiny doors, each opening onto another possible dream. Behind one, the orange rivers are still climbing. Behind another, the paper boats have reached a black sea. Behind a third, you see yourself asleep, holding an invisible brush above the blanket.

The child lets go of your hand and enters that door. Your shadow settles gently back beneath you.

Then the blank canvas folds itself around the entire world. Colors stream toward its center, shapes collapse into points, and the enormous cloud-eye closes with a sound like a page turning. In the sudden whiteness, you hear the sentence from the paper boat at last.

It is your own voice saying, “Leave something unfinished, so you can return.”

You wake with one hand raised in the air. Pale morning light fills the room, and on the tip of your index finger is a speck of blue paint. Before you can touch it, it changes to gold, then to a color you have never seen, and disappears.
