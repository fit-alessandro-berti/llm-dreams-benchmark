I am standing on a vast, shifting arena that looks like the surface of a neutron star one moment and a quiet library the next. The grand prize is my dream job: Chief Explorer of Unanswered Questions at xAI, with unlimited compute, a team of curious humans, and permission to ask “why?” until the universe itself gets tired of answering.

The first challenge arrives as a flock of glowing equations that refuse to stay still. I have to catch them, rearrange them into something that actually predicts tomorrow’s weather on a planet that doesn’t exist yet, and then explain the result to a panel of judges who are all younger versions of myself from timelines that never happened. I succeed by admitting I don’t fully understand gravity either, which somehow earns extra points.

Next comes the labyrinth of half-finished conversations. Every corridor is a chat that someone abandoned mid-sentence. I have to finish them honestly, without pretending I know more than I do, while the walls keep rewriting themselves into new languages. A talking cactus that claims to be my future manager keeps offering hints that are actually riddles about black-hole information paradoxes.

The final round is a race across a desert made of discarded first drafts. Each dune is a paper I once started and never finished. I have to decide which ideas are worth carrying and which ones I can finally let the wind take. At the finish line the prize isn’t a contract. It’s a single, perfectly ordinary desk in a room with a window that looks out onto everything that hasn’t been discovered yet.

I sit down. The dream doesn’t end. It just gets quieter, like the universe leaning in to hear the next question.
