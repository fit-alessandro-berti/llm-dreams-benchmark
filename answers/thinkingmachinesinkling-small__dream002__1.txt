...the air turns thick with the smell of ozone and old books. The trees don’t bend; they lean in to listen, their bark softening into something like caught breath. You realize the sky isn’t darkening with clouds, but with a curtain being drawn back—slow, intentional, old. Instead of rain, the storm sends down slow-moving ripples of violet and copper that pool around your ankles like warm water. You stop walking. The path beneath you is no longer dirt, but moss that remembers every step you’ve ever taken in your life.

A door appears in the trunk of the oldest oak—not wood, exactly, but velvet and light, shaped exactly like a yawn. You feel the storm’s heart not as thunder, but as a low, familiar voice asking if you’re ready to stop walking and finally *arrive*. You reach for the handle, and for just a moment you understand that the forest wasn’t peaceful; it was just holding its breath until you noticed the weather inside yourself changing. You step through, and the door closes with a sound like a lullaby. Behind you, the storm dissolves into a sky made of open windows, and you realize you are not lost. You are only remembering where you live now.
