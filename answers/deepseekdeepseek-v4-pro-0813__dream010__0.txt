...and each challenge seems to reshuffle reality. In the first round, you must cross a tightrope made of your own résumé while former bosses shout impossible interview questions from below. In the second, you have to assemble a working clock from old daydreams, and the hands only move when you tell the truth. By the final round, the stage turns into a long hallway of doors, each opening onto a version of your future. The judges—a trio of owls wearing tiny glasses—ask only one question: “What would you do if you could not fail?”

You open your mouth, and instead of words, thousands of paper cranes fly out, each carrying a small golden key. They gather at the ceiling and form a single job offer: YOUR NAME, written in light. You reach for it, and the floor melts into warm, dark water. You swim upward, fingertips almost touching the light, and then the alarm scatters everything. You wake with your hand still reaching, the taste of salt and possibility on your lips, and the certainty that the job—whatever it was—was already yours.
