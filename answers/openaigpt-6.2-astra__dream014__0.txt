“I don’t remember the address,” you say. “There was a pear tree. The front step wobbled.”

“That’s more useful than an address,” she says. She hands you the cake so she can button her coat.

You set off together. At each corner someone asks you a question. What did you hear from the bedroom window? A train dragging its brakes. What did the hallway smell like? Wet wool. Little by little the city obliges. A railway bridge appears at the end of a street. Rain begins to darken the shoulders of your coat. Then a hard little pear drops onto the cake and leaves a dent in the icing.

You’re standing outside a narrow green house. When you put your foot on the step, it wobbles.

“Will you come in?” you ask.

“We hoped you’d ask,” says the woman.

The key is in your pocket. Inside, you put on the kettle while someone hunts for plates. There aren’t enough forks, so you pass out teaspoons, and nobody minds. You give yourself the slice with the dent.
