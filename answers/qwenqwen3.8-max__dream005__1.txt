…each group seems to know a different version of the person you’re searching for.

One circle of people is laughing around a table covered in photographs, and when you look closer, every photo shows your friend in a place you’ve never seen them: a lighthouse, a desert, a train platform at night. They tell you, “They were just here,” in voices that overlap like echoes.

You move on.

Near the window, a group is whispering over a small glass bowl filled with water. Inside it, ripples keep forming the shape of your friend’s face, then dissolving before you can be sure. When you ask if they’ve seen them, someone hands you a key and says, “Only if you remember which door they walked through.”

But you don’t remember.

The room keeps shifting. Hallways stretch longer than they should. Doors open into other parties, each one slightly different: one where everyone is wearing masks, one where no one is speaking, one where all the guests are standing perfectly still, looking toward a distant music only you seem unable to hear.

Finally, in a quiet room at the end of a corridor, you find the one person you know.

They are sitting alone, calm, as if they have been waiting for you all night. When you ask why they were so hard to find, they smile and say, “Because you were looking for me instead of looking with me.”

Then the gathering fades into soft light, and you realize the party was never in a house at all, but inside a single shared dream—and now that you’ve found them, you can both wake up.
