You are in a maze filled with complex puzzles. Each puzzle requires intense concentration and logical thinking to solve, and as you move deeper into the maze, you...

...notice the maze begins to change.

At first the walls were cold, grey stone, tall and damp. The puzzles were mechanical - shifting tiles, rotating dials, doors with interlocking symbols that clicked loudly when you got them right. Each solution gave you a small rush of satisfaction and the path forward would grind open.

But as you move deeper, the air gets warmer. The stone walls soften. They are no longer stone at all.

They become bookshelves, then hedges heavy with flowers, then corridors of soft, humming light. The puzzles stop being *in front* of you and start being *around* you.

A door doesn't have a lock anymore. It has a question written in your own handwriting: *"When was the last time you felt completely calm?"*

A bridge doesn't need the right sequence of pressure plates. It needs you to remember the name of your childhood pet to appear.

You realize you are no longer solving the maze with logic. You are solving it with memory. With feeling.

The intense concentration you needed at the start begins to melt away, replaced by something quieter. Intuition. The harder you *try* to think, the more the walls seem to close in. The moment you let go, breathe, and just *know* - the walls exhale with you and a new path reveals itself.

Deep in the center of the maze, there is no final, impossible puzzle.

There is a small, sunlit courtyard. No walls at all. In the middle is a low table with one last puzzle - a simple wooden cube, unfinished, with one piece missing.

And you understand the missing piece has been in your pocket the whole time.

You place it. It doesn't click. It doesn't flash. It just fits, perfectly and silently.

The maze doesn't congratulate you. It simply lets you sit down. For the first time since you entered, there is nowhere else you need to go, and nothing else you need to figure out. The silence is complete and kind.

And somewhere, far away but getting closer, you can feel yourself waking up - bringing that feeling of quiet certainty back with you.
