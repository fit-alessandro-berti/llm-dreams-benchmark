Not your handwriting now — the fat, laborious print of a seven-year-old, each letter built separately like a small brick wall, the *a* a snail with a stick beside it. Someone has filled in every blank with tremendous confidence. *The capital of the thing is the thing.* *Show your work: I showed it to her and she said it was fine.*

You turn the page. Question 4 asks: **Where did you put it?**

You know exactly what it means. That's the terrible part. The knowledge sits in your chest like a swallowed coin, and you have no idea what the *it* is, only that you put it somewhere reasonable and reasonable places are the hardest to search.

You look up to ask the proctor and the proctor is your grandmother, dead eleven years, wearing the lanyard of a woman who works at the DMV. She mouths *no talking* with enormous tenderness and goes back to her crossword. The other students are all people you were once briefly close to. Their papers are blank. Their papers are also, somehow, finished.

The clock above the whiteboard has the right hands but the numbers keep politely trading places, the way guests do at a dinner where no one has been assigned a seat.

You start writing anyway. The pencil grows softer with each word until you're holding something warm, and the answers come out in your adult handwriting now, which surprises you, and what they say is: *I have been preparing for this since I was nine. I have been preparing for the wrong exam.*

Question 11 is a photograph of a hallway. You recognize the carpet.

Question 12 is simply: **Are you?**

And you sit there with the soft pencil and the coin in your chest, and you understand that this is the only question that counts, that it has always been the only question, and that the answer is not something you can put on paper — it's something you'd have to stand up and do, in front of everyone, in a room where no one is allowed to talk.

You put the pencil down.

You stand up.

The bell rings — and keeps ringing, and thins out into a beep, and the beep is your neighbor's truck reversing out of the driveway at 6:40 a.m., and the exam is still on the desk in the room you're now remembering, unsolved, patient, waiting for you the way those things wait.

Question 12 doesn't go away just because you woke up. That's the trick it plays.
