Thunder rolls across the sky, not as a boom, but like a deep, resonant cello chord vibrating up through the soles of your shoes. When the first drops finally fall, they aren't water. They are warm beads of silver light that chime like tiny glass bells whenever they strike the ferns.

Instinctively, you hurry to find shelter and notice a tall, weathered wooden door standing completely alone in a small clearing between two oaks. There are no walls attached to it—just the frame, the hinges, and a brass handle. You pull it open and step across the threshold, instantly leaving the roaring wind behind.

On the other side, the forest is gone. You are standing inside an endless, silent conservatory with a glass dome arching high overhead, where the violet storm rages harmlessly against the panes. At the center of the tiled floor sits a still reflecting pool, and drifting slowly across the water toward your feet is a small origami boat. As you crouch down to pick it up, you realize you are entirely weightless—and just as your fingers brush the paper, the water turns into swirling mist and you begin to float gently upward toward the storm.
