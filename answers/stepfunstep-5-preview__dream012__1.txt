…and you realize your hands are shaking, but not from fear. You grip the ship’s wheel—except it’s not wood; it’s warm and breathing, a pulse beneath your palms like a sleeping animal. The crew hasn’t spoken in minutes. Their faces are lit green by lightning, but their eyes are steady, fixed on you like compass needles.

You could shout orders. You could demand they bail water, tighten sails, pray to whatever gods they remember.

Instead, you close your eyes and listen.

The storm isn’t noise. It’s language. The thunder is a consonant, the crashing waves a vowel, the howling wind the breath between words. It’s saying something enormous and simple: *Let go.*

So you open your eyes and let go. You release the wheel and climb onto the bowsprit, standing at the very nose of the ship where the sea can reach you. The crew gasps, but you raise one hand—not to the sky, but to the water, palm open.

The ocean hesitates.

A wave rises like a cathedral wall, curls over you, and pauses. You can see through it. The water is transparent, crystalline, and inside the crest are thousands of tiny lights—memories, shipwrecks, wishes that sank. They aren’t drowning. They’re orbiting.

“Steady,” you say, but your voice has changed. It doesn’t just carry over the wind; it rides underneath it, turning the storm’s frequency into harmony. The wave lowers itself gently, deposits its lights onto the deck like offerings, and flattens into obsidian glass.

The clouds tear open—not to reveal sun, but to reveal more sea, an endless inverted ocean above where fish swim through constellations. Your ship hangs between two waters, a creature of iron and courage floating in the space between.

The crew stands. No one cheers. They simply stand taller, their shadows no longer cringing against the mast. They look at you not with hope, but with recognition. You’ve always been the captain. The storm was just the world asking you to remember.

And somewhere in the distance, though there is no shore in sight, you hear a bell.

You know, with the certainty of dreams, that it’s ringing just for you.
