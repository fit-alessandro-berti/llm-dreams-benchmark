...and you open your mouth to give the order, but what comes out is the sound of a cello.

It is a low, steady note, and somehow the crew understands it perfectly. The first mate nods gravely and begins lashing the mainsail with ribbons of silk. The cabin boy, who is also, you realize, your third-grade teacher, climbs the rigging with a lantern that burns blue.

The waves rise higher than cathedrals. You grip the wheel, but it is warm and soft, like bread fresh from the oven. When you turn it left, the ship turns up. You are sailing *into* the wall of water now, and instead of crashing it parts like a curtain, and you are inside the wave, where it is perfectly quiet.

Fish drift past the portholes, but they are wearing tiny spectacles and reading newspapers. One of them taps on the glass and mouths a word you almost recognize, a word you've been trying to remember your whole life. You lean closer. The glass fogs.

"Captain," says the first mate, who now has your mother's voice, "the map."

You unroll it on the deck. It is not a map of the sea. It is a map of the house you grew up in, every room drawn in careful ink, and in the hallway closet, where the winter coats used to hang, someone has marked a red X.

"That's where the storm is coming from," you say, and everyone agrees this makes sense.

The ship surfaces. The storm is still raging, but the rain falls slowly now, each drop taking a full second to land, and you can see your reflection in every one. The crew gathers around you, waiting. You know that if you can just say the right thing, the sea will calm.

You take a breath. You feel the words forming, heavy and golden, the most important words you've ever known.

And then the ship's bell begins to ring, and ring, and ring, and it sounds exactly like your alarm clock...
