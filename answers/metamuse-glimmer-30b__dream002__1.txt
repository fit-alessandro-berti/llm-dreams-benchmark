You are walking through a peaceful forest when suddenly the weather changes dramatically. You feel a storm approaching and the air gets heavy and cold in an instant.

The birds stop mid-song. The light turns greenish-gray as thick clouds roll in so fast you can watch them move, low and fast over the treetops. The pine needles are suddenly slick with cold mist, and every step you take sinks a little deeper into the moss than it should.

You try to find shelter, but the trees you knew a moment ago are different now. Their trunks are wider, their bark is wet and warm to the touch like skin. The path you were on has turned to a river of dark water that reflects a sky you don't recognize. It's still the same forest, but it's breathing.

The first thunder cracks and the ground answers with it. Roots shift and curl up like fingers, rearranging the path behind you so you can't go back. A smell like ozone and old pennies fills your nose. The wind doesn't howl, it whispers your name from different directions at once.

Then you see it: in the clearing ahead, where there should have been ferns, there's a door. Tall, wooden, with a brass handle, standing alone in the middle of the woods. It's ajar. Light pours out from it — not storm light, but warm, golden afternoon light, the light from before the storm.

You feel an overwhelming pull to go through it, even though the rain is starting to fall in thick sheets now, each drop cold enough to sting. Your feet are moving before you decide to. The storm is roaring all around you, but as you reach the door, the sound mutes to a soft hum, like it's happening to someone else.

You push it open.

And you wake up with the feeling of wet moss still under your palms.
