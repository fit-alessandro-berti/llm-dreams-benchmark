You are walking through a peaceful forest when suddenly the weather changes dramatically. You feel a storm approaching and the birds go silent all at once, as if someone has pressed mute on the world. The light turns a strange greenish-bronze, the color of old photographs.

The first raindrops fall upward.

You watch them rise past your face, defying gravity, and you feel no fear—only a deep curiosity that seems to come from somewhere outside yourself. The wind begins to speak in a voice you almost recognize, though you can't place it. It might be your grandmother's. It might be yours from years ago.

The trees start to lean away from you, bowing in unison like courtiers, revealing a path that wasn't there before—a corridor of stones leading toward a small house with warm yellow windows glowing despite the gathering dark.

Your feet move on their own. That's how it is in dreams; you are both the walker and the watched.

Inside the house, there is a long table set for dinner, and every chair is occupied by people whose faces you can't quite see—they shimmer, like reflections on moving water. One of them gestures to the empty seat beside them. You sit. Someone hands you a bowl of soup that tastes exactly like a memory of summer, though you couldn't say which one.

Outside, the storm finally breaks—not with thunder, but with a sound like a great exhale. And through the window, you see lightning strike a distant hill, and where it strikes, flowers bloom instantly across the whole slope, purple and gold, spreading like spilled paint.

Someone at the table laughs softly and says, *"It always does that here."*

And just as you turn to ask what they mean—

you wake, with the smell of rain still faintly in the room, and the strange certainty that somewhere, the flowers are still blooming.
