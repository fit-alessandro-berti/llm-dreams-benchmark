In one round, you're asked to give a speech in a language you've never studied—yet the words flow out of you like water, and only afterward do you realize your mouth was moving but no sound was coming out. The judges nod anyway, as if they heard everything.

In another, you're led down a hallway that keeps getting longer. The interview room is at the end, but every door you open leads back to the beginning of the hallway, where your childhood bedroom has somehow been installed. Your old teddy bear sits on the bed, wearing a tiny name tag that reads "ASSISTANT TO THE REGIONAL JUDGE."

The final challenge arrives without warning. You're handed a blank piece of paper and told: "Prove you deserve this." No instructions. No criteria. Just the paper and the weight of every eye in the room—hundreds of them, floating slightly above the faces they belong to, all watching you.

You pick up the pen. Your hand shakes. You start to write, and the letters crawl off the page like insects, reforming into sentences you never intended: *I don't deserve this. Someone else worked harder. They're going to find out I'm a fraud.*

The judges lean in. One of them smiles, and you realize with a jolt that it's you—an older version of you, calm and sure, wearing the exact job title you've always wanted on a laminated badge.

"You know," this older you says gently, "the prize was never the job."

Then what was it? you want to ask, but the floor tilts, and the room begins to dissolve like sugar in warm water. The floating eyes blink out one by one, like windows going dark in an office building at night. The last thing you see is the blank paper, now covered in your handwriting, but when you try to read it—

You wake up.

It's 6:47 a.m. Your alarm is about to go off. You lie there for a moment, heart pounding, the dream already slipping through your fingers like the paper's crawling words. You have a big presentation at work today. The one you've been anxious about all week.

You get up, make coffee, and realize the dream wasn't really about winning anything at all. It was about the moment before the blank page—when anything is still possible, and you haven't yet decided whether you believe in yourself.

You believe in yourself today. You decide that before the coffee's even done brewing.
