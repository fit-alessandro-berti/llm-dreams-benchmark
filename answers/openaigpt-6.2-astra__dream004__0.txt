a small brass plaque that reads: *Portrait, sitter unknown.*

You look around for the artist. A blue triangle is busy becoming a flock of pears. A hill folds itself into a green envelope and slides beneath the horizon. Nothing seems inclined to sit still for a portrait except you.

So you sit.

Slowly, a kitchen appears on the canvas. There is a chipped yellow cup on the table and a square of late sunlight on the floor. You know this kitchen. You know which drawer sticks and how to lift it by the handle so it closes without a bang. You haven't thought of it in years.

Something moves beneath the table. A little brown dog peers out, one ear up and one ear down.

Outside the frame, the sky turns plaid. Inside it, the dog waits.

You have forgotten his name. This seems a terrible thing until your hand, which remembers more than you do, taps twice against your knee. He scrambles out of the painting and into your lap, warm and absurdly heavy. His left ear smells of dust.

For a while you sit together as the world tries on its colors. Then the dog jumps down and trots toward a flight of stairs that only exists when you look away. You follow by walking backward, a trick you were very good at once.

Behind you, on the canvas, the yellow cup casts its long blue shadow. The brass plaque now reads: *Both sitters stepped out. Back shortly.*
