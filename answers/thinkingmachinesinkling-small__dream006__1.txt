...begin to speak, but the microphone is made of woven moonlight. Your first word doesn’t come out as sound—it unfolds as a silver fish that swims through the air and lands in the front row, where someone catches it like a bouquet.

The crowd doesn’t applaud; they lean in, and their faces begin to blur into soft clouds, then into pages of a book you haven’t written yet, then into fireflies that blink in unison with your heartbeat. You realize you don’t need notes, because the stage beneath your feet has become a river of paper, and you are floating slightly above it, not because you are weightless, but because the dream has decided you should be.

As you continue, your voice isn’t heard through ears—it’s tasted. Someone in the back stands up and offers you a cup of tea made from your own shadow. You begin to understand that this speech is not about policy or purpose, but about letting yourself dissolve. The audience is not human anymore; they are your own unspoken thoughts, gathered to listen. And when you finally reach the part where you’d normally say *thank you*, the stage opens like a mouth made of velvet, and you step inside the crowd, becoming part of their expectant, glowing, endless gaze.

You wake up with the taste of silver on your tongue.
