One of them, a woman with copper-colored eyes, tells you that in this city, “lost” just means your map is made of a different kind of dream. You realize the strangers aren’t guiding you to a single destination—they’re mirrors. Each one carries a piece of the direction you need: courage, memory, laughter, rest. Together, you weave a path of glowing stones across a bridge made of silver fog. Before you lies not a house or a street sign, but a garden growing out of your own chest—roots of calm, blossoms of a name you’ve been too tired to say out loud.

As the first light of an impossible dawn touches the skyline, the strangers begin to fade—not into sadness, but into the soft glow of stars becoming invisible, leaving you with only one gift: a small lantern that beats like a second heart, and the understanding that feeling lost was never a failure. It was the city’s only way of asking you to stop running.

You wake with the scent of rain in your hair and a quiet you haven’t felt in a long time. The bustling world outside is still loud, but now you carry the secret of the garden. You are not lost. You are found—not because someone saved you, but because they helped you remember that you were always the map you were searching for.
