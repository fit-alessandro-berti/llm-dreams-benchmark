Despite the hardships, you decide to become a keeper of small, stubborn things. You gather rusted cans, catch rain in torn tarps, and trade a bullet for a packet of tomato seeds. You walk at night to avoid raiders, follow dead power lines like old veins, and sleep in the ruins of a library where books have become insulation. Everywhere, people have forgotten how to hope, but you remember.

You plant the seeds in a cracked parking lot and water them with rationed drops. Days pass. Nothing. Then a green thread. Then another. The wasteland doesn’t heal, but it pauses. Others come, drawn by the impossible color. A child asks if she can help. You say yes.

And in that moment, the dream shifts: the sky is not ash but dawn. You wake with dirt under your nails and a seed in your palm, unsure which world is real.
