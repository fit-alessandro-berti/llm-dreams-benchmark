...you notice the puzzles becoming stranger.

The first rooms were mechanical—levers and counterweights, symbols to sequence, doors that opened only when you recited prime numbers backward. But now the walls have started to breathe. The puzzles have begun using things you recognize.

In one chamber, a chessboard stretches to the horizon, and the pieces are all people you've lost touch with. To advance, you must sacrifice one. You stand there for what feels like years, dream-years, the kind that pass in a blink, trying to find a piece you can bear to lose. Eventually you sacrifice yourself—the little pawn that looks oddly like you from a photograph you don't remember being taken—and the board dissolves into sand.

In the next chamber, the puzzle is a telephone. It rings. The voice on the other end asks you a question, but the question is in a language you don't speak, and yet you understand it perfectly: *What did you forget that you promised never to forget?* You open your mouth to answer and realize you've forgotten the answer. That was the puzzle. There is no solving it. You walk through anyway, because in dreams, unsolvable doors still open for those who stop trying to solve them.

Deeper now. The maze has stopped pretending to be architecture. The corridors are made of arguments you lost, replaying in wallpaper patterns. The floor tiles are the faces of clocks, each showing a different time from your life, and you step carefully, choosing which moments to walk on. You find yourself avoiding one tile in particular—a small yellow clock showing 3:47 on some afternoon you can't place—and you understand, without knowing how, that stepping on it would make you remember something you've spent a long time not remembering.

The puzzles require intense concentration, yes, but the concentration has changed. It's no longer logic. It's the kind of focus you need to hold a soap bubble without popping it, or to keep your face still when someone says something that cuts deep. The maze isn't testing your intelligence anymore. It's testing your willingness.

At the center—or what feels like the center, though in dreams the center moves like everything else—there is a final door. No lock. No riddle. Just a small sign, handwritten in your own handwriting:

*"You only had to ask."*

And you realize you've been pushing every door in this maze when all of them pulled.

You reach for the handle, and—

—you're awake. 3:47 AM. The ceiling of your room. The dream already dissolving the way dreams do, sand through fingers, and the only thing you retain is a strange, persistent feeling that you left something unfinished. Not a puzzle.

A door you finally learned to open, in a maze you'll never find again.
