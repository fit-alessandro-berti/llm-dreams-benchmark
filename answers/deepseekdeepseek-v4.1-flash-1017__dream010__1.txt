…various challenges and the dream keeps shifting its rules. First you’re in a glass elevator racing up a building that has no top floor, answering trivia questions while everyone you’ve ever met watches from the lobby. Then you’re in a maze made of filing cabinets, where every drawer contains a version of your résumé—some impressive, some embarrassing, some written in a language made entirely of deadlines.

The other contestants are strangely familiar: a childhood version of you, a stranger wearing your future face, and a person who keeps saying, “You don’t really want this—you just want to be chosen.” You lose a round when you can’t explain your greatest weakness without apologizing for it. You win the next when you help a rival find a door they missed. The scoreboard flickers between numbers and words like *brave*, *tired*, *almost*, and *ready*.

For the final challenge, you enter a room with a single desk. On it sits your dream job, but it isn’t a contract or a title—it’s a small, glowing object that changes shape when you look at it: a paintbrush, a microphone, a map, a key, a pair of hands. A voice asks, “What will you give up to have this?” You realize every previous challenge was asking the same question in disguise. You think of sleep, certainty, the safe answer, the version of yourself who never tries. You reach out—but instead of taking the prize, you ask it, “What do you need from me?”

The room goes quiet. The object turns into a door. You open it and step through into a life that doesn’t look like a victory ceremony at all—it looks like mornings, mistakes, practice, and a thousand small choices. The competition dissolves around you, and the announcer’s voice becomes your own: “The grand prize was never a job. It was the courage to keep choosing the work that makes you feel awake.”

Then the dream gives you one last test: a stack of application forms appears, and every box is already filled in with your name. You laugh, sign the first page, and wake up just before the alarm goes off—carrying the strange, steady feeling that the competition is still continuing, but now you know how to play.
