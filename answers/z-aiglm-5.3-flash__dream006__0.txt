...speak.

The words come easily — too easily. You're reciting the lyrics to a song you haven't heard since childhood, and no one seems to mind. The audience sways gently, like seaweed in a current.

You glance down at your notes. The pages are blank, but as you watch, words begin rising to the surface like bubbles through dark water. You can't read them fast enough, but somehow it doesn't matter. The crowd is patient. The crowd is always patient.

Your microphone flutters in your hand — it's become a small gray bird, and it flies off toward the ceiling. You cup your hands to your mouth instead, and your voice carries perfectly, warm and enormous.

In the third row, your third-grade teacher raises her hand. When she opens her mouth to ask a question, goldfish swim out. One lands in your palm, soft and blinking, and you understand it completely, the way you understand everything in this place: *it's almost time.*

The lights dim. The audience rises, applauding, and their faces soften and brighten into candle flames — hundreds of them, gold and flickering in the dark. You feel lighter than you've ever felt. You look down and your shoes are no longer touching the stage.

You float up past the exit signs, past the ceiling tiles, into a wide blue—

You wake with your heart drumming, sheets tangled around your legs like something that tried to keep you there. Through the window, a bird is singing, and it takes you a sleepy moment to realize it's the melody from your speech.

The one you never actually wrote.
