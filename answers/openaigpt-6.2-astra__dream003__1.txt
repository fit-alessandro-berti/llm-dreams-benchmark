a tiny brass keyhole that keeps drifting out from under your thumb. You turn the box over. The keyhole slides around the corner. From inside comes an unmistakable giggle.

“All right,” you say. “I won’t peek.”

With your eyes shut, you feel the patterns rise into little houses, a bridge, the chipped rim of a fountain. Your finger knows the way. Left at the fountain, twice around the mulberry tree, then up three steps to the house where you grew up. Without thinking, you lift the corner of the mat.

There’s the key.

The lid opens onto your old kitchen. Rain ticks against the window. Under the table sits a child with your face, holding a much smaller box.

“What’s in that one?” you ask.

The child tips it toward you. Inside, no bigger than a postage stamp, is the room where you fell asleep. You can just make out your own hand curled on the pillow.

“Watch this,” the child whispers, and pokes your palm.

You wake up laughing. In your fist is a single ripe mulberry, warm from the sun.
