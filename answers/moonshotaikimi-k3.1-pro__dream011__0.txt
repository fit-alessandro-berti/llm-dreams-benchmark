…not about the course at all.

The first reads: **Describe the moment you first mistook fear for ambition.**

The second: **If your future self entered the room now, would you recognize them? Show your work.**

You look up, but the other students are writing calmly, their pens scratching like rain. At the front of the hall, the proctor turns around. It is you—older, tired, wearing a coat you have never owned.

“Time remaining?” you ask.

The proctor points to the clock. It has no hands, only a small door where the twelve should be.

You turn back to the paper. A final question has appeared:

**You may leave whenever you wish. What makes you stay?**

You write *the career*, but the words vanish. You write *my parents*, then *the money*, then *because I’ve come this far*. Each answer disappears, a little more slowly than the last.

Outside the windows, the campus begins to dissolve. The library folds itself into a paper bird. The lecture halls drift away like icebergs. Still, the pens scratch on.

At last, you write: **I don’t know who I’ll be if I stop.**

The older you walks down the aisle and places a hand on your desk.

“Neither do I,” they say. “That was never one of the questions.”

The clock’s little door opens. Morning light pours out, followed by the sound of your alarm.

As the exam hall disappears, you notice one sentence written across the back of your hand in fading blue ink:

**Begin anywhere.**
