You are at a large social gathering where you only know one person. As you try to find them, you encounter various groups of people and

...each group seems to have been waiting for you, though no one says so.

The first cluster stands beneath a chandelier made of tiny hanging teacups. They're debating whether Tuesday is a color or a temperature. When you walk up, a woman in a green velvet coat hands you a glass and says, "You're just in time. We need a tiebreaker." You hear yourself answer, "Tuesday is a temperature. Lukewarm." They nod gravely, as if you've settled something that has gone on for years.

You slip away into a hallway that is longer than the house should allow. A group of men in mismatched shoes is passing a single lit candle from hand to hand, whispering names. One of them leans toward you and says, "Yours is coming up soon." You don't stay to hear it.

In the next room, the floor is slightly tilted, and everyone leans at the same angle without noticing. A girl with silver paint on her hands is tracing a map of the party on the wall, and you see that it's wrong. There are more rooms on her map than you've walked through, and in the farthest corner she's drawn a tiny door with a star above it. "That's where they are," she says, not looking at you. "The person you know."

You realize you can't remember their face, only the feeling of their laugh, like a screen door banging in summer wind. You follow the map anyway.

The crowd thins, and the music warps into something slower and underwater. You pass a circle of people all wearing your childhood nicknames on name tags. You pass a piano playing itself. Finally you reach a small door with a star painted above it, slightly crooked, as though someone did it in a hurry.

You put your hand on the knob. It's warm.

Behind you, the whole party goes quiet. You can feel every face turn toward your back, and you know that whatever is on the other side, you've been heading there since before you arrived.

You turn the knob, and the laugh you've been looking for spills out, like sunlight through a screen door.
