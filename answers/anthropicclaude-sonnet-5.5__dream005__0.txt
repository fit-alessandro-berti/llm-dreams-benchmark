...the room keeps rearranging itself every time you look away.

The first group you come across is gathered around a grand piano with no keys. They take turns pressing their palms against the smooth black lid, and somehow music comes out. A woman in a green coat turns and says, "You're late, but you're right on time," and everyone nods as if that makes sense. You nod too.

You push on. The ceiling is higher now, and the chandeliers are slowly lowering and rising like jellyfish. In the next cluster, a man is handing out small folded maps of the party. You open yours and find it's a map of your childhood home, with a little red X in the kitchen. "That's where they are," he whispers. You know he means the person you're looking for, though you can't quite remember their face anymore. You only remember how it feels when they laugh.

You drift past a circle of people speaking in a language made entirely of weather. One says "drizzle, drizzle, thunder," and everyone else looks heartbroken. Another group is playing cards with a deck of unlabeled mirrors. A child in a tuxedo tries to sell you a minute of silence for the price of a secret. You give him a small one, about a dentist appointment you never went to, and he tucks it in his pocket with a solemn bow.

Then you hear it, the laugh, from behind a tall door you're certain wasn't there a moment ago. The door is warm under your hand. The handle turns before you can.

Inside is the kitchen from the map, quiet and golden, with the smell of toast. Your friend is sitting on the counter swinging their feet, holding two mugs, and they say, as if no time has passed at all, "There you are. I saved you a seat."

The only chair in the room is one you recognize as yours, though you've never seen it before. As you sit, the party hums on behind the door, softer and softer, like the ocean in a shell.

Would you like me to keep going, maybe toward a twist, a nightmare turn, or a waking-up ending?
