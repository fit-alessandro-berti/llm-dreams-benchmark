...and none of them quite notice you at first.

You are in a house that is too big to be a house. Or maybe it's a museum after hours, or a hotel ballroom — it keeps shifting when you're not looking directly at it. There are warm lights strung overhead and the murmur of a hundred conversations, clinking glasses, soft music from a band you can never quite see. Everyone is dressed like they all agreed on a theme you weren't told about.

You start moving through the crowd looking for the one familiar face.

The first group you pass is gathered in a tight circle around a low table, laughing at a joke that was told just before you arrived. As you approach, they all turn and smile at you politely, expectantly, as if you're supposed to deliver a punchline. You open your mouth, but you can't remember what was funny. They keep smiling. You keep walking, and you can hear them resume laughing behind you, but now it sounds like static.

The second group is standing by an impossibly long buffet table. The food is all things you almost recognize — fruit that is too shiny, cakes that breathe faintly. The people here aren't talking at all. They are all watching the entrance, just like you, searching for someone. One of them leans over and whispers, *"Have you seen her? I thought she was right here,"* and for a second you're sure they're looking for the same person you are.

Deeper in, the music gets louder and the lights get dimmer. There's a group dancing very slowly in a circle, even though the song is fast. In the center of their circle is an empty chair with a coat draped over it — *your* friend's coat. You are sure of it. You push through the dancers to grab it, but when you reach the chair, it's just a coat someone left behind, and the dancers have closed the circle around you. They aren't dancing anymore. They're just looking at you kindly, curiously, like you're the guest of honor and you've forgotten your speech.

Suddenly the crowd parts on its own and you see them.

Your one person. They are across the room, by a large window that looks out onto an ocean that shouldn't be there. They haven't seen you yet. They are talking and laughing easily, completely at home, as if they belong to this place.

You call out to them, or try to — but your voice doesn't work right. It's as if you're calling from underwater. You start to run, but the room stretches. The more steps you take toward them, the more groups of people appear between you: people with your childhood friends' faces, people whispering your name wrong, people who all look strangely like you.

And then your friend finally looks up. They see you. Their face lights up with relief — the same relief you feel — as if they had been searching for you the whole time too.

You wake up just before you reach them.
