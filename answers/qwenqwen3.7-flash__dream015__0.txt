...protect the impossible thing growing in the hollow of your palm.

It isn't food, and it isn't a weapon. It is a tiny, crystalline bud that hums with a frequency you feel in your teeth rather than hear. In this world of gray ash and twisted metal, its glow is violent and holy, casting long, dancing shadows that seem to have minds of their own. The shadows writhing at the edges of your vision—the scavengers who trade memories for bullets, the skittering constructs that hunt by thermal echo—are drawn to it, their hunger palpable.

But the dream logic holds you firm. You know, with absolute certainty, that if you crush this bud, the sun will never rise again. If you drop it, the wind will carry its essence away forever. If you keep it hidden, the hope it emits will eventually fade into the ambient misery of the wasteland.

So, you decide to grow it.

You dig into the pocket of your tattered coat and pull out a handful of soil salvaged from the grave of an ancient library. It still smells of vanilla and old paper. You kneel on the rubble of a skyscraper jutting out like a broken tooth toward a bruised, violet sky. As you press the soil around the crystal stem, the ground beneath you trembles.

A drone, shaped like a metallic spider with a single camera eye for a head, descends silently, its servos whirring. It raises a manipulator arm tipped with a scalpel. The danger is immediate, lethal.

Yet, in the dream, you do not fear. You simply speak to the drone, though your voice sounds like thunder rolling through a canyon. *"Prune,"* you command. *"Make room for the light."*

The drone freezes. Its eye dilates, cycling through colors until it settles on the same violet as the sky. It lowers the scalpel and extends a different tool: shears of polished silver. With delicate precision, it clips a dying leaf from a nearby withered oak tree and places it beside the sprout, then retreats, melting back into the gloom. The wasteland itself seems to pause, holding its breath.

As the first true drop of nectar forms on the bud, it drips onto the steel floor of the building. Where the drop lands, the metal doesn't corrode; it blooms. Flowers of iridescent ceramic unfurl in seconds, releasing spores that drift upward. Each spore carries a fragment of a memory: a laugh, a hug, the taste of rain on hot pavement, the sound of a cello.

The spores rise into the smog, dissolving the gray clouds. The atmosphere clears. For the first time in decades, a beam of unfiltered sunlight pierces the crust of the world, striking the ceramic flowers. They resonate, turning the sound of the wind into a harmonious chord that vibrates through your bones, rewriting the code of decay into a rhythm of renewal.

The horror of the world begins to recede. The jagged silhouettes of the ruins soften, morphing into rolling hills and distant forests in your mind's eye. The scarcity vanishes; your pockets fill not with cans or fuel, but with seeds that pulse with the same warmth as the bud.

You realize then that the apocalypse was merely a sleep cycle, a nightmare fed by fear and neglect. You are not just a survivor; you are the gardener of a new reality. The bud opens, revealing not a flower, but a mirror.

In the reflection, you see yourself, standing in a bedroom bathed in morning light. On the nightstand, a digital clock flips from 3:00 AM to 3:01 AM. Your heart beats a steady, strong rhythm against your ribs.

You take a deep breath, filling your lungs with air that smells of coffee and dust motes dancing in real sunlight, and you smile. The dream is ending, but the seed you planted in the ashes has taken root in your waking mind, waiting for you to build it once more.
