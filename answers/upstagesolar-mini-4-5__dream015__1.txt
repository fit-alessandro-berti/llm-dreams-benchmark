I gathered dry twigs from beneath the overhanging branches, my fingers numb and clumsy. The wind carried the smell of ash and something older—decay, maybe, or the ghost of a world that no longer existed.

I crouched there, warming my hands, and watched the light flicker across the ruined cityscape. Skyscrapers had been stripped of glass, their skeletons jutting into a bruised sky. Somewhere far off, I heard the low growl of something with too many legs. I froze until it passed.

Then I noticed the door—a rusted bulkhead half-buried in rubble, hanging ajar. Inside, in the dark, I could see the faint outline of shelves. My stomach tightened, but I pushed the door open anyway.

The shelf held jars. Glass, tarnished, but intact. Inside each one, things that looked like food—though my mind refused to name them. I took the closest one, felt its weight, and held it against the firelight. It caught the flame for a moment, reflecting a world that might still be remembered.

I ate slowly, listening. The fire crackled. The city groaned. And in the space between, I decided to hope—just a little—that tomorrow, I might find a door that led somewhere different.
