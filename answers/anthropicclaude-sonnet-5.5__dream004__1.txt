...a single brush hovering in the air beside it, as if it had been waiting for you.

The ground beneath you ripples from violet to tangerine to a green that has no name, and the sky folds itself into origami cranes that unfold again into clouds. The canvas is the only still thing here. Its whiteness hums quietly, like a held breath.

You reach for the brush. It's warm, and it pulses faintly like a small heartbeat. The moment your fingers close around it, the landscape goes quiet. The shifting slows, and the colors lean in to watch.

You touch the bristles to the canvas, and a single line spills out. It isn't paint, exactly. It's more like a *decision*. The line curls into a hill, and the real hill behind you shudders and rearranges itself to match. You paint a door, and a door-shaped hush opens in the air, leaking golden light and the smell of rain on warm stone.

You realize the world hasn't been changing randomly. It has been *searching*, cycling through every shape and color, waiting for someone to choose.

So you paint slowly and carefully: a river that runs uphill, a tree with lanterns for fruit, a small boat with room for exactly one passenger. Then, in the corner, almost as an afterthought, you paint a tiny figure standing at the edge of everything, looking out.

The figure turns and waves at you.

The canvas ripples like water, and the brush slips from your fingers, dissolving into a swirl of color. The boat you painted bobs against the edge of the frame, its rope trailing out into the air toward your hand.

Somewhere far off, the cranes are folding themselves into a new sky, and you understand that the dream is asking you one last question: *Do you step into the boat, or do you keep painting?*

What do you choose?
