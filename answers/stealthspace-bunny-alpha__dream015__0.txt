Despite the hardships, you decide to build a boat.

The sea was only a rumor by then—something elders mentioned in whispers and children mistook for a myth. You had a rusted cargo frame, two sealed storage tanks, a sail stitched from radiation-warning sheets, and a compass that spun uselessly whenever you tried to steer.

You built at night, when the wind carried less dust and the ruined skyline seemed less likely to watch. By day, you scavenged for wire, tools, and fuel. Twice, figures appeared between the shattered buildings. Twice, you hid beneath the carcass of an old bus, listening to boots crunch across glass before moving on.

On the final evening, you dragged your boat onto the salt flats.

The moon hung low and white. Your useless compass trembled in your hand, then pointed—not north, but toward the horizon.

You stepped aboard.

“I believe there is water,” you said.

The ground made a sound like a long-held breath.

Cracks split the salt. The ruins trembled. Then the ocean rose through them, lifting your boat above the drowned streets and filling the empty world with the scent of rain. Reflections of vanished buildings slid beneath the hull. The sails caught a wind that smelled of salt and flowers.

On the distant shore, figures waited beside bonfires no one had lit.

You looked back at the wasteland—the broken towers, the empty roads, the narrow horizon that had seemed permanent. Then you turned toward the impossible dawn spreading across the water.

Behind you, the ruins shrank to a smudge of ash.

Ahead, the whole world waited to begin again.
