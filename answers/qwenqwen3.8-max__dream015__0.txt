Despite the hardships, you decide to follow the faint green light flickering beyond the ruined skyline.

You carry almost nothing: a cracked canteen, a rusted key, and a seed wrapped in a scrap of cloth. The road beneath your feet is gray with ash, and the wind moves through the hollow buildings as if whispering the names of places that no longer exist. Every shadow feels hungry. Every silence feels like something waiting.

Still, you keep walking.

Near dusk, you find a shelter made from salvaged doors and sheets of corrugated metal. Inside, a small fire burns in a metal drum, and a few strangers sit around it, sharing a can of peaches as though it were treasure. They do not ask your name, but they make room for you. One of them points toward the old library at the edge of the city and says, “There’s a vault down there. Something still grows.”

That night, you dream within the dream: you are running through flooded halls, clutching the rusted key, while something unseen echoes behind you. You reach a heavy iron door and fit the key into its lock. It turns with a soft click.

Inside, there are no weapons, no supplies, no forgotten gold. There is only sunlight pouring through broken glass, rows of living soil, and green sprouts rising from the earth. The air smells like rain and roots. You kneel, unwrap the seed you have carried so long, and press it into the ground beside the others.

As soon as you do, the ruins around you begin to soften. Vines climb the walls. The city breathes again. Somewhere far above, birds take flight.

You wake with dirt under your fingernails and the taste of morning in your mouth.
