# The Dream

You wake up in a completely different era, with no modern technology around. As you explore your new surroundings, you realize you need to **figure out where — and *when* — you are.**



The morning light filters through the window of a small stone cottage. Rough-hewn wooden beams stretch across a low ceiling. A fire smolders in an open hearth, and the smell of something simmering in a black iron pot fills the air.

Your clothes are gone. In their place, you wear a simple linen tunic and woolen trousers. There's a leather pouch on the rough wooden table, and inside you find a few unfamiliar coins — heavy, stamped with profiles of faces you don't recognize.

You step outside into a narrow, muddy lane lined with timber-framed houses. Chickens scatter underfoot. A blacksmith hammers rhythmically down the road. A woman carrying water glances at you with weary, tired eyes and says something — but the language is one you can barely follow, thick with vowels and rolling consonants.

Then a bell begins to ring.

It's deep, resonant — a church bell. Everyone stops. A man gestures urgently toward a stone structure at the end of the lane, and the crowd begins to move. People are *scared*. They clutch at crosses around their necks and hurry toward the church.

A child tugs at your sleeve and points upward.

You look.

In the pale morning sky, visible even in daylight — a faint, ghostly **tail of light** streaks across the heavens. A comet.

A woman near you whispers through trembling lips: *"The star of fire returns… the Black King's herald."*

And suddenly, fragments click into place. The clothing. The coins. The comet. You're in **medieval Europe** — somewhere around the turn of the first millennium. The people here believe comets are omens of doom, of plagues and wars to come.

A soldier in chainmail and a tabard bearing a crest you don't recognize grabs your arm roughly and drags you toward the crowd. *"You — where were you last night? The elder says a stranger appeared with no wagon, no horse, no name. You will answer to the lord."*

You realize the terrifying truth:

You need to **survive this world without anything you know — no electricity, no medicine, no phone, no knowledge of who rules this land or what wars loom on the horizon.**

The crowd surges toward the church. The comet hangs overhead like a promise of catastrophe.

And somewhere deep in your chest, a voice — your own voice, but from somewhere older and wilder — whispers: *"You didn't come here by accident."*

The dream shifts.

You are standing at a crossroads — one path leading back toward the village, toward the safety of the unknown crowd. The other leading into a dark, ancient forest, where something waits — something that *called* you here.

The bell is still ringing.

And the child is still holding your sleeve.

You must **choose**. 🌑

---

*...and just as your hand reaches out toward one of the paths, the ground begins to tremble — and the dream folds in on itself like paper.*
