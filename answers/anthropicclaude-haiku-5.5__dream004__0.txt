...and as your fingers brush its surface, the white begins to hum. It is not silent but warm, like the moment before a song remembers its first note. You lean closer, and a single line of silver drifts across the canvas, thin as a thought, then curls back on itself and becomes a door.

The door is the exact blue of a robin's egg, though it has no frame, and it opens inward onto a room made entirely of falling leaves. They spiral upward instead of down, catching the light and releasing it as small notes of music. Somewhere a clock is ticking, but each tick is a different shade of green.

You step through. The floor gives beneath you like wet sand, and where your footprints form, they bloom into tiny gardens that whisper your name in a language you somehow understand. A figure stands at the far end of the room, tall and shifting, made of the colors that have been forgotten by the waking world: the orange of a sound, the violet of an old memory, a yellow that tastes like the word *home*.

"You're late," it says, though its voice comes from the canvas behind you, from the leaves, from the hum in your own chest. "The painting has been waiting for someone who knows how to finish a sentence that was never started."

You look down at your hands. They are holding a brush, its bristles dripping with light that has no name yet. Every stroke you make rearranges the room: the walls soften into a sea, the sea lifts into a sky, and the sky folds itself into a staircase that climbs toward a moon made of unopened letters.

You begin to paint the first step, and as you do, you realize the canvas is painting you back.
