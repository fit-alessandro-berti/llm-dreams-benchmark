as you move deeper into the maze, you begin to realize that the walls are not stone but memory—each puzzle is a moment from your waking life, slightly rearranged. The more you solve, the more you forget; names, faces, and directions fall away like dust. At the center of the maze, you find a mirror that does not reflect you, but a child’s drawing of a door. You trace the outline with your finger, and the drawing becomes warm. A voice—your own, but softer—says, “You don’t have to solve your way out of a dream.” You step through the drawing and fall upward through layers of color and sound until you wake, heart racing, with the distinct feeling that some puzzle in you has just been solved.
