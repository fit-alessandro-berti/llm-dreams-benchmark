You start to run.

The streets are strange—buildings made of glass and old stone stitched together like a patchwork quilt. Signs twist into letters you almost recognize, then melt before you can read them. You check your phone: 11:47. The meeting is at noon. But there’s no map, no address, no name.

You stop a passerby. She turns slowly, her face a blur of warm light, and says, “You’re already late.” Then she points to a narrow alley between two towers. “But the elevators still work, if you remember the password.”

You sprint into the alley. The ground hums under your feet. At the end, there’s a single, brass elevator with no buttons, only a small plaque engraved with a single word: *Remember.*

You step inside. The doors close. You feel the elevator sink, not rise. When they open again, you’re in a room full of clocks—hundreds of them, all ticking out of sync, each showing a different time. In the center stands a table. On it, a folder with your name.

You open it. Inside is a single paper with one sentence:

*You were never meant to find the meeting. The meeting was meant to find you.*

And then you hear footsteps behind you. You turn.

You wake up, breathless, at 8:00 a.m.—with an hour before the meeting, and a strange, familiar address on your left hand, written in ink you don’t remember getting.
