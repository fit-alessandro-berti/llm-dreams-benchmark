You feel a storm approaching and the trees begin to whisper your name.

The leaves overhead twist into dark silver as the wind rises, carrying the scent of rain and something older—like stone, smoke, and forgotten memories. You hurry forward, but the forest path stretches longer with every step, the soft moss beneath your feet turning into a glowing trail of pale blue light.

Thunder rolls, but it doesn’t sound like thunder. It sounds like a door closing somewhere far away.

Then the first raindrop falls. When it touches your hand, it doesn’t feel wet—it feels like a tiny spark, warm and alive. More drops fall, each one landing around you like falling stars, and where they touch the ground, small lanterns bloom from the earth.

You realize the storm isn’t dangerous. It’s searching for something.

At the center of the forest, the trees part to reveal a quiet lake. The sky above it is split in two: one side black with storm clouds, the other bright with morning sunlight. Standing at the edge of the water is a figure made entirely of rain, watching you.

It holds out its hand.

In its palm rests a small key, shimmering as if made from lightning.

You understand, without knowing how, that the key opens the part of the dream you have been avoiding.

The storm grows louder.

The rain figure waits.

And you take a step forward.
