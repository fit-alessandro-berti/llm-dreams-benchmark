"Good," she says. "Then you understand. It's due yesterday."

She hands you a folder. It's heavier than it should be, warm, and faintly humming. When you open it, the pages are blank, but the moment you look away, text crawls across them like ants. You catch fragments: *Quarterly projections for the ocean. Rebrand the moon. Please include a slideshow.*

You turn to your desk, but your desk is no longer where you left it. The office has become longer, much longer, a corridor of cubicles stretching toward a horizon where the fluorescent lights curve down like a sunset. Somewhere far away, a phone rings in the rhythm of your heartbeat.

You start walking. Your coworkers look up as you pass, and each of them is someone you knew in elementary school, now wearing lanyards and holding coffee mugs that say WORLD'S OKAYEST EMPLOYEE. One of them, the kid who sat behind you in third grade, whispers, "You didn't study for this, did you?" You realize, with a sinking dread, that you're also supposed to take a math test in an hour, and you haven't been to that class all semester.

At last you find a desk. It might be yours. The chair is made of jelly, but it holds you. You turn on the computer, and the screen shows a clock counting *up* instead of down. That seems worse, somehow.

You type furiously, but the keyboard's letters keep rearranging themselves. Every word you write comes out as the name of a vegetable. *Dear stakeholders, cauliflower cauliflower turnip.* You delete it. You try again. *Leek.*

Your boss appears at your shoulder. She's taller now, tall enough that her head brushes the ceiling tiles, and her voice echoes as though from the bottom of a well.

"How's it coming?"

"Great," you say. "Almost done." You gesture at the screen, which now shows a video of a golden retriever skateboarding.

She squints. She leans in closer. Then she smiles. "This is exactly what the client wanted."

Relief floods through you. It is warm and golden and smells like cinnamon toast. The whole office erupts into applause. Confetti falls from the air vents. Someone brings out a cake shaped like the folder, still humming.

But as you reach for a slice, you notice the clock on the wall. Its hands are spinning faster and faster, and the ringing phone is getting louder, closer, more insistent, until it isn't a phone at all, but...

...your alarm.

You open your eyes. Morning light is leaking through the curtains. Your heart is still racing, and for a moment you lie there, half-convinced there's a folder humming somewhere under your pillow.

Then you remember it's Saturday.

You roll over, pull the blanket up to your chin, and decide the ocean's quarterly projections can wait.
