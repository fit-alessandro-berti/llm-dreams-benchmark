...you notice intricate patterns and realize, after a moment, that they're moving.

Fine threads of silver drift beneath the dark surface like fish under ice, tangling into spirals whenever your eyes try to follow them. The box is warm — warmer than it has any right to be, warm the way a sleeping animal is warm.

The lock has no keyhole. Instead there's a small shape pressed into the brass: a tight, whorled spiral. A fingerprint. Yours.

You press your thumb against it. The silver threads surge toward your hand, gathering under your skin until you can see them glowing faintly through your own fingers — which ought to frighten you, but in the way of dreams, it feels less like something happening and more like something being remembered.

A click. Not the lock — something deeper, like a heartbeat starting somewhere inside.

You lift the lid. No gold, no letter, no answers. Only a darkness that smells impossibly of rain on hot pavement, of the hallway of a house you lived in when you were seven, of someone's coat brought in from the cold. The smell of every time you were ever completely safe.

And deep in that darkness, something small shifts, and sighs — contentedly, patiently — as if it has been waiting a very long time for you to come home.

You reach in.

The dream folds closed around you like a hand, gentle as anything, and you wake with your fingers curled around empty air and the ghost of silver light still swimming behind your eyelids.
