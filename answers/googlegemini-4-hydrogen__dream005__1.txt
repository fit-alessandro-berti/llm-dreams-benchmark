...each cluster seems to belong to a slightly different reality. 

At first, the room feels like a standard, crowded house party. You squeeze past a circle of people in sharp evening wear gathered earnestly around an empty punch bowl. They are nodding and laughing in perfect unison, but as you lean in to hear what’s so funny, you realize they aren’t speaking at all—their mouths are moving to the faint, rhythmic sound of crashing ocean waves.

You spot a flash of your friend’s bright yellow sweater disappearing through an archway on the far side of the room. You call their name, though your voice comes out thin and weightless, barely carrying past your own shoulders. 

Hurrying after them, you step through the archway and find that the living room has given way to a long, carpeted hotel corridor that stretches much farther than the house should allow. A group of people from your old high school—though you can't quite remember any of their names—are sitting cross-legged along the walls, quietly passing around a glowing glass orb. As you walk past, one of them looks up and hands you a set of car keys, whispering, *"Don't let the tide come in."* You take the keys without questioning why.

Ahead, the yellow sweater slips around another corner. You break into a clumsy, heavy-legged run, the kind where your feet feel like they’re wading through thick mud. 

You round the corner and push open a heavy wooden door, expecting a kitchen or a patio. Instead, you step out onto a wide, moonlit pier. The party noise vanishes instantly, replaced by cool air and the creak of wet timber. The wooden planks are crowded with silent figures standing shoulder to shoulder, all staring up at a sky filled with two pale moons. 

Right at the edge of the pier, illuminated by a single hanging lantern, stands the yellow sweater. 

Relief washes over you. You weave through the silent crowd and tap their shoulder. When they turn around, however, their face is blank—not frightening, just smoothed over like an unpainted canvas. 

You look down at your own hands, still holding the car keys, and notice for the first time that you are wearing the exact same yellow sweater. A sudden, quiet realization settles over you as the figure tilts its head and asks, in your own voice, *"Wait—I thought I was looking for you?"*

Before you can answer, the lantern swings in the breeze, and the pier slowly begins to drift away from the shore.
