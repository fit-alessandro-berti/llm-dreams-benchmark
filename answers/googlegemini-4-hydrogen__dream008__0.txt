You glance up nervously, expecting impatience, only to find that your boss has silently morphed into your third-grade gym teacher, holding a stopwatch the size of a dinner plate. "We need the quarterly projections before the tide comes in," they say, pointing toward the breakroom, where ankle-deep ocean water is already seeping under the door.

Desperate, you stare at your monitor. The spreadsheet is completely blank, save for a blinking cursor and a tiny, photorealistic drawing of a sandwich sitting in cell B4. You frantically mash the piano keys, hoping numbers will appear. Instead, the screen begins filling with the lyrics to a song you haven't heard since childhood, alongside architectural blueprints for a treehouse.

The water is up to your knees now, swirling with stray paperclips and floating Post-it notes. You notice you aren't wearing any shoes. The stopwatch chimes—a loud, metallic clang. Time is up.

Trembling, you hit "Enter." 

Your computer lets out a satisfied sigh, folds itself neatly into an origami crane, and glides across the water into your boss's hands. They inspect the paper wings with intense, corporate scrutiny, then nod slowly. "Incredible work," they say, their voice echoing as if you're standing in a vast cathedral. "You've saved the quarter. Now, you just have to deliver it upstairs."

You turn around and see a spiral staircase made of glowing glass rising straight through the office ceiling into a starry night sky. You take the first step, feeling suddenly weightless and profoundly relieved. As you climb higher, a strange, insistent ringing begins to chime from somewhere inside your pocket—louder and louder, shaking the stars loose—until you blink your eyes open to the sound of your morning alarm.
