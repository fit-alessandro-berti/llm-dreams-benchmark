...a single brush lying beside it, its bristles still wet with a color that has no name.

You pick it up. The handle is warm, as if someone just set it down, though there's no one for miles. Only the hills, which ripple from violet to amber like breathing animals, and the sky, which keeps folding itself into origami cranes that fly off before they're finished.

You touch the brush to the canvas.

The color spills outward on its own, blooming into a doorway. Not a painting of a door, but an actual door, with a brass knob that hums faintly when you reach for it. You hesitate. Behind you, the landscape has gone quiet. The shifting stops. Every triangle, spiral, and melting cube seems to be watching, waiting to see what you'll do.

You open it.

On the other side is a room you recognize but have never been in. It smells like rain on warm pavement and your grandmother's kitchen at the same time. The walls are covered in canvases, hundreds of them, and each one shows a moment from your life you'd forgotten: the first time you saw snow, a stranger who smiled at you on a train, a song you used to hum before you knew the words. The paintings aren't still. They move gently, like memories do when you almost remember them.

In the center of the room is one more blank canvas.

A voice that sounds like your own, but older, or maybe younger, says, *"This one hasn't happened yet."*

You look down. The brush in your hand has changed color again. It's now the exact shade of something you've been hoping for but never said out loud.

You raise the brush, and just as the bristles touch the surface, the whole world tilts, the colors rush together like water down a drain, and you feel yourself floating upward...

...into the soft gray light of morning, the feeling of that color still on your fingertips, fading, fading, but not quite gone.
