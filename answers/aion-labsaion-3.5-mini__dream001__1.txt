You start to walk, hoping the city will somehow orient itself around you, the way places sometimes do in dreams—except it doesn't. The streets refuse to hold their shapes. You pass a bakery, turn a corner, and pass the same bakery again, except now it sells shoes, and the mannequins in the window are wearing your clothes.

You pull out your phone. The map app opens, but the map is a photograph of your childhood bedroom, and the blue dot that's supposed to be you is sitting on your old bed. You type the meeting address into the search bar, but the letters keep rearranging themselves into words like "later" and "too late" and "where were you even going."

A stranger approaches—a woman in a green coat, her face pleasantly indistinct, the way faces are when you try to look directly at them. You ask her where the Meridian Building is. She smiles and says, "Oh, you're already there," and gestures at the sky, which is now an office lobby. You can see through the glass doors of it, up into the clouds, where people in suits are seated around a long table. They're all turned toward the empty chair at the head.

You check your watch. The hour hand is spinning backward. The meeting, you realize with a cold flood of certainty, started forty minutes ago. Or it starts in five minutes. Or it started years ago and you've been late this entire time—late to something you never even knew was happening.

You start to run. The city cooperates for once, streets stretching like taffy to carry you forward, buildings leaning in to read the address crumpled in your fist. But the paper is blank now. It was always blank. You can't remember what the meeting is about. You can't remember who you're supposed to be there. You only know that everyone is waiting, and the chair is empty, and it's yours.

You burst through the glass doors into the clouds—and the room goes quiet. Twenty faces turn to you. Someone slides a document across the table. You look down at it.

It's a map of the city. The city you were just in. With a red circle drawn around a small blue door, and one word written beneath it in your own handwriting:

*Start here.*

You wake up. Your alarm is going off. You have eleven minutes.

And you can't remember where you're supposed to be.
