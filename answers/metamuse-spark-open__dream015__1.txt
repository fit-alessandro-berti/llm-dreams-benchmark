Despite the hardships, you decide to build a garden.

Not just for food — for color.

You find an abandoned rooftop in the middle of the ruined city, where the wind still carries ash, but the sun breaks through the clouds in perfect, golden shafts. Someone before you left behind a few rusted cans, a cracked bathtub, and a faded packet of seeds clutched in a skeleton's hand like treasure.

You take the seeds.

Day after day in the dream, you carry water up countless stairs from the old rain collector below. You chase away the crows. You pull weeds that grow through concrete. The other survivors think you're wasting precious water and time.

"What good is a flower when the world is ending?" one asks you.

But then — the first green sprout pushes through the dark soil.

And then another. And another.

Soon, the rooftop is alive. Tomatoes redden in the sun, beans climb the old antenna, and wildflowers bloom impossibly bright against the gray ruins. The scent carries on the wind, and people follow it. First one, then a few, then a small crowd.

They don't come to take. They come to see. To sit among something living. To remember what the world felt like before.

And you realize in the dream — the danger is still out there, the world is still broken, but you decided to grow something anyway. And now, others are deciding to stay and help you tend it.

You wake up just as someone hands you a fresh, warm tomato, the sun on your face.

Would you like me to continue the dream and explore what happens as the garden grows?
