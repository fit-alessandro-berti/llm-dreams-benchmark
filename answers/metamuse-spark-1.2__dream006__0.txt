You begin to speak, but no sound comes out at first.

Your mouth opens, and for a strange, suspended second, you feel the familiar panic of dreams - *you have no notes, you have no prepared speech, you don't even remember what you were supposed to talk about.*

The crowd doesn't seem to notice. They are still looking at you, patiently, kindly. Their faces are a little blurry around the edges, like watercolor.

You try again, and this time your voice comes out, but it's not quite your voice. It's lighter, like it's echoing slightly.

> "Hello," you say.

And as soon as you say it, the word doesn't just hang in the air - you can *see* it. The word "Hello" floats out of your mouth in soft, golden letters, drifting up toward the lights above the stage. The audience gasps, then laughs with delight. A child in the front row reaches up and gently bats at the word as it floats by.

Suddenly you aren't nervous anymore. You understand the rules of this dream.

You speak again, more confidently this time:

> "I was going to tell you something important... but I forgot what it was. So instead, I'll tell you what I remember from the dream before this."

And as you speak, every word you say becomes something. "Ocean" becomes a small wave that ripples across the stage. "Light" becomes a flock of little paper lanterns that rise from your palms. "Home" becomes the warm smell of rain and coffee that fills the entire auditorium.

The crowd isn't just listening anymore - they are *inside* the speech with you. You realize the audience isn't made of strangers at all. Every face is someone you've ever loved, ever passed on the street, ever seen in another dream - all watching you with total, unconditional attention.

You feel weightless. The podium dissolves under your hands. The stage floor feels soft, like standing on a cloud. You step forward, right to the edge, and you aren't giving a speech at all anymore - you're having a conversation without needing to be perfect.

You take a deep breath to say one last thing, and just as you do, you feel that gentle, familiar tug - the feeling of waking up. The lights get brighter and brighter until they're not stage lights anymore, but morning sunlight through a window.

You wake up with the echo of your own voice still in your ears, and for a moment, you can still see that golden "Hello" hanging in the air.
