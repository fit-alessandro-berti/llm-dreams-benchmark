...and the floor beneath you suddenly turns into a giant keyboard.

The host — a tall woman holding an umbrella indoors, even though it's raining *upwards* from the carpet — smiles and announces the first challenge.

"Type your way across. But only with words you're afraid to say out loud."

The other competitors start frantically typing with their feet: *PERFECT. FEARLESS. EXPERT. QUALIFIED.* Their bridges collapse under them.

You take a shaky step.

I... *tap.*
Don't... *tap.*
Know... *tap.*
Yet... *tap.*

A bridge made of honest, glowing letters forms under you, and you make it across.

The second challenge is an interview. You walk into a small, round room and sit down at a table. Across from you are three interviewers: you at seven years old, swinging your legs, you as a teenager, pretending not to care, and you at eighty, with kind eyes and your exact laugh.

The seven-year-old asks, "Do you still think it's fun?"

The teenager asks, "Are you just doing this to look impressive?"

The eighty-year-old just asks, quietly, "Will you be proud that you tried?"

You don't have perfect answers. Your voice cracks. But you answer all three truthfully, and the room fills with warm afternoon light, and the door unlocks itself.

For the final challenge, you're placed in a maze with all the other competitors. In the center, floating above a pedestal, is a golden key with a ribbon that reads: **DREAM JOB - ONE WINNER ONLY.**

Everyone starts running. Walls shift. Staircases go sideways. Someone gets stuck in a room filling with unsent emails. Someone else is lost in a hallway of mirrors where every reflection is saying, "You're too late."

You could run for the key. You almost do.

But instead, you stop. You help pull the first person out of the emails. You hold up a mirror for the second so they can see only their real self. One by one, the others stop running too. You solve the maze together — lifting each other over walls too high to climb alone, shouting directions, laughing when the maze tries to trick you by turning into your childhood school, then an office, then a forest.

When you finally reach the center together, the single golden key splits — not breaks, *blooms* — into a key for each of you, each one a different shape and color.

The host appears again, her umbrella now blooming with flowers.

"Congratulations," she says. "The prize was never about defeating everyone else. We were looking for someone who could become who they needed to be, without leaving themselves — or anyone else — behind."

She hands you your key. It's warm. It fits perfectly in your palm.

You turn it in a door that wasn't there a second ago, and step into your dream job.

The office overlooks the clouds. Your desk is already there, with a cup of tea made exactly how you like it, a window open to a breeze that smells like rain and new paper. On the desk is a nameplate, blank at first, until letters slowly write themselves across it — not just a title, but a promise you made to yourself a long time ago.

You sit down, smile, and get to work.

And just as you pick up your pen... you hear a soft beeping.

*BEEP. BEEP. BEEP.*

You open your eyes. You're back in your bed, morning light coming through the curtains, your alarm going off.

The dream is fading, like dreams do.

But the key still feels warm in your hand.
