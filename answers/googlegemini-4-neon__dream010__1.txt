At first, it looks like a normal corporate assessment center—glass walls, humming fluorescent lights, and sharp-suited rivals clutching leather portfolios. You don’t actually know the exact title of the dream job, only that it comes with a corner office overlooking an ocean that doesn't exist on any map, and that winning it will somehow make everything in your life click into place.

The first challenge is a timed exam, except when you open the booklet, the questions are written in a language made entirely of sheet music, and your pencil keeps bending like warm licorice. Around you, the other candidates are writing furiously. One of them is your seventh-grade math teacher; another is a remarkably focused golden retriever wearing a silk tie. Panic rises in your chest—until you realize that if you hum softly, the notes rearrange themselves into words you understand. You finish just as the ceiling dissolves into a gray, overcast sky.

Suddenly, you are sprinting down an endless hotel hallway for the group presentation round. You’re carrying your resume, which has folded itself into a fragile paper crane that keeps flapping weakly, trying to escape your hands. You reach the conference room doors, only to realize you’re barefoot and wearing one winter glove. Nobody seems to notice.

Inside, the room is knee-deep in clear, warm water, and the conference table floats gently in the center. The judges sit on the far side—three tall figures whose faces are obscured by the glare of afternoon sunlight streaming through floor-to-ceiling windows. 

"Convince us," the middle judge says, their voice echoing like a distant train.

You open your mouth to recite your qualifications, to talk about your five-year plan, but all that comes out is the sound of wind moving through pine trees. Mortified, you brace for disqualification. Instead, the judges lean forward, captivated. You try again, gesturing toward the glowing paper crane now perched calmly on your shoulder, and out pours a vivid slideshow projected straight onto the surface of the water—every half-finished idea you’ve ever loved, every quiet triumph, every strange detour you thought was a mistake, all fitting together like clockwork.

The rivals fade into the background like fog lifting off a lake. 

The middle judge stands and reaches across the vast table, which shrinks to the width of a single step. They place a heavy brass key in your palm. It feels warm, humming faintly with possibility. 

"Welcome aboard," they say. "Your office is right through there."

You turn toward a wooden door standing free in the middle of the water, no walls attached to it. Your heart swells with an overwhelming, weightless relief. You slide the key into the lock, turn the knob, and pull it open—

A blinding rush of salt air and golden light spills out, carrying the faint, insistent chiming of a melody you almost recognize. You take one step across the threshold, looking down at the official badge pinned to your chest to finally read what your dream job is, just as the letters begin to blur and the chiming sharpens into the steady beep of your morning alarm.
