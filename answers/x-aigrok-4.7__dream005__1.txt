You weave through the crowd and the conversations keep shifting under your feet. One cluster is arguing, very seriously, about whether the punch bowl is actually a small inland sea. Another group is wearing name tags that only show other people’s names, and they keep introducing you as someone who just left. A third circle is silent except for the clink of glasses, all of them listening hard to a story you haven’t told yet.

You spot a familiar jacket near the far wall and start toward it, but the room lengthens the way hallways do in dreams. Someone laughs behind you and says your name in the voice of the person you’re looking for. When you turn, it’s a stranger holding your coat, already sure you’ll want it later. The lights dim for a toast no one announced, and in the brief dark you feel a hand find yours—warm, known—before the room brightens again and the crowd parts just enough to show them waiting, as if they’d been there the whole time, smiling like the dream had only been stalling.
