You feel a storm approaching, and the forest seems to lean in with you. The light turns the color of old silver. Leaves flip their pale undersides, and the first cold drops strike the path like someone tapping a window.

You duck beneath a wide cedar. Its branches knit overhead, and the rain comes all at once—soft at first, then a steady hush that drowns the birds. Thunder rolls far off, more like a door closing in another room than anything meant to frighten you. The air smells of wet bark and crushed fern.

Between flashes you notice a narrow deer trail you hadn’t seen before, shining where the water runs along it. It leads downhill toward a clearing where the rain falls thinner, almost gold where a break in the cloud lets a little light through. You step out from the cedar. The storm is still there, loud and close, but it no longer feels like it is chasing you. It feels like the forest breathing, and you walk on with your hood up, listening.
