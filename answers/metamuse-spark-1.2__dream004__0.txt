Here is how your dream continues:

You are in a world where colors and shapes are constantly changing. As you explore this surreal landscape, you come across a blank canvas and it is floating in mid-air, waiting for you.

It’s impossibly white — whiter than anything else in this shifting world. While the hills behind you ripple from purple to gold and the trees bend into spirals and cubes, the canvas remains perfectly still, perfectly empty.

As you step closer, a paintbrush appears in your hand. It has no paint on it.

You hesitate, then touch the brush to the canvas. The moment the bristles make contact, color bleeds out — not onto the canvas, but *out of it*. A deep ocean blue pours from your single brush stroke, spilling into the sky. You make another stroke, this one jagged and quick, and the ground beneath your feet shudders and folds into sharp, crystal mountains.

You realize — you aren’t painting *on* the canvas. You are painting the world *with* it.

You swirl the brush in a wide circle, and a warm, honey-yellow sun blooms overhead, melting the strange shapes into soft, rolling clouds. You dot the canvas with specks of green, and instantly, a field of glowing flowers blossoms around your feet, opening and closing in time with your breath.

Curious, you place your palm flat against the canvas. It’s warm. It pulses, like a heartbeat. And for a second, the canvas shows *you* — not as you are, but as shifting color and light, the way everything else is in this world.

Then you lift your hand, and the image is gone. The canvas is blank again, patiently waiting for your next idea, for whatever you want this dream to become.
