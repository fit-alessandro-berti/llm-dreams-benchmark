...in a script you almost recognize. Every other character looks familiar, then dissolves into something like insect legs.

*Question 1: Define everything.*
*Question 2: What did the professor say on the first day, when everyone was talking over her?*
*Question 3: Translate this sentence into your mother tongue, then answer it without speaking.*

You look up for help. The clock above the chalkboard has no hands—only a single rotating eye, and it's watching you specifically. Down the row, another student scribbles furiously with a pencil that keeps growing, stretching toward the ceiling like a bamboo stalk. He glances over and smiles with too many teeth, the way people do in dreams—as if politeness itself has been swapped for something adjacent to politeness.

You reach for your pen. Good—the pen is here. One thing makes sense. But when you write, the ink lifts off the page as small gray moths that flutter upward and crowd against the fluorescent lights.

Flip the page. Another hundred faces staring up at you from the paper—no. Questions. They were questions. Look again quickly before—

Then you find the last sheet, folded in half, still warm, as though someone had been sitting on it a moment ago.

*Extra credit, worth 100% of your grade: Why did you wait so long?*

And suddenly the knowing arrives, dense and cold like swallowed stones: you've been enrolled in this course your whole life. You never attended a single day. Every answer was handed out years ago, in rooms you left too early, and the exam has been running the entire time, quietly waiting for you to sit down, and the clock—

Your childhood nickname crackles over the PA system. The one only your grandmother ever used.

"Time," it says, gently.

You wake with your fist clenched around a pencil that isn't there.
