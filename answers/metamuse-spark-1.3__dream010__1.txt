Of course — let's slip back into that dream:

---

You are participating in a competition where the grand prize is your dream job. As the competition progresses, you face various challenges **and the floor beneath you just... dissolves.**

Not in a scary way — in a dream way. The stage turns into soft, warm clouds. You're barefoot. Everyone else in the competition is there too, floating on their own little clouds, all wearing name tags that keep changing names.

A voice over a loudspeaker that sounds suspiciously like your favorite teacher announces:

**"Challenge One: The Maze of Almost-You's"**

You walk through doors made of giant filing cabinets. Inside each room is a version of you doing a job you *almost* wanted — you the accountant, you the astronaut, you the barista who remembers everyone's orders. They all hand you a piece of paper. Only when you unfold them together do they form a map that says: *Keep going.*

**"Challenge Two: The Interview With No Questions"**

You enter a room where three owls in blazers sit behind a desk. They just stare at you. You realize you're supposed to *ask* them questions, not answer. So you do — "What do you love about this work? What scares you? Can I laugh here?" With each honest question, one owl turns into a human and smiles. You pass without even trying to be perfect.

**"Final Challenge: The Mirror Room"**

It's just you and a mirror. But the reflection is 10 seconds ahead of you, already confident, already doing your dream job — whatever that looks like to *you*. Paintbrush in hand, or code on the screen, or kids laughing, or lights on a stage. It reaches out a hand.

That's when you understand: the competition was never against the other dreamers. It was against the voice that said "you're not ready yet."

You take your future-self's hand, step *through* the mirror —

and you're standing on a stage bathed in golden light. Confetti made of offer letters rains down. The loudspeaker crackles one last time, now in YOUR voice:

*"Congratulations. You've created the position."*

You wake up just before you read the job title, with that fizzy, almost-remembering feeling in your chest — and for the rest of the morning, everything feels a little more possible.
