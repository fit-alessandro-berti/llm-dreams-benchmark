...realize the pen in your hand has turned into a small, warm key. Your boss, whose face keeps shifting between people you half-remember, taps a watch that has no hands and says, "Due yesterday. Well, due *before* yesterday."

You look down at the project folder. It's empty, but it's getting heavier.

The office has changed, too. The cubicle walls have stretched into long hallways, and the ceiling is lost in a soft gray fog. Somewhere far off, a printer is singing in a low, mournful key.

You try to speak, but what comes out is, "I'll need a bigger calendar."

Your boss nods as if this is perfectly reasonable and points to the wall. A calendar unrolls down it, month after month, tumbling past the floor and into a stairwell that wasn't there a moment ago. "Take as many days as you need," they say. "They're all in the basement."

So you go down, the key glowing in your palm. Every door you pass is labeled with a day of the week, and from behind them come muffled sounds: a meeting, rain, someone laughing, a clock being wound. At the bottom is a door with no label. The key fits.

Inside is a quiet room with a single desk, a lamp, and a window looking out on a sunlit field. A note on the desk, in your own handwriting, reads:

*"You don't need more time. You need to decide what matters. Start there."*

The folder in your arms grows light. When you open it, there's one page with one sentence, and you know, the way you know things in dreams, that the sentence is the whole project.

You pick up the pen, which is a pen again, and begin to write. Behind you, the door clicks softly shut. The deadline, somewhere above, begins to dissolve like sugar in tea.

And then the alarm goes off, and you wake up, still holding the feeling of having *just* enough time.
