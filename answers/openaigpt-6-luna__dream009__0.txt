You wake up in a completely different era, with no modern technology around. As you explore your new surroundings, you realize you need to find your way home.

The village is all crooked roofs and muddy lanes, and everyone you meet insists it’s the year 1423. You ask for a map. A child offers you a piece of parchment covered in stars.

“Follow the one that doesn’t belong,” she says.

That night, you climb a hill and look up. The sky is crowded with unfamiliar constellations—except for one bright point of light, blinking steadily above the horizon. You begin walking toward it.

The road winds through a forest where the trees whisper your name. At dawn, you reach a lake smooth as glass. Your reflection looks back at you, but it’s dressed in clothes you’ve never seen before. Behind it, in the water, stands your bedroom door.

You reach for the handle.

When you wake, morning light is spilling across your room. For a moment, you can still hear the forest whispering. Then your phone buzzes on the bedside table, and the sound seems strangely ancient.
