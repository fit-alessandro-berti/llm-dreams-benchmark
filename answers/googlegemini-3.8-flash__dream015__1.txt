...build a lighthouse. 

Not by the sea—for the oceans have long since pulled back into cracked, salt-white basins—but on the highest precipice of a shattered glass tower, overlooking the endless expanse of the dust storms. 

In a world obsessed with the brutal arithmetic of survival—counting calories, hoarding ammunition, rationing drops of grey water—your decision makes no sense to the wanderers in the dunes. They call it madness to waste precious copper wire and cracked solar panels on something that cannot be eaten or fired from a barrel.

Yet night after night, you climb the rusted iron spine of the skyscraper. You drag up polished shards of mirrors salvaged from the husks of antique automobiles. You scavenge phosphorescent moss from deep subway tunnels and wire together dying batteries. 

And then, as a violet, ash-choked dusk swallows the horizon, you strike the spark. 

The light catches. Magnified by the mirrors, a brilliant, sweeping beam of warm amber cuts through the suffocating smog, carving a path miles into the dark. It sweeps across the ruined freeways, over the skeletal silhouettes of dead cities, reaching places where desperate people huddle in silence, afraid of the shadows.

In the dream, the light doesn't attract raiders; it disarms them. It pierces through the survivalist terror that turned humanity cold. Down below, you begin to see faint flickers responding—mirrors flashing in the dark, small campfires daring to light, distant figures turning their faces upward toward the warmth of the beam. 

You lean over the railing, the wind humming through the rusted rebar like a cello, carrying the scent of long-forgotten rain. You realize you aren't just signaling that someone is alive up here. 

You are reminding the world below that the dark is not the only thing left.
