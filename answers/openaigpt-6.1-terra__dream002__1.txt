Ahead, a small house appears where no house stood before. Its windows glow warmly. You hurry to the door, and it opens before you can knock.

Inside, a kettle whistles on the stove. A coat hangs by the door, still damp, though it is exactly like the one you’re wearing. On the kitchen table lies a note in your handwriting:

*Don’t be afraid of the thunder. It’s only the sky moving its furniture.*

You laugh, and the storm answers with a long, low rumble. Then you notice a tiny door beneath the table. It swings open, and a fox in a yellow raincoat steps out, carrying an umbrella.

“Good,” the fox says. “You’re just in time.”

“For what?”

The fox points to the window. Beyond the glass, the forest is slowly lifting into the air, roots and all.

“Someone has to show it where to land.”
