...reach down to steady the brass compass, only to find its glass missing and the needle pointing straight upward into the clouds.

The rain isn’t cold anymore; it falls heavy and warm, smelling faintly of cedar and static. You open your mouth to shout an order—*hard to port, drop the anchor*—but the wind catches the words before they leave your chest. All that emerges is a low, resonant hum that vibrates through the soaked timber beneath your boots.

Your crew doesn’t flinch. In the flashes of violet lightning, you notice their yellow slickers are buttoned high over empty collars, yet they nod in unison as if you’ve given them the clearest command in the world. Without a sound, they rush to the rigging, hauling on ropes that stretch up, endlessly up, disappearing into the belly of the thunderhead.

A wall of black water rises ahead, impossibly tall, blotting out the rest of the world. There is no time to steer around it. You wrap both hands around the spokes of the ship’s wheel and brace yourself, only to feel the polished wood soften beneath your palms like warm clay. Instead of crashing, the bow tilts upward and begins to climb the sheer, glassy face of the wave as if gravity has suddenly forgotten which way is down.

At the crest, the roar of the tempest vanishes all at once.

The ship breaks through the foam into a breathless, silver quiet. There is no ocean left—only an endless, mirror-still expanse of mist beneath a sky crowded with two pale moons. Looking around the deck, you see the crew has gone, leaving their raincoats draped neatly over the railings like shed skins. The sails fill with a gentle breeze you cannot feel, carrying the ship smoothly across the quiet haze toward a small, familiar doorway glowing on the horizon—just as the edges of the mist begin to brighten into morning.
