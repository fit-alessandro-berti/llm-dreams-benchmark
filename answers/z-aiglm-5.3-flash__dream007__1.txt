...walk out before I say something unforgivable. I grab my coat from the hook by the door—except it isn't my coat, it's the green jacket from third grade, sleeves already too short, and somehow I put it on anyway.

"I'm leaving," I say, but the words come out as smoke, curling up toward the ceiling fan, which spins far too slowly for how loud the wind sounds outside.

Behind me, my friend says my name. Not angrily—that's the worst part. They say it the way you'd call someone back across a dark parking lot, gently, as if the argument happened years ago instead of one sentence ago.

I turn around to answer, and now we're standing in their kitchen, except the kitchen is also my grandmother's kitchen, and there's a swimming pool where the table used to be. Our coffee cups drift past us, patient little boats. Whatever we were fighting about has slipped away entirely—I've lost the thread—but the anger is still there, humming in my chest like a refrigerator, searching for something new to attach itself to.

"It was never about the thing," I hear myself say.

The water rises to our knees, warm and deep blue. My friend nods slowly. "It never is," they agree, and then they hand me a fish, or possibly a phone ringing underwater, and I lift it to my ear—

—and I wake with the argument still clenched tight in my jaw, straining to remember what *the thing* was. It felt enormous. It mattered to both of us, I'm sure of it. But all morning, no matter which way I turn it over, all I can recall is the warmth of the water, and the way my name sounded in their mouth when they weren't angry anymore.
