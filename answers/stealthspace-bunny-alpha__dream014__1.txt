The woman in the red coat reaches for your hand. “We’re not going anywhere important,” she says. “We’re just going together.”

So you fall into step beside her. An old man carrying a newspaper leads the way, counting the turns under his breath. A courier with a bicycle basket full of letters walks backward in front of you, pointing out beautiful things: a cat on a windowsill, silver rain caught beneath a streetlamp, the first pale light between the buildings.

For a while, you have no idea where you are going. But the city no longer feels quite so loud.

The strangers lead you up a narrow staircase hidden behind a bakery, through a door that opens onto the rooftops. Above the chimneys, the streets unspool in every direction, glowing softly beneath a sky the color of peach skin.

“Where did you say you wanted to go?” the courier asks.

You realize you never said. You had been so focused on finding a way that you never stopped to ask yourself where you were trying to reach.

The woman in red squeezes your hand. “It doesn’t matter,” she says. “You can choose.”

And standing there, surrounded by strangers who became companions, you see a hundred possible paths. None of them feels frightening anymore.

Below you, the city breathes.

This time, when you step forward, you are not lost.

You are simply beginning.
