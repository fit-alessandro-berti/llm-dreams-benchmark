...float. Literally. Your feet lift gently off the stage as if gravity has simply forgotten about you. The crowd doesn't seem to notice. Their expectant faces remain calm, patient, as if you're a balloon that's always drifted up before a speech.



You look down and realize your notes have transformed into origami birds, each one flapping gently in your hands before launching themselves out over the audience. As they soar overhead, they release soft whispers — your speech, scattered into the air in no particular order. Some people in the crowd nod along. Others tilt their heads in confusion. A child in the third row giggles and reaches up to catch a crane carrying the words *"and in conclusion."*

You try to speak, but your voice comes out as a cello — deep, warm, resonant. The sound ripples through the auditorium like water. The audience begins to sway. The chairs around them dissolve into lily pads, and the floor becomes a vast, still lake reflecting a sky full of swirling galaxies.

You float higher now, your body weightless, your cello-voice echoing across the water. The notes bloom into flowers wherever they land — white lotuses opening in slow motion on the surface of the lake.

And then you notice: there is no microphone stand, no stage, no auditorium. There never was. You are simply *speaking*, and every person in this impossible world is listening with their eyes closed, smiling like they are hearing the most important song ever written — one that only exists right now, in this moment, and will never be heard again.

The lily pads gently rock.

The galaxies turn.

And you feel, in your drifting, floating, impossible dream-self, that you have said exactly the right thing — though you could never say what it was.

Then slowly, the lake begins to warm, and the light turns golden, and you realize the dream is dissolving at the edges like morning fog —

...and somewhere, a real alarm clock is beginning to chime.
