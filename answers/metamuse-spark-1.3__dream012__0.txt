Here's how your dream continues:

... and you **plant your feet wide on the slick deck, grip the wheel with both hands, and feel strangely calm.**

The rain stings your face and the salt spray burns your lips. The wood groans under you, the masts creak, and lightning splits the black clouds into jagged rivers of white. Your crew is shouting, scared, eyes all on you.

And you shout back — not in fear, but with a steady, clear voice that somehow carries over the roar:

"Lower the mainsail! Tie everything down! Light the lanterns!"

As soon as you speak, the panic breaks. They move. Ropes fly, boots thud, lanterns flicker to life one by one along the deck like small suns.

The wave in front of you rises — impossibly tall, a dark wall of water. Instead of turning away, you steer *straight into it*. For a heartbeat, everything goes silent and weightless underwater, blue-green and muffled.

Then you burst through the other side.

Behind the wave, the sea is suddenly glass-calm. The storm clouds part. The sky above is full of low, brilliant stars, and beneath the water you can see those same stars reflected — as if you're sailing through the sky itself.

Your crew laughs in disbelief. Someone claps you on the shoulder. You realize you knew all along the storm wouldn't last.

You breathe in the cool, clean air, and let the ship drift.
