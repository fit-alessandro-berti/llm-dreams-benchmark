

You are the captain of a ship in the middle of a turbulent sea. Your crew looks to you for guidance as the storm worsens, and you feel the wheel grow warm beneath your hands, as though the ship itself is breathing.

Thunder rolls across the sky like a warning, and the waves rise into dark mountains, but your voice remains steady. You call out orders into the wind, and your crew moves as one, trusting you even as the masts groan and the sea seems to open beneath you.

Then the lightning flashes, and for one impossible moment you see that the water is not water at all, but a vast, churning sea of memories—faces, voices, forgotten rooms, old fears, and unfinished promises all swirling together beneath the ship.

You realize then that the storm is not chasing you. It is answering you.

You release the wheel and walk to the bow. The rain strikes your face like cold silver, and you speak directly into the storm:

“I am afraid, but I will not be ruled by fear.”

The wind stills for a heartbeat.

“I am lost, but I can still choose my course.”

The waves begin to slow.

“I am dreaming,” you say at last, “but even in dreams, I can wake.”

The clouds part.

The sea turns from black to deep blue, then to glass. Above you, the sky softens into dawn, and the crew gathers behind you, silent, watching. When you turn to face them, their faces are no longer strangers. They are the parts of you that had waited long to be seen: your courage, your grief, your hope, your doubt.

They bow their heads gently, and you understand that you were never only their captain. You were the sea, the storm, the ship, and the shore.

The vessel drifts forward into the light, and when the first ray of sun touches the deck, the dream begins to fade—but not into darkness. It fades into morning, into breath, into your own quiet voice saying:

“You have guided yourself home.”
