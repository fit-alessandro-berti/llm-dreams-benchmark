You start to ask for directions, but everyone you meet gives you a different answer. A woman in a silver coat points toward the river. A boy with a paper crown says the meeting is underground. An old man simply checks his watch and tells you, “You’re already late.”

You hurry down streets that keep changing their names. The buildings lean close overhead, their windows glowing like watchful eyes. Your phone has no signal, and the map on its screen shows only a blank blue ocean.

At last, you find a narrow door between a bakery and a telephone booth. A brass plaque bears your name. You pull the handle and step inside.

The room is full of people sitting around a long table. They all look up as you enter. At the far end, your own chair is empty. Beside it lies a sealed envelope, warm to the touch.

Someone asks, “Shall we begin?”

You open the envelope. Inside is a small brass key—and a note in your handwriting:

**The meeting is wherever you decide to wake up.**
