When you reach for the brush, it turns into a small blue bird. The bird settles on your wrist and asks, “What color were you before you had a name?”

You try to remember. Around you, the hills fold into triangles, then soften into enormous sleeping cats. The sky changes from violet to the particular yellow of a kitchen you haven’t seen since childhood.

“I don’t know,” you tell the bird.

“Good,” it says. “Then we can begin.”

It dips its beak into your shadow and touches the canvas. A doorway appears—not a picture of a doorway, but a real one, with warm light beneath it.

On the other side, someone is washing dishes. You recognize the sound, although you cannot remember whose hands made it.

You open the door.

Inside is a room full of unfinished things: half-knitted clouds, a staircase ending in a question mark, letters addressed to people you almost became. At the center stands a child wearing your shoes.

“You’re late,” the child says.

“For what?”

The child points to a window. Beyond it, the sun is waiting just below the horizon, round and green.

“It won’t rise until you choose.”

You look back at the canvas. The bird has become a paintbrush again. You pick it up and paint a little red boat.

Immediately, the room fills with water.

The child climbs aboard, takes your hand, and makes room for you beside them. As the boat drifts through the window, the green sun rises, and every unfinished letter unfolds into a sail.
