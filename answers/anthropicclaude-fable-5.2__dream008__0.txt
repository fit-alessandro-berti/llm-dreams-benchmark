...open the project folder, only to find it's full of water. Not wet paper, just water, perfectly folder-shaped, and somehow you understand that this is the brief.

"It's due yesterday," your boss says, checking a watch with no hands. She doesn't seem angry. She's standing very still, the way furniture stands, and you realize she's been waiting there since before the building was built.

You sit down at your desk, but your desk is now the desk from your childhood bedroom, stickers still on the drawer. The keyboard has too many keys. Some of them are letters you've never seen before, yet your fingers know them. You begin typing, and with each keystroke the office gets a little longer, the far wall drifting away like a boat leaving a dock.

Your coworkers gather around to help, though they're people from other parts of your life: a third-grade classmate, a dentist you saw once, a neighbor's dog wearing a lanyard. They all nod seriously at your screen, which shows a spreadsheet of the ocean.

The clock on the wall is melting, not dramatically, just slowly, like a candle. You notice that the more you rush, the slower your hands move, as if the air were honey. So you stop and take a breath.

The deadline, it turns out, is a door. It's been behind you the whole time. Your boss gestures toward it, and now she's smiling, and she has your grandmother's voice when she says, "You finished this ages ago. We were just waiting for you to notice."

You turn the handle. On the other side is a field at dusk, and the project, whatever it was, is rising into the sky like a paper lantern, small and glowing and done.

Somewhere, far off, an alarm clock begins to sound like birdsong.
