You find yourself in a post-apocalyptic world, with resources scarce and dangers everywhere. Despite the hardships, you decide to leave the fortified settlement and search for a place whispered about in old radio transmissions—a valley where rain still falls and seeds can grow.

You travel beneath a sky the color of rust, carrying a dented canteen, three matches, and a small envelope of seeds that everyone else believes are dead. Along the road, abandoned cars lie half-buried in ash, their windows reflecting movements that vanish whenever you turn to look. At night, distant creatures call to one another through the ruins, sounding almost like people saying your name.

On the fourth day, you encounter a child standing beside a broken railway signal. She wears a bright yellow coat untouched by dust and claims she knows the way to the valley. You do not entirely trust her, but she leads you through an underground station where hundreds of clocks cover the walls. Every clock shows a different time, except one, which begins ticking only when you approach.

Behind it, you find a hidden door.

Beyond the door is not a valley but a vast greenhouse glowing beneath the earth. Trees stretch toward artificial sunlight, clear water runs between rows of vegetables, and birds you thought were extinct flutter among the branches. Yet the greenhouse is completely empty of people. In its center stands a control panel with two switches. One will open the greenhouse to every surviving settlement. The other will seal it forever, protecting its resources from violence and greed.

As you hesitate, the girl removes her yellow hood. Her face is yours, only much younger.

“You have made this choice before,” she says.

You look down and realize the envelope in your hand is now full of fresh, living seeds. Instead of touching either switch, you smash the control panel with a stone. The doors throughout the greenhouse unlock at once, and alarms echo through the tunnels.

When you wake, you are standing in the rain outside your settlement. Green shoots are pushing through the cracked ground around your feet, and in the distance, people are running toward you—not with weapons, but with empty bowls held open to the sky.
