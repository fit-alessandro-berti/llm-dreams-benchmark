

…walk toward the nearest window.

It is no longer a window, not really. It has become a mouth made of glass, and the sky outside it is the color of old brass. You and your friend both stop talking, because in the dream you both understand, without explanation, that the argument has changed into weather.

You raise your hand to touch the glass, and the room bends around you like paper in fire. Your friend’s face appears in the pane, but it is a little younger than you remember, and their eyes are full of rain that does not fall.

“I’m not angry at you,” they say, though their voice comes from somewhere behind you.

You turn, but the room is now a hallway stretching too far in both directions. Your footsteps make no sound. The walls are lined with objects you own, except every object is frozen mid-movement: a cup pouring itself into the air, a book falling page by page, a pair of shoes stepping through the wall.

Your friend is gone.

Then, from the hallway behind you, you hear your own voice saying, “You’re the one who changed.”

You spin around, and there is no one there, but the hallway behind you is now a narrow staircase spiraling upward into a darkness that smells like rain on hot pavement.

You begin to climb.

With each step, the argument returns in fragments: one sentence, then a memory, then a silence that used to mean something. By the time you reach the top, the disagreement is no longer about what happened. It is about what you each chose not to say.

At the top of the stairs is a door made of your friend’s handwriting. You do not need to read it to know it says: *I’m sorry, but not for what you think.*

You put your palm against the door.

It opens.

Beyond it is a small garden at dusk. Your friend is sitting on a bench under a tree with silver leaves. They look up, and for the first time, you are not in the middle of anything. You are only two people in a quiet place, and the dream softens like a sound being lowered.

Your friend says, “Do you want to talk about it?”

And you do.
