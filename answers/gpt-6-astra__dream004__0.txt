You reach for the brush. It darts away, shy as a fish, then settles into your hand.

Your first stroke becomes a doorway.

Beyond it, rain falls upward through a room full of sleeping trees. Their roots curl around small brass beds, and their leaves rustle with voices you almost recognize. One tree wakes as you step inside.

“You’re late,” it says gently.

“For what?”

It shakes a silver apple into your palm. Inside the skin, something ticks.

You want to ask another question, but the floor folds itself into a staircase. You climb past windows looking out on different versions of the same afternoon: a picnic abandoned in the rain, a train pulling away, someone turning at the sound of your voice.

At the top sits a small blue dog with your handwriting all over its fur.

It brings you a ball.

You throw it down the stairs. The dog bolts after it, and for a moment everything is ordinary—the scrape of claws, the thumping bounce, your hand still raised.

Then the ball rises past you, enormous and luminous. The dog follows, swimming through the air.

You look down at the apple. It has stopped ticking.

Somewhere nearby, a kettle begins to whistle. You remember, with sudden urgency, that you left a cup waiting, though you cannot remember in which world.

The brush is still in your hand.

You paint a handle in the air and turn it.
