The puzzles grow stranger. In one chamber, you must weigh three shadows on a brass scale, though the lightest shadow casts the darkest pool beneath it. In another, a clock with thirteen hours asks you which of them is lying. You solve both by refusing to trust your first answer, and each time, somewhere far ahead, a heavy door opens.

At last you reach the center. There is no monster there—only a desk, a lamp, and a small machine made of interlocking gears. Whenever you solve a puzzle, the machine clicks and produces another. Around it, stacked to the ceiling, are thousands of completed puzzles, all in your own handwriting.

You look back and understand: every solution has built another section of the maze.

On the desk sits one final puzzle: two silver rings, seamlessly joined. Your fingers ache to separate them. You can already see the beginning of the method, the delicate chain of reasoning that might lead to the answer. But beyond the desk, where there should be a wall, you notice a narrow door with no lock at all.

You try the handle. It does not move.

A voice from inside the machine whispers, “One puzzle remains.”

So you sit before the rings. Minutes pass, or years. Then, with enormous effort, you place them back on the desk without touching them again.

The machine stops.

Behind you, the maze gives a long, weary sigh. Its walls unfold like paper, revealing not corridors but a quiet field beneath a paling sky. When you try the door once more, it opens easily.

Just before you wake, the machine clicks a final time and prints a slip of paper. You catch only the words written on it:

**NOT EVERY LOCK IS A REQUEST.**

You wake with the curious, peaceful feeling that somewhere inside you, a door has been left open—and a mystery has been allowed to remain alive.
